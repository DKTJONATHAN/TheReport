Right, so the Central Bank of Kenya is out here telling us not to worry about El Niño, which apparently is going to make things biblical from October 2026 into early 2027. They're also tossing in "Middle East shocks" for good measure. Apparently, our forex buffer is so robust, we can just shrug it all off. Sounds familiar, doesn't it? Like that time they told us inflation was down while we were still struggling to afford ugali. Ruto's 'Stable' Economy: Inflation Down, Cartels Still Cashing In? indeed.

They're painting this rosy picture of resilience, of being able to withstand external pressures and natural disasters. It’s the same song and dance we always get. When the rains come, it's always a surprise, even when forecasters like the Meteorological Department have been shouting about it for ages. And "Middle East shocks"? That's just the latest excuse for whatever economic pain is coming our way.

You'd think with all this talk of buffers and resilience, the average Kenyan would feel a bit more secure. But let's be honest, when has that ever happened? We're the ones who will be footing the bill, one way or another, whether it's through higher taxes, inflated prices, or just the sheer inconvenience of a country that seems perpetually unprepared.

And while we're talking about preparations, remember how we were promised better infrastructure with the SGR to Kisumu? More tracks, more debt, and for what? Let's just say the "same old story" is a recurring theme in this country. So, when the CBK talks about weathering storms, just remember who actually feels the downpour.

The Eastleigh Voice might be reporting this with all the seriousness of a national crisis, and perhaps it is, in its own way. But for those of us living on the ground, it's just another reminder that while the suits in their air-conditioned offices might have a plan on paper, the reality on the streets is often a lot messier, especially when the rains are coming.
