The so-called AI boom is being hailed as a technological miracle, but for the realists at the MAS, it is simply another inflationary parasite. This technological gold rush demands massive amounts of energy and infrastructure, driving up domestic costs while the promised productivity gains remain trapped in corporate pitch decks. Singapore’s economy is growing faster than expected, but this is the feverish growth of an overheating engine, not the steady progress of a healthy system. The positive output gap is widening, which is just central-bank-speak for a system that is running out of room to breathe.

Geopolitics has finally punctured the veil of globalized trade. Between the grinding attrition of the Middle East conflict and the return of the American tariff wall, the city-state is being squeezed from both ends. A 12.5 percent duty from the Trump administration is not a marginal impact; it is a signal that the era of frictionless commerce is dead and buried. The MAS talks of export diversification, which is a desperate hope that they can find new buyers before the trade war goes nuclear.

The failure of thirteen out of eighteen analysts to predict this move highlights the terminal blindness of the financial-expert class. These individuals are still operating on a pre-2020 playbook, expecting a return to a stability that has been structurally destroyed. While neighbors like Indonesia and Malaysia sit on their hands and hope for the best, Singapore is acknowledging that price risks will not moderate until 2027 at the earliest. In the world of global finance, three years is an eternity of uncertainty.

Ultimately, this calibrated adjustment is a pathetic attempt to anchor expectations in a world that has lost its moorings. When the MAS says it stands ready to curb excessive volatility, it is admitting that it no longer controls the narrative. We are entering a period where the only way to keep your head above water is to make your neighbors’ imports more expensive. This is the new global realist standard: a race to the top of a sinking ship, where a slightly stronger currency is the only life jacket left.
