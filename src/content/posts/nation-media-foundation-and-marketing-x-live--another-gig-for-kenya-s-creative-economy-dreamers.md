So, Kenya's big shot creatives apparently went to Cannes and didn't embarrass themselves. Mbithi Masya, a director with the fancy title of being the first Black African on some jury, comes back claiming we're all the same as the big boys. Really? Because last I checked, "talent" doesn't pay the rent or magically conjure up multi-million dollar budgets. It’s nice he felt good about himself, I suppose, but let's not get ahead of ourselves. This sounds like the usual Kenyan optimism that crumbles under the weight of actual reality, much like promises from politicians these days. Remember when we thought those anti-corruption reforms would actually mean something? [World Bank Demands Anti-Corruption Reforms in Kenya, Again](/posts/world-bank-demands-anti-corruption-reforms-in-kenya--again).

Now, Nation Media Group, bless their hearts, is getting all involved with this "Marketing X Live" thing. They want to be "ecosystem enablers," which I'm pretty sure is corporate speak for "let's be seen as relevant." Monica Ndungu from Nation Media seems thrilled to be in a room with "decision-makers." Fascinating. It's all about understanding the "ecosystem from within," she says. Translation: they want to schmooze clients and figure out how to sell more ads, but dressed up as supporting the "creative economy." They've been in the "creative economy" for 66 years, apparently. And how much of that has trickled down to the actual creators struggling to make a living?

And this campaign, "Paid Sick Leave for Cows"? A Grand Prix at Cannes. Apparently, it’s a revolutionary way to sell milk by solving a dairy farmer's problem. Groundbreaking. Megha Dutta, who worked on it, thinks it's changing the definition of creativity. Oh, really? Because last I checked, advertising is still about selling things, even if you slap a fancy, feel-good label on it. It’s not exactly a cure for the common cold, is it? It’s just another way to get consumers to feel good about a brand, by pretending it cares about cows. Makes you wonder what else they're calling "creativity" these days. Probably the same people who thought [Another Monday, More Madness](/posts/karoti-girls-horror--officer-shoots-wife--then-himself---another-monday--more-madness) was just a story.

The big takeaway from all this is that the lines are blurred, and advertising is now entertainment. Crazy Kennar is mentioned as an example of content so "natural" you don't even realize it's an ad. That's not creativity, that's manipulation, plain and simple. We're supposed to be inspired because some Kenyans won an award for a campaign about cows, and now Nation Media wants to be a "catalyst." All I see is a lot of talk about "connections" and "ecosystems" and "future of creativity," while the people actually doing the grunt work are still hoping for fair pay and reliable work. It’s a nice idea, this "creative economy," but let's see if these bigwigs actually manage to make it work for more than just themselves.
