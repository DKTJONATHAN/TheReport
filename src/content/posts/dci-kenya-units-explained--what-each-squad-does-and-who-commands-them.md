THE DCI'S SO CALLED ELITE UNITS: ALL SHOW, NO REAL GO!

WARNING: This is your official rundown of the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) and its various units. Don't expect miracles; just more of the same old song and dance, Nairobi style.

PHOTO: DCI Kenya

THE DCI is supposed to be the sharp end of law enforcement in Kenya, led by the esteemed Mohamed I. Amin. They’ve got their fancy specialized units for everything, from sniffing out terrorists with the Anti-Terrorism Police Unit (ATPU) under CP Johnstone Kola, to chasing down murderers with the Homicide Investigations Directorate. And let's not forget the Anti-Narcotics Unit (ANU), headed by Samwel Laboso, apparently battling the drug trade since 1983. It all sounds very professional, doesn't it? Like something you’d see on a cable TV drama, but this is Nairobi, darling. The reality is usually a bit more… complicated.

Then you have the Investigations Bureau, currently helmed by Francis Ndiema, which supposedly handles the big stuff: economic crimes, cybercrime, land fraud, you name it. They’ve even got a Serious Crime Unit for the really messy bits. And to top it all off, there's the Crime Research and Intelligence Bureau (CRIB) under Dennis Okadapau, supposedly predicting crime before it happens. It’s a whole ecosystem of acronyms designed to impress, no doubt. They claim to be all about gathering intelligence and preventing crime, but sometimes I wonder if they’re just gathering gossip. Remember when the fuel scandal saw top officials resign? What else is new? That's the kind of "prevention" we usually get.

The Economic and Commercial Crimes Unit (ECCU), the Financial Investigations Unit (FIU), the Banking Investigation Unit, and the Land Fraud Investigations Unit all sound like they’re tackling the real rot - the money and the land that disappears faster than a politician’s promise. They claim to trace financial trails and examine records to root out the bad guys. But how many of these big fish ever actually see the inside of a cell? It’s a bit like watching a cat chase a laser pointer; a lot of frantic movement, but does it ever catch anything substantial?

And the specialists keep piling up: units for human trafficking, organized crime, even a Counter-Organized Immigration Crime Team that sounds like it swallowed a whole dictionary. Then there’s INTERPOL coordination, which I assume means they can finally talk to the other countries when someone flees with our money. And if all else fails, they have the Forensic Services, led by a Director whose name isn't even important enough to mention, to provide the "scientific evidence." They’ve got everything from fingerprint analysis to bomb experts. It’s all very impressive on paper, a veritable smorgasbord of crime-fighting prowess.

But here's the kicker: at the end of the day, it all trickles down to the Regional Criminal Investigations Officers (RCIOs), County Criminal Investigations Officers (CCIOs), and Sub-County Criminal Investigations Officers (SCCIOs). These are the foot soldiers, the ones who are supposed to coordinate everything locally. They can apparently call in the specialists when things get too hot. So, while Amin and his top brass are busy running the show from their plush offices, it’s these guys on the ground who are supposed to be doing the actual dirty work. Let’s hope they’re getting paid enough for the headache.
