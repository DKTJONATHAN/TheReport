So, Farouk Kibet, one of Ruto’s closest men, has decided to start the 2032 presidential race right now. According to this report, he's throwing his weight behind Moses Wetang’ula. Yes, *that* Moses Wetang’ula, the Speaker who’s supposed to be neutral, now being positioned as Ruto’s successor. Apparently, his "national leadership experience" and "wisdom as National Speaker" are the magic ingredients. Don't laugh too hard; it's Kenya.

The whole shindig went down in Kakamega County, where Western Kenya leaders are suddenly united behind Wetang’ula’s presidential bid. It’s always the same story: regional leaders endorsing their own, hoping for a slice of the national pie. Wetang’ula himself was there, naturally, busy campaigning for Ruto's re-election next year. Because, you know, you've got to secure the present before dreaming of the future. He’s talking about sugarcane reforms and completed projects, the usual song and dance. It’s almost like they’re trying to convince us that things are magically better now.

Likuyani MP Innocent Mugabe is already laying out the "2032 strategies" while simultaneously pushing for Ruto's second term. It’s a neat trick, juggling present loyalty with future ambition. He claims Wetang’ula understands the region's political history and has a vision. Let’s hope that vision includes something more than just more promises and more political maneuvering. It’s a familiar script, and frankly, it’s getting stale. We've seen these "strategies" before, haven't we? It reminds me of the constant inheritance wars, where the focus is always on who gets what next, instead of actual progress. Navakholo MP Emanuel Wangwe chimed in, talking about safeguarding the "gains" from the Ruto administration by re-electing him. "Safeguarding gains" - that's a nice way of saying "keeping things the way they are, or slightly better, so we can continue to benefit." And then, the grand finale: preparing Wetang’ula for President in 2032. It’s a long game, but for the people footing the bill, it feels like a perpetual election cycle.

Wetang’ula also threw in a warning about "goonism" and the importance of the national ID card and voter registration. Wise words, I suppose, if you believe that your vote actually makes a difference in these grand schemes. It’s a lot of talk about unity and shaping destiny, while the same old faces are lining up for the next turn at the trough.

So, here we are. Ruto’s aide is backing Wetang’ula for 2032, Western leaders are rallying behind him, and everyone’s busy campaigning for the present and future. It’s a well-rehearsed play, and the audience is expected to applaud. But for those of us watching from the cheap seats, it’s just another reminder of how the political game is played in this country.
