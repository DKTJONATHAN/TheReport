ERIC OMOND'S GRAND STAND: QUINTUPLET MOTHER LOSES FOUR, HE'S NOW ON THE CASE











Here we go again. Eric Omondi, Nairobi's self-appointed messiah of the moment, has decided to grace us with his presence and his pronouncements. This time, the unfortunate subject is Mercy Kanini, a woman who has already endured more pain than most of us will in a lifetime. Birth five babies, a feat that should be a joyous occasion, and then watch four of them slip away? That's a tragedy, pure and simple. But leave it to Omondi to turn it into a stage show, complete with appeals and pronouncements from his "away" location.

He's trotting out the usual script: KNH needs to step up, hospital charges should be waived, and this poor woman is a "NATIONAL TREASURE." Honestly, the dramatics. While the sentiment of helping a grieving mother is commendable, the performative aspect of it all is exhausting. It's as if our suffering isn't real unless a celebrity is there to put a spotlight on it. This is the same city where we have people struggling with basic needs, yet a celebrity’s tears are somehow more valuable. We've seen similar spectacles before, like when [Kenyans Back From Haiti, But One Officer's Fate Remains a Sick Joke](/posts/kenyans-back-from-haiti--but-one-officer-s-fate-remains-a-sick-joke), where the focus quickly shifts, and the initial outpouring of support fades into forgotten news cycles.

Omondi's plea to transfer the deceased to the mortuary and arrange burials sounds like basic hospital protocol, not some grand gesture of salvation. And the surviving son? Of course, he needs care, but again, is this a job for the state hospitals and social services, or another one for Eric Omondi's fame machine? It's a sad reflection of our society when we rely on the whims of entertainers for fundamental human support and dignity.

He's already "on his way," no doubt planning the photo opportunities and the heartfelt interviews. Let's hope KNH actually does something concrete, and that this isn't just another fleeting moment of Omondi's charitable flash-in-the-pan. Because for Mercy Kanini, the real struggle - raising a child and coping with unimaginable loss - is just beginning, and it’s a marathon, not a celebrity-endorsed sprint.
