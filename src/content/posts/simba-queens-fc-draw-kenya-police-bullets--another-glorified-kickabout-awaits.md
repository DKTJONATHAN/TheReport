So, the grand draw for the 2026 CAF Women's Champions League CECAFA qualifiers has happened. Rwanda, bless their hearts, is hosting again. And guess who's staring down the barrel of another encounter? Tanzania's Simba Queens and our very own Kenya Police Bullets FC. This isn't exactly a seismic shift in the continental landscape, is it? It’s more like that same old rerun you’ve seen a hundred times, just with slightly different commentary.

The last time around, we saw Kenya Police Bullets showing some grit, even reaching the semi-finals in 2025. They even managed to beat Kampala Queens, though JKT Queens, the Tanzanian giants, sent them packing. And Simba Queens? They're the 2022 Zonal champs, which sounds impressive until you remember these are the CECAFA qualifiers - a tournament that often feels more like a local derby with pretensions of grandeur. This whole affair is being held in Kigali, because apparently, Rwanda is now the permanent venue for any regional footballing spectacle that needs a bit of a facelift.

Group C has Simba Queens, Kenya Police Bullets, and Burundi’s Top Girls Academy. Group A has the home team Rayon Sport WFC, and so on. It’s the usual suspects. The format guarantees that the top teams - or at least, the ones who manage to avoid complete humiliation - will shuffle into the semi-finals. It’s all about who can perform just well enough to avoid being completely embarrassed on the regional stage. Don't get your hopes up for groundbreaking football; it's more about the drama of who gets eliminated next.

Frankly, it all feels a bit like chasing shadows. We've seen this dance before. The hype builds, the matches are played, and then… well, then the cycle repeats. It’s a reminder that while some might see a pathway to glory, for most, it’s just another opportunity to collect participation medals and perhaps earn a bit of travel allowance. It’s reminiscent of how many of our local enterprises struggle to stay afloat, much like the issues plaguing the [Kenya Energy Sector's Fuel Data Scheme Exposed: More Corrupt Officials Playing Games](/posts/kenya-energy-sector-s-fuel-data-scheme-exposed--more-corrupt-officials-playing-games). The real competition is often just staying relevant.

And let’s not forget the past performances. JKT Queens, for instance, has been to two finals and won one. Rayon Sport WFC is heading to a final *right now* against them in Nairobi, having scraped through penalties. Kenya Police Bullets themselves were eliminated by JKT Queens in penalties. So, the draw has thrown together teams that have already danced this dance, with predictable outcomes. It’s less a draw and more a confirmation of who’s likely to bump into each other again, perhaps with the same result.

This whole CECAFA Women’s Champions League qualifier circus is just another reminder that while the ambition might be there, the consistent quality often isn’t. We’ll watch, we’ll groan, we’ll hope for a surprise, and then we’ll probably get exactly what we expect. Another year, another qualification tournament, and for most, another lesson in managing expectations.
