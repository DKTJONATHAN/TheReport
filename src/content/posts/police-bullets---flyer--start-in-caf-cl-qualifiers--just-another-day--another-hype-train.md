So, Kenya Police Bullets FC apparently "flew" off to a good start in their CAF Women’s Champions League qualifiers. They edged out some Tanzanian team called Simba Queens, which sounds like a pet store's nightmare. Faith Mboya and Martha Amunyolete apparently did the honours, while some new signing from Simba managed a consolation. Big whoop. It’s one game, people. Let’s see if they can keep this up, or if it’s just more hot air before the usual Nairobi reality check hits.

Coach David Vijago is already talking about winning the whole damn thing and qualifying for the big league. Bless his optimistic heart. He’s thankful for divine intervention, which is a nice way of saying he’s hoping luck bails him out when skill falters. This is football, not a sermon. Praying for strength? Maybe they should pray for better finishing or tighter defence. Remember when [Mobilising pension savings for development without risking workers’ futures](/posts/mobilising-pension-savings-for-development-without-risking-workers--futures)? That’s the kind of solid planning we need, not just hopeful wishes.

Then there’s striker Terry Engesha Obunyu, who claims they "overpowered" Simba in midfield. Right. And I suppose they also discovered the secret to world peace while they were at it. They’re warning their next opponents, Top Girls Academy, to expect a "tough competition." Oh, you don't say. It's a tournament, not a tea party. Everyone’s expecting tough competition. The real question is, can they deliver consistently, or will this just be another flash in the pan?

The team is in Group C with Simba and Burundi’s Top Girls Academy. Winner goes to the semis. Sounds simple enough, but in Kenyan football, simple usually gets complicated. They missed qualification last season, so this "step further" is basically a desperate scramble to avoid another year of "almosts." Let's see if their "underdog" bravado translates into actual silverware, or if it’s just more noise before the silence.

This whole CECAFA qualifier business is just a stepping stone, apparently. They want the real CAF Women’s Champions League. It’s a nice dream. But Nairobi is built on dreams that often end up as broken promises and traffic jams. Let’s just hope this particular dream doesn't lead to too many disappointed fans when the dust settles.
