COMESA Pushes Regional Economic Unity... For Whom?

WARNING: More summits, more talk, same old problems. Don't hold your breath for prosperity.

The suits are back in town, patting themselves on the back for "encouraging improvements" while the real issues of high debt and pathetic revenue collection are still staring them in the face. They're talking about a monetary union, a common central bank, a common currency - sounds like a recipe for more bureaucracy and less actual progress to me. They've even extended Stage I of this grand plan to 2027. Because, you know, rushing things is never the African way.

This "peer review mechanism" sounds like a fancy way for them to tell each other they’re not doing enough, while doing very little themselves. They promise businesses easier planning and bigger markets, but let's be real, for most of us, it just means more paperwork and maybe a slightly less painful currency conversion if we’re lucky enough to travel. They're also dabbling in AI and digital inclusion. Sure, why not add more buzzwords to the mix when fundamental economic stability is still a distant dream? It’s like trying to build a skyscraper on a foundation of sand, just like how some markets seem to have an artificial resurrection, only to suffocate real growth later.

The stark reality is that consistent progress on convergence targets, especially the fiscal ones, has been a struggle. This Nairobi meeting is a loud and clear message: genuine integration needs sustained reforms and disciplined public finances, not just fancy pronouncements. The big talk of becoming an economic powerhouse and creating opportunities sounds good on paper, but history shows us that when governments commit, they often get distracted by the next shiny object or simply lack the discipline to follow through.

So, while they’re busy dreaming of unified economic powerhouses and millions of citizens prospering, let's keep our feet firmly on the ground. The promises of more jobs, lower costs, and greater access to goods are a long way off. For now, it’s just another summit, another round of meetings, and another chance for the chosen few to strategize while the rest of us wait for a trickle-down that might never come.
