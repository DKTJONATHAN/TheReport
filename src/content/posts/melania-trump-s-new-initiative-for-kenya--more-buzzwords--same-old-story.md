So, Melania Trump, bless her heart, has a new "initiative" called Fostering the Future Together. It's all about AI, EdTech, and digital literacy for kids in ten countries, and guess who's on the guest list? Kenya, of course. Intel is supposedly scaling up its programs here, promising our young ones a taste of artificial intelligence and the skills they'll need for this "increasingly technology-driven economy." Sounds grand, doesn't it? Like a shiny new toy for kids who are already struggling to get a decent meal.

This whole song and dance happened on the sidelines of some UN General Assembly, where FTFT apparently snagged seven new "private-sector partnerships." Seven partnerships, huh? Let's see how many of those translate into actual boots on the ground, beyond a few select schools in leafy suburbs. They talk about giving Kenyan learners "greater exposure," but for now, it's all vague promises. Specific programs, schools, number of beneficiaries - all still in the "to be announced" file, which in Kenyan terms usually means "never."

They're throwing around numbers like $4 million for an AI education program and $2.75 million in grants. Big figures, sure. Enough to make headlines and make the powers-that-be feel good about themselves. But will this money actually trickle down? Or will it get lost in the usual channels, ending up funding more consultants and glossy reports? We've seen this movie before. It's like investing in fancy AI when your basic infrastructure is crumbling. Speaking of AI and truth, remember how this technology can be a death sentence for reality? This initiative might just be another layer of that same problem. [The Global Hall of Mirrors: Why AI is a Death Sentence for Truth](/posts/the-global-hall-of-mirrors--why-ai-is-a-death-sentence-for-truth)

It's important to note, this is separate from what Rachel Ruto is doing. Her focus is on children, special needs, HIV, teenage pregnancy, and gender-based violence. Good work, I suppose, if it's actually happening. But this FTFT thing? It's a global program, and Kenya just tagged along as part of a coalition of First Spouses. Along with countries like France, Poland, and the UAE. Because nothing says "empowering Kenyan youth" like being listed alongside wealthier nations.

So, here we are. Another grand announcement, another set of lofty goals. Will it be another case of "much ado about nothing"? Or will these AI and digital skills programs actually lift a significant number of Kenyan children out of the digital divide? Given our history, I'm not exactly holding my breath. Just be prepared for more press releases and fewer tangible results. It's Nairobi, after all. We've learned to be a bit cynical.
