Ruto's Machakos Church Trip: Another Day, Another Promise of Development That Won't Come

WARNING: President Ruto attends a church event in Machakos, touting a "broad-based government" to end tribal politics and ensure equitable development. Expect the usual hot air, not real change.

Machakos: President Ruto, surrounded by the usual suspects of local politicians and clergy, graced the ABC Women of Faith Congress with his presence. His speech, delivered amidst the scent of incense and hope, was a familiar tune about ending regional discrimination and ushering in an era of development for every corner of Kenya. He claims his "broad-based government" is the magic wand to achieve this, conveniently forgetting that the old style of politics has been "ending" for decades.

The President waxed lyrical about how his administration is committed to unity and equitable development, stating that the days of regions facing discrimination are over. It's a lovely thought, really. Like a unicorn at a matatu stage. He insists his government won't allow tribalism and discrimination to take root, a noble sentiment that sounds particularly hollow when you consider the political landscape he navigates.

Then came the defence of the Social Health Authority (SHA). Ruto dismissed criticisms as mere "propaganda" from people who haven't "visited hospitals" or "experienced it as patients." It’s a classic deflection, isn't it? Blame the ignorant masses instead of addressing the fundamental flaws. It’s almost as if the scheme is designed to put a pretty face on a broken system, much like some politicians put a shiny suit on their old, worn-out promises. Reminds you of the kind of spin we saw with [Free SHA for Inmates While You Starve? Zakayo's Newest PR Stunt Is a Prison Break for Logic](/posts/free-sha-for-inmates-while-you-starve--zakayo-s-newest-pr-stunt-is-a-prison-break-for-logic).

He claims SHA is there to prevent Kenyans from selling their assets for medical treatment. A commendable goal, in theory. But in practice, for many, selling assets is already the first step to affording any kind of treatment, let alone comprehensive health insurance. The real issue isn't whether the scheme *exists*, but whether it actually *works* for the average Mwananchi struggling to make ends meet, or if it's just another layer of bureaucracy and promises that crumble under scrutiny.

The president’s pronouncements are always grand, always full of the promise of a brighter tomorrow. Yet, here in Nairobi, we see the same struggles, the same hustles, the same broken systems. The broad-based government is a nice idea on paper, but until the development actually trickles down to the ground and tribalism stops being a convenient vote-buying tool, these words are just that-words.
