So, the big man himself, William Ruto, has gathered his UDA and ODM choir in Naivasha to belt out the same old tune: win the 2027 election, or else. Apparently, the alternative is so grim it makes him "shudder." This isn't about Kenya's future, of course, it's about them keeping their seats, their perks, and their ability to continue whatever it is they do. They're already busy trying to formalize this UDA-ODM love affair, with five members from each side tasked with cooking up a joint manifesto. Because, you know, putting together a party manifesto is *so* complicated, it requires a special committee.

Ruto’s been spinning yarns about how this broad-based government is all about progress and a clear agenda, unlike the opposition, which he paints as a bunch of disorganised mumblers. The man’s got a nerve, accusing others of lacking vision when he himself seems to be just rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic. He’s even praised Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi, claiming he "understood what we were doing better than any other CS." This is the same Mbadi who was supposedly keeping the books for a government that was supposed to be his opposition. Pure genius, this political theatre.

It’s all about securing the future, they say. The future of their wallets, perhaps. The fact that they're even considering a formal pre-election coalition with ODM, the very party he fought tooth and nail against in 2022, speaks volumes. He claims it’s about ending divisions, but let's be real, it's about consolidating power. He's even referencing the "grand coalition" of Mwai Kibaki and Raila Odinga as proof that this kind of arrangement isn't a "miracle." No, it's not a miracle; it's just another chapter in Kenya's ongoing saga of political expediency. We've seen this movie before, and frankly, the plot twists are getting a bit tired. It reminds me a bit of how some bureaucratic playpens are set up, just with more suits and less actual substance.

Oburu Oginga, bless his heart, is out there defending the party's decision to jump into bed with the government. He's saying it's necessary to stabilize the country and advance development. He even boasts about getting more government positions this time around compared to the "handshake" with Uhuru Kenyatta. It’s all very noble, isn’t it? Meanwhile, the rest of us are left wondering if all this "stabilizing" and "advancing" will actually translate into anything tangible beyond another election victory for this merry band of coalition architects.

The retreat, a grand gathering of 250 MPs, 46 senators, and a host of governors and CSs, is meant to solidify their campaign strategy. They're busy hammering out a roadmap, all while the President warns that an opposition win would "jeopardise the gains his administration claims to have made." Gains? What gains? Besides making their own pockets fatter and solidifying their positions, I'm struggling to see the tangible gains for the average Kenyan. They're so busy congratulating themselves on putting together this "largest meeting of elected leaders," it’s easy to forget they’re supposed to be serving the people.

And just like that, the political landscape shifts again. Kenya Kwanza and ODM are teaming up, promising to "look into the issues of our country which will catapult this coalition to power in 2027." It's a classic move, a bid for power disguised as patriotism. The real challenge, as the article points out, will be managing the egos and ambitions within this newly formed alliance. But hey, who are we to question the architects of coalitions? They've got "enough experience," apparently. Let's just hope this experience doesn't lead us down another familiar, disappointing path.
