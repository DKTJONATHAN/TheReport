So, the government, in its infinite wisdom, has decided our livestock need digital IDs. They’re calling it ANITRAC, and it’s meant to track these animals from birth to the slaughterhouse. Think of it as a fancy, bovine passport. They say it’s for food safety, preventing disease, and finally making that informal meat trade accountable. Right. Because what this country *really* needs is more government tracking, especially for things that don't pay taxes. It’s always about making things look good for the international market, isn't it? Like that [Passport to the VIP Club: Why Your Identity is Cheaper than a Bottle of Moët](/posts/passport-to-the-vip-club--why-your-identity-is-cheaper-than-a-bottle-of-mo-t) nonsense, where "identity" means something completely different depending on who you are.

They're throwing around fancy words like "traceability system" and "digital trail," claiming it will help fight livestock theft and banditry, particularly in the North Rift. For decades, they’ve been telling us this, yet the problem persists. Now, it’s a digital solution. I’ll believe it when I see it. But hey, if it means those fancy export markets in Saudi Arabia and the UAE are happy because their beef is "provenanced" and "consistent," then great. More paperwork, more chips, and more opportunities for some clever Kenyan universities to develop "locally sourced" tech.

The real kicker? CS Kagwe is whining that exporting live animals sends jobs elsewhere. So, the grand plan is to keep processing everything here, turn our animals into value-added products, and command better prices. Good. Because we definitely don’t have enough slaughter facilities. Oh wait, we have about 2,000, but the quality is questionable, hygiene is a joke, and half of them are probably just glorified slabs. Instead of more facilities, they’ll just "improve the quality and performance" of the existing ones. Sounds like a familiar tune.

And let's not forget the other promises: better genetics, artificial insemination, improved nutrition, water infrastructure, climate-smart production, and livestock insurance. All that sounds lovely, but it’s just more things to invest in, more projects for committees, and more chances for funds to disappear. They even claim better handling will improve hides and skins for the leather industry. So, from one animal, they'll try to squeeze every last shilling. It’s not about a premium product, they admit, it’s about providing the "missing thread." Translation: more ways to connect the dots, and more dots to be monitored.

This whole ANITRAC thing feels less about helping the small farmer and more about creating a more palatable product for wealthy foreign palates. It’s about making our informal economy look like Silicon Valley’s wet dream, all while the same old problems of corruption, inefficiency, and insecurity fester beneath the shiny digital surface. We're good at talking about grand plans, but the execution? That's where the real Nairobi realism kicks in.

At least the government is trying to make it sound like progress. They've got numbers: 260,000 tonnes of beef, KSh160 billion. Plenty of room for expansion in goat, mutton, poultry, camel, pork, and rabbit. All that, and the promise of cleaner meat. We can only hope the chips don’t run out of battery before the cow gets to the market.
