Jackie Matubia: Another TV Face Chasing Clout From High School to Mommyhood

She's the actress, the personality, the creator, the entrepreneur. Jackie Matubia. Born Jacqueline Matubia Mwangi, September 16, 1990. Apparently, she moved around a lot as a kid, London to Kawangware. Imagine that. Trying to "block people" because of "change." Sounds like a recipe for a lifetime of drama, doesn't it? She went to Lavington Academy and Gathirimu High. And wouldn't you know it, drama club became her "thing."

Her big break? Tahidi High. Jolene, they called her. Apparently, this brought her "recognition." Then she took a break, probably to figure out her next move. Because in this town, breaks are for strategizing. She popped back up in Zora as Nana, then Salem as Zahra Mufasa. Between those, she's scooped up awards. Best Actress in TV Drama, Best Actress at TUKO Entertainment Awards. It's all about the shiny trophies, isn't it? And the constant need for validation.

She’s also dabbled in presenting at Switch TV and Rembo TV. Now, she's a "digital content creator," which is just a fancy way of saying she’s on social media, showing off her life and work. And then there’s this "The Mommy Club NBO" reality show. Because what the world *really* needs is more mothers balancing careers and public life. She’s got two daughters, Zari and Zendaya. And apparently, Zari is off to Europe for a summer holiday. "His promises are bigger than our prayers," she says. Bless her heart.

Away from the camera, she’s CEO of Fabulously Forward, selling "natural beauty products." Because who wouldn't want to buy snake oil from a TV star? It's the standard Nairobi playbook: act, present, create content, sell something. And if the fuel prices keep going up, like that diesel hitting Sh242 per litre, maybe those beauty products are the only luxury some of us can afford.

She met Zendaya’s father, Blessing Lung’aho, on the set of Zora. Love blossoms on screen, or so they say. Then there's the whole entrepreneurial thing, "Fabulously Forward." Natural beauty products. You know, the kind that probably cost an arm and a leg. It's all about building that brand, that empire, one sponsored post at a time. From a high school drama to a reality show and a beauty business, it's the Kenyan dream, I suppose. Just don’t expect any miracles.
