So, the Central Bank of Kenya is telling us our economy is going to do a little jig over the next 12 months. Apparently, businesses are feeling all warm and fuzzy about "stronger demand," "expansion," and splashing cash on "technology." It's almost like they've forgotten about the rent, the constant electricity hikes, and the fact that most Kenyans are still counting coins for bus fare. The report mentions "sector-specific opportunities" and "favourable seasonal factors" - code for "we hope for the best, and maybe it rains a bit."

And of course, the Middle East conflict. The apex bank breathlessly notes that a "calming of geopolitical tensions" would be lovely, particularly if things de-escalate "over there." As if our problems here are just a minor inconvenience waiting for a global ceasefire. They even suggest global energy markets might "stabilise." Right. Because oil prices have *never* been unpredictable, especially when wars are brewing. It’s a bit like saying our problems would ease if only the sun would shine consistently.

Never mind the fact that these very CEOs are also pointing fingers at "elevated energy prices," "inflation," and "weak global demand." Supply chains are still a mess, and getting raw materials is harder than finding a politician who tells the truth. Manufacturers are complaining about delays and shortages. Farmers are dealing with soaring freight costs and, you guessed it, not enough rain. It’s the same old song and dance, just with more fancy charts and jargon. We've seen this optimism before, like when they were talking about the East African Community being some kind of economic miracle worker, only for it to turn out to be [Arusha’s High-Stakes Begging Bowl: Why the EAC is a House of Cards](/posts/arusha-s-high-stakes-begging-bowl--why-the-eac-is-a-house-of-cards).

The report does admit "external risks" are still lurking, which is a polite way of saying the whole world is a dumpster fire and we're just the slightly less smouldering part of it. They expect global growth to weaken, thanks to all those delightful geopolitical dramas and inflated prices. And while they mention "improved access to credit at lower lending rates" - a nice thought, assuming you can actually *get* that credit without selling a kidney - they also highlight "weaker consumer purchasing power" and businesses struggling to "pass higher costs on." So, growth for whom, exactly?

Still, let's not forget the silver linings they've polished up. Technology and innovation, resilient domestic demand, and a "faster resolution of geopolitical conflicts" are apparently our saviours. Basically, if everything miraculously gets better everywhere else, we might just manage to limp along. It’s the classic Kenyan playbook: hope for divine intervention and the world not collapsing, while we continue to navigate the same old potholes.
