So, the coast has done it again. Two supposed "close friends" in Mombasa, high on miraa, decided to spice up their afternoon by turning a pair of scissors into a murder weapon. Because, you know, that’s what happens when you chew leaves and have nothing better to do. The locals are shocked, naturally. As if we haven't seen this movie a million times before in this country.

These brilliant minds are now "seeking answers" about what triggered the disagreement. My guess? Someone probably hogged the best miraa, or maybe a petty squabble over something as profound as who looked at whom funny. It's always the smallest things that lead to the biggest messes, especially when your brain is already pickled. This is the kind of drama that makes you wonder if [parenthood should require a license like driving or flying?](/posts/should-parenting-be-lincensed).

The police are doing their usual song and dance: suspect fled, manhunt underway. Good luck with that. By the time they even remember where to look, this character will likely be halfway to Tanzania or sporting a new beard and a fake name. The efficiency of our law enforcement is truly a marvel to behold, especially when it involves actual effort.

Meanwhile, you have shows like "Fixing the Nation" talking about "challenges facing Kenyan society." This incident isn't a challenge; it's a symptom. It’s the predictable outcome of desperation, poor choices, and a culture that often glorifies escapism. We're too busy talking about "solutions" and "moving forward" on these fancy shows to acknowledge that sometimes, people are just spectacularly bad at being human.

And then there’s “The Politalk podcast,” where they “pull no punches.” Great. Maybe they can pull some punches at the root causes of this madness: poverty, lack of opportunity, and a general disregard for human life. But that’s too much work, isn't it? It's easier to just report on the dead bodies and the fleeing suspects.

Honestly, it’s just another Tuesday in Kenya. A man is dead, a friend is a murderer, and the rest of us are left to shake our heads and wonder when things will ever change. Spoiler alert: they probably won’t.
