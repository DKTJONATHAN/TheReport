So, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki is out there, flapping his gums about unity for the Mt Kenya region, especially with the next election season already peeking around the corner like a nosy neighbour. He’s talking about resisting divisions, keeping communities together, and not letting political squabbles tear everything apart. Apparently, elections are just a blip, but the community? That’s forever. It’s the same song and dance we’ve heard a million times before, isn’t it? Like trying to reason with a matatu driver in peak hour traffic.

Kindiki’s pleading for the "community of Mount Kenya" to stick together, reminding them of their historical "collective support" for leaders like Kenyatta and Kibaki. He’s even suggesting that political spats should be about performance, not insults or throwing stones at businesses. Yes, because that's how politics works, right? Leaders suddenly transform into saints and start comparing development records when the heat is on. It’s a charming fantasy, but down here in the real world, it’s more like [Same Script, Different Year: Kindiki and Matiang’i Dust Off the Ruaraka File](/posts/same-script--different-year--kindiki-and-matiang-i-dust-off-the-ruaraka-file) - the same old grievances, just repackaged.

Then comes the part where he doubles down on his loyalty to the "Kenya Kwanza administration" and President Ruto. He’s not leaving, he declares, because "this is our government." And the region should stay engaged, "struggle there," and keep moving forward. He trots out the usual development talk - markets, housing, title deeds - as proof of their commitment. And he’s waxing lyrical about leaders like Alice Wahome, calling her "mashujaa" for standing her ground and facing down critics head-on. It’s all about a strong stance, no fear.

The whole spiel about elections being temporary and communities enduring? It’s supposed to sound profound, but it’s just a way to tell people to shut up and accept whatever comes. Because at the end of the day, regardless of who wins or loses, the same faces will still be around, the same problems will still need solving, and the same politicians will be back asking for your vote with more empty promises. It’s a cycle, and Kindiki is just trying to ensure his side of the carousel keeps spinning.

He’s all for defending national and community unity with "all our strength, with all our might, with all our resources, with all our intellect." It sounds noble, but when you’re this deeply entrenched in the government, defending unity often just means defending the status quo, especially when your own leadership is on the line. It’s a convenient argument for anyone who wants to maintain power and deflect criticism.

So, Kindiki’s message is clear: stay united, support the government, and judge leaders by their "records" - a record that conveniently includes the projects he’s touting and the "courage" of his allies. It’s a call for blind faith and unwavering loyalty, all wrapped up in a nice, tidy package of political platitudes. Don’t expect any real change; just expect more speeches about unity and more defence of the current administration.
