At 163 yen to the dollar, Japan has effectively entered a period of voluntary economic hara-kiri. The old narrative that a weak yen fuels export-led growth is a relic of a manufacturing era that no longer exists in its prime form. Today, the weak yen is nothing more than a tax on the living. It drives up the cost of every liter of fuel and every gram of imported food, turning Tokyo into a bargain-basement destination for tourists while its own citizens see their purchasing power evaporate.

The Bank of Japan is trapped in a cage of its own making. If they raise rates to save the yen, they risk detonating a debt bomb that has been ticking for decades. If they stay the course, the yen continues its slide toward irrelevance. This paralysis is mirrored in the government’s latest roadmap, which promises 370 trillion yen in investment. It is the same flavor of delusion seen in Mbadi’s Statistical Fairytales Cannot Fill Empty Stomachs (/posts/mbadi-s-statistical-fairytales-cannot-fill-empty-stomachs), where grand numbers are used to distract from the reality of shrinking domestic demand and an aging workforce.

Global energy markets are the final nail in the coffin. With Brent crude hovering above 92 dollars and tensions in the Strait of Hormuz threatening supply, Japan’s total reliance on imported energy is a strategic liability. A weak yen combined with high oil prices creates a feedback loop of inflation that the BOJ cannot control with verbal warnings or bold action from Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama. These are the desperate gasps of a bureaucracy that has run out of tools.

Investors are currently rotating into banks and materials as a hedge, but this is merely moving deck chairs on the Titanic. The split between the Nikkei and the broader TOPIX shows that the smart money is fleeing the high-priced tech names that require infinite growth to justify their valuations. In a world where oil is expensive and the local currency is failing, the AI revolution is an expensive luxury that Japan can no longer afford to subsidize.
