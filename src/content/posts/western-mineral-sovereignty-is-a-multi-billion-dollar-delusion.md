The numbers look impressive on a balance sheet, but they are hollow in the field. Perpetua has nearly three billion dollars in federal backing, yet they are still years away from actually delivering a single gram of product to a customer. In the world of global warfare and industrial survival, a promise of minerals in 2030 is as useful as a paper shield. Meanwhile, US Antimony Corporation watched its market value evaporate as prices halved through 2026, proving that "strategic importance" is a fickle god that does not protect shareholders from the reality of a volatile spot market manipulated by the same dragon it seeks to slay.

RUA GOLD is touting high-grade intercepts in New Zealand, attempting to navigate a "fast-track" regime that remains subject to the whims of local politics and green litigation. Even if the gold is there, the antimony remains a secondary prize in a game where the rules are written by the very bureaucrats who strangled the domestic mining industry in the first place. The only players showing any real survival instinct are those like Americas Gold and Silver, who are scavenging antimony as a byproduct of existing silver mines because they know that trying to permit a new primary site in the current regulatory climate is a fool's errand.

This is the terminal state of Western industry. We have the money and we have the maps, but we have lost the physical ability to actually build anything of consequence within a relevant timeframe. While China controls the smelting and the supply, the United States and its allies are left printing press releases and stockpiling dreams of future independence. The "critical mineral" designation is less of a strategy and more of a tombstone for a manufacturing sector that traded its autonomy for cheap imports decades ago and now finds the exit door locked from the outside.

Ultimately, the global antimony market is a microcosm of the new reality. Resource nationalism is the only currency that matters, and the West is currently broke. It does not matter how many "high-grade" holes RUA GOLD drills or how many billions the U.S. Export-Import Bank pledges if the permits take longer to secure than the conflicts they are meant to support. We are witnessing the slow-motion collapse of a supply chain that assumed the world would always be open for business. It is not, and the price of that realization is only going to get higher.
