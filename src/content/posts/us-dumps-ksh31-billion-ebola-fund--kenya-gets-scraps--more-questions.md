US Dumps Ksh31 Billion "Ebola Fund" - Kenya Gets Scraps, More Questions

The Americans are at it again, throwing money around like confetti at a wedding you weren't invited to. This time it's Ksh31 billion, or USD242 million, to "support Ebola response and preparedness in Africa." And of course, our own dear Kenya gets a piece of the pie, supposedly for strengthening our "health system." Don't get too excited; it's probably just enough to buy a few extra boxes of gloves and a slightly less broken ambulance.

The statement from the U.S. Department of State makes it sound like a grand gesture, all about surveillance, detection, and community engagement. But let's be real, this isn't just about saving Africa. The US Embassy in Nairobi is quick to point out that Kenya is getting "vital medical supplies" and a stronger health system. This comes after they were already coughing up another Ksh64 billion for Ebola efforts. It’s a lot of money, especially when you consider how many times we’ve seen similar promises turn into nothing more than empty rhetoric. Remember Kenya's empty rhetoric? Unite or Watch Africa's Moment Become a Nightmare. Yeah, that.

Meanwhile, the deadly virus continues its march, with thousands dead in the DRC. And here in Kenya? We're still dealing with the fallout from that ill-conceived, US-backed Ebola isolation facility in Laikipia. Remember the uproar? Court challenges, protests, and a whole lot of shouting about sovereignty and whether it was just for expats. The High Court had to step in to halt its operations. So much for "strengthening our health system" when they can't even get their own facilities right without causing a national scandal.

And don't forget the US government’s immediate reaction to keep their own citizens safe: a blanket ban on travel to DRC and Uganda. Level 4, "Do Not Travel." Apparently, these places are so dangerous, they can't even offer consular assistance. It’s a classic move, isn't it? Close the borders, issue stern warnings, and then pat yourselves on the back for your generosity elsewhere. It’s all about managing their own risks, and if Kenya benefits incidentally, well, that’s just a happy accident for us, I suppose.

The funding, mind you, follows a G7 summit commitment. So, it’s not just a spur-of-the-moment decision. It’s part of a larger game, a geopolitical chess match where Ebola is just another piece. While we're busy being grateful for a few more supplies, the real story is about who benefits most and what strings are attached to this "aid." It's a tale as old as time, and frankly, it’s exhausting. This whole Ebola situation feels just like those ivory busts we keep hearing about - a lot of noise, but the same old story of follow-through questions.
