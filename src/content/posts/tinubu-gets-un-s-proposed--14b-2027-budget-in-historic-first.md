TINUBU GETS UN'S $14 BILLION BUDGET, PROBABLY MEANS NADA FOR NAIROBI

The UN's got a hefty $14 billion budget for 2027, and guess who got to hold the seven volumes? Our very own Nigerian representative, Senator Jimoh Ibrahim, who's now chairing the committee that decides where all that cash goes. Apparently, this is a "historic milestone" because a Nigerian is actually in charge of the purse strings for once. So, President Tinubu gets to shake hands and look important in Abuja, receiving this weighty proposal. Big deal.

Now, Ibrahim is puffing his chest out, calling it a "rare diplomatic achievement." He's talking about how Nigeria is now involved in "critical decisions" and how President Tinubu is the "first Nigerian leader" to get this ceremonial handover. He even dropped a juicy tidbit about a $260 million conference facility being built in Nairobi, funded by the UN. So, while Nigeria is busy presiding over budget meetings, other places are actually getting the development. Typical.

The real kicker? When asked what Nigeria actually *gets* out of all this "influence," Ibrahim clams up. Negotiations are ongoing, he says. It's premature to talk about benefits. Translation: don't expect a single extra shilling or a new road for us. It's all talk and handshakes while the real money is being juggled by committees and debated behind closed doors. We're just the messenger boy, holding the chequebook. This whole song and dance feels a lot like the [political theater of greed]( /posts/sonko-s-kes-609-000-phone-purchase-exposes-kenya-s-political-theater-of-greed) we see back home, just on a global scale.

He keeps stressing it's a "proposed budget," subject to change. Which means it’s more likely to get slimmer for everyone else before it gets fatter for anyone specific. The $3.4 billion for regular operations and $8.5 billion for peacekeeping sound massive, but when it's spread across 193 member states, our slice will probably be microscopic. We're sitting at the head of the table, but we're not necessarily getting the biggest serving.

So, the UN budget is presented, a Nigerian is in charge, and the President got his photo op. The rest of us? We'll keep waiting for the crumbs that might, possibly, maybe, trickle down. It’s a grand show of international cooperation, sure, but let’s not pretend it translates into tangible progress for the average Kenyan or Nigerian anytime soon. We’re good at talking, not so good at delivering.
