EL NINO RAINS: KENYA LEARNS NOTHING FROM PAST DISASTERS, ANOTHER CRISIS LOOMS

The skies are darkening again, and you know what that means. Kenya's dancing with El Niño once more, a dance that usually ends with us picking up the pieces. The Red Cross is out here shouting from the rooftops, warning that these rains could be a doozy, potentially hitting 46 out of our 47 counties with more water than they know what to do with. They've got "painful memories" of 1997/98, and let's not forget the pathetic show in 2023/24 and that little flood fest in early 2026. We’ve seen it all before, the soggy homes, the displaced families, the economy doing a nosedive.

So, here we are, staring down another potentially biblical downpour, and the million-shilling question is: did the people in charge actually learn anything, or are we just waiting for the next soggy disaster movie to unfold? The Red Cross is saying preparedness isn't about scrambling when the water hits, but what we do *before* it starts. They're talking about early warnings and early action, like it's some kind of revelation. We’ve had enough warnings to fill a dam, but turning those warnings into actual, tangible solutions? That's the real Kenyan drama.

Dr. Ahmed Idris of the Red Cross is now upping the ante, talking about "something much bigger in scale." Beyond the obvious floods, we're looking at landslides, rivers deciding to take a stroll through town, and our pathetic urban drainage systems choking on every drop. And let's not forget the public health nightmare waiting to happen: contaminated water, overflowing toilets, and diseases spreading faster than gossip. It’s like we’re setting the stage for a grand humanitarian crisis, complete with all the usual suspects.

The government and counties are apparently "stepping up preparedness measures." This usually translates to a lot of meetings, some photocalls, and maybe a few drains here and there. They talk about clearing drainage, identifying evacuation centres, and making sure emergency teams can get around. The public, bless their naive hearts, are supposed to take these warnings seriously and, you know, *evacuate*. It’s a nice thought, but let’s be honest, most of us are too busy trying to make rent to worry about a little flood. Remember when Ruto was busy playing global main character while we were dealing with our own mess? It feels like the same old story, just with more water. We've been through this rodeo before. We know the script: predict the rain, ignore the warnings, watch the chaos, then blame the weather. The question isn't whether we *can* predict it, but whether we'll finally do something about it *before* the water rises. This El Niño season isn't just about the weather; it's a test. A test of whether we're still stuck in the reactive mud, or if we've finally learned to build something solid before the next storm hits. Given our track record, I wouldn't bet the farm on it.

WARNING: KENYA BRACES FOR POTENTIALLY DEVASTATING EL NIÑO RAINS AMIDST CONCERNS OF RECURRING CRISES

Nairobi, Kenya - The nation is once again staring down the barrel of extreme weather as forecasts indicate an impending El Niño season that could rival past disasters. Warnings from the Kenya Red Cross Society paint a grim picture, with nearly the entire country expected to experience above-normal rainfall, raising significant concerns about the country’s preparedness and whether lessons from previous catastrophic floods have been truly learned.
