Haiti begging for help in 2022, Kenya volunteering to lead a police response in 2023. Sounds like a heroic movie, doesn't it? Well, it turned out to be more of a slapstick comedy, with the punchline being a massive failure. We sent a fraction of the promised troops, a thousand to face over 150 armed gangs that basically owned the capital. Talk about setting up our boys for a fall.

Then, in a panic, they decided we needed a bigger force, up to 5,500, with Chadian police and military joining the fray. Kenya started pulling out its already insufficient numbers in March 2026. Of course, it wasn't just the Kenyan officers' fault. The whole mission was a shambles from the start. We're talking about voluntary funding, equipment that arrived slower than a snail in traffic, and what we did get was half of what was needed. No transport, no proper intelligence, no logistics, no language skills, and weapons that were about as useful against these heavily armed gangs as a fly swatter. It’s like sending a child to do a grown-up’s job, and expecting them to win.

This whole affair reminds me of some of our own local dramas. Remember when Murkomen was talking about AI cameras and first-world dreams? [AI Cameras and First World Dreams: Murkomen’s Latest Nairobi Fairy Tale](/posts/ai-cameras-and-first-world-dreams--murkomen-s-latest-nairobi-fairy-tale). Same energy, different continent. Big promises, grand visions, and then reality hits you like a matatu on a potholed road. We’re good at talking a big game, but when it comes to the nitty-gritty, especially when it involves significant risk and sustained effort with proper resources, we tend to fall short.

Now they're holding discussions about "lessons learned" with Ambassador Charles Ray and some other big names. Lessons? The lesson is simple: don't send a weak team to do a strong job, especially if you're not willing to equip them properly. It’s always the same story, isn’t it? Some international body decides a country needs saving, a few nations puff out their chests and volunteer, and then it all collapses because the real work, the hard work with the necessary resources, never materializes.

And the future for Haiti? Don’t hold your breath. They've replaced our failed mission with a new one, but history has a nasty habit of repeating itself, especially when the underlying issues of funding, equipment, and political will remain unresolved. This is just another example of the foreign policy circus, where promises are made and broken, and the people on the ground are left to deal with the mess.
