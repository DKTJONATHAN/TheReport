DR MUTISO MURDER: POLICEMAN ARRESTED OVER NEW KILLING WAS CHARGED BEFORE

WARNING: Another cop with a history of lethal force is back in the spotlight for murder. This one, Police Constable Elijah Kimoi, was previously accused of murder alongside five other officers over the shooting of a motorist. Looks like the system just keeps recycling its problems.

This story isn't about justice; it's about the revolving door at Gigiri. Elijah Kimoi, service number 91232 - a number that apparently means something in this town - is now suspected of killing Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso. Funny, isn't it, that the same service number pops up from a 2011 murder charge. It’s almost as if they don’t bother with proper vetting, or maybe they just don’t care.

Back in 2011, Kimoi and five other officers from Gigiri Police Post were charged with the murder of Stephen Gichuhi Njoroge. The prosecution painted a grim picture: the officers signalled Njoroge's car to stop and then just opened fire. The judge eventually found they all had a case to answer, but in typical Kenyan fashion, only two - Stephen Ariga and Kennedy Ominde - were convicted of the lesser offense of manslaughter. Kimoi, along with three others, walked free. This is the kind of "justice" that makes you wonder if we're actually moving forward or just stuck in a loop, much like the empty rhetoric about unity we often hear; [Kenya's Empty Rhetoric: Unite or Watch Africa's Moment Become a Nightmare](/posts/kenya-s-empty-rhetoric--unite-or-watch-africa-s-moment-become-a-nightmare).

Now, over a decade later, Kimoi's name is back in the headlines, this time for allegedly gunning down Dr. Mutiso. The DCI, bless their diligent hearts, managed to recover a police-issue Mini Jericho pistol from Nderi Police Post, serial number KP47302127, which ballistics say is the murder weapon. And guess who had access to it? An inspector who’s now also on the run. It seems the guns issued for "patrol duties" have a tendency to end up on the wrong side of the law, or perhaps, they’re just used as intended by some.

The details of the 2011 incident are eerily familiar. A carjacked vehicle was being pursued, and then, on the way back, another vehicle was encountered. The police, claiming they saw someone flee and shoot at them, opened fire on the vehicle, killing Njoroge and injuring others. The Court of Appeal acknowledged the shooting was unlawful but couldn't prove intent to kill, hence manslaughter. It’s a convenient escape clause, isn’t it? Just enough to avoid murder, but not enough to be truly accountable.

So here we are again. A police officer, previously cleared of murder but convicted of manslaughter, is now a suspect in another killing. The DCI says two other officers have also escaped. This isn't a system that's fixing itself; it's a system that's perpetuating its own rot. We see these stories, we get angry, and then what? More arrests, more investigations, more court cases, and eventually, more acquittals or light sentences. Nairobi realism, folks. It’s a grim show, and the curtain never seems to fall.







The system doesn't learn, it just repeats. Elijah Kimoi, service number 91232 - a number that apparently carries a certain notorious weight in this city - has been arrested again, this time suspected of murdering Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso. The kicker? This isn't his first dance with a murder charge. Court records show this same officer was previously indicted alongside five other police officers for the shooting death of a motorist in Ruaka. It's a dismal testament to how little accountability truly exists within our forces.

Back in 2011, Kimoi was one of six officers from Gigiri Police Post hauled before the courts for the death of Stephen Gichuhi Njoroge. The prosecution argued that Njoroge's vehicle was signalled to stop, and then the officers simply opened fire, killing him instantly. The judge, finding a case to answer for all six, eventually saw only two, Stephen Ariga and Kennedy Ominde, convicted of the lesser offense of manslaughter. Kimoi, service number 91232 PC Elijah Kimoi, was among those who managed to skirt the more serious charge. It’s a common story in Nairobi: a lot of noise, a lot of arrests, and then, often, a quiet fizzle.

The Ruaka incident, as detailed in court documents, involved a pursuit of a carjacked vehicle. After recovering the car and the driver, the officers encountered another vehicle driven by Solomon Karomo Kiarie, carrying Njoroge and others. The police ordered them to stop, but the driver sped off. The officers then opened fire, claiming they saw someone fleeing and shooting at them, and that they were trying to immobilize the vehicle. They admitted firing from behind, and while the Court of Appeal later upheld the conviction of two officers for manslaughter, they found no sufficient proof of intent to kill. This is the kind of legal gymnastics that allows history to repeat itself, much like the empty promises of reform we often hear; [Kenya's Empty Rhetoric: Unite or Watch Africa's Moment Become a Nightmare](/posts/kenya-s-empty-rhetoric--unite-or-watch-africa-s-moment-become-a-nightmare).

Now, more than a decade later, Kimoi is back in the hot seat. The DCI nabbed him on August 7, 2026, and recovered a Mini Jericho pistol loaded with ammunition - a police-issue weapon from Nderi Police Post. Ballistics have confirmed it's the murder weapon used on Dr. Mutiso. Two other officers, Inspector Kenneth Sang and Constable Collins Bett Kiplagat, are also suspects and have apparently gone on the run. It’s a pattern that’s as predictable as the sunrise: the tools of the state, meant to protect, are readily available for its most egregious abuses.

The recovered pistol, serial number KP47302127, was indeed issued from the armoury. The DCI's statement, as per Mr. Amin, explicitly links the firearm to the Nderi Police Post and notes its issuance to the inspector now on the run. This isn't some rogue weapon acquired on the black market; it's equipment that was officially in police hands. The reliance on digital evidence - CCTV, ballistics - is a modern touch, but the underlying problem of cops pulling the trigger and walking away, or being charged with lesser offenses, remains stubbornly old-fashioned.

So, Kimoi is arrested. Again. The system trots out the evidence, the DCI makes statements, and the media reports the "shocking" revelations. But for those of us living in Nairobi, this is just another chapter in a long, grim story. It’s the same old song, played on a broken record. We’re left to wonder if the next time, the record will finally be smashed.
