So, Chief Justice emeritus David Maraga is out here, parading himself as the "pioneer" of anti-corruption in Kenya. Bless his heart. As if we haven't had enough self-proclaimed saviours crawling out of the woodwork every election cycle. This one's apparently trying to get ahead of the 2027 circus, which is already in full swing, judging by the noise from the UN General Assembly. Ruto and his rivals are already jostling for positions on the global stage, making their usual pitches. Maraga's claim just adds another layer to the predictable theatre.

It’s a tale as old as time in this country: the promise of a cleaner Kenya, delivered by people who have benefited from the very system they claim to want to fix. Maraga might have a title, but has he delivered anything more than rhetoric? We've seen countless "initiatives" and "task forces" that end up as paperweights or launching pads for the next political appointment. It's a grand illusion, much like the "mirage of liquidity" we've seen in the crypto world, leaving ordinary Kenyans holding the bag.

And let's not forget the "two ways to access our content" spiel from whoever published this. Free access by accepting cookies to collect your personal data for "content personalization, audience measurement, advertising, and social media interaction." Or pay 1 euro for full access with no advertising cookies. It's a clear choice: give them your data or give them your money. Either way, someone's profiting from your attention. This whole setup reeks of the same old hustle, just dressed up in digital garb, not unlike how Nebz and Nyathira dole out a new home for clout.

The truth is, while Maraga might be waving the anti-corruption flag, the real battles are fought in the trenches, not on podiums. We've seen the shambles of fake military arms tenders; do we really think a "pioneer" can magically clean up decades of rot with a few pronouncements? The UN assembly is just another stage for politicians to polish their images. The real election previews are happening in the streets, in the queues for basic services, and in the constant struggle to make ends meet.

The UN General Assembly, with Ruto and his rivals making their pitches, is just a preview of the August 2027 Kenyan election. This is the usual song and dance. While we're being offered supposed solutions to corruption, the machinery of politics grinds on, fueled by the same old strategies and the same old promises. It's a familiar script, and frankly, it's getting tiresome.

So, when Maraga declares himself the "pioneer" of anti-corruption, it's hard not to roll your eyes. It’s a nice soundbite, but in Nairobi, we deal with realities, not aspirations. We'll see if his "pioneering" spirit translates into anything tangible, or if it’s just another footnote in the long, frustrating history of Kenyan reform efforts.
