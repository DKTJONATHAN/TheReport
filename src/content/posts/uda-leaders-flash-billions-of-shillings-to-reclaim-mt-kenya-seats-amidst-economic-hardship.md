UDA Fights to Reclaim Mt Kenya Seats With Billions, Locals Still Starving

This ruling party, UDA, is scrambling to claw back some political ground in Mt Kenya, a region that’s shown them the door lately. Their big pitch? Billions in development projects, a classic move when the belly’s empty and promises have dried up faster than a riverbed in a drought. They've lost their grip, and folks are tired of being told "wait for development" while they can barely afford ugali.

Now, they’ve set their sights on Naivasha, a place where they’re trying to pry the MP seat from the jaws of Jayne Kihara, who’s apparently joined Rigathi Gachagua’s DCP. Ndia MP George Kariuki is trotting out figures - over 10 billion shillings in road projects supposedly set to "open up the region." As chair of the roads committee, he’s singing President Ruto’s praises, saying all those development promises are being fulfilled. It's a tired tune, frankly.

Kariuki’s even eyeing the deputy president spot in 2027, which is rich considering the current deputy is apparently failing to rally support in Mt Kenya, leaving the President open to "increasing attacks from opposition leaders." So, the plan is to throw money at the problem and hope people forget they're still struggling.

Then you’ve got youth leader Dennis Muigai, also known as ‘Gaucho’, echoing the same "Ruto invested over 10 billion shillings" line for over 130 km of roads. He’s also after the Naivasha MP seat on a UDA ticket, naturally. He’s begging for Ruto’s re-election based on these "ongoing development projects." It’s starting to sound like a broken record, except this record is costing taxpayers a fortune.

He even bragged about bothering the President about police and army recruitment criteria, specifically the "brown teeth" issue caused by fluoride water. Apparently, the nation’s security is less important than whether your teeth have a bit of a tan. It's the kind of "development" that makes you wonder if they're even living in the same Nairobi we are. This whole situation feels a lot like how even sports teams get left behind when the money dries up, as seen with [Kisumu All Starlets: Broke and Busted, While Police Bullets Take the Top Spot](/posts/kisumu-all-starlets--broke-and-busted--while-police-bullets-take-the-top-spot).

At the end of the day, it’s the same old song and dance. They dangle billions, promise infrastructure, and expect us to forget about the mounting cost of living. It’s the kind of political theatre that’s becoming more common, especially with the youth opting for the streets over ballot boxes. How long can they keep this up before the whole system implodes?







The ruling UDA party is in a frantic scramble to reclaim lost political ground in the voter-rich Mt Kenya region. Their go-to strategy? Flaunting billions of shillings in ongoing development projects, a move that rings hollow for a populace grappling with harsh economic realities. The party’s grip on this crucial bloc has loosened significantly, with residents openly blaming the government for their financial woes, despite the grand campaign promises of yesteryear.

Now, their focus has sharpened on Naivasha, where they’re attempting to make inroads and challenge the incumbent, third-term MP Jayne Kihara, who has shifted allegiance to Rigathi Gachagua’s DCP. Ndia MP George Kariuki has been rallying support by touting road projects worth over 10 billion shillings, claiming they will "open up the region." As the chair of the parliamentary committee on roads, Kariuki is singing President William Ruto’s praises, asserting that all development promises across roads, water, and land sectors have been met.

Kariuki, who has already declared his ambition to be Ruto’s running mate in 2027, highlighted the dualling of the Rironi-Mai Summit road as a significant achievement. He’s also subtly criticizing the current deputy, suggesting a lack of leadership and political support, particularly in mobilizing voters in Mt Kenya, which he claims has left the President vulnerable to opposition attacks. It’s a classic case of deflection, blaming others while offering more of the same.

Youth leader Dennis Muigai, known as ‘Gaucho’, is also on the UDA bandwagon, reiterating the claim of over 10 billion shillings invested in more than 130 km of road projects. He's eyeing the Naivasha MP seat on a UDA ticket and is urging for President Ruto's re-election based on these ongoing developments. This narrative of development as a panacea feels particularly disconnected, especially when basic necessities are out of reach for many, much like how other sectors can be left behind when resources are misallocated, as seen with [Kisumu All Starlets: Broke and Busted, While Police Bullets Take the Top Spot](/posts/kisumu-all-starlets--broke-and-busted--while-police-bullets-take-the-top-spot).

Muigai even boasted about interceding with the President to adjust recruitment criteria for police and army, specifically mentioning the "brown teeth" issue stemming from fluoride-rich water, which has disqualified many local youths. It’s a peculiar focus for a political campaign, suggesting a desperation to connect with voters on any level, even if it’s through trivial matters while larger economic crises loom.

The UDA’s intensified campaign in Mt Kenya, armed with hefty development figures, appears to be a desperate attempt to regain lost territory. However, with the economic realities on the ground far from rosy, residents are likely to see through these grand pronouncements. The promise of billions in infrastructure rings hollow when the cost of basic commodities continues to soar, and the promise of a better future feels increasingly out of reach.
