So, everyone in the office wants to know about Beijing. Honestly, what do you even say? You land in this digital utopia, and suddenly your trusty "Sasa" and "Poa" are about as useful as a screen door on a submarine. I thought "Ni Hao" would cut it, thinking it was the Kenyan "hujambo" for foreigners. Turns out, it gets you a polite nod, not a damn haircut. Which, ironically, was exactly what I desperately needed. My passport photo and the mirror were starting a very public divorce.

I stumbled into this spa near the university, hoping for a quick fix. The Gen Zs running the place spoke zero English, and my "Ni Hao" repertoire was clearly not paying the bills. Then comes this guy, muscles and tattoos, eyeing my hair like it’s some kind of exotic science experiment. He gestures a haircut. I nod. Simple, right? Wrong. Turns out, in Beijing, you don't sit up and chat about the latest Kenyan police drama; you lie down like you're at a morgue while they wash your head. My kinky hair was apparently a whole new world for this guy.

The wash was surprisingly gentle, like he was tending to a newborn, but the confusion was real. Then came the cut. In Nairobi, you just say "kama inamea" and hope for the best. Here, a series of frantic hand gestures landed me a fade that went way too deep. Gestures are great, until they're not. But the real kick in the teeth? The payment. I hand over a 100 Yuan note, expecting change. Silence. The place goes into full-blown crisis mode. No change. This went on for what felt like forever, guys running around like they were looking for parking in Sakaja's CBD. It finally dawned on me: Beijing is cashless. Not just a bit, but *cashless*. Forget M-Pesa; this is M-Pesa on rocket fuel. I was the last analogue man in a digital superpower.

After finally sorting out the change and leaving with a somewhat sharp haircut and 55 Yuan lighter, I thought I was done with my Beijing lessons. Then I got lost. Seriously. Trying to find my hotel in the glittering, identical streets felt like being trapped in a loop. After 20 minutes of circling like a lost Uber, I swallowed my pride and asked a student. He pointed around the corner. I was standing at the *rear entrance* of my own hotel.

Beijing is supposed to be the future, the Singapore dream for every Kenyan. The trains are on time, the cars wait, the toilets salute you. Apparently, you can even haggle for shoes without saying a word. It's all ultra-modern, super-efficient, and frankly, humbling. You go for a haircut and get a masterclass in the cashless economy. You try to pay with cash and cause a national incident. You can get lost at your own hotel's back door. That’s Beijing: it'll show you exactly how far behind you are, faster than you can say "Ni Hao." It's a reality check, a digital slap in the face that makes you wonder if our own progress, much like the search for change in that barbershop, is just going in circles. Even with all the advancements we've seen, like the [Kenya's First Homegrown ETF: Another Foundation Laid, Let's See If It Crumbles](/posts/kenya-s-first-homegrown-etf--another-foundation-laid--let-s-see-if-it-crumbles), we're still miles away from this level of integration.
