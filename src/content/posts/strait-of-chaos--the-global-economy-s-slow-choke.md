Washington’s decision to let the Iran ceasefire expire is the geopolitical equivalent of dousing a house fire with gasoline. The subsequent threat to bomb Oman - a nation whose primary sin is attempting to broker peace - reveals a total abandonment of traditional diplomacy in favor of raw, uncalculated aggression. The markets are reacting predictably: futures are bleeding out as the realization sinks in that $90 oil is merely the floor, not the ceiling. This is not a "cautious tone" as some analysts claim; it is the sound of capital looking for an exit in a room with no doors.

North of the border, the mask of the "special relationship" has been discarded. The impending 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, ranging from wine to hockey sticks, illustrate a regression into 1930s-style protectionism. Prime Minister Mark Carney’s attempt to offer concessions to President Trump highlights the desperation of satellite states caught in the orbit of an increasingly volatile hegemon. It is a zero-sum game where even the closest allies are treated as piggy banks for domestic political posturing, regardless of the damage to integrated supply chains.

On the retail front, the upcoming Home Depot earnings will likely confirm what the average consumer already knows: the era of cheap credit and discretionary upgrades is over. When a CEO warns that customers are too scared of war-driven inflation to fix their bathrooms, it is a signal that the rot has reached the foundation of the economy. Even the artificial intelligence hype, fueled by Nvidia and Anthropic, feels like a hollow distraction. Silicon Valley’s dreams of a digital utopia mean very little when the physical world cannot even transport a barrel of oil without it being targeted by a missile.

Gold’s decline despite the chaos is perhaps the most cynical indicator of all. Rising Treasury yields are sucking the air out of the room, forcing investors to bet on U.S. debt as the only "safe" harbor in a storm the U.S. itself is actively brewing. This is not a standard market cycle; it is a systemic unraveling. The world is learning the hard way that when the global policeman decides to set fire to the station, there is nowhere left to call for help. The coming months will likely be defined by scarcity, hostility, and the cold reality that "globalization" was always a fragile truce rather than a permanent state of being.
