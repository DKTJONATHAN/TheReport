So, 2027 is just around the corner, and what's the hot new trend? Apparently, it's "mole politics." Just another fancy term for the same old song and dance: politicians sniffing around, switching sides like it's a fashion show, and everyone accusing each other of being a spy. President Ruto, Gachagua, Kalonzo, Oburu - they're all positioning themselves. It's all about who's got the inside scoop, who's selling secrets, and who's just looking for the next best deal. This isn't new, mind you. Back in the 90s, when multiparty politics first crawled out of KANU's shadow, the opposition was a den of vipers, each suspecting the other of being in bed with the ruling party. The script hasn't changed, just the actors and the costumes.

Remember 2013 and 2017? CORD and NASA were drowning in suspicions. Leaked campaign plans, internal polls, rally routes - all conveniently landing in Jubilee's lap. Raila Odinga himself famously wailed about his own allies "eating with us during the day and dining with our enemies at night." Classic! It’s the same paranoia, the same whispers in the corridors, the same inability to trust anyone outside your immediate clique. They even had to set up their own tallying centre in 2017, only for that information to leak too. Honestly, it’s a wonder any elections ever get done with all this cloak-and-dagger stuff.

Then came 2022, with Uhuru and Ruto's spectacular divorce. Uhuru backed Raila, Ruto went his own way. And surprise, surprise, loyalty became the hot commodity. Who was really in bed with whom? Who was playing both sides? It’s a constant game of musical chairs, and everyone’s trying to get the best seat before the music stops. The analysts are already calling it. This "mole narrative" is going to be the buzzword, a handy weapon to paint anyone who crosses the floor as a traitor. Ndindi Nyoro, for example, is already trying to shake off the "mole" label after his little detour. Good luck with that.

The government folks are already pointing fingers at anyone who looks too cozy with the opposition. And vice versa, of course. If you're an opposition figure suddenly finding yourself on the government side, you're automatically suspect. It’s all about controlling the narrative, making sure the public sees the other side as untrustworthy. This is how they’ll distract you from the real issues, from the fact that our economy is still struggling, and that projects like the [Two Rivers SEZ seeking $37m]( /posts/two-rivers-sez-seeks--37m-in-kenya-s-first-green-dollar-i-reit) are still in the pipeline.

But here's where it gets slightly less predictable, if you believe the pundits. Martin Andati reckons the electorate, especially the youth with their internet access, might actually be getting too smart for these tired tricks. They can dig around, find information themselves, and maybe, just maybe, see through the smoke and mirrors. They might actually make their own choices instead of just swallowing whatever political garbage is fed to them. It's a nice thought, isn't it? A populace that actually thinks for itself? We'll see, won't we? But don't hold your breath. This "mole" game has been going on for decades, and it's likely to be played with even more vigour as 2027 approaches.
