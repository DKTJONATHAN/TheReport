AfroExchange Brings More Of The Same African Music, Fashion, And Creativity To Nairobi. Big Deal.

Johnnie Walker, bless their expensive hearts, decided Nairobi needed another "experience." Apparently, the city's creatives and music lovers, who are always clamoring for more opportunities to spend money they don't have on things they don't need, converged at Capital Noir. It was a celebration of African music, fashion, and culture - all the buzzwords that sound good on a press release. The usual suspects were there: live performances, DJ sets, and curated cocktails. Because what’s a cultural celebration without overpriced drinks?

The headliner, Okello Max, belted out his hits. Good for him. DJs Festa, Sir M, and DJ Mish spun their tunes, and MC Hypegad did his best to keep the masses entertained. Beyond the predictable music, people were splashing paint on mural boards, apparently inspired by Nairobi’s nightlife. Because nothing says "authentic Nairobi" like a brand-sponsored art project and a bunch of tipsy attendees pretending to be Picasso. And of course, there was fashion from 4mtaa Fashion, where people could try on artistic pieces. You know, because slumming it at Capital Noir isn't exclusive enough without a fashion show.

Then came the mixology masterclasses, where Kingkline Ojal, a mixologist and cultural curator, taught people how to make Johnnie Walker cocktails. Because when you’re already spending a fortune on drinks, learning to make them yourself at home is the ultimate sign of progress and self-expression, right? Johnnie Walker Shopper Manager Duncan Mokaya spouted the usual corporate jargon about "progress" and "people who keep moving culture forward." It’s all very inspiring, I’m sure, especially when you’re watching your wallet empty itself with every sip.

This whole "AfroExchange" thing sounds suspiciously like a marketing ploy to sell more whiskey. They talk about supporting African creatives and the creative economy, but let's be real, it's about creating a trendy backdrop for their product. They've done this in Kisumu and Nanyuki, and now Nairobi. Soon they'll be promising a major concert with an international lineup, which will likely be another excuse to charge exorbitant ticket prices. Honestly, with all the real issues facing Kenyan creatives, like the FKF being fined and threatened with relocation, this feels like a distraction. Perhaps they should redirect some of that marketing budget to something more substantial, or at least less performative.
