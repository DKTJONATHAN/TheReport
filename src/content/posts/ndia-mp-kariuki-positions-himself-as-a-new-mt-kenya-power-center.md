Ndia MP Kariuki Thinks He's the Next Big Thing in Mt Kenya - We'll See

Another politician thinks he's got the key to Mt Kenya's divided house. Brace yourselves for consultation season.

Ndia MP George Kariuki, riding the wave of President Ruto’s popularity, has decided his ten years in Ndia are enough and it's time for "bigger things." He's throwing open his Gacharu home in Kirinyaga for a three-month "consultation" with delegations, claiming he’ll emerge with a plan for the region's future. Apparently, he's waiting to see if he can pull off a national gig, maybe even deputy president, just in case the current lot falters. Funny how these ambitions always pop up when there's a vacancy or a power vacuum.

Kariuki’s strategy involves positioning himself as the unifying force Mt Kenya desperately needs, while simultaneously trashing current heavyweights like former DP Rigathi Gachagua and DP Kithure Kindiki. According to him, neither has the "ability to run the affairs of this region the right way." High praise from someone who hasn't even declared his own ambitions yet. He’s also against the East vs. West Mt Kenya split, which, let's be honest, is just a way for people to carve out their own little kingdoms. He wants "one region, one people," which sounds great, but usually translates to "my region, my people."

He’s touting his consultations as a way to "listen to the people" and determine the region’s "bargaining power" with the next administration. It’s the same old song and dance. Politicians trot out the "listening tour" when they’re unsure of their next move or when they need to gauge public sentiment before making a power play. Remember when President Ruto was promising good things for national teams? [President Ruto Promises Goodies For National Women’s Teams Ahead Of World Cup And WAFCON](/posts/president-ruto-promises-goodies-for-national-women-s-teams-ahead-of-world-cup-and-wafcon) - it’s all about optics and promises until the next election cycle.

The Ndia MP's ambition to be a "Ruto pointman" in Mt Kenya West and his dismissal of Gachagua and Kindiki are telling. He's clearly trying to capitalize on the perceived divisions and weaknesses within the current leadership. It's a classic Nairobi political game: play the factions against each other, offer yourself as the stable alternative, and hope the voters are tired enough of the usual suspects to fall for it. The fact that he’s considering a national role after just ten years locally speaks volumes about the insatiable appetite for power in this country.

Let's not forget the constant talk of unity when it's politically convenient. Kariuki’s plea for "one region, one people and one nation" is a nice slogan, but it’s often a smokescreen for individual ambition. The reality is, regional politics are rarely unified; they’re a complex web of interests, loyalties, and, frankly, ego. His consultations will likely end with him declaring a "popular" direction that conveniently aligns with his own advancement.
