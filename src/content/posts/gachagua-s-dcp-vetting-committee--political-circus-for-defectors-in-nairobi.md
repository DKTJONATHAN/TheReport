Gachagua's DCP Vetting Committee: A Comedy of Errors for Defectors

This whole drama with Rigathi Gachagua's Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) forming a seven-member vetting committee for defectors is pure Nairobi theatre. After 18 sittings, which sounds more like a long, drawn-out divorce than political strategy, they finally landed on a committee. Led by John Methu, who I assume has the most patience or the least to lose, they're supposed to sort through the chaos of people wanting to jump ship from UDA. It's a joke. They’re so busy quibbling over who gets to sit at the table, they’re missing the bigger picture - this whole thing reeks of desperation.

Murang’a Senator Joe Nyutu, bless his dramatic heart, led a protest walkout of 16 people. Sixteen! Clearly, democracy for *some* citizens is more important than for others. Now they’ve got this committee, which includes people like Senator Karungo Thang’wa and Woman Representative Njeri Maina. They're all meant to be impartial judges, deciding the political fates of 816 supposed defectors. That includes five Cabinet Secretaries and four governors, apparently. This is less about unifying the party and more about Gachagua playing gatekeeper, deciding who’s worthy of his political crumbs.

The conditions are hilarious. Public declarations against Ruto? As if anyone who's been in bed with the government suddenly has a change of heart for ideological reasons. Methu even admits they’re worried about people recording conversations and selling them. Well, when you're dealing with politicians, especially those looking for a new lifeboat, you can't expect saintly behavior. It’s all about survival, and anyone who thinks these defectors are joining DCP for the love of Gachagua's vision is deluded. They're probably just trying to hedge their bets for 2027, like everyone else. It's the same old song and dance, just with a different chorus.

The supporters in Mt Kenya are apparently still fuming about Gachagua's impeachment. Good for them. At least someone remembers. The fact that Gachagua is now pretending to forgive everyone, even those who tried to boot him out, and wants to partner with people like Edwin Sifuna and Uhuru Kenyatta, just shows how fluid this political landscape is. It’s less about principles and more about power plays. It’s enough to make you wonder if [Billionaire Saviors and Baby Bribes: Why 2026 is the Year of Brain-Rot Drama](/posts/billionaire-saviors-and-baby-bribes--why-2026-is-the-year-of-brain-rot-drama) is actually a documentary about Kenyan politics.

And then you have MPs like Mary Maingi and Mwangi Kiunjuri chiming in, calling Gachagua a demigod and warning against "dictatorial and condescending mobilisation." They're right. This isn't about principled politics; it's about Gachagua trying to control his dwindling base. The idea that aspirants should "not feel boxed into a corner by DCP" is a nice sentiment, but in this game, corners are precisely where you end up. The voters will vote for whoever they damn well please, and Gachagua’s bullying tactics won't change that. This whole vetting process is just a way for him to maintain a semblance of control in a party that’s clearly fracturing.

Naivasha MP Jayne Kihara spilled the beans, saying the "traitors" tag applies only to those in Mt Kenya. Those outside the region get a free pass. So, in other words, if you’re from Mt Kenya and you crossed Gachagua, you’re a traitor. If you’re from anywhere else and have a government past, you're suddenly welcome. This isn't about unifying the party; it's about appeasing the local base while welcoming anyone from elsewhere who might bring some political capital, or just more chaos. It’s a classic political move - divide and conquer, or in this case, vet and discriminate.
