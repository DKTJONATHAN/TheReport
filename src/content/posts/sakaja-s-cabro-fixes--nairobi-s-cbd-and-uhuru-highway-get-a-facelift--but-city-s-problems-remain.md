SAKAJA’S CBD CABRO CHARADE: MORE PROMISES, SAME OLD NAIROBI GRIME

WARNING: Governor Sakaja’s latest infrastructure blitz, featuring more cabro blocks and grand plans, is just a fresh coat of paint on a city drowning in neglect. Expect the same old traffic jams and flooded streets, just with fancier walkways.

PHOTO: Johnson Sakaja

From CBD to Uhuru Highway, it’s the same old song and dance.

So, Governor Sakaja is back, telling us about the "next phase" of Nairobi’s transformation. Apparently, after all that shiny new cabro in the CBD and along Uhuru Highway, we’re in for even *more* road expansion, drainage upgrades, and pedestrian walkways. Because clearly, what Nairobi needs is more places to trip and fall while staring at more half-finished projects. He’s going on about improving our image as the capital, as if visitors aren't just going to get stuck in traffic for two hours anyway. It’s all about "high-quality walkways" and "attractive boulevards," but let’s be real, it’s just lipstick on a pig.

They’re talking about replacing old pavements with cabro blocks on streets like Mama Ngina and Kaunda. Great. As if those were the biggest problems plaguing this city. Meanwhile, road construction continues on Kasuku Road, Jabavu Road, and a host of others. This is supposed to be about addressing "years of neglected infrastructure," but we’ve heard this tune before. Remember when they promised us the moon and stars, only for us to end up with more potholes than progress? It’s like they’re just rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic, and frankly, I’m tired of the endless talk about a "modern, world-class city" while we’re still struggling with basic services.

Beyond the endless roadwork, Sakaja is suddenly worried about El Niño. They’ve identified thousands of "vulnerable households" and designated "emergency holding centres." Good to know they’re *finally* thinking about flood mitigation after years of ignoring the obvious. Desilting drainage systems and unclogging rivers - sounds like a proper job for once. And then there are the streetlights. Apparently, procurement is done, and the equipment is "expected to arrive." Let’s hope they actually make it and, more importantly, get installed and working. Because improving security and supporting businesses by having lights on at night seems like a radical idea, I know.

And the grand plans don’t stop there. They’re talking about modern markets, environmental restoration, and even redesigning Globe Roundabout into a landscaped public space. It’s all very pretty on paper. But the real kicker is the Nairobi Metropolitan Mass Rapid Transit System. A metro line connecting the CBD with Eastlands, with underground sections planned. He wants to turn Nairobi into a "20-minute city." Honestly, the ambition is almost laughable. We can barely get across town in two hours now, and he thinks a metro is going to fix it overnight. This whole thing is just another example of how things are, unfortunately, [Constitution Frees Us? More Like It Chains Us Back To Old Ways](/posts/constitution-frees-us--more-like-it-chains-us-back-to-old-ways). It’s all part of some cooperation agreement with the national government that unlocked billions of shillings. Money that, knowing Nairobi, will likely disappear into vanity projects and inflated contracts.
