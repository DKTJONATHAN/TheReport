Nairobi Lawmakers Beg Ruto to Fix Ethiopia Border Chaos, While Kenya Bleeds

WARNING: Another neighboring mess is about to dump on our doorstep, and our politicians are just now remembering they have a President. Get ready for more handouts and more headaches.

"Our leaders are finally noticing the chaos next door," says a bystander at the market.





So, the Kenyans, bless their hearts, are finally noticing that the shouting match across the border in Ethiopia is now a full blown ethnic brawl, and guess who’s getting the leftovers? Us, of course. Fifteen thousand people, mostly women and kids looking like they’ve wrestled a crocodile and lost, are now our problem. Our leaders, like Saku MP Dido Ali Rasso, are "deeply concerned." Shocking. It's almost like they didn't see this coming.

And these "leaders from northern Kenya’s Marsabit County" are now wailing for "urgent government intervention." What do they think President Ruto’s been doing, polishing his shoes? They’re worried about food, shelter, and medical aid. That’s rich. Meanwhile, businesses are shutting down along the trade corridor. Because, you know, when your neighbor is busy with an ethnic cleansing party, your KRA targets can wait. It’s like that time we got all excited about [Kenya's Drug Import Bet: Another Grand Plan, More Empty Promises?](https://www.example.com/posts/kenya-s-drug-import-bet--another-grand-plan--more-empty-promises) - all fanfare, no actual delivery.

They're also begging their own people not to attack the refugees. Good luck with that. When resources get stretched thin, and people are scared, common sense takes a vacation. They’re calling for stronger border security, which means more roadblocks and more opportunities for bribes, I’m sure. And "peaceful coexistence"? That’s a good one. Try telling that to someone whose farm is being trampled by a fleeing crowd.

The whole thing kicked off, predictably, over a flag. A flag. Because apparently, in Ethiopia, you can’t even fly your regional banner without starting a bloodbath. Now we’ve got over 10,900 people crammed into six camps, and another 4,000 are being squeezed in with relatives. And this is just the beginning. Our leaders are calling for "international humanitarian support." Translation: we want the world to clean up our mess, because we’re not very good at it ourselves.
