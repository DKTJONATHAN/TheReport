Ruto Warns Opposition Will Take Kenya Back to the Stone Age, Naturally

President William Ruto, fresh from a working tour of Bomet and Kericho counties, has unleashed his usual brand of election-season rhetoric, painting opposition leaders as visionless relics destined to drag Kenya back to the dark ages. Speaking at Kapkatet grounds, he declared these opponents to be an "existential threat" to progress, conveniently overlooking any potential shortcomings of his own administration. It's the classic politician's playbook: demonize the other side while conveniently ignoring the mud that might be clinging to your own boots.

The President, busy inspecting roads and launching market stalls that are, no doubt, vital to the nation's immediate future, insists he has delivered on all his 2022 promises. He's pointed to roads, housing, and health coverage as proof, challenging the opposition to present their own plans. Honestly, the man has a talent for making even the most mundane infrastructure project sound like a revolutionary leap forward. Meanwhile, the cost of living continues its relentless march upwards, but who's counting when there are roads to be opened and stadiums to be built?

Ruto’s pronouncements about "equal development" across Kenya ring hollow when you consider the sheer scale of debt this country is drowning in. While he boasts about paying contractors and reviving stalled projects - a move that sounds suspiciously like cleaning up messes left by previous administrations, though he'd never admit it - one can't help but wonder about the long-term sustainability of such an approach. It’s a bit like patching up a leaky roof with expensive wallpaper; it looks good for a while, but the fundamental problem remains. Remember how that Ksh149.8 billion scheme was costing us? This constant spending spree feels like a similar story, just with more ribbon cuttings.

The President also took the opportunity to tout his agricultural reforms, highlighting Kericho coffee farmers' earnings. It's a nice touch, but it conveniently sidesteps the ongoing struggles of many other farmers across the country who are grappling with rising input costs and unpredictable markets. He's promising a Special Economic Zone and a new medical school, all while the average Kenyan is struggling to put food on the table. It's the usual song and dance: big promises, shiny projects, and a convenient distraction from the economic realities facing most citizens.

His emphasis on inclusivity and fairness, stating no region will be left behind, is admirable in theory. However, the reality on the ground often tells a different story, with resource distribution remaining a perennial political battleground. The simultaneous launch of multiple projects, from stadium upgrades to market stalls, feels like a well-orchestrated campaign to showcase "delivery" in every corner. It’s a political performance, and Kapkatet was just another stage for the grand show, with the opposition conveniently cast as the villains.

Ruto’s efforts to transform Kericho into a city, with thousands of housing units and a billion-shilling medical school, are certainly ambitious. But one can't shake the feeling that these grand pronouncements are aimed at solidifying his political base rather than addressing the fundamental economic challenges facing the nation. As he continues his tour, one can only hope these investments translate into tangible improvements for ordinary Kenyans, and not just another round of political theatre.
