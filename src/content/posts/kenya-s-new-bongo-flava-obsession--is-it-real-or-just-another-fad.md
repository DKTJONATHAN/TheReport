So, Bongo Flava is back, they say. Young Kenyans are apparently rediscovering their "deep, passionate romance" with Tanzanian tunes. You'd think with all the noise about Afrobeats, Amapiano, and hip-hop, there wouldn't be room for anything else. But here we are, with Bongo music "progressively becoming part of the local entertainment and weekend scene." Whatever that means.

It’s not just some nostalgic trip for the good old days of Diamond Platnumz and Alikiba, apparently. Gen Z has apparently "taken Swahili coastal culture" and added "modern internet humor" to make it a "full-fledged lifestyle aesthetic." Sounds exhausting. So, instead of actual culture, we get a curated "vibe" for Instagram stories and TikTok dances. Pretty sure that’s how most of these fleeting trends start and die.

This whole Bongo revival is being propped up by the likes of KonQa, throwing out lineups with Chris Kaiga and a "Bongo brunch" hosted by Mzazi Willy M Tuva. It’s all about getting "the weekend sorted" with a mix of music, entertainment, and that all-important "social experience." It’s the same old story, just with a different soundtrack. Remember when dual citizenship and its complexities became the hot topic? Now it’s Bongo music for your Sunday brunch.

Honestly, it’s hard to get worked up about this. Kenyans are easily swayed by whatever’s trending on social media for five minutes. One minute it’s political drama and the next it’s a "lifestyle aesthetic" built around a music genre that’s always been lurking around. Let’s see how long this particular "obsession" lasts before the next big thing comes along and sweeps it all away. Just another day in Nairobi, really.
