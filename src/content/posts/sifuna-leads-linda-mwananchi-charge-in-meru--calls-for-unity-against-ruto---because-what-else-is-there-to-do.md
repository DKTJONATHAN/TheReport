So, Linda Mwananchi, the movement that’s apparently "undeterred" by the rather serious "fatal goon attacks" in Homa Bay, has decided Meru is the next stop. Because why wouldn't they? Senator Edwin Sifuna, alongside former governor Kawira Mwangaza (who I’m sure is just thrilled to be hosting), is dragging his entourage through Deputy President Kithure Kindiki's supposed backyard. They say it was a "rousing welcome," which in Nairobi probably translates to a few dozen people waving banners and hoping for some free chai.

The Homa Bay incident, where two people ended up dead and millions in property went up in smoke, is conveniently brushed aside as a mere "attempt to block opposition leaders." These guys, led by Sifuna and the ever-present Babu Owino, are talking a big game about upholding democracy and human rights. They're blaming President Ruto for everything from "disregard for the rule of law" to failing to keep his election promises. And you know, they've got a point about the cost of living, but honestly, who’s surprised? This is Nairobi realism talking here, not political analysis.

Sifuna is on record saying he’ll fire Interior CS Murkomen if they ever get into power. Bold words, especially considering the current government's track record with bandits and, well, everything else. He’s accusing them of sponsoring violence and trying to fake popularity, which, let's be honest, is a standard playbook for any government trying to stay in power. The man even threw in that traders were tear-gassed in Nairobi for protesting taxes, just a day after some constitution anniversary. It’s all about optics, isn’t it? They’re trying to convince us that they’re the righteous ones, the ones who will actually *restore* something, as if whatever is broken can actually be fixed by another bunch of politicians. It's a bit like reading about how Nairobi's economic reign is being challenged; it's a slight hiccup for the big fish, not a seismic shift.

The usual suspects are there: Babu Owino eyeing the governor's seat, Governor Natembeya, Senator Khalwale, and Senator Osotsi. They're all chanting the same tune about opposition unity for an "easier win" against Ruto. Because apparently, the goal is an easy win, not necessarily good governance. Babu Owino is also chiming in about the Sh2.5 billion police uniforms tender being a "misplaced priority" while youth remain jobless. Yes, Babu, we get it. Money spent on uniforms could have built industries. Groundbreaking. And Natembeya is worried about the IEBC dishing out IDs to underage youth, which, if true, is a whole other can of worms they're conveniently opening up.

They’re also throwing support behind Kawira Mwangaza, claiming she was "unfairly bundled out of office." It’s all very dramatic, this constant cycle of blame and promises. They talk about restoring meritocracy and respecting the constitution, while also being involved in the same political maneuvering that got us here in the first place. They’ll go to Meru, rally the troops, complain about the government, and then do the exact same thing when it’s their turn. It’s the Kenyan political theatre, and we’re all just part of the unpaid audience.
