So, Ababu Namwamba is over the moon because Nairobi is apparently going to host the 2029 World Athletics Championships. He's comparing it to the FIFA World Cup, all sunshine and rainbows about how this is the "pinnacle of world athletics" coming to Africa for the first time. You'd think he'd discovered gold. It's great for the ego, I suppose, and another feather in the cap for the ministry. We'll see if that translates to anything tangible for the actual athletes, though.

He’s also crowing about how Kenya has seemingly cleaned up its act on doping. Apparently, Lord Sebastian Coe visited last year and Kenya was on the verge of being banned. Now, three years later, they've landed this hosting gig. He even mentioned a KSh. 600 million war chest against doping. While it’s good to hear they’re *trying* to tackle it, let’s not forget the history. Kenya has been a hotbed for doping scandals, and this hosting gig might just be a way to distract from the fact that we’re still seeing our runners fall by the wayside, sometimes tragically, like the late Kelvin Kiptum.

Namwamba spins this win as part of a grand government plan to use sports and the creative economy for economic development and youth empowerment. He even claims to have helped write this plan. It's a nice story, a "solid plan" to "revolutionise our infrastructure" and make Kenya a "hub for hosting significant regional and international events." We’ve heard these grand pronouncements before, haven’t we? Remember the promises about supporting local talent and infrastructure development that never quite materialized? It’s a familiar tune.

Honestly, the timing of this announcement, so soon after the tragic death of Kelvin Kiptum, feels a bit off. While official statements pour in, one can't help but wonder if the focus is truly on the well-being of our athletes, or just on the prestige of hosting another event. It’s easy to talk about empowering youth and developing the economy when there’s a shiny new trophy to be won. But what about the systemic issues? We’ve seen how easily things can spiral out of control, even something as seemingly straightforward as the global skin trade impacting our donkeys. [Kenya’s donkeys on the edge of extinction in growing global skin trade](/posts/kenya-s-donkeys-on-the-edge-of-extinction-in-growing-global-skin-trade). The same lack of oversight can affect our sports.

So yes, congratulations to Namwamba and the ministry for securing the 2029 bid. It’s a significant achievement on paper. But for those of us living on the ground, the cynicism remains. We’ll be watching, waiting to see if this is more than just another photo opportunity and a collection of empty promises. The "world cup of athletics" coming to Nairobi is one thing; ensuring the integrity and well-being of the athletes who compete in it is an entirely different matter.
