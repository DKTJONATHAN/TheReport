So, the elders have spoken. At Kirigiti Stadium, a bunch of old men, claiming to represent the Gikuyu, Embu, and Meru folks, decided that Deputy President Kithure Kindiki is now their official mouthpiece. They’ve supposedly “endorsed and mandated” him to advance the region’s interests. Because, you know, that’s how politics works in this country - some old dudes meet in a stadium and suddenly, bam, you have a spokesman. It’s all about looking like things are under control before the next election, while behind the scenes, it’s probably a different story.

And naturally, they’ve thrown their full support behind President William Ruto and Kindiki, showering them with praise for “development” in infrastructure and agriculture. Coffee, dairy, tea - the usual suspects. It's the same song and dance every time. They’re clinging to the hope that these projects will somehow translate into votes, conveniently ignoring the fact that the region's political loyalties have been “under strain.” It’s a desperate attempt to paper over the cracks, hoping the elders’ blessing will magically heal all divisions.

Then you have Rigathi Gachagua, the deposed king of Mt Kenya, barking from the sidelines. He's calling the whole thing a “futile move” and telling the elders to give back their symbolic axe. Gachagua, bless his heart, claims Kindiki and his allies have “miserably failed” and are just trying to split the region again. He's framing it as an attempt to divide the former Central Province, and he’s convinced the "intelligent mountaineers" won't fall for it. Honestly, it’s a mudslinging contest that would make even the most jaded Nairobi resident roll their eyes.

The elders are trying to invoke historical ties and past support for leaders like Kenyatta and Kibaki, and even Gachagua himself. They’re pleading for unity, saying political divisions and rivalry can undermine the region's future. It’s a nice sentiment, but in Kenya, political differences are as constant as the matatus on Thika Road. They're also bringing up social issues like alcoholism and political violence, which is great, but let's be real, the main agenda here is power. The Ruto-Kindiki camp is banking on elders and development projects to rebuild their support, a strategy that has seen them lean on cultural platforms to gain traction, though how that translates to actual votes is anyone's guess. It's all part of a larger effort to counter Gachagua's narrative of betrayal and opposition.

This whole "East vs. West" debate, with CS Geoffrey Ruku at the forefront, has been a classic political manoeuvre to create leverage. Ruku wanted Kindiki recognized as the main spokesman from the West before he'd "put his axe away." Now that the elders have done just that, it’s supposed to be a done deal. But the real question is whether this declaration can actually close the chapter on the East-West split. Given how this country operates, don't hold your breath.

Ultimately, this elder endorsement is just another chapter in the ongoing scramble for Mt Kenya's political soul ahead of 2027. It's a direct challenge to Gachagua's influence, but Gachagua isn’t backing down, arguing that Kindiki's appointment doesn't erase his perceived betrayal by the administration. The Ruto-Kindiki camp is pushing a narrative of unity and development, while Gachagua is rallying opposition. It’s a political tug-of-war, and the voters are the ones who will eventually decide who gets the last laugh, or perhaps, the last laugh after a long, frustrating journey. It reminds me of the complexities in managing large projects, much like the challenges seen at the Talanta Stadium, where oversight and execution are paramount.
