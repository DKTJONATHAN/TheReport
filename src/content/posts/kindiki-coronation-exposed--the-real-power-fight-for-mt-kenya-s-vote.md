So, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki gets himself a shiny new title, "political spokesman of the Mt Kenya region," all thanks to some elders at Kirigiti Stadium. They dressed him up in traditional gear, making it look like a genuine cultural endorsement. Meanwhile, the whispers are loud: former DP Rigathi Gachagua is apparently behind the scenes, trying to undermine this whole charade. It’s the same old song and dance - who controls the mountain's vote for 2027.

The whole thing just highlights the deep cracks in Mt Kenya's political unity, or rather, the lack of it. While the official line is about preserving unity, it’s really about who gets to claim authority and, more importantly, who President Ruto's people think can deliver the votes. Some elders are openly disowning the event, calling it a "political circus." Makes you wonder how much of this "traditional legitimacy" is just a paid performance, especially when claims surface about elders being paid Sh3,000 to show up. It sounds like more political shenanigans than genuine cultural heritage.

This isn't the first time elders have been trotted out to anoint a regional kingpin. We’ve seen this movie before with Kenyatta, Karume, Uhuru, Raila, and even Justin Muturi. Now it’s Kindiki's turn, apparently for the second time in a year. But when established elders like Kung'u Muigai from Kiama Kia Ma are calling the declaration "null and void" and stating their council wasn't involved, you know something's fishy. He even points out that some of these same elders were seen cozying up to Gachagua at his home, declaring *him* the leader. Talk about playing both sides of the political fence.

Public Service CS Geoffrey Ruku, a big proponent of this "realignment," is trying to spin it as a "solemn declaration" of support for Ruto. He’s trying to mend fences after his own attempts to split Mt Kenya into East and West with symbolic axes. Now, it’s all about unity, according to him. But Gachagua, now leading his own party, isn't buying it. He’s out in the US, calling it all a plot to divide the region and warning of a "reckoning time" in 2027. He even claims some elders were bribed, which, frankly, isn't the most shocking accusation in Kenyan politics.

The real question, buried under all the talk of tradition and unity, is whether President Ruto's camp has secured Mt Kenya's loyalty for the next election. Kindiki is being positioned as the bridge, but he's facing a formidable lineup of contenders, including Gachagua, Uhuru Kenyatta, Martha Karua, and others who are all vying to be the voice of the region. This isn't just about who holds an official position; it's about who can actually mobilize voters. Given the history, and the way these political maneuvers often unfold, it's a safe bet that more "cultural gatherings" and political backstabbing are on the horizon, much like the challenges Kenya's economy faces, as hinted by the EBC's domestic borrowing plans.

This whole Kindiki coronation is just another chapter in the ongoing saga of who truly controls the political narrative in Mt Kenya. It's a cynical game of thrones, played out with elders and traditional symbols, all in the pursuit of votes. Don't expect any genuine unity or cultural preservation; expect more political maneuvering, more accusations, and ultimately, more division disguised as progress.
