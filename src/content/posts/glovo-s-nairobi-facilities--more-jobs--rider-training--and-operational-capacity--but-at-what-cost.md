GLOVO'S JOB MIRAGE: MORE FACILITIES, SAME OLD GRIND IN NAIROBI

Glovo thinks it's building Kenya's future with Ksh10 billion. What they've really built are more fancy offices and another shiny "Glovo Centre" - just more boxes for the same old hustle. They're talking about jobs and opportunities, but let's be real, it's mostly just more ways for young people to chase a meager wage while Glovo pockets the profits. They’ve increased their operational capacity, sure, but that just means more eyes watching the riders work themselves ragged.

They're bragging about creating "income-generating opportunities," as if delivering someone else's samosa is a career path. With plans to onboard another 1,000 riders by year-end, pushing the total to over 3,700, they're essentially flooding the market with more desperate individuals willing to work longer hours for less. This is their highest onboarding figure since 2019, which means more competition, more pressure, and even slimmer pickings for those at the bottom of the food chain.

The Co-founder, Sacha Michaud, spouts sweet nothings about earning, learning, and building futures. But is he talking about building futures for the riders, or for Glovo's shareholders? The rider-led SACCO and financial literacy training are just lip service. It's a classic move: give them a tiny bit of control and a few tips on saving a few shillings, while the real money keeps flowing upwards. It’s like talking about the weather when you should be talking about the systemic issues, much like how some might discuss [Ruto Daughter's 'Koito' and Traditional Wedding: More Pomp Than Purpose?](/posts/ruto-daughter-s--koito--and-traditional-wedding--more-pomp-than-purpose) in the face of larger national concerns.

This whole expansion feels less like a genuine investment in Kenyans and more like a calculated move to solidify their market dominance. They're expanding their platform to connect over 6,000 businesses, and have "empowered" over 12,400 riders to earn income. But at what cost to their well-being and long-term prospects? The Principal Secretary's quote about strengthening national infrastructure sounds nice, but it's likely just jargon to justify a foreign company's expansion while we're left to deal with the realities of precarious work.
