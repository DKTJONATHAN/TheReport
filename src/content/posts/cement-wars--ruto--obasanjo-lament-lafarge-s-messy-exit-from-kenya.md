So, the big boys are at it again, this time over cement in Lamu. President Ruto and former Nigerian President Obasanjo are having a go at Lafarge, that old French outfit, for supposedly messing up the African cement game. Obasanjo's calling them out for not investing in good faith, while Ruto's busy celebrating Kenyans and East Africans taking over. It's the usual song and dance, isn't it? Blame the foreigner when things don't go according to plan.

Lafarge, once the king of African cement, apparently didn't take kindly to competition. According to Obasanjo, they threw every obstacle in Aliko Dangote's path, even dragging him to court over a plant in Senegal that sat idle for two years. Twelve million dollars later, Dangote could finally churn out his concrete. All this over a desire for monopoly. Now Lafarge is gone from Africa, and everyone's breathing a sigh of relief, or so they say.

Back in Kenya, Lafarge's exit was a bit more… domestic. Ruto slapped a levy on imported cement in 2023, and poof, the French decided Kenya wasn't worth the trouble anymore. He's quite chuffed about it, proclaiming that Kenyans and Tanzanians are now in charge. Apparently, Lafarge wasn't keen on expanding their facilities, preferring to import raw materials instead of digging them up locally. One "Adan" is even credited with driving them out. It’s a real-life corporate drama, far more interesting than anything you’d read about police uniforms changing.

The whole Dangote-in-Kenya saga is a bit of a mess, too. He announced a massive plant back in 2013, eyed some land, and then… nothing. Now, the excuse is that Kenya doesn't have the right kind of limestone, or it's too far away. But before that, Dangote was quite vocal about bribe demands during the Uhuru Kenyatta administration. So, we have foreign giants pulling out for one reason or another, and local politicians patting themselves on the back. It makes you wonder who's really pulling the strings.

It’s the same old story, really. Foreign investors come in, make their money, and then either get driven out or leave when the going gets tough, or when local interests start demanding their cut. And our leaders? They’re always ready with a narrative to make themselves look good, whether it’s about national pride or fighting corruption. The reality on the ground for the average Kenyan trying to build something is probably far less glamorous and a lot more about navigating these same old intrigues.

As for Citizen Digital wanting our "perspective"? My perspective is that this whole "cement war" is just another spectacle, designed to distract us from the real issues. While Obasanjo and Ruto debate Lafarge's alleged sins, we're still stuck with the same economic challenges, the same old political games, and the same old promises that never quite materialize. It’s a predictable corporate squabble over profits, wrapped up in patriotic rhetoric.
