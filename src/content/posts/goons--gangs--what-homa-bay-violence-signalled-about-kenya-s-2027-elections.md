Homa Bay: Goons, Gangs, and the Usual Kenyan Election Nonsense for 2027











So, Homa Bay. Fancy that. They're calling it a "democratic outlier" because a woman got elected governor and a bunch of other women snagged parliamentary seats. Wow, groundbreaking. Meanwhile, the same county is busy setting the stage for 2027 by staging a spectacular display of political thuggery, complete with dead bodies and injured folks. This isn't exactly a surprise, is it? It's Kenya. We do this. Gladys Wanga, the rising star, is already being whispered about as a potential running mate. Funny how fame and alleged gang orchestrations go hand-in-hand in this country.

The usual accusations are flying, of course. Wanga denies orchestrating attacks on her rivals, naturally. Instead, she's pointing fingers at other big names like James Orengo and Babu Owino, who are apparently trying to build their own "generational change" movement. This whole Linda Mwananchi thing sounds like another faction fighting for scraps, but this time with a slightly younger face at the front, pushing for Senator Sifuna. It’s the same old song, different players. The death of Raila Odinga has just thrown fuel on the fire, turning Nyanza into a wrestling ring for the remnants of his legacy.

This isn't just some local spat. This is textbook Kenya. We've seen this movie before, especially after the 2007/2008 debacle. Politicians and their cronies whipping up criminal gangs - "goons" as they so delicately call them - to do their dirty work. The fact that opposition leaders are now talking about "private security" just confirms the rot runs deep. It’s a race to the bottom, and the ACLED report saying a fragmented opposition means less pressure on the ruling party to moblise goons? That’s just a polite way of saying they’ll all be arming up. It’s a grim prediction, much like some of the economic woes we've discussed, like [KenGen's 'Renewable' Energy Strategy: Kenya Pushes, You Pay](/posts/kengen-s--renewable--energy-strategy--kenya-pushes--you-pay).

The Interior Minister himself admitted the political class funds and protects these gangs. Over 100 of them, he says, operating across the country. And the Homa Bay incident? A stark reminder that these thugs are still very much in business, ready to serve whoever pays them. It's not about policy, it's not about progress, it's about raw power and the willingness to crush anyone who gets in the way. Welcome to the pre-election season, Kenya style. Get ready for more of the same.
