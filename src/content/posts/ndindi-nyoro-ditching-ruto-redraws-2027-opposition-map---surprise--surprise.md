So, Ndindi Nyoro, the chap who was practically glued to Ruto's hip helping him "build the wheelbarrow," has decided his former boss is now the "number one enemy." And what's the grand plan? To unite the motley crew of opposition leaders he once probably scoffed at, like Gachagua, Matiang'i, and Kalonzo. Apparently, a year of "soul-searching" involves realizing that promises are just words and that power corrupts even the most ardent "wheelbarrow" enthusiasts. It’s almost inspiring, if you squint hard enough and ignore the stench of opportunism.

He’s hopping over to the Peoples’ Party of Kenya (PPK), because clearly, the UDA wasn't "yellow" enough for him anymore. Nyoro's promising to criss-cross the country, just like he did for Ruto, but this time with the sole purpose of seeing Ruto out. He even dangled the possibility of himself being the flagbearer, or supporting another equally flawed opposition leader. It’s all about unity and defeating Ruto, he claims. Let’s hope this isn't just another elaborate ploy to fragment the opposition vote in Mt Kenya, a tactic Ruto himself is probably smiling about from his corner office.

The man is essentially admitting he's ready to deploy the same political machinery he used to boost Ruto's image against him. He even had the audacity to say, "You will know the stuff we are made of." Oh, we know the stuff you're made of, Nyoro. It's the same stuff that gets everyone else into trouble. Remember when we talked about how Ruto's health town hall was a system that only works if you're lucky? Well, this political realignment is like that - a gamble where the only guaranteed winners are those who know how to play the game.

His immediate task is to wrangle together Gachagua, Matiang'i, Musyoka, Sifuna, Muturi, Karua, and Wamalwa. That's a gathering of egos and ambitions that would make Solomon scratch his head. Nyoro’s begging them not to be selfish and to pick a candidate and running mate by next year. "We don't want to take chances in our journey to liberate Kenyans," he declared. Liberate Kenyans from what? From having to deal with the same old faces recycled into new "opposition" movements?

Of course, some of Gachagua's allies are already sniffing around, accusing Nyoro of being a Ruto mole, creating another party to split the vote. Nyoro, naturally, denies this, promising to "take head-on" anyone who attacks opposition leaders. This whole "Wantam" movement sounds less like a liberation army and more like a political speed dating event where everyone's trying to find the best seat at the table for 2027. It’s all about who inherits Mt Kenya's support, and Nyoro's just the latest contender throwing his hat into the ring, hoping to benefit from the chaos.
