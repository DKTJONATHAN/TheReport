So, they hauled in 37 "suspected gang members" after the Linda Mwananchi rally in Homa Bay went up in flames. Crude weapons, they say - machetes, ropes, rungus. The usual party favors for a political gathering these days. The police are still chasing one Oliver Jaoko, alias "Olivetti," who’s apparently at large. Sounds like they're trying to catch smoke with a sieve, but hey, at least they're pretending to do something. It’s always the small fish caught while the big sharks swim free.

And let's not forget the collateral damage. A police officer dead, several others nursing injuries, and a trail of torched vehicles and damaged property. This is what happens when politics becomes a contact sport. They talk about "rule of law" and "impartiality," but when the cash flows, everyone has their price, don't they? It's a classic Kenyan script: chaos erupts, arrests are made, and then the real players get away with it, probably already planning the next show.

Bishop Hudson Ndeda is tut-tutting about police getting involved in "partisan politics." Give me a break. You think the police are just innocent bystanders? They’re part of the system, and the system thrives on this circus. They preach about impartial security, but when has that ever truly happened in this country? It’s all theatre. The real issue isn't the police's impartiality, it's the underlying rot that fuels this madness in the first place.

Then you have the politicians, wailing about "political intolerance" and "goonism." Dr. Fred Matiang’i is warning of "final descent into chaos and anarchy." Oh, please. These are the same people who orchestrate this nonsense, then cry foul when it gets a bit too messy. They fund these thugs, use them to intimidate opponents, and then act surprised when things get out of hand. It's a calculated risk, and the average Kenyan is the one left picking up the pieces, just like after that [Kenyan Passport Scandal](/posts/kenyan-passport-scandal--state-denial-meets-us-sanction-data---big-mess).

The Jubilee Party and People's Renaissance Movement are demanding action. ODM youth leader Brian Midiwo is crying about the region's image being tarnished. As if Homa Bay's image was pristine before this. This is just another Tuesday in Kenya. The politicians point fingers, the police make a show of arrests, and the cycle of violence and corruption continues, all under the guise of "democracy." Don't hold your breath waiting for justice; it's a luxury most Kenyans can't afford.
