So, X is now slapping "misinformation" labels on DMs. As if that's going to stop the Nairobi rumour mill. We saw it in 2022, didn't we? False claims about candidates, voting, you name it, spread faster than a hot matatu on a Friday. By the time the fact checkers were wiping the sweat off their brows, the damage was done. The 2027 poll? Don't hold your breath for a cleaner race.

The "lessons" from Kenya's 2022 election are as predictable as the traffic jams on Waiyaki Way. Apparently, false claims have wings, while verified facts crawl. It's a tale as old as time, or at least as old as the last presidential election. We're supposed to be smarter this time around, but let's be real, how many of us actually bother to verify before we share that juicy gossip?

This whole "X rolls out direct messages" thing sounds like slapping a band-aid on a gaping wound. The real problem isn't the platform; it's us. We're addicted to drama, to outrage, to anything that confirms our biases. The truth is often too boring, too nuanced, and frankly, too inconvenient. Expect the same old song and dance, just with fancier digital packaging.

And while we're on the subject of predictable political cycles, it's worth remembering that some issues never go away. We've seen reports of [MARAGA: KENYA HEADS FOR STATE FAILURE AMID KIKUYU CLASHES AND SOUTHERN BYPASS CHAOS](/posts/maraga--kenya-heads-for-state-failure-amid-kikuyu-clashes-and-southern-bypass-chaos) and the unrealistic dreams of [Kenya's First World Vision: Not Abstract, Just Unrealistic For Most](/posts/kenya-s-first-world-vision--not-abstract--just-unrealistic-for-most). These are the undercurrents that fuel the misinformation, the real grievances that get twisted and amplified.

So, as we brace for 2027, know this: the misinformation machine is already revving up. The "insights" are just a fancy way of saying "nothing will change." We'll be drowning in fake news, just like before, while the powers that be figure out new ways to play us. Cheers to that.
