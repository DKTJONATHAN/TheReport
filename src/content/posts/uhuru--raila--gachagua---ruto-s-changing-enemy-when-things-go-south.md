RUTO'S EVER-SHUFFLING ENEMY: UHURO, RAILA, GACHAGUA AND THE SHIFTING BLAME GAME

WARNING: President Ruto's presidency is a masterclass in blame-shifting, with his enemies changing faster than the price of unga. Don't expect accountability, just more drama.

Ruto's been on a blame-game marathon since day one, first pointing fingers at Uhuru Kenyatta for a broken economy and empty coffers. Then, Raila Odinga and his protests became the bogeyman, the reason for every economic headache. It was all Uhuru's fault for his "handshake" with Raila, which apparently poisoned Ruto's "nascent rule." Kimani Ichung'wah even threatened Uhuru's properties, because that's how you solve national crises - with threats and vague accusations.

But as 2026 rolls around and the pressure mounts, the enemy is no longer a single face. It’s everywhere and nowhere, a convenient phantom, always vaguely resembling Uhuru. Now, Ruto's crying foul that his predecessor is orchestrating the opposition for 2027. This is the same Uhuru who apparently "set him up for failure" and promised his presidency would collapse in six months. It’s almost poetic, this constant search for a scapegoat, a distraction from the real issues. It reminds me of how Kenya's fuel market is constantly struggling, holding its own against shipping chaos, but always looking for someone to blame for the price hikes.

Then came Rigathi Gachagua. Once the loyal deputy, now the enemy within, impeached for being "incompatible with the values of a progressive president." Gachagua, however, calls it all "explanations of a man who appeared not to understand why and how he had won the presidency." He rightly points out Ruto's ego trip, thinking he's the brightest and best, incapable of listening to advisors. According to Gachagua, Ruto's gone full paranoid, seeing Gen Z protests as a coup plotted by Kenyatta or external forces, leading to brute force crackdowns.

And the blame continues to morph. Courts are derailing progress, activist judges are in cahoots with cartels. The media amplifies negativity, and even the church, after receiving millions in donations, is now criticized for "missing out on cash" if they dare to voice dissent. It's a circus of accusations, from politicians impregnating and murdering women to parents failing their children for raising kids who question high taxes. It's a classic case of the captain blaming the entire crew when the ship is sinking, when perhaps, the captain himself is steering them into the iceberg. The problem, as Gachagua and others have pointed out, is very likely him, and his policies.

Political scientist John Okumu sums it up perfectly: Ruto has become a "crybaby," unsure who's eating into his re-election bid. He's so busy chasing phantoms - Uhuru, Raila, Gachagua, the Ford Foundation, Mt Kenya, even his own Deputy President Kithure Kindiki - that he's blinded himself to the most obvious enemy: himself. The constant shifting of blame isn't political genius; it's desperation, a sign of a leader who has no answers and is terrified of facing the music.
