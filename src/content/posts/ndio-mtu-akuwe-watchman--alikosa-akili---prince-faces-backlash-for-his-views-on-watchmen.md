'Prince' of What? Nairobi Watchmen Earn More Respect Than This Flop

Nairobi's finest, or rather, its most clueless, are at it again. This time it's some self-proclaimed 'Kenyan Prince' who's decided to grace us with his "intelligence" during a chat with Oga Obinna. Apparently, this digital guru, who trades forex and, one assumes, makes a killing doing so, has no earthly idea how anyone could possibly be a watchman. Yes, you heard that right. He thinks being a security guard is a sign of an intellect deficiency. Bless his cotton socks.

It’s always rich when these social media types, who likely spend their days chasing likes and shilling products, suddenly develop opinions on honest work. The public, thankfully, has a bit more sense. Many Kenyans actually grew up with parents who donned the watchman uniform, providing for their families and ensuring kids got a decent education. This guy’s "enlightened" opinion, as his girlfriend clearly found out in real time, is just an insult to a massive chunk of our population.

Then there's the follow-up, because of course, there is. After getting caught looking a bit too friendly with another woman in Malindi, our 'Prince' decided an apology was in order. And what better way to say "sorry" than a public display of wealth? A suitcase full of sh1,000 notes, a party, and a Range Rover - all for his girlfriend, Stephanie Ngunjiri. It’s the kind of spectacle that makes you wonder if these people have ever heard of discretion, or perhaps even basic financial planning, beyond showing off stacks of cash like some kind of backwards progress. Speaking of questionable financial decisions, one can only hope this isn't as poorly thought out as some of the schemes we've seen discussed elsewhere; remember how [BRIBES, BETS, AND THE NEW BOARDROOM BOSS: CAN GEORGE KITHI FIX THIS MESS?](/posts/bribes--bets--and-the-new-boardroom-boss--can-george-kithi-fix-this-mess) turned out?

The reactions online were, as expected, a mix of awe at the sheer audacity and disgust at the insensitivity. While some might be impressed by the extravagant apology, many are questioning the whole charade. Is this genuine remorse, or just another PR stunt to keep his name in the headlines? Given his prior comments about watchmen, it's hard to believe he's suddenly developed empathy. It’s just another day in Nairobi, where the rich flaunt their riches and the rest of us try to make sense of it all.

The digital age has a funny way of elevating people with more ego than sense. This time, it’s a forex trader and social media personality going by the rather grand moniker 'Kenyan Prince' who’s managed to land himself in a social media dumpster fire. During a video interview with Oga Obinna, this so-called 'Prince' decided to share his profound insights, claiming that people who work as watchmen are, to put it mildly, lacking in the intelligence department. Apparently, he’s baffled as to how anyone could choose such a profession.

The internet, as it often does, responded with the swiftness of a well-aimed banana peel. A significant portion of the public, particularly those who understand the realities of life in Nairobi beyond the filters of Instagram, were rightfully offended. For many, a parent’s job as a security guard wasn’t a choice born out of intellectual deficiency, but a means to put food on the table and ensure their children received an education. The shock on his girlfriend’s face in the video, a silent testament to his insensitivity, likely mirrored the disbelief of many viewers.

Following the public outcry, which is a predictable outcome when you insult a large segment of the population, our 'Prince' felt the need to issue an apology to his girlfriend, Stephanie Ngunjiri. And how does one apologize in the era of influencers? With a grand, over-the-top display of wealth, naturally. A TikTok video showcased a suitcase stuffed with sh1,000 notes, alongside a party and a Range Rover, all part of this elaborate peace offering after he was apparently seen "getting close with another woman" in Malindi. It’s a classic case of trying to buy forgiveness with cash, a strategy that rarely ends well. One hopes he’s not employing the same flawed logic in other areas of his life, considering the precarious nature of some financial ventures, as highlighted in discussions about [BRIBES, BETS, AND THE NEW BOARDROOM BOSS: CAN GEORGE KITHI FIX THIS MESS?](/posts/bribes--bets--and-the-new-boardroom-boss--can-george-kithi-fix-this-mess).

The flamboyant "apology" predictably drew a torrent of mixed reactions. While some followers might be momentarily dazzled by the sheer volume of cash, more discerning observers are questioning the practicality and safety of such a display. Is this a genuine act of contrition, or simply another calculated move to generate engagement and keep his name relevant? Given his initial dismissive attitude towards honest work, it’s safe to assume this is more about managing his own public image than any deep-seated remorse. Nairobi has seen its share of these spectacles, and the 'Kenyan Prince' has just added another chapter to the ongoing saga of fame, fortune, and questionable judgment.
