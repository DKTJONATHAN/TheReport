Ruto: Kenyans To Keep Paying For Housing Levy Until Uhuru's Ghost Is Evicted

WARNING: President Ruto promises the housing levy will continue indefinitely, a move that sounds like another one of those endless hustles that go wrong. Don't expect a house anytime soon, unless you're dreaming of a cardboard box.

The president, bless his heart, is telling us the revolving fund for affordable housing is the next best thing since sliced bread. Apparently, every time someone buys one of these "affordable" houses on public land, the money magically reappears to build more. It's like a real-life version of the Kenyan economy, where you put in money and it disappears. He claims this will go on until "every Kenyan has access to a home." Yeah, right. We'll be paying this levy until the sun cools down, and even then, I doubt it. The man is basically saying, "Keep paying, because the shortage is growing. And even if you can't afford it, tough luck, it's for the collective good." Sounds familiar, doesn't it? Like a good old-fashioned Kenyan hustle gone wrong.

He’s defending this whole public land for housing scheme, saying it's "held in trust for Kenyans." So, we own it, but we also have to pay for the privilege of someone else developing it and then selling it back to us. It's a classic Kenyan move: you own the land, but you'll be renting your own plot from someone else. And heaven forbid you ask too many questions, like why salaried workers who might not even qualify for these houses are still contributing. The president’s answer? "What is the problem?" The problem, Mr. President, is that some of us are tired of being treated like ATMs.

The whole idea of a revolving fund is just a fancy way of saying they'll keep collecting. He’s got this big number, 2.5 million housing units deficit, and he’s using it as an excuse to milk Kenyans dry. He says it’ll stop "until and unless each and every one of us has got a house." This is the same kind of talk we hear from leaders warning against divisive politics, but when it comes to their pockets, suddenly unity means everyone chipping in for their grand plans. We've seen this movie before.

Even the idea of using public land sounds like a good deal, until you realize it's public land that’s going to be developed and sold by private entities, with the proceeds supposedly going back into the pot. It’s a beautiful circular economy for them, and a one-way ticket for our cash. They're essentially saying, "We'll use what's yours to build something that will be sold back to you, and you'll keep paying for it until we say so." It's a masterclass in creative accounting, Nairobi style.

So, when will Kenyans stop paying the housing levy? According to Ruto, it's when every single Kenyan is tucked into their own affordable house. Given the current pace of development and the growing deficit, you might want to start looking for a good permanent spot under a bridge. It's the ultimate hustle, alright, and it looks like we're the ones being hustled.
