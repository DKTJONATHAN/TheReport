PS SING'OEI OFFERS CONDESCENDING APOLOGY TO BURUNDI NATIONALS HARASSED IN KENYA
WARNING: Another government mess, more empty promises. Expect the usual runaround.
PS Sing'oei visits Burundi Embassy, promises documentation and safety.
Gossip
Harassment, Deportation, Bureaucracy
Foreigners
Belinda Achieng' looks at the latest round of government theatrics.

So, our Principal Secretary, Mr. Korir Sing’oei, has decided to grace the Burundi Embassy with his presence to offer an "apology" to their nationals. Apparently, after some poorly communicated government directive about unlicensed small businesses, these "neighbours" were subjected to "ridicule." Yes, because clearly, the most pressing issue facing Kenya is the potential offense taken by a few Burundians. This is the kind of high-level diplomacy we’ve come to expect - a carefully orchestrated performance of empathy to paper over another policy blunder.

Sing’oei goes on to assure everyone that the intention was to foster peace and brotherhood among Africans, especially East Africans, while ensuring everyone is "properly documented." Right. Because nothing screams "brotherhood" like a government suddenly waking up to the fact that some foreigners are living and working here, and now they need to be catalogued. It’s a bit like finding out you have guests in your house and instead of offering them tea, you start taking their fingerprints. Reminds me of the chaos we saw during the doctors' strike violence, where authorities only seem to act when things get so messy you can't ignore it anymore, thanks to Koome's appeal allowing police to continue playing games. [Koome's Appeal Allowed: Police Boss Still Playing Games After Doctors' Strike Violence](/posts/koome-s-appeal-allowed--police-boss-still-playing-games-after-doctors--strike-violence)

The PS promises to tighten security and conduct public awareness campaigns. Translation: they'll make a show of "tightening security" to soothe ruffled feathers, and then they'll explain to us Kenyans why we shouldn't be annoyed by the very foreigners they're now scrambling to document. It’s a classic case of the government creating a problem, then swooping in to "solve" it, all while patting themselves on the back for their "good intentions."

Meanwhile, hundreds of Burundians are flocking to their embassy and cyber cafes, passports and IDs in hand. This "routine documentation exercise" has become a frantic dash, fueled by anxiety over President Ruto's recent pronouncements. Videos show them queuing, some allegedly preparing to flee back home. It’s a grim reminder that when the government starts talking about "directives," it often translates into real people scrambling for their lives, or at least their livelihoods.

And let's not forget the police, who, despite the visible anxiety and the very real surge of activity, claim they haven't received any reports of harassment. Of course not. Because harassment, in their book, only counts if it's formally lodged with a report. This whole situation is just another day in Nairobi: a bit of governmental posturing, a lot of public confusion, and the ordinary folk, both Kenyan and foreign, left to navigate the fallout.

It’s all just a charade, really. The government wants to know where everyone is, what they're doing, and ensure they're "documented." It’s less about brotherhood and more about control, dressed up in diplomatic platitudes. And we, the citizens, are left to watch, cynical as ever, as the same old script unfolds.
