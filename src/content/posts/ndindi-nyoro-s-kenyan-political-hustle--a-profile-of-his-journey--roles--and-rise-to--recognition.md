So, Ndindi Nyoro. From a nobody MP in 2013 to a face you can't escape on Kenyan television. Apparently, this is his "rise," all tied to President Ruto's power play. What a surprise. Now he's out, declaring himself "no longer part of the government," ready to announce his "political direction." You mean like a chameleon changing colours? Ahead of 2027, everyone's scrambling, and Nyoro’s just making sure he’s still in the game, no matter the jersey.

His big break? Joining the Uhuru-Ruto alliance back when they were building Jubilee. He stuck around as things got spicy between them, and then, the handshake. Ruto and his crew felt threatened, so Nyoro, like many of the young ambitious ones, flocked to Ruto's emerging camp. Suddenly, he’s a star in the "Tanga Tanga" circus, the loudmouth defending Ruto and barking at anyone associated with that handshake. By 2022, he was no longer just a constituency guy; he was a key Ruto campaigner, especially up in Mount Kenya.

Ruto wins, and Nyoro gets a seat at the big table. He’s part of the "influential young politicians" in this Kenya Kwanza administration, even snagging the chairmanship of the National Assembly Budget and Appropriations Committee. Nice gig, keeping an eye on the national purse. But then, the real drama started. The spat between Ruto and his deputy, Gachagua. When Gachagua faced impeachment - an impeachment Nyoro conveniently "didn't participate in" - citing "conscience and Christian values." Oh, please. A few months later, poof, he's out of the Budget committee, then out entirely. Apparently, nobody bothered to tell him why. This is how the game is played, my friends.

Suddenly, the loudest Ruto defender is questioning everything: taxes, debt, fuel prices, government spending. He's become the "persistent parliamentary voice" against the government he helped put in power. But then, the Finance Bill vote in June 2026. After all his grand pronouncements, he’s nowhere to be seen. He scrambles back with some flimsy excuse about "engagements abroad." Right. It's clear his calculations are more about survival than principle. Now he's declared himself "out of government," asking for a few weeks to figure out his next move. This whole saga is just another reminder of how Kenya justice negotiations with power often means more disappointment for the rest of us.

Nyoro's problem is he built his whole identity around Ruto. He was a product of that project, a loyal soldier. Now, breaking away is a big deal, not like some small-time politician jumping ship. The Mount Kenya political landscape is also a mess, with Gachagua building his own base. Nyoro's options? Side with Gachagua, try to build his own thing, or join another coalition. Or maybe just keep mouthing off as an "independent" while playing the field. His public statements scream ambition, but for whom? He hasn't spilled the beans yet.

So, what’s next for Nyoro? The young TNA politician, the Ruto ally, and now the disillusioned insider. Will he use the political capital he’s gathered, or was he just a passenger on Ruto's rocket? Given Kenyan politics, it's probably a bit of both, and a lot of strategic maneuvering. We'll be watching, mostly with a sigh and a rolling of the eyes, as the political musical chairs continue.
