Nyoro Calls Out Ruto Over Insults Against Uhuru, Gachagua

Ndindi Nyoro, the self-appointed guardian of presidential decorum, has decided it's time to tell President Ruto to pipe down. Apparently, the Head of State needs a lesson in how to talk without sounding like a drunk uncle at a wedding. Nyoro’s sudden concern for dignity and respect, especially towards retired President Kenyatta, is just rich, considering the history. It's almost like he forgot Ruto was Uhuru's deputy for years, spewing whatever he wanted then. Now, suddenly, it’s about looking out for the next guy, as if Ruto will suddenly care about his own legacy when he's out chasing chickens.

The Kiharu MP’s defense of Rigathi Gachagua is equally theatrical. He's begging Ruto to stop hurling insults at his second-in-command. This whole drama is just a clear sign of cracks in the "Hustler Nation" narrative. When allies start publicly begging for civility from the boss, it’s not a good look. It screams internal squabbles being aired out for all of us to see, while we’re still trying to figure out how to make ends meet. Speaking of economic woes, remember how the shilling is still hurting? It’s almost like political drama distracts from the real issues, and frankly, [Mt Kenya Workers Urged to Prioritise Savings as Only 18% Achieve Financial Health Despite High Financial Inclusion](/posts/mt-kenya-workers-urged-to-prioritise-savings-as-only-18--achieve-financial-health-despite-high-financial-inclusion) has more useful advice than this political theatre.

Nyoro then pivots to lecturing Ruto on how to handle young leaders. Apparently, political differences shouldn’t devolve into playground name-calling. This is the same country where leaders, including Nyoro himself, have not exactly been shy with their tongues. It's a bit rich for someone to suddenly demand a higher standard of discourse when it suits them. The hypocrisy is so thick you could cut it with a panga.

But wait, the man isn't just criticising; he’s also trying to position himself as a voice for the people. He’s telling Kenyans to get involved in choosing the opposition flag bearer for 2027. He wants us to judge leaders on their "qualities" and "track records." As if we haven't seen the track records of most of these characters already. They’ve had their chances, and frankly, most of them have left a trail of broken promises and empty coffers.

Nyoro’s assurance that Kenyans want change and that the opposition will unite behind one candidate sounds more like wishful thinking than a concrete plan. We’ve heard this song and dance before. The opposition usually finds a way to implode before it even gets off the ground. Let’s see if they can actually rally behind someone credible, or if it’s just another round of political musical chairs, with the same old faces playing different tunes.
