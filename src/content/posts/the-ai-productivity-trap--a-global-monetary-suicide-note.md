As corporations dump hundreds of billions into data centers and specialized chips, the global economy is entering what optimists call the Productivity J-Curve. In reality, this is a period of profound economic friction. Massive capital expenditure is being diverted into infrastructure that produces nothing today but promises everything tomorrow. This creates a surge in demand for resources and energy, driving up costs while actual output remains stagnant. For the average citizen, this means Transition Inflation - a fancy academic term for being poorer while the tech elite build their digital cathedrals.

The international impact is a coordinated descent into chaos. While the Federal Reserve or the European Central Bank might attempt to restrain this inflation with higher interest rates, they risk strangling the very innovation they claim to covet. If they keep rates low to facilitate AI investment, they feed a speculative bubble that makes the 1990s dot-com era look like a modest yard sale. This is the ultimate policy trap. There is no neutral rate when the underlying economy is being re-wired in real time by algorithms that even their creators do not fully comprehend.

Compounding this disaster is the erosion of truth. As we have previously seen in the way AI chatbots can be manipulated in minutes to provide false information (see: /posts/ai-chatbots-manipulated-in-minutes--a-new-threat-to-information-accuracy), the data feeding economic models is becoming increasingly unreliable. If an algorithm is pricing a loan or managing a supply chain based on synthetic data or hallucinated demand, the resulting productivity is a ghost. We are building a global financial system on top of a digital mirage, where expectations move faster than reality and factories cannot keep up with the hype.

The cynical truth is that central banks will likely choose the path of least resistance. They will allow inflation to run hot, blaming the structural transition while households are crushed by the cost of living. They will prioritize the survival of the tech-industrial complex over the stability of the currency. By the time the supposed productivity gains finally arrive, the social and economic fabric of the West may already be shredded. The AI revolution is not a rising tide that lifts all boats; it is a tidal wave that the captains of industry are trying to surf while everyone else is left on the shore without a life jacket.

In the end, AI is transforming the economy by transforming the mechanisms of measurement. When you can no longer trust the statistics, and when the central bank can no longer distinguish a supply shock from a technological leap, the result is not progress. It is a slow-motion car crash disguised as a digital miracle. The world is betting its entire financial future on a black box, and the people holding the levers have no idea what is inside.
