NPS Announces Police Cadet Recruitment, Sets December 10 Deadline - Get Ready to Queue

WARNING: More university graduates about to trade their degrees for batons and low pay. The National Police Service is looking for fresh faces, but don't expect a walk in the park. Just another bureaucratic exercise to sift through the desperate.

The National Police Service (NPS) is once again throwing open its doors for police cadet recruitment, inviting university graduates aged between 22 and 30 years to throw their hats in the ring. So, you've got your degree, your C+ in KCSE, and enough English or Kiswahili to string a sentence together? Good for you. Just make sure you meet the endless list of requirements, including having no criminal record - unlike some of the less savory characters operating in places that might remind you of these [Cambodian Scam Compounds Exposed](/posts/cambodian-scam-compounds-exposed--fake-police-stations-serve-as-fronts-for-global-fraud). The application window is a generous two weeks, from November 25 to December 10, 2026, with shortlisted candidates to be announced a mere month later.

Apparently, holding a degree in forensic science, medicine, or even engineering is now a "plus." Because a police officer with a pharmacy degree is exactly what we need to tackle petty theft. They’re even looking for journalists and media folks, probably to help spin the official narrative better. Don't forget to upload copies of everything - your ID, your certificates, your school leaving certificates, and probably your birth certificate as well, just to be sure you're not an imposter.

And for the ladies, a crucial reminder: no pregnancy allowed. Apparently, a baby bump is more dangerous than a loaded firearm during training. The NPS is also quick to remind everyone that this whole process is "FREE AND OPEN," which, in Nairobi terms, usually translates to a lot of waiting, a lot of forms, and a healthy dose of cynicism. They're already warning against bribes and corruption, which, let's be honest, is like telling a fish not to swim.

For those brave or desperate enough to apply, get your biodata, qualifications, experience, courses, and referees ready. Submit it all online between the specified dates. If you're shortlisted, you might get a call for a physical interview. And remember, if anyone promises you a shortcut or a guaranteed spot, they're either a scammer or already part of the problem. Report them, they say. Good luck with that.
