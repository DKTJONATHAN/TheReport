So, the Central Bank of Kenya, bless their predictable hearts, has dropped another report confirming what any matatu tout knows: money breeds money. Apparently, a whopping two-thirds of all commercial bank branches are crammed into the top 10 wealthiest counties. Nairobi, the undisputed kingpin, has nearly 40% of the entire country’s branch network. Kiambu and Mombasa trail behind, also swimming in concrete and tellers. It’s as if banks have discovered that where the cash is flowing, that's where they should set up shop. Groundbreaking.

These golden 10 counties, making up a measly 68% of the nation's economic output, are also where you'll find 68% of the physical bank branches. Shocking, right? While the rest of the country is out here trying to survive on hope and good Wi-Fi signals, these chosen few get the full, in-person, "sir, your loan application has been approved" treatment. The banks, bless their strategic minds, have apparently figured out that putting branches where the established markets and commercial activity are located makes… sense. Who would have thought?

And don't even get me started on the "expansion." Nairobi added seven new branches last year, Kiambu six. Meanwhile, some counties probably lost a branch to a rogue hyena or a business decision that finally made sense. The CBK blathers on about "emerging growth areas" and "sector modernisation" with digital products. But let's be real, for anything beyond sending your mum Sh500, you still need to walk into a building. And those buildings are in the places that already have everything. It's a cycle as predictable as the traffic on Mombasa Road. We saw a similar concentration of corporate failure masked as incidents recently, and this feels like the financial equivalent, just less dramatic and more… expected.

Of course, they'll tell you about financial inclusion rates. Kiambu and Nairobi are strutting around with 94% and 93.7% respectively. Kisumu, Uasin Gishu, Machakos - they’re doing their best, bless their hearts. But these numbers don't change the fact that if your business is in, say, Garissa, and you need actual banking services beyond a mobile money transaction, you might as well be on the moon. The banks are happy to collect deposits from everywhere, but they're only willing to build fancy offices where the deals are already being signed. It's the Nairobi way, the Kiambu way, the Mombasa way. The rest of us? We'll just keep checking our phones.
