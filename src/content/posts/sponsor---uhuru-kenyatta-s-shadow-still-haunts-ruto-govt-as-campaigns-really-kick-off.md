It's the same old song and dance, isn't it? Barely a week goes by without some Kenya Kwanza big shot pointing fingers at retired President Uhuru Kenyatta, not as some dignified elder, but as the shadowy financier of every critic breathing down President William Ruto's neck. This "Uhuru the puppeteer, Uhuru the bankroller" narrative has become so tired, it's less news and more the soundtrack to the never-ending 2027 campaign. Ruto himself, after a music festival gig - because nothing screams serious leadership like critiquing your predecessor at a concert - decided to mock Uhuru's legacy and accuse him of not even funding his supposed proxies properly. Seriously, "collect fuel money"? Is that the best they can come up with?

Ruto isn't the only one with this Uhuru obsession. His cronies are chiming in, labelling opposition figures as "proxies" and accusing the "retired man" of financing everything from political convoys to internal party rifts. ODM’s Junet Mohamed is out here claiming Uhuru is orchestrating splits to weaken Ruto - as if the opposition needs help fracturing itself. Even Western Kenya loyalists are pointing fingers. It's a convenient way to dismiss genuine public anger over the cost of living and taxation by painting it all as a mercenary project orchestrated by a bitter former boss, rather than, you know, actual people being fed up. It's a clever move to undermine the opposition's "crowdfunded" image, a tactic Ruto himself used in 2022 against the "dynasty."

But the real fear for Ruto isn't just the rhetoric. It’s structural. Uhuru still holds cards: a functioning Jubilee party, a grip on some Mt Kenya votes that Ruto desperately needs to counter, and, of course, actual money and networks. The mere *possibility* of Uhuru pulling together disparate opposition factions - Gachagua's crew, Kalonzo's bloc, Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi, and Jubilee - into a unified force against Ruto is enough to send shivers down State House spines. That's the nightmare scenario: not a single strong challenger, but a Kenyatta-brokered coalition reminiscent of the 2002 anti-incumbent wave. It’s a far cry from the simple "Kenya Kwanza versus the opposition" story they want to sell.

Then there's the whole Moses Kuria "fattened for slaughter" comment. It implies Uhuru isn't just funding Sifuna, but using him as a pawn, with Kalonzo Musyoka already lined up as the preferred 2027 candidate. It suggests Uhuru's moves are less about elevating individuals and more about strategic positioning for a succession already decided behind closed doors. Kalonzo’s camp welcoming such talk confirms the internal rivalries are real, meaning the fight over who benefits from Uhuru's alleged patronage might be more significant than the funding itself. This internal squabbling is far more interesting than the predictable blame game.

On the formal side, Uhuru has installed Fred Matiang'i as Jubilee's deputy party leader and endorsed him for the 2027 presidential candidacy. This is a calculated move, giving Jubilee a loyalist with institutional standing in the United Opposition and ensuring the party isn't just swallowed up. Matiang'i, as Uhuru's former Interior CS, brings a technocratic image, a stark contrast to the populist energy of others. This ensures Jubilee, and by extension Mt Kenya's political weight, isn't sidelined in any future deal. As long as no single opposition figure consolidates the anti-Ruto vote, Uhuru remains the kingmaker, his endorsement the prize everyone is vying for, all while he operates from the shadows.

For Ruto, the constant Uhuru baiting is a strategic play. It's about stripping the opposition of its "people's movement" image and painting it as recycled dynasty politics. By forcing Uhuru into a public spat, or at least dragging him into the mud, they hope to destabilize the opposition's narrative. Whether Uhuru personally re-enters politics or not, his influence is undeniable. With less than a year to the elections, the most crucial political conversations are about a man who no longer holds office, a clear sign of how much his shadow still looms large over the country. It's a fascinating display of political maneuvering, and frankly, quite entertaining to watch from the sidelines, especially when you consider how Kenyan 'Top Influencers' grab millions while the creator economy is mostly hot air.
