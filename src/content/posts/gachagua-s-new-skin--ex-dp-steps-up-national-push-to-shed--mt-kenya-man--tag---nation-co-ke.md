Gachagua's New Skin? Ex-DP Tries to Shed 'Mt Kenya Man' Tag - And Nairobi is Watching Closely

WARNING: This is not a revolution, it's a rebranding exercise. Gachagua, the former DP, is desperately trying to convince everyone he's not just a tribal kingpin anymore. He's hitting the road, kissing babies, and promising the moon, all while hoping you forget his "shares" narrative. Don't hold your breath.

The man who once told us government benefits should flow to those who voted for him is now playing the national opposition leader. Gachagua is trotting all over the Rift Valley, South Rift specifically, trying to drum up support for his "Democracy for Citizens Party" (DCP). He's even planning a trip to America, presumably to ask for more money to fund this whole charade. He claims he's accepted nationwide, but let's be real, it’s a long shot. The man needs a miracle to shed that "tribal package" image, as one observer put it.

His strategy? To unite Mt Kenya with "serious blocs" and talk to leaders from "various parts of the country." He's talking about bringing Kikuyu and Kalenjin elders together, promising to secure the Narok gubernatorial seat, and even wants to help a certain candidate win in Emurua Dikirr. It’s all about building a "nationwide political movement" to kick Ruto out in 2027. He's so sure of himself, he even claims to have the backing of a million Maasai and 600,000 Kipsigis voters. We’ll see about that. It’s all sounding a bit like the new "supremo" with the same old hunger for power.

Gachagua is also throwing shade at President Ruto, accusing him of neglecting his own backyard and funneling resources to opposition strongholds. He's using land disputes and cattle rustling in Kilgoris as a platform, blaming the government for killings. Meanwhile, government officials claim they've bought land to settle families. It’s the usual political theatre, designed to make you forget who was in power just yesterday and promises a better tomorrow that never comes.

The ambition is clear: unseat Ruto in 2027. He’s presenting himself as the voice of the people who feel abandoned, especially in the South Rift. He's even threatening to reveal what he knows about the death of a former MP. It's a bold move, but considering his past, it's more about political maneuvering than genuine concern. The man's trying to convert his Mt Kenya base into a "pillar for national outreach," but the question remains: can he truly transform himself from a "manner less bully" to a national leader, or is this just another well-rehearsed illusion?

Let's not forget the context. In 2022, Mount Kenya overwhelmingly backed Ruto. Now Gachagua wants to be the leader of the opposition from that very region. It's a tricky tightrope walk. His allies are singing his praises, talking about "exchange programs" and "loyalists working in fringe parties." It all sounds very organized, but in Nairobi, we know how quickly these alliances crumble. We’ve seen it all before.
