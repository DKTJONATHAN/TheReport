So, the President decided to grace us with his presence at a town hall, a grand finale to this Health Summit thing. Naturally, everyone expected him to, you know, *actually* address the mess that is our healthcare system. He wheeled out the usual Kenya Kwanza promises, a little scorecard, and then launched into the star attraction: the Social Health Authority (SHA), which he declared is "working" and has "delivered." Right. Because intern doctors, clinical officers, and nurses all striking for months over delayed salaries and unfulfilled agreements are just figments of our collective imagination, aren't they?

Apparently, two years on, promises are just… well, promises. While Ruto was busy patting himself on the back about a health system for "every Kenyan," doctors were out in the cold, fighting for the basics. And it's not just them. We’re still hearing about drug shortages and vaccine stock-outs, even though Kemsa suddenly claims a 91% fill rate. Funny how these numbers always seem to magically improve when the President is about to speak, isn't it? It’s like watching a magician pull a rabbit out of a hat, except the rabbit is usually a politician's empty promise.

Then there's the whole "Community Health Promoter" saga. Changed their name, changed their terms, and now, by August 24th, they'll all be "fully covered" under SHA. And the nappies for new mothers? Plans are "underway." Just like the plans to fix the roads, or the plans to create jobs, or the plans to stop the bleeding economy. He also bragged about quadrupling the payout for normal deliveries, conveniently forgetting that the NHIF had already increased payouts substantially years ago. It’s a classic case of taking credit for what was already there, or what was already promised. We saw something similar with the red carpets and ruined kiosks; grand pronouncements that don't quite match the grim reality on the ground. [Red Carpets And Ruined Kiosks: The Great Diplomatic Masquerade](/posts/red-carpets-and-ruined-kiosks--the-great-diplomatic-masquerade).

And the digital health system? Apparently, it didn't cost Sh104.8 billion, it's on a "lease-style contract" and we only pay "five per cent." This five per cent, mind you, is supposedly less than NHIF's 17% management cost. But then the DHA chief comes along and says SHA has only paid Sh500,000 million for it, and every active SHA member pays Sh25 a year for the tech. Confused yet? You should be. It's a masterful dance of numbers designed to confuse, distract, and ultimately, impress the uninformed.

The town hall itself was a masterclass in controlled chaos. Instead of genuine questions from citizens, the President seemed to be asking himself questions and then answering them through his officials. A social media user even called it a "Churchill Show live audience." And Geoffrey Mosiria from Nairobi County had the guts to admit what many are thinking: SHA might be working for civil servants, but for the common mwananchi, especially when referred up the healthcare ladder, it’s back to out-of-pocket payments. The "system that works" seems to be working best for those already in the system, not those desperately trying to get into it.

So, here we are. Promises made, promises claimed to be delivered, and a whole lot of people still waiting for decent healthcare. The President assures us he'll be ready for "the exam next year" with a 100% record. Let’s hope the exam isn't marked by actual patients and their experiences, because right now, that scorecard looks pretty grim.
