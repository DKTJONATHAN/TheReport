Ruto, ODM Cement 2027 Alliance: "Win Or Else," Says President

This is not about Kenya's future, it's about their survival. Ruto has basically admitted that the UDA-ODM marriage of convenience is purely about clinging to power. Forget the lofty talk of development and a bright future, this is a desperate scramble to avoid the alternative - a concept the President finds so terrifying he "shudders."

The Naivasha "retreat" was nothing more than a glorified pep rally for the already converted, a lavish display of political maneuvering where 250 MPs, 46 senators, and a gaggle of governors were herded together to endorse a foregone conclusion. Ruto is essentially telling us that UDA and ODM are the only two game in town, and if you're not with them, you're with the "dangerous" opposition, which apparently consists of people who "fumble and mumble." It’s all about ensuring their gravy train keeps rolling, with ODM getting a slightly better seat this time around, unlike that handshake with Uhuru where they got promises and zero tangible gain.

This alliance, born out of a mutual fear of being out of power, is now formalizing itself to contest the 2027 elections. ODM has predictably bowed out of the presidential race, agreeing to back Ruto for another term. It’s a classic political sidestep, trading their presidential aspirations for Cabinet positions and a guaranteed role in whatever the next government looks like. Oburu Oginga, bless his heart, is trying to sell this as a patriotic duty to "stabilize the country," but let's be real, this is about securing their positions and making sure they don't end up on the outside looking in, perhaps even questioning the very foundations of our economic systems, much like the discourse around [The Empire of Debt: Dismantling the Global Ponzi Scheme](/posts/the-empire-of-debt--dismantling-the-global-ponzi-scheme).

The "common manifesto" and coalition framework agreement are just window dressing. The real agenda is power consolidation. They're building a united front, not out of shared ideals, but out of a shared fear of losing. This is the Kenyan political playbook: find an enemy, unite against them, and then divide the spoils. The fact that they're so open about the "must win" mentality is almost refreshing in its cynicism.

Oburu's challenge to "fence-sitters" is a thinly veiled threat. Anyone not openly endorsing this UDA-ODM pact is essentially marked. This isn't about democratic ideals; it's about purging dissent and ensuring a unified front, however manufactured it may be. The grand coalition meeting of Kibaki and Raila is invoked as a benchmark, but this feels more like a desperate attempt to legitimize a politically expedient union.

The ultimate goal here is clear: to ensure William Ruto secures a second term, with ODM conveniently by his side. The "development agenda" and "country's future" are just the sugar-coating on a bitter pill of political expediency and self-preservation. Kenya's future, according to this pact, hinges on the continued reign of the current administration.
