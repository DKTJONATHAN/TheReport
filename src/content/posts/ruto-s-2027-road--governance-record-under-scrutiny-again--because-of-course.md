So, the eggheads at the Institute for Economics & Peace have dropped their Positive Peace Report 2026, and guess what? Kenya's supposedly gotten a bit "better" at being peaceful. We've shuffled up three spots to 108th out of 163 countries. A grand achievement, really. Lower scores are good, they say. So, our score dropped from 3.494 to 3.414. Amazing. It’s like saying you’re healthier because your doctor gave you a lower number on a scale that measures how much you’re dying.

But let’s not get too excited, shall we? This "improvement" is like putting a fresh coat of paint on a crumbling building. Beneath the shiny numbers, the same old rot persists. The report itself admits governance and corruption are "significant challenges." Shocker. We scored a whopping 3.921 for "Low Levels of Corruption," which, on their weird inverse scale, means we're actually doing quite poorly on that front. It's no surprise when you consider the EACC survey that found a pathetic 14.6% of Kenyans believe the government is serious about fighting corruption. Most people just pay the bribes, like they’re buying bread at the market.

As we inch closer to this whole 2027 election circus, the question isn't *if* there will be an election, but *what kind* of mess we'll be voting in. The IEP report highlights that countries like ours, stuck in the "medium" peace category, are heavily influenced by "Well-Functioning Government" and "Acceptance of the Rights of Others." Sounds familiar, right? It’s the same old song and dance. We're still stuck dealing with middlemen starving in the cold, just like we talked about before. The system hasn't magically fixed itself.

The numbers are clear, even if the government wants to pretend otherwise. While the overall "peace" score nudged up, the individual pillars show a gaping chasm. Corruption remains a beast, and our faith in the institutions meant to curb it is practically non-existent. Bribery is up, and the amount people are paying to get basic services is climbing faster than our national debt. So, as the IEBC sets its fancy electoral timetable, remember that you're not just voting for a leader; you're voting within a system that’s still fundamentally broken.

This report isn't a verdict on Ruto, or any president for that matter. It's a mirror reflecting the state of our institutions. And right now, that mirror shows a lot of smudges, especially when it comes to how things actually work on the ground. The real question for 2027 isn't just who has the best campaign slogan, but what kind of dysfunctional machinery will we be asked to trust our future to? Because if history is any indication, it’ll be the same old story, just with new faces promising the moon.
