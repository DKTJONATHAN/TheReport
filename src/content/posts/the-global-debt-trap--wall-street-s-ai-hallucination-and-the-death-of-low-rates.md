The much-lauded normalization of bond yields is less of a return to health and more of a violent shock to a global system addicted to cheap credit. For nearly a decade, the world operated under the delusion that money was free. Now, as ten-year Treasury yields march toward 4.8 percent, the cushion that bonds once provided has turned into a bed of nails. This is not just a domestic issue for the United States. Global fiscal imbalances are intensifying as Japan considers dumping Treasuries to protect its own currency, and emerging markets find themselves crushed under the weight of a dollar-denominated debt trap that refuses to snap shut.

The artificial intelligence narrative has shifted from a technological revolution to a circular firing squad of capital expenditure. We are witnessing a cycle where vendors and spenders pass the same pool of speculative capital back and forth, upping their guidance to justify valuations that have no basis in current earnings power. The risk of over-earning is not just a possibility; it is a mathematical certainty. When companies like Nvidia outpace their peers by thirty-five percentage points in a matter of months, we are no longer looking at a market. We are looking at a localized mania that is sucking the oxygen out of every other sector.

Nowhere is the rot more evident than in the proliferation of exotic exchange-traded funds. The financial industry is now engineering products like the Defiance Daily Target 2x Long OKLO ETF, which has successfully incinerated nearly ninety-six percent of investor capital on an annualized basis. These baroque derivatives-of-derivatives, focusing on pre-IPO swaps and perpetual futures, are the final symptoms of a market that has run out of legitimate ways to create value. They are the financial equivalent of scrap-mining a graveyard.

History suggests that the current resilience is a trap. Just as the post-September 11 rally only served to prolong the eventual tech-bubble reckoning, today’s steadfastness is likely forestalling a much deeper correction. The locomotive of the AI bull market is already showing signs of leaking steam, with memory stocks cracking and industrial backlogs looking increasingly fragile. When the institutional sponsors finally decide to exit, the retail traders who stayed on the sidelines will be the only ones left to realize that the safety net has been replaced by a void.
