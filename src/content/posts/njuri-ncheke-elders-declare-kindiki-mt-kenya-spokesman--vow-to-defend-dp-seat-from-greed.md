Njuri Ncheke Elders Declare Kindiki Mt Kenya Spokesman, Vow to Defend DP Seat Against Political Hounds

So, the esteemed Njuri Ncheke elders are stepping up to "defend" Deputy President Kithure Kindiki’s running mate slot for 2027. Apparently, their son is too precious to be touched by the grubby hands of other "aspiring politicians." It’s a real "charm offensive," they say, targeting communities, particularly in Mt Kenya, to rally support. Because who needs genuine political maneuvering when you have elders in traditional regalia declaring you the spokesperson? It’s the kind of desperate move that screams "we’re losing ground, and someone needs to plaster over the cracks."

This whole spectacle is clearly Kindiki's last gasp to cobble together enough votes to even *dream* of staying in the second-in-command's seat. With the opposition sniffing around Mt Kenya like hyenas around a carcass, President Ruto is evidently looking elsewhere for his ticket to a second term. And these elders? They’re trying to shore up their "son's" position by, of all things, opposing the division of Mt Kenya. They need the *entire* mountain, you see, to fend off the political suitors. It’s all about power, not about what’s best for the people. This reminds me of how these same kinds of power plays often leave the ordinary citizens feeling exploited, much like the sick business of forced begging.

The Njuri Ncheke elders, having adorned Prof Kindiki in some serious traditional bling, have officially crowned him "the Mt Kenya spokesperson." Apparently, he's the *only one* authorized to speak for "our people." And these elders? They’re going to lead "talks" with other communities. Because that’s how politics works now, right? Through ancestral pronouncements and promises of traditional attire. They even declared the Deputy President's seat "not vacant" and certainly not up for any "political negotiations." Governor Mutuma chimes in, bless his heart, saying any discussion about the DP seat can only happen in 2032, *after* Ruto’s term is up. As if anyone listens to these pronouncements when there are bigger fish to fry.

The elders, in their infinite wisdom, have vowed to "fight tooth and nail" to keep the DP seat. They're showering President Ruto with praise for giving "our son" the seat, calling it the "greatest honor." It's a classic quid pro quo, dressed up in tribal loyalty. And then they pivot to advising the Ameru and the wider Mt Kenya region to "overwhelmingly support President Ruto." Because unity, peace, and prosperity only come when you blindly follow the elders' directives and vote for the ruling party. They're even linking arms with other elder councils - because a united front of traditional leaders is just what this country needs to navigate its complex political landscape.

Kindiki, basking in the glow of his newfound title, now feels "more empowered" to traverse the region and "consolidate support for President William Ruto." He’s all about unity, he says, and no "divisive politics" will happen under his watch. He even extends an olive branch to his rivals, offering them "senior positions in government after the elections." And then there's the jab at Rigathi Gachagua, asking him to avoid "confrontational politics" and promising civility if Gachagua keeps his word. It’s all very theatrical, isn’t it? A DP trying to look statesmanlike while his elders are out there playing political gladiators.

This whole charade is culminating in an audience with President Ruto himself. The elders, having secured their "son's" position for now, are now looking to cement it further by meeting the big man. The aim is to "avert a change of guard" in the deputy president's office. It’s a display of power, a show of force, and a stark reminder of how ethnicity and traditional influence continue to be wielded in Kenyan politics, often at the expense of genuine public discourse.
