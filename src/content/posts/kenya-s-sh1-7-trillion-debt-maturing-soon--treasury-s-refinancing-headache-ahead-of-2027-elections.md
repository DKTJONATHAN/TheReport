Nairobi's Debt Nightmare: Sh1.7 Trillion Due Before Next June, Treasury Panics

Kenya's public debt is a runaway train, and the brakes are clearly off. Sh1.7 trillion of it is coming due before June next year, and the National Treasury is scrambling like a matatu driver on a Friday evening trying to make rent. That's a massive chunk of Sh13.2 trillion in total debt, with over 12.7 percent needing to be paid off pronto.

This means our beloved government will be begging for hundreds of billions from foreign lenders - yes, the same ones who funded projects like the SGR that we're still paying for. Think China Exim Bank, the World Bank, the IMF, and others. The problem? A big chunk of this debt is short-term, meaning it's a constant headache with interest rates that keep climbing. It’s like borrowing small amounts from your mama mboga every week, but on a national scale.

They talk about "roll overs" and issuing new Treasury securities. This is just fancy talk for borrowing more money to pay off old debts, usually at a much higher interest rate. Our interest payments alone are already among the highest in Africa, eating up nearly half of government revenue. This leaves us with peanuts for roads, power plants, and water - the things that actually make life bearable. It's a vicious cycle, and we're the ones stuck in the middle.

The Treasury's own debt management strategy is a joke. They aimed to spread out maturities and rely less on short-term bills, but guess what? Treasury bills, the super short-term debt, now make up 7.3 percent of the total debt, more than double their target. It’s like they wrote a plan to eat healthy, then went and downed a whole tray of mandazi instead. The report even admits to "increased refinancing risks." Big surprise.

And it’s not just the external lenders. Local investors - banks, pension funds, and the rich - are expecting a massive Sh873.25 billion in interest and Sh208.61 billion in principal repayments this year. The Treasury's overall budget for debt servicing is a staggering Sh2.32 trillion. For context, that's more than the entire country earns in taxes some years. This whole mess is making it harder to even think about things like the 2027 General Elections, especially when you consider the performance of leaders.

They claim they're taking steps, like switching bonds and buying them back. But how much? The report is vague, as usual. It’s like telling someone you’ll fix their leaky roof without saying if you'll use a tiny patch or just a bucket. They managed to push the average maturity of our debt up a bit by raising some money through a Eurobond, but let's be real, this is just rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic.

The success of any strategy hinges on whether they can actually secure affordable financing without making our debt servicing payments even more unbearable.

Ultimately, this is just another chapter in Kenya's ongoing debt drama. We keep borrowing, we keep paying sky-high interest, and we're left with fewer resources for development. The Sh1.7 trillion due is a stark reminder that the bill is always coming due, and it’s the common Kenyan who will feel the pinch the most.
