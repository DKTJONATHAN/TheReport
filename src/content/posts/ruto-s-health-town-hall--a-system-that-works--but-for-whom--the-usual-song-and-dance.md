So, President Ruto decides to grace us with his presence at a town hall about healthcare. First day of a summit, the big man himself, ready to answer questions. Of course, they laid out all the Kenya Kwanza promises, a little scorecard, and then invited "candid discussion." Candid discussion, my foot. It was more like a coronation. The big noise was about ditching the old NHIF for this new Social Health Authority (SHA). Ruto claims it’s delivering, it’s working. Funny, because the intern doctors have been on strike for 56 days over delayed letters and a CBA that’s collecting dust. And they aren't the only ones. Clinical officers, universal health workers - all on strike. While he was up there in his fancy ballroom, the nurses, lab guys, and public health officers were still home, their grievances apparently not fancy enough for the spotlight.

He’s going on about promises kept, like those community health promoters (CHPs). They were volunteers, now they’re paid, sort of. A stipend, national and county government chipping in. And by August 24th, all CHPs will be under SHA. Great. But what about the nappies for mothers after childbirth, a promise from the UDA manifesto? Plans are underway, he says. The Linda Jamii program is now focusing on the whole household, not just the mother and baby. Apparently, it’s a step up from Linda Mama. A study from 2023 said Linda Mama increased skilled deliveries and dropped under-five mortality. But then the Council of Governors drops a report showing skilled deliveries are down 23% and fourth ANC visits are down 19%. So, is Linda Jamii an improvement, or just a new coat of paint on a crumbling wall? It makes you wonder if these promises are ever truly fulfilled, like the ones that seemed to vanish after [The Oratory Trap: Why Sifuna’s Stories Won’t Fill Your Plate](/posts/the-oratory-trap--why-sifuna-s-stories-won-t-fill-your-plate).

Then there's the Kemsa drug shortage saga. The President confirmed Kemsa’s claim that their fill rate has shot up to 91%. Nine out of ten requests fulfilled. Sounds good on paper, doesn't it? But you ask anyone who's actually tried to get medicine at a public facility, and the story's usually different. He also boasted that SHA has quadrupled the payout for normal deliveries compared to the Sh2,500 during the Linda Mama era. But my fact-check shows NHIF was already paying Sh6,000 to Sh10,000 back in 2015. SHA is now paying the same for C-sections as they were, Sh30,000. So much for quadrupling.

And the digital health system. The President got all riled up about headlines claiming it cost Sh104.8 billion. Apparently, it's not bought outright, it's on a lease-style contract, and Kenyans only pay five percent. He even threw shade at NHIF's 17% management cost in 2022. But then the DHA chief says only Sh500,000 million has been paid for the digital system, and every SHA member pays Sh25 per year for it. The Health Cabinet Secretary admitted the Sh104 billion isn't paid by the government, but by Kenyans through the Digital Health Agency, a contract signed in September 2024. It’s a whole lot of numbers and explanations designed to confuse more than clarify.

The town hall itself was a bit of a circus. People were invited to ask questions, but it seems the President did most of the asking, directing them to his own officials and CEOs of private facilities. One attendee complained it was like a Churchill Show audience, with only one person getting a chance to speak. Even the Chief Officer for Citizen Engagement in Nairobi admitted that while SHA is working for civil servants and at lower levels, the common citizen is still asked to pay out of pocket when referred to higher-level facilities. So, a system that works, eh? Works for some, apparently. The rest of us just get promises and a show.

The President assures us he'll discharge all commitments by the end of his term, aiming for 100% next year. He's ready for the exam. We’ll see if the patients are ready for the results. Judging by the current situation, where healthcare workers are striking and the promised benefits seem to be more spin than substance, it feels like we’re just getting the same old story with a new government.
