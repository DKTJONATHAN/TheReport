So, Edwin Sifuna, the Nairobi Senator and Linda Mwananchi leader, has decided to throw a verbal grenade back at President Ruto. Apparently, our Head of State thinks the opposition is just a bunch of slogan-shouting amateurs with no real agenda. Sifuna, ever the realist, points out the bleeding obvious: Ruto himself rode into power on the back of the "Hustler versus Dynasty" narrative. It's the classic case of the pot calling the kettle a dirty color, isn't it? When did political buzzwords suddenly become the enemy, especially when they were the very tools used to climb to the top?

Sifuna’s retort is as sharp as a newly-minted shilling: "When did the bar rise, and by who?" He’s right. Ruto didn’t win with a detailed economic white paper back then, he won with a story that resonated. Now, Sifuna vows to do the same, brandishing slogans like "Ruto Must Go" and the rather cryptic "Wantam" for the 2027 General Election. According to him, Kenyans are smart enough to see past the noise. It’s funny, because last time I checked, some of our political "leaders" seem to think voters are easily swayed by the same old recycled tunes, just like those old songs from the days when [Riggy G's New Gospel: Same Wolves, Different Sheepskin](/posts/riggy-g-s-new-gospel--same-wolves--different-sheepskin) was all the rage.

The Senator is convinced that the "agenda" of the Linda Mwananchi movement and the United Opposition is crystal clear to the wananchi. The problem, he implies, is with Ruto himself, who apparently can’t grasp their grand plan, hence the accusations of lacking direction. It’s a convenient deflection, of course. While Ruto boasts about his impending re-election, citing his opponents’ lack of ideas as his trump card, Sifuna is busy sharpening his slogan arsenal.

But let’s not get too carried away with the slogans. Even Jubilee’s own Jeremiah Kioni is raining on Sifuna's parade, cautioning that mere chants won't win elections. He’s suggesting that the opposition needs more than just catchy phrases; they need a proper roadmap, a manifesto that shows they actually have a plan for governing this chaotic country. Kioni’s point about "Wantam" being countered by "Tutam" is actually quite astute. In this political circus, a vague slogan is just an invitation for your opponent to throw an equally vague, but perhaps more appealing, counter-slogan. It's all a bit of theater, really.
