MOUNT KENYA UNIVERSITY UNVEILS YET MORE PHARMACY COURSES, BECAUSE WHY NOT

WARNING: This is just more university fluff designed to sound important while doing… well, who knows what. Don't get your hopes up for miracles.

MKU's grand plan to churn out more pharmacists with fancy new postgraduate degrees in Cosmetic Technology, Industrial Pharmacy, and Pharmacognosy is apparently "at an advanced stage." The Vice-Chancellor, Prof Deogratius JAGANYI, is spouting the usual nonsense about contributing to Kenya's industrialisation drive and preparing graduates for "modern healthcare realities." Honestly, it sounds like they're just adding more syllables to the job titles.

He drones on about strengthening training in every buzzword imaginable - pharmaceutical technology, innovation, quality assurance, marketing. Students are apparently "exposed" through coursework and visits. You know, the usual song and dance. Meanwhile, the rest of us are still grappling with basic healthcare access. But sure, let's add "Cosmetic Technology" to the mix. Because what Kenya really needs right now is more people making fancy creams, instead of, say, accessible medication. It's almost as if they're trying to distract us from bigger issues, a tactic we've seen before when [Cookie-Cutter Scandals: Why Your Privacy Settings Won't Save Your Favorite Flop](/posts/cookie-cutter-scandals--why-your-privacy-settings-won-t-save-your-favorite-flop).

Prof JAGANYI insists pharmacists are "key players" beyond just dispensing, which is a nice way of saying their jobs are expanding while salaries probably aren't. They're into disease prevention, health promotion, and "public health interventions." Sounds great on paper, but let’s be real, the system’s still the system. They're also planning a Doctor of Pharmacy (PharmD) training in two years, to "further entrench our training towards a patient-focused approach." Let's hope this isn't another case of putting lipstick on a pig, because the good old PPB's Good Pharmacy Practice Standards seem to be a bit of a moving target these days.

MKU boasts about being the "first private university in Kenya to offer pharmacy programmes," which is a historical footnote at best. Now they're accredited and regulated, and supposedly building a "strong reputation." Students get "modern training facilities" and "experienced faculty." They're trained to be caregivers, communicators, decision-makers - basically, super-pharmacists ready to tackle any healthcare challenge. It's a lot of emphasis on being adaptable and innovative, which sounds suspiciously like being prepared to do more with less.

So, there you have it. Mount Kenya University is adding more courses. They’re preparing graduates to serve Kenya, Africa, and the global community. They’ll be equipped with scientific knowledge, clinical skills, leadership, and a passion for community service. Will these graduates actually fix the broken healthcare system? Don't hold your breath. This is just another university looking to boost its numbers and its image, while the rest of us continue to navigate the realities of a healthcare landscape that’s often more about paperwork and promises than actual care.
