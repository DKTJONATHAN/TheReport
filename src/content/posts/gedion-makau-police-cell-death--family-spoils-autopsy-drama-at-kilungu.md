So, another one bites the dust in a Kenyan police cell. This time it's Gedion Makau, 44, who the pathologists are now saying choked himself to death, or rather, was asphyxiated. The family, bless their hearts, are calling it a load of rubbish. They found him with his trousers around his neck, which, frankly, sounds like a pretty clear indicator of something going wrong, but *who* did the tightening is the million-dollar question they’re asking. And why were they denied a look at the "scene of suicide"? Classic.

This whole song and dance reminds me of when the country was reeling from the loss of Baba; [BABA IS GONE AND THE VULTURES ARE HAVING A FIELD DAY](/posts/baba-is-gone-and-the-vultures-are-having-a-field-day) and everyone was scrambling for answers, or worse, for their own gain. Here, the family wants answers, but the authorities are more interested in telling them what they want to hear. Refused access to the scene? Denied a clear explanation? It’s the same old script, different poor soul. The wife, Pauline, has lost faith. Can't blame her.

The official story is that Makau, arrested for carrying a machete, decided to end it all in his cell using his own trousers. But his mother isn't buying it. She's questioning how the autopsy can pinpoint the cause of compression without revealing the perpetrator. It’s a valid point, isn’t it? Unless the trousers have fingerprints of the deceased magically appearing on them, this is where the "official" narrative starts to look shakier than a poorly built kiosk in a storm.

Now, the IPOA is supposedly on the case. Let’s see how much actual oversight they provide. Meanwhile, the Inspector General is issuing directives about interdicting officers when a detainee dies. About time, maybe? Or is it just another public relations exercise to placate the masses while the real corruption and brutality continue unchecked? You can bet your last shilling that nothing will truly change until the very foundation of this corrupt system is shaken, much like [Siasa Ni Vurugu: Why Kenyan Politics Is A Death Trap For Disabled Women](/posts/siasa-ni-vurugu--why-kenyan-politics-is-a-death-trap-for-disabled-women).

The family is digging their heels in, refusing to bury Makau until they get to the bottom of this. Good for them. They want the officers at Kilungu Police Station out, and who can blame them? This isn't just about Gedion Makau anymore; it's about every other Kenyan who might find themselves in a similar situation, with no one to speak for them when the system decides their life is disposable.

The Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) has taken up the matter and said it would investigate the events surrounding the death. The family is calling for the officers attached to the station to be removed from duty while investigations continue and has indicated that it will not release Makau’s remains for burial until the circumstances of his death are addressed. Amid renewed scrutiny of deaths in police custody or shortly after release, Inspector General Douglas Kanja on Wednesday directed the immediate interdiction of the Occurrence Book recorder, cell sentry, and duty officer whenever a detainee dies in custody, pending investigations.
