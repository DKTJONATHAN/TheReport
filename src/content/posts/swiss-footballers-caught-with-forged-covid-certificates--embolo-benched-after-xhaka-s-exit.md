So, the Swiss national team is having a bit of a moment, isn't it? First their captain, Granit Xhaka, decides to bail on international duty over these dodgy COVID certificates. Now Breel Embolo, another big name, is following suit. Apparently, these chaps thought they could cheat the system with forged documents back in 2021. Honestly, you’d think by now people would learn. It’s not exactly a masterclass in deception, is it? This whole saga just screams of the same old incompetence we see everywhere. It’s almost as if playing football at that level doesn't automatically grant you a brain.

The federation’s official statement about Embolo’s departure is pure PR fluff - “to allow the team to remain fully focused.” Focused on what? Not getting caught next time? This leaves their attacking options looking as bare as a politician’s promise, especially with away games against Scotland and North Macedonia looming. It’s a blow, sure, but is anyone really surprised? This is the same continent that gave us some of the most baffling refereeing decisions at the World Cup. Remember Embolo's red card against Argentina? The officials later admitted they messed up. So, while they're busy dropping players for faking paperwork, they can't even get the game itself right. It’s all rather predictable, isn't it?

And it's not just Embolo. Xhaka, the guy with more caps than common sense apparently, is knee-deep in this too. He’s admitting to getting a forged document in 2022. You know, the year after the world supposedly got back to normal. He’s stepping away to “shield his teammates.” Right. More like shielding himself from the inevitable headlines. It’s a familiar script, much like the stories we've seen right here in Nairobi about powerful figures trying to dodge consequences.

Even before this certificate drama, Embolo was already out of sorts. He was suspended for a red card that was later deemed a mistake by the very people who make the rules. It’s a chaotic circus. Yet, somehow, through all this, Embolo still manages to score. He was even playing at the World Cup despite visa issues, and scored against Qatar and Algeria. It just goes to show, even with all the drama and questionable integrity, these guys can still produce when they feel like it. But let's not pretend this is about talent; it's about who's willing to play by the rules, or more importantly, who's willing to bend them without getting caught. And lately, the Swiss aren't doing a great job at the latter.
