Kenya Seeks EU Lifeline to Exit Financial Crime Shame

Nairobi's finest, President Ruto, is out here begging the European Union for a bailout, not from debt this time, but from being on two embarrassing watchlists. Apparently, we’ve only sorted out 13 out of 21 "deficiencies" in our anti-money laundering systems. So, while we’re busy patting ourselves on the back for fixing a bit more than half the problem, the EU and FATF are still looking at us like we’re the new kids on the criminal block. Great.

They say we've made progress, but the fact remains we're still on the FATF grey list, a fancy term for "under increased monitoring." This means we’re not exactly the picture of financial integrity. The big issues? Breaches of transparency for companies - surprise, surprise - and not enough money laundering investigations and prosecutions to match the actual risks. It's like admitting you’ve stopped stealing only half the biscuits from the tin. And to top it off, the EU’s got its own high-risk list for us, effective August 2025, which means more hassle and more scrutiny for anyone dealing with Kenyan entities.

Ruto’s timing isn't exactly subtle, trying to sweet-talk the EU with 50 years of diplomatic relations and how they’re our biggest export market. He’s talking about partnerships covering everything from trade to security, even throwing in a request for reforms to the UN Security Council because, apparently, Africa is under-represented. Meanwhile, he’s also celebrating EU cash for regional security missions in Somalia and for our own KDF, which is nice, but doesn’t exactly erase the money laundering stink. It’s like offering to fix the leaky roof while the house is on fire.

And let’s not forget the upcoming August 2027 General Election. Ruto’s welcoming EU observers, but only if it’s done according to our "laws and procedures." Translation: they can watch, but don’t expect them to step on any toes, and certainly don’t expect any real accountability if things go sideways. It’s all a bit of theatre, isn't it? We make a fuss about transparency, then hope our neighbours - and the global financial powers that be - will just forget about our little indiscretions, much like how some politicians seem to operate with impunity, as seen with [TikTok Algorithm Kenya: How 'Ruto' Keywords and Controversy Dominate Feeds](/posts/tiktok-algorithm-kenya-ruto-keywords-controversy-data).

So, here we are, practically grovelling for approval from Europe. We want to get off these lists, not because we suddenly love honesty, but because it’s bad for business and even worse for appearances. The question is, can we actually clean up our act, or are we just good at talking the talk while the real issues fester beneath the surface? Given our track record, I wouldn't hold my breath for a magic wand.
