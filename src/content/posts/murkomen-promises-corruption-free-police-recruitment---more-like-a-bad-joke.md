Murkomen Promises Corruption Free Police Recruitment - More Like a Bad Joke

A brand new police recruitment exercise is coming, and the man in charge, Kipchumba Murkomen, is falling over himself to promise it’ll be a clean, fair, and transparent affair. December 10th, mark your calendars. He’s even inviting “independent observers” to watch the show. Right. Because when has anything in this country ever truly been free of the usual Nairobi handshake deals?

Apparently, the National Police Service needs fresh blood, and they’re looking for Kenyan citizens between 18 and 28, with a D+ in KCSE and a D+ in either English or Kiswahili. Oh, and don’t forget the height requirements - five feet eight for the men, five feet three for the women. Because we all know the most important qualification for chasing down criminals is being tall enough to see over the crowd. They’re also telling everyone not to pay bribes, warning that such acts are criminal. As if people haven’t been paying bribes for decades to get these jobs. We’ve seen similar promises before, haven’t we? Remember how the energy sector was supposed to be cleaned up? [Kenya Energy Execs Step Down Amid Fuel Manipulation Probe](/posts/kenya-energy-execs-step-down-amid-fuel-manipulation-probe). This is just more of the same song and dance.

Murkomen, however, wasn't just there to talk about future police recruits. He was busy flapping his gums in Baringo County about strengthening security and tackling banditry in the Kerio Valley. He vowed to increase operations “sixfold” to liberate the region. Sixfold. That’s a nice round number. Let’s see if that translates to anything more than hot air and more government vehicles stuck in the mud.

He’s directing officers to use “intelligence-led approaches” and target “identified criminals.” Sounds good on paper, but we all know how these operations usually go. Innocent people get caught in the crossfire, and the real big fish swim away, probably with a little nudge from someone who “paid to get in.” The CS assures us the government won’t relent, and operations will be intensified. We’ll believe it when we see it, which, knowing Nairobi, will be never.

The whole police recruitment spiel is just a PR exercise. They’ll go through the motions, maybe even catch a few low-level bribe-takers to show they’re serious, but the system itself is rotten. The real question is, who is actually going to be watching these observers? And who’s going to be watching them? It’s a hall of mirrors, and the truth gets lost somewhere in the reflection. So, while Murkomen makes his pronouncements in Tiaty, Baringo, about crackdowns and clean recruitment, the rest of us in Nairobi will just be waiting for the next inevitable scandal. Because that’s how this country rolls. Promises are cheap, and execution is even cheaper.
