Remembering Baba: Raila Mausoleum Handover Next Wednesday - More Pomp Than Substance?

So, they're finally finishing the mausoleum for Raila Odinga, the man who's been "president in waiting" for longer than some of us have been paying taxes. Ninety-eight percent complete, they say. As if that last two percent is where all the actual substance lies. And who's handing it over? President Ruto. The same man who just took power from Raila's own coalition. Talk about a circus.

The whole thing is being turned into a national heritage site by the National Museums of Kenya. Because clearly, what Kenya needs right now is more monuments to politicians who, let's be honest, have had their chance and then some. The Ministry of Defence, with their fancy engineers, rushed this in three months. I guess when it comes to grand gestures for the "Baba," resources suddenly appear, unlike when we're talking about fixing potholes on Lang'ata Road.

Mama Ida will be there, along with some Deputy Chief of Staff and a PS. All smiles and military honours, no doubt. But don't you dare think about showing up, the common mwananchi. They’ll broadcast it on TV, like another state-sponsored drama, and you can watch it on giant screens at Nyamira School or some university named after Jaramogi. Because your presence, apparently, is too much of a security risk. Heaven forbid the people who were supposed to benefit from all this political maneuvering actually witness the final act.

After the official handover, the public gets their turn on October 8. A few days later, there's a family vigil, followed by a public memorial. All this hoopla, the Democracy Institute at UoN, the projects at the Kenya School of Government - it’s all just part of the script, isn't it? A way to cement a legacy, a political dynasty. It reminds me of how leaders try to look busy while the real issues, like those [IEBC voter enrolment targets being a sham](posts/2027-vote-battle-zones--iebc-voter-enrolment-targets-a-sham), get swept under the rug.

They call it "Remembering Baba." I call it another taxpayer-funded spectacle. While they're busy building tombs, the cost of living continues to climb, and promises remain just that - promises. But hey, at least we’ll have a shiny new mausoleum. That’s what really matters, right? Especially when you've got [nepo babies in handcuffs and LAX PR walks](posts/nepo-babies-in-handcuffs-and-lax-pr-walks--the-week-the-rich-forgot-how-to-act) making headlines elsewhere. Priorities.

Ultimately, this is about optics and legacy building. The military hardware and the national heritage designation are just window dressing for a political play. The real Kenya, the one struggling to make ends meet, is expected to simply watch from afar, clap politely, and maybe shed a tear if the broadcast is good enough.
