Ruto: Next Election Will Come and Go, Kenya Must Think Beyond 2027

The President preaches long-term vision while the country is busy positioning for the next political scramble.

President William Ruto says Kenya needs to look past the 2027 elections and focus on a grand, multi-generational plan. He argues that elections have historically derailed development by forcing leaders to chase votes instead of progress, a sentiment that rings hollow when one considers the current political climate. Ruto's call for a "people's project" sounds nice on paper, but we've heard these lofty pronouncements before, usually just before the next round of politicking kicks into high gear.

He wants a national conversation that factors in AI, climate change, and the 2010 Constitution. Fine. But who is truly listening when the next election is already looming large? This talk of thinking beyond political cycles feels a bit rich coming from a man whose administration has been accused of serious land grabs ahead of elections, a detail conveniently left out of this optimistic forecast. Developing a shared national vision that outlasts administrations is a noble idea, but it's difficult to take seriously when the immediate reality is a country bracing for yet another electoral battle.

Ruto wants farmers, entrepreneurs, and every Tom, Dick, and Harry to contribute ideas. He claims the government will facilitate, not control, this grand vision. We'll see. History teaches us that when it comes to national development plans, the "people's project" often ends up being the government's project, with citizen input amounting to little more than a rubber stamp. And let's not even get started on the "fight against corruption" through digitisation - a familiar song and dance that rarely leads to genuine accountability, especially when it comes to powerful interests.

The President also touched on Africa's growing economic importance, positioning Kenya to take advantage of expanding markets and arable land. He acknowledged the widening gap between the rich and the poor, and the need to support vulnerable Kenyans, micro, small and medium enterprises, and tackle inequality and unemployment. These are indeed critical issues, but they are also the same issues that have been on the agenda for decades, with little tangible progress for the average Kenyan struggling to make ends meet. It's easy to talk about progress when you're not the one feeling the squeeze.

Ultimately, Ruto insists this long-term plan is about ensuring constitutional rights become a reality for all. Access to water, healthcare, education - the basics that should already be guaranteed. He believes technology will help combat fraud and corruption, improving public service delivery. It’s a hopeful vision, but one that requires more than just talk and digital initiatives. It requires a fundamental shift in how power is wielded and how resources are distributed. Without that, these grand plans will remain just that - plans that come and go, much like the elections he so readily dismisses.
