So, Ndia MP George Kariuki has decided to grace us with his wisdom, warning the good people of Mt Kenya against "divisive politics." Apparently, leaders are busy with insults and ethnic mumbo jumbo instead of, you know, actually securing the region's interests for 2027. It’s almost as if they’ve forgotten that this whole political game is about more than just shouting at each other.

Kariuki’s finger-pointing is a spectacle worth observing, especially his jabs at DP Kithure Kindiki and CS Ruku for allegedly splitting the region. And don't forget the veiled criticism of former DP Rigathi Gachagua's style - apparently, it’s all wrong for a region trying to play nice with others. It’s a real drama, isn't it? Like watching a soap opera, but with higher stakes and less glamorous outfits.

It's always the same script, isn't it? As soon as elections are on the horizon, the ethnic drums start beating louder, and everyone scrambles to claim victimhood or superiority. These leaders, while decrying the very tactics they often employ, are essentially asking voters to ignore the stench of corruption and broken promises, and focus instead on who hates whom. It's a classic distraction, and frankly, it's exhausting. You'd think after all these years, people would see through it, but then again, [views don't pay the bills, even for Kenyan content creators](/posts/views-don-t-pay-the-bills--even-for-kenyan-content-creators), and neither does critical thinking, apparently.

The call for a "new political approach" is a nice sentiment, I suppose, but it’s usually just hot air. When the real money and power are on the table, principles tend to evaporate faster than dew in the Nairobi sun. We’ll see if Kariuki’s warning actually lands anywhere or if it’s just another politician’s attempt to sound relevant before the next round of handshakes and backroom deals.

The whole point of these pronouncements is to position oneself as the "sensible" one, the beacon of reason in a sea of madness. But let's be honest, it's a calculated move. Everyone wants a piece of the 2027 pie, and a bit of moral posturing can go a long way in securing those votes, or at least distracting from the fact that the shamba might be a bad investment in the long run.

Ultimately, the residents of Mt Kenya, like the rest of us, are left to sift through the noise. Will they fall for the same old song and dance, or will they finally demand something more substantial than empty promises and tribal chest-thumping? My cynical Nairobi realist heart says the former, but a small, foolish part of me hopes for the latter. Don't hold your breath.
