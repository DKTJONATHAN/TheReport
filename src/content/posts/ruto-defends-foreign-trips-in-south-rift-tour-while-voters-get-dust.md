So, the South Rift has officially been declared a political battleground. Shocking, I know. It’s not like this happens every election cycle. President Ruto, fresh from whatever continental escapade he was on, is now gracing Narok with his presence. Just days after his former second-in-command, Rigathi Gachagua, rolled into town, and a week after Fred Matiang’i, who probably remembers when Jubilee was a thing, also decided Narok needed his wisdom. The ODM crowd, who used to dominate here, are apparently so yesterday, having ceded ground to Jubilee and Gachagua's new outfit. It’s a regular political potluck, and we’re the main course.

Ruto, bless his heart, is out there defending his constant jaunts abroad. Apparently, these trips are boosting tourism, which is now supposedly at 2.7 million annually. Funny, I haven’t seen any of that extra cash trickling down to my *chai* budget. He’s claiming Kenya’s global status is soaring, bringing in foreign investment and tourists. Meanwhile, his critics point out the colossal bills these trips rack up, while basic infrastructure and, you know, actual governance, seem to be perpetually on hold. It’s a classic case of "look at the shiny object, ignore the hole in your pocket." Speaking of holes, he’s also talking about an electric fence for Mau Forest. Let's hope it's more effective than the promises about its conservation have been for decades.

The president’s got his talking points ready: new airports, improved infrastructure, and the promise of 20,000 new teachers. He even admitted the university funding model, a grand idea to replace HELB, had ‘failed’. We’re all shocked. What’s next, admitting that some of those billions allegedly poured into road construction actually reach their destination? He’s touting Sh8 billion for affordable housing and Sh2 billion for markets, as if these are brand-new concepts. And then there's the 10,000-acre land giveaway in Kedong, with promises of title deeds. You can bet your last shilling that the actual distribution will be as smooth as a greased Masai shield.

Meanwhile, the local leaders, the usual suspects who cling to whoever is in power, are singing Ruto’s praises, loudly declaring the South Rift will not back the opposition. Senator Aaron Cheruiyot, who probably thinks he’s reinventing the wheel, is taking potshots at Gachagua, calling his claims of community support "hallucinations." The man was impeached, apparently. Good to know our leaders are keeping track of each other's résumés. The Jubilee folks, Matiang’i and his crew, are busy calling Ruto out for his insults and threats, claiming he’s more interested in name-calling than policy. It’s all very dramatic, isn't it? You'd think we were watching a telenovela, not a country trying to figure out its future.

Gachagua, for his part, is busy marketing his Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP), claiming the Maasai and Kipsigis communities have abandoned UDA because of unfulfilled promises. He's off to the US next, no doubt to lobby for support from the diaspora who are probably just as tired of political theatre as the rest of us. He promises that Ruto and his people will rush in with projects *after* his tour, implying they've been sitting on their hands. It's a race to the bottom, folks, and they're all sprinting.

And let's not forget Dr. Fred Matiang’i and his Jubilee brigade, who are also out there telling us Ruto is failing to unite the country. Matiang’i, in particular, has some strong words about the President's rhetoric. “You cannot insult and threaten the people,” he says. You’d think this is a revelation. The man in charge, supposedly representing us, is too busy trading barbs with opponents instead of fixing the country. It’s a predictable dance: promises, accusations, defections, and the same old faces vying for the same old seats. Just another election season in Kenya, where the real winners are the ones who manage to stay disillusioned. Even with all the talk of development, the persistent problem of [Kenya Top Energy Officials Out Over G2G Fuel Scam](/posts/kenya-top-energy-officials-out-over-g2g-fuel-scam) seems to be just one symptom of a much larger, ongoing issue of accountability.
