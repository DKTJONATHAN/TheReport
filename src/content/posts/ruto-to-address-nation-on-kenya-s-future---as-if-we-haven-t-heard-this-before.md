President William Ruto is set to address the nation yet again on Thursday evening, a special broadcast from State House that's supposedly all about Kenya’s "long-term development agenda" and "future direction." Honestly, you'd think by now we'd be tired of these prime-time sermons. It's 7:00 p.m. - prime time for dinner, not for more hot air about visions that never quite materialize on the ground where real people live.

This whole spectacle comes days after some report landed on his desk, proposing a "new national development framework" to guide us beyond the legendary Vision 2030. Vision 2030, remember that? The one that was supposed to turn us into a middle-income country by... well, 2030. We're nearly there, and look around. This new framework sounds suspiciously like a fresh coat of paint on a very leaky roof. They love to talk about "ambitious national visions" and "bold goals," as if simply saying it makes it true.

State House, in its infinite wisdom, says Ruto will "challenge Kenyans to embrace" this vision. It's always us, isn't it? The citizens are the ones who need to "work collectively towards their realisation." And let’s not forget the usual spiel about "partnership between government and citizens," and how "national progress is a shared responsibility." Funny how the responsibility for failures always lands squarely on the *mwananchi*, while the government's role in creating the mess, like the countless [Riggy G’s Pharmacy Fail: When Your Chemist Is Just A Quack With A Smartphone](/posts/riggy-g-s_pharmacy_fail--when_your_chemist_is_just_a_quack_with_a_smartphone) incidents, seems to get conveniently overlooked.

Now, let's cut through the fluff. This address isn't happening in a vacuum. The country is neck-deep in debates over economic recovery - or lack thereof - governance nightmares, and the ever-present shadows of the 2027 General Election. And let's not pretend the ruling UDA party’s recent smack-down in the Ol Kalou by-election isn't a fresh wound. Suddenly, the "long-term transformation agenda" looks a lot like a convenient deflection tactic. State House insists it won't focus on "current political developments," but when has a presidential address *not* been political, especially when the ground beneath them is shifting?

So, while Kenyans are told to "watch closely for possible policy announcements," most of us will just be watching for the usual grand pronouncements that sound good on paper but do little to change the price of ugali or the state of our roads. Another Thursday evening, another speech, another promise of a better tomorrow. Don't hold your breath for today to actually get better.
