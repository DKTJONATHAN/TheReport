Bahati's Son Morgan's Rate Card Sparks Outrage: Another Cash Grab?

The latest buzz in Nairobi is Bahati's ludicrous pricing for his son Morgan's musical and endorsement services. This isn't about art; it's about a calculated business move, leveraging a child's nascent talent for profit.

So, Bahati, the former gospel singer now wading knee-deep in politics, has decided to package his son Morgan's budding music career like a commodity. We're talking Sh5 million for a music collab, Sh7 million for brand endorsements, and a cool Sh10 million for a political endorsement. Yes, you read that right. The boy's barely out of his teens and he's already a political pawn with a price tag. This is less about nurturing talent and more about cashing in before the hype dies down.

Morgan's debut song, "Zoza (Nikifika 18)," apparently blew up on YouTube, raking in millions of views and landing him on trending lists. Convenient, isn't it? Just when Bahati himself is stepping back from his own music to chase political dreams, his son is suddenly ready for the spotlight and, more importantly, the income. It’s a classic Nairobi hustle, but this time it’s packaged as a family business.

The kicker is Bahati's invitation for clients to "engage his son." This isn't about inspiring the next generation; it's about securing bookings and transforming online buzz into cold, hard cash. We've seen this playbook before, whether it's a teacher couple pulling off a Sh2.4 million land scam at Ardhi House or Eric Omondi’s baby mama navigating her own Nairobi hustle. It’s all about the money, honey.

Morgan's next move is crucial: can he escape the shadow of his famous father and build something genuine, or will he just become another expensive endorsement for hire? Given the astronomical prices and the father's political aspirations, it's hard to be optimistic. This isn't about artistry; it's about a father maximizing his son's perceived market value before the novelty wears off.
