So, another poll is out, and surprise, surprise, President Ruto is still technically "leading" the 2027 presidential race. But let's be real, in Nairobi terms, that's like saying you're leading the queue for a matatu that might never show up. He's at 45.1 percent, which sounds impressive until you remember you need over 50 percent to avoid the indignity of a second round. That's a whole 4.9 percent he’s just… *not* getting. And don't even get me started on the county numbers. It’s the same old song and dance: he’s got the most votes, but not enough to actually seal the deal without a fight.

Meanwhile, the opposition is still apparently negotiating their way to a candidate. Kalonzo Musyoka, Edwin Sifuna, Rigathi Gachagua, Fred Matiang’i - they're all in some room, probably arguing over who gets the biggest chair. This whole indecision is exactly why Ruto's numbers, though not overwhelming, look so good. They're a divided mess, and Sifuna is the only one who seems to have woken up, surging to a respectable 19.6 percent. He's now the "top opposition contender," which, in this lot, is like being the tallest dwarf.

The numbers show a definite shift. Kalonzo, who used to be the darling of the polls, has plummeted from 22 percent to a measly 10.3 percent. Sifuna, on the other hand, is on the rise, leaving the others in the dust. This whole drama reminds me of that time Dennis Lusava walked into a police post and ended up dead - things are never as they seem, and the "leading" opposition is looking more like a comedy of errors than a serious threat. And let's not forget the 18 percent of Kenyans who are still undecided. They're the real MVPs of apathy, probably waiting to see which side offers the most bribes.

The polls also paint a grim picture of party loyalty. UDA is looking stronger, but the opposition parties are all over the place. ODM has dropped, Wiper is barely hanging on, and Jubilee is just… there. It’s a fragmented mess, and it’s clear that most Kenyans are tired of the usual political tribalism. Even the government's performance is a mixed bag. Roads and housing are apparently doing well, which is nice, but corruption and the economy? Still a disaster zone.

And get this, Ruto's support is apparently waning in Mt Kenya while Western Kenya is becoming a battleground. It’s a whole lot of shifting sands. While he’s still strong in Rift Valley and northern Kenya, the fact that he's not a shoo-in is telling. This entire election cycle feels like we’re just rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic, and at the end of the day, trust in all these institutions is below 40 percent. So yeah, Ruto's leading. For now.
