Ruto's Nairobi Puppets Get TSC Seats, Parliament Plays Along

Another day, another bunch of appointments that smell like yesterday's fish. Parliament, bless their rubber-stamped hearts, have given the nod to Wilson Sossion and Antonina Lentoijoni to sit on the Teachers Service Commission. Apparently, our esteemed President, William Ruto, decided the TSC needed a little… *enrichment*. After all, why let a good shortage go to waste when you can fill it with old faces?

This whole song and dance was apparently "unanimous," thanks to the Education Committee's sage recommendation. After a bit of a kerfuffle - a High Court injunction that was promptly lifted, because our courts are always so decisive - Sossion and Lentoijoni have officially landed their seats. Sossion, bless his former KNUT robes, is back in the education leadership game, promising to protect teachers while ensuring the TSC, you know, does its job. We’ll see how that goes, won't we? It’s almost as predictable as the [gold illusion](posts/the-gold-illusion--why-the-world-s-oldest-safe-haven-is-failing-its-biggest-test) - promises made, promises expected to crumble.

And as if that wasn't enough excitement for one week, the August House also found time to approve Dancun Oburu as the IPOA Chairperson. He's stepping into Justice Ahmed Issack Hassan's rather large shoes. One can only imagine the stern pronouncements and furrowed brows that will soon grace that office. Just another Tuesday in the grand theatre of Kenyan politics.

The sheer audacity of it all. These are the same people who will tell you to trust the system, while clearly demonstrating that the system is designed to benefit those with the right connections. Sossion, who was once a nominated MP and a loud voice for teachers, now joins the very body that often finds itself at odds with the unions. It’s a masterclass in political maneuvering, or perhaps just a sign that everyone eventually finds their place in the machinery, no matter how much they claimed to be outsiders.

It all feels like a carefully orchestrated performance, where the same actors keep getting new roles. The real winners, as always, are those who are adept at playing the game. The teachers, well, they get to hope that these new appointments actually translate into something tangible, something beyond the usual political theatre. Given the history, I wouldn't hold my breath.













Parliament, in its infinite wisdom, has decided that Wilson Sossion and Antonina Lentoijoni are indeed fit to join the Teachers Service Commission (TSC). The MPs, after a recommendation from the Education Committee which had apparently done its homework - or perhaps just followed instructions - unanimously approved the duo on Tuesday. This follows President William Ruto’s nominations to fill a shortage, a shortage that seems to conveniently appear whenever it's time to place certain individuals.

The path to these esteemed positions wasn't exactly a walk in the park. There was a bit of drama, with the High Court issuing a conservatory order to halt the vetting process. But as is often the case in this city, legal hurdles tend to have a rather short lifespan when powerful interests are involved. The orders were lifted, and Sossion and Lentoijoni were cleared to face the committee. It’s a familiar script, where the law bends and flexes to accommodate the political will, much like how even the [DOGE Operatives' New Haunts](posts/doge-operatives--new-haunts--a-cynical-look-at-where-the--efficiency--experts-landed) eventually found their niche.

Sossion’s return to national leadership in the education sector is being framed as a homecoming, given his lengthy tenure as the KNUT Secretary General. He’s pledged to champion teachers’ interests while ensuring the TSC fulfills its mandate. One can only imagine the fervor and conviction with which he'll deliver these pronouncements, now from the other side of the fence. It’s a strategic move, no doubt.

Meanwhile, the August House also managed to confirm Dancun Oburu as the Chairperson of the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA). He’s set to take over from Justice Ahmed Issack Hassan. It’s a whirlwind of appointments and confirmations, all happening with a speed that suggests a carefully planned agenda is unfolding.

Ultimately, these appointments signal another round of familiar faces in positions of influence. The language used, the procedures followed, it all serves to maintain the illusion of a functional, meritocratic system. But for those of us watching from the ground, it’s a stark reminder of how Nairobi operates: a complex web of connections and political expediency, where the outcome is rarely a surprise.
