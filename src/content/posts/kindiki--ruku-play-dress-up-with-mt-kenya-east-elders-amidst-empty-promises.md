So, Kithure Kindiki and Geoffrey Ruku are off playing dress-up with the Mt Kenya East elders in Nembure, Embu County. Pictures show a whole parade of old chaps in traditional gear, singing their hearts out. And our Deputy President? He gets kitted out in some fancy local threads, like he’s about to join a cultural festival rather than engage with the actual problems Kenyans face. This is the same man who was recently crowned a Njuri Ncheke elder himself. Apparently, that makes him the new mouthpiece for the mountain, complete with a spear and a sword to symbolise… what, exactly? The ability to stab us with more debt?

The elders, bless their traditional hearts, have thrown their weight behind President Ruto’s 2027 re-election bid and, crucially, insisted he keep Kindiki as his running mate. Josphat Murangiri, the secretary-general of this esteemed council, makes it clear: anyone wanting to talk politics with the Mt Kenya region has to go through Professor Kindiki. It’s all very chummy, with talk of rallying behind Ruto and warnings against anyone trying to unseat the DP. You’d think with all this talk of unity and support, they’d be discussing how to get us out of this economic mess. But no, it’s all about political positioning and who gets to speak for the region, as if that’s going to fill empty stomachs.

Murangiri’s speech is a masterclass in political theatre. He scolds other leaders for eyeing the deputy president's seat, conveniently forgetting that the Ameru community has supported deputies from other regions before without making such a fuss. It’s a predictable power play, cloaked in tradition. They even plan to rope in elders from Isiolo, Samburu, Maasai, and Kikuyu communities to build this wider support for Ruto and Kindiki. Meanwhile, the rest of us are left trying to figure out how to pay for basic necessities, a situation eerily similar to the [Bankruptcy Of A Billion Dollar Mirage](/posts/the-bankruptcy-of-a-billion-dollar-mirage) we keep witnessing.

Kindiki, as expected, gets emotional and thanks the elders for their "confidence." He pledges to unite the Mt Kenya region and have it speak with "one voice" to strengthen its bargaining power. He even spouts about setting aside political differences for the "common good" and ensuring the region "continues benefiting from national development programmes." It sounds inspiring, but let's be real. This is the same old song and dance: elders bless politicians, politicians promise unity and development, and the average Kenyan is left holding the bag. While they're busy with ceremonial swords and spears, the real battle for economic survival continues unabated.
