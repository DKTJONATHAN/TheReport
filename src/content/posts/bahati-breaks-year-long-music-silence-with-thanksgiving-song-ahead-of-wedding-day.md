Bahati's "Gratitude" Track: Another Excuse for a "Big Day"?

Bahati, the gospel artiste who seems to think Nairobi needs constant reminders of his existence, has graced us with his presence again. After a year of silent treatment, he's dropping a "thanksgiving" song, conveniently timed just before some grand celebration he's calling "The Bahatis' Big Day." Because what better way to usher in a major event than with a soundtrack of self-congratulation, right?

The singer, who apparently discovered God's goodness only after a prolonged hiatus, took to Instagram to announce his return. He's crediting the Almighty for his progress, as if life in Nairobi is just a smooth ride for everyone. This new track, "Uinuliwe," is supposed to be a "gratitude letter" to the Lord, but let's be honest, it sounds more like a prelude to a publicity stunt. After all, who needs a new song when you have a "Big Day" to promote? It’s almost as if he’s trying to distract us from the real issues, like why firms must pay attention to employment practices risk, which seems a lot more pressing than Bahati's musical comeback.

He’s calling out his "Day 1 Gospel Fans" to tune in at 8 pm, because apparently, their devotion is as unwavering as the fuel prices. This isn't just a song; it's a testament to his faith, a reflection of his journey, and a reminder that "all I have is from Jesus." Bless his heart. One can only hope this artistic endeavor doesn't come with the same hidden costs as the JKIA expansion, where Kenya drains Sh38.7 billion from state asset sales.

The original headline boasted about his "wedding day," but now it's a vague "Big Day." Whatever it is, it's clearly an event worth breaking a year-long silence for. This whole thanksgiving song ahead of a supposed celebration feels less like genuine piety and more like a well-calculated marketing strategy. Maybe next time he’ll release a song about why Kenyans feel the fuel price 'pain' again, instead of focusing on his own blessings.

Bahati's return to gospel music is being hailed as a "notable" event. For whom, exactly? The same fans who have been waiting patiently for another dose of his spiritual tunes? Or perhaps for the people who will be attending "The Bahatis' Big Day"? This "special thanksgiving song" is undoubtedly a preview of what's to come, a carefully curated narrative designed to showcase his piety and pave the way for whatever Friday holds.
