So, you're one of those people who can't sleep without a nightlight blazing? Good for you. This new study from some clever clogs says that extra light exposure at night is apparently thickening your heart muscle. Less room for blood to do its job, apparently. Just what we needed, another way modern life is slowly killing us.

And it's not just about a tighter squeeze for your blood. This heart muscle is apparently getting less flexible. Researchers, bless their concerned hearts, are calling it an "early sign that the heart was not working normally." Translation: your body is already starting to pack it in, and you're probably too busy scrolling through [hackers hit Ruto's website, demand Bitcoin ransom from Kenya's president](/posts/hackers-hit-ruto-s-website--demand-bitcoin-ransom-from-kenya-s-president) to notice.

They figured this out using a simple thigh ultrasound. Yes, a thigh ultrasound. Apparently, they can tell how your heart is doing by poking around your leg. Nairobi is already a concrete jungle, and now we have to worry about the faint glow from our phones making our hearts chunky? What a joke.

The study even suggests this thigh ultrasound might help predict heart surgery risks. Great. So now, before they carve you open, they'll point to your thigh and say, "Ah, yes, you liked your bedside lamp too much." It's almost as if everything we do, from staying awake late to, well, existing, is a ticking time bomb for our internal organs.

So, what are we supposed to do? Live in absolute darkness like bats? If you're already struggling to get a decent night's sleep because of the noise, the heat, or the general chaos of this city, adding "total blackout" to your to-do list might just push you over the edge. This is Nairobi, not some fancy spa retreat. We're just trying to survive, not curate perfect biological conditions for our increasingly demanding bodies.

And as for the "Eastleigh Voice" publishing this in 2026, well, that just adds another layer of 'why bother?' to the whole situation. By then, we'll probably be relying on microscopic robots to patch up our hearts while our phones broadcast our every bodily malfunction in real time. This study is just another nail in the coffin of convenient, normal living.
