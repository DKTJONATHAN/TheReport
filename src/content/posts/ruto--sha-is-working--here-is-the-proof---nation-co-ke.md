Ruto Claims SHA Is Working, But Kenyans Are Still Waiting for the "Proof"

WARNING: President Ruto insists the Social Health Authority (SHA) is superior to NHIF, but critics and ordinary Kenyans remain unconvinced, questioning the actual impact of the new health scheme.

CAPTION: President William Ruto speaks at State House, Nairobi, defending the controversial Social Health Authority.





So, the big man himself, William Ruto, has come out swinging, telling us that this new Social Health Authority (SHA) is the bee's knees, far better than the old National Health Insurance Fund (NHIF). He’s throwing around figures like Sh12 billion a month from SHA versus Sh5.6 billion every three months from NHIF. It sounds impressive on paper, doesn't it? He even claims Sh170 billion has been dished out to hospitals in the last year and a half, with Nairobi scooping up Sh32 billion. But let's be real, who’s actually seeing this money trickle down to their sick relatives or their own health needs?

The President says those clamoring for a return to NHIF are “threatening the health of our people.” Bold words, Your Excellency. He suggests we ask the Community Health Promoters (CHPs) if SHA is working. These are the same folks who have been tasked with convincing us to sign up for this new scheme, often with little more than good intentions and a promised stipend. It’s a classic move: throw the frontline workers into the fray and hope they absorb the public's frustration.

Duale, the Health CS, is chiming in too, talking about international recognition and Kenya being a model for UHC. He claims other governments tried and failed, but CHPs are now enabling early detection of common ailments. And Nairobi’s Governor, Sakaja, is quoting statistics about primary healthcare saving money. All this high-level talk about “preventive healthcare” and “reducing overall healthcare costs” sounds lovely, but for the common mwananchi struggling to pay for basic medication or a doctor’s visit, it’s just more hot air. It’s starting to feel like another case of [Global Capital Feasts on the Rust Belt’s Last Breath](posts/global-capital-feasts-on-the-rust-belt-s-last-breath), where big pronouncements rarely translate into tangible relief for the people on the ground.

The President is also patting himself on the back for promising medical insurance to all 107,000 CHPs. Sh390 million from the national government, and counties matching it. A nice gesture, I suppose, recognising their "critical role." But let's not forget these are the same people who are supposed to be the "backbone" of the system, the first point of contact. They are being brought "under the scheme" themselves, almost as an afterthought, after being sent out to do the Government's bidding.

Ultimately, Ruto wants us to “judge the reforms by their impact rather than political rhetoric.” Fair enough. The impact for many so far is more confusion, more mandatory deductions, and a nagging feeling that the promises don't quite match the reality. We'll see if these billions magically appear in hospital beds and patient wallets, or if it's just another elaborate reshuffle of public funds with the same old outcomes.
