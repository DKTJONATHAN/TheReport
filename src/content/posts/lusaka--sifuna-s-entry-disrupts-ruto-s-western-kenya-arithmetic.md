Lusaka Admits Sifuna's Entrance Shakes Up Ruto's Western Kenya Grip

Lusaka claims Ruto will win 2027 despite Sifuna's "youth excitement," but the real panic is about losing the region.

Governor Ken Lusaka, a Ruto loyalist and Western Kenya re-election czar, has finally admitted what we all saw coming: Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna has indeed thrown a spanner in the works for President William Ruto’s grand plans in Western Kenya. Apparently, the region was all sweetness and light, solidly behind the "hustler" narrative, until Sifuna waltzed in and messed up their carefully calculated arithmetic. It’s always the same story, isn’t it? A bit of fresh energy, a bit of youthful enthusiasm, and suddenly the old guard is sweating like a cheap suit in a Nairobi heatwave.

Lusaka tries to brush it off as "slight disruption" and "excitement among the youth," claiming it won't last. Bless his heart. He’s saying the same thing about Sifuna that they said about every other politician who dared to challenge the established order. It's the classic "don't worry about them, they’re just a fad" defense, usually deployed when the rug is being pulled out from under your feet. He waxes lyrical about Ruto not being a "walkover" or a "political novice," as if that's supposed to make us forget the sheer tidal wave of humanity that turned up for Sifuna's homecoming in Bungoma. That's not just excitement, Governor; that’s a political earthquake.

He goes on to lament that no current leader in the region possesses the "political machinery" of the late Vice President, trying to diminish Sifuna's growing influence by comparing him to a ghost of the past. It’s a classic deflection tactic, folks. Instead of addressing the real issue - that people are genuinely responding to Sifuna - they’re busy conjuring up historical figures and talking about how things *used* to be. Meanwhile, back in the real world, where the cost of living is soaring faster than a matatu on Waiyaki Way, people are looking for alternatives. It reminds me of when leaders promise the moon, but all we get is another tax increase, just like that [NTSA’s New Price List: How Much It Costs to Breathe on Nairobi Roads](/posts/ntsa-s-new-price-list--how-much-it-costs-to-breathe-on-nairobi-roads).

Lusaka even has the audacity to tell Sifuna and the opposition to aim for 2032, suggesting 2027 is too ambitious. This is the kind of patronizing nonsense that makes you wonder if they’re actually living on the same planet as the rest of us. It’s a desperate attempt to manage expectations and buy time, while Sifuna’s allies are out there, crisscrossing all 38 constituencies, a move that sounds far more effective than any of Ruto’s re-election coordinators’ pronouncements. With seasoned figures like Musikari Kombo and Amos Wako rallying behind him, this isn't just a "youthful excitement"; it's a well-orchestrated political movement.







Bungoma Governor Ken Lusaka, who also wears the hat of President William Ruto’s Western Kenya re-election coordinator, has finally coughed up the truth: all was supposedly peachy in the region's political landscape until Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna decided to make an entrance and, as Lusaka puts it, "disrupted their arithmetic." Speaking on Sunday, September 27, 2026, the former Senate Speaker tried to downplay the buzz around Sifuna, labeling it a mere "small disruption" and fleeting "excitement among the youth" that wouldn't endure. He insisted that visits to Kakamega, Vihiga, and Busia all confirmed unwavering support for the head of state, with only Sifuna’s emergence causing a "slight disruption."

Lusaka went on to argue that Ruto's victory in 2027 is a foregone conclusion, emphasizing that the president is no pushover and certainly not a political novice ripe for the picking. He reiterated his stance that the fervor surrounding Sifuna and the youth's apparent embrace of him is nothing new, having witnessed similar "excitement" in the past. He even threw in a thinly veiled warning to the people of Western Kenya to tread carefully, suggesting that this wave of support might be illusory. He further lamented the absence of a leader in the region with the same galvanizing power as the late Vice President, who, according to Lusaka, managed to unite the region behind his ticket with Kibaki back in 2002.

This admission comes amid a palpable battle for influence in Western Kenya, a region that seems to be increasingly drawn to the Nairobi senator, particularly after his homecoming event in Bungoma, which reportedly drew massive crowds. This surge of support has clearly unnerved leaders aligned with President William Ruto from the region, who are quick to dismiss it as mere "euphoria" destined to fade. It's the same old script when established powers feel their grip loosening; they label any new energy as temporary and ultimately insignificant, much like the hollow promises that often follow [Africa Capital Week's Nairobi Talkfest: Moving From Empty Promises to Actual Cash](/posts/africa-capital-week-s-nairobi-talkfest--moving-from-empty-promises-to-actual-cash).

Adding insult to injury, Ken Lusaka has openly urged Edwin Sifuna and the opposition to set their sights on 2032, implying that 2027 is an unrealistic goal. This patronizing advice, however, seems to be falling on deaf ears. Allies of the Nairobi senator are reportedly committed to a rigorous campaign, vowing to comb through all 38 constituencies in Western Kenya to secure unwavering support for Sifuna’s ambition for the country's top seat. With veteran leaders like former Webuye MP Musikari Kombo, former Attorney General Amos Wako, and Fred Gumbo now backing Sifuna, this "slight disruption" is rapidly shaping up to be a significant political force that Lusaka and his allies can no longer afford to ignore.
