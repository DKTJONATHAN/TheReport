King Kaka's Britam Podcast: Another PR Stunt to Trick Mama Mbogas and Boda Boda Riders











So, Britam is apparently "bringing conversations about insurance, investment and financial planning closer to ordinary Kenyans" with their "Blue Mic Podcast." Hosted by musician King Kaka, who, let's be honest, needs the gig more than the average Kenyan needs another financial lecture. They’re using "everyday experiences" to explain things like insurance premiums and investment options, because apparently, we all just need a celebrity to tell us how to not get fleeced.

The "third episode" apparently aims to "challenge misconceptions" that insurance is only for the wealthy and investing needs a fat bank account. Oh, please. These are the same old lines trotted out every few years. It’s like listening to politicians promise change during election season. We’ve seen this movie before, and it usually ends with the same people losing their shirts, just like the [South Sudan Embassy in Kenya Running on Fumes, Operations Hobbled by Cash Woes](/posts/south-sudan-embassy-in-kenya-running-on-fumes--operations-hobbled-by-cash-woes) shows some organisations are already struggling.

What's really telling is who they're bringing in: "mama mbogas, security guards and boda boda riders." Great, let’s parade the most vulnerable and struggling to show how they *might* be able to afford insurance. And of course, they’ve got a "Gen Z mama mboga" to add that "authentic" touch. It’s all about making it seem relatable, isn’t it? Like a well-dressed exec explaining poverty from a comfortable armchair.

The whole point is to make financial information "more accessible." Accessible, or just another way to get your hard earned cash into their pockets? They’re talking about protecting families, businesses, and assets. Meanwhile, most of these people are worried about where the next meal is coming from. Talking about investing when you’re barely making ends meet is like discussing the finer points of a yacht when you can't afford bus fare. It's a nice fantasy, but that's all it is.

And yes, you can watch these pearls of wisdom on YouTube or listen on Spotify. Because that's what we all need, more screen time and less actual money. Let's just hope King Kaka's delivery is good enough to distract from the hollow promises. It's a classic Britam move: throw a bit of PR dust in our eyes and hope we don't notice the empty pockets afterward.

This whole initiative is supposedly part of "broader efforts to promote financial literacy." More like broader efforts to promote Britam. Don't be fooled by the shiny podcast and the celebrity host. The reality is, financial stability for ordinary Kenyans is a long, hard road, and a few podcast episodes won't magically pave it.
