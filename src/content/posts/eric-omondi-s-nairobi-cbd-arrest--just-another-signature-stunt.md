Eric Omondi, the self-proclaimed activist who was a comedian just yesterday, is back at it. His 'Punguza Mzigo' campaign, which sounds lovely in theory - who wouldn't want less burden? - saw him arrested in Nairobi's Central Business District. Shocker. He was outside the National Archives, collecting signatures for his grand plan to dismantle our over-inflated government. As if anyone expected it to go differently.

His big ideas? Slash counties from 47 to a neat eight. Get rid of nominated members, abolish the Senate, ditch Women Representatives, and trim the number of MCAs. The logic, he says, is to "cut the cost of running government" and "ease the financial burden on Kenyans." Bless his heart. As if politicians, regardless of number, won't find a new way to siphon funds. We've seen this script before; it's like watching a repeat episode of [Larry Madowo, Edith Kimani, Lulu Hassan, Rashid Abdalla: Kenyan Media's 'Couples' - For Show, Or For Real?](/posts/larry-madowo--edith-kimani--lulu-hassan--rashid-abdalla--kenyan-media-s--couples----for-show--or-for-real). All for show.

His arrest was, of course, a spectacle. Plain-clothes officers, handcuffs, the usual questions about what he'd done wrong. It's the standard playbook for any 'activist' seeking maximum media exposure. Released the next day, naturally, to continue "pushing his message." This isn't about real change; it's about staying relevant and keeping the camera pointed at him. Our problems run deeper than the number of seats in Parliament.

He needs one million signatures for the IEBC. Reports say he's already got "hundreds of thousands." Convenient, isn't it? He's declared he'll be at the Archives until Friday, drumming up support. It gives the illusion of a grassroots movement, but let's be honest, how many of those signatures translate to actual, sustainable political will, or just people signing because they're told to, or want to be part of *something*?

His antics have "reignited debate" about the cost of devolution. Oh, fantastic. Another debate. Because debates solve everything, don't they? We'll talk and talk, and then the next election cycle comes, new faces promise new things, and the mzigo remains, perhaps just repackaged. It's a cyclical nightmare, this country. Don't hold your breath for any actual relief from Omondi's theatre.
