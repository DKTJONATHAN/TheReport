Police Brass Say Goodbye to Another One of Their Own - Big Surprise, Huh?

Another high-ranking officer bites the dust. This time it's Lazarus Opicho, a Senior Assistant Inspector General, who's shuffled off this mortal coil. The Inspector General himself, Douglas Kanja, sent his condolences, praising Opicho as a "valued colleague" and "trusted friend." Apparently, he was one of the best, climbing the ranks and securing government buildings and VIPs. Shocking, I know.

So, the Inspector General's message, delivered by Deputy Inspector General Eliud Lagat, was a whole lot of flowery language about dedication, leadership, and Opicho's "amiable character." They say he had this knack for building "strong social capital and networks" across the public service. Sounds like he was good at the office politics, then. Opicho apparently also devoted himself to his family, which is nice, I suppose. But let's be real, the man was a Senior Assistant Inspector General. His job was to be at the "centre of security operations." That's a fancy way of saying he was probably knee-deep in whatever deals and drama keep this whole system running.

The Kenyan police fraternity apparently turned up in droves for the burial in Kitale. All those uniformed bodies, paying their respects. It’s a big loss, they say. A profound loss. For the service, of course. And for his wife, Irene, and the kids. It’s always the same story, isn't it? One falls, another rises, and the rest of us just keep paying the bills and hoping we don't end up on the wrong side of someone's "security operations."

The police bigwigs are all in a flap, sending their warmest regards and deepest sympathies over the passing of Senior Assistant Inspector General Lazarus Opicho. Deputy Inspector General Eliud Lagat, standing in for the Inspector General Douglas Kanja, delivered the official line at Opicho’s burial in Kitale. According to them, Opicho was a paragon of virtue, a man whose dedication to the National Police Service was as high as his rank.

Kanja’s message waxed lyrical about Opicho's "dedication and leadership," highlighting his ascent to one of the highest positions in the service. He was apparently the guy in charge of guarding government buildings and keeping VIPs safe, and also the founder of the Judiciary Police Unit. Sounds like a busy man, probably very good at making sure the right people stayed secure, while the rest of us worried about things like [Transport Operators vs. Govt: Who Will Crumble First? Fuel Prices Continue to Squeeze Kenyans](/posts/transport-operators-vs--govt--who-will-crumble-first--fuel-prices-continue-to-squeeze-kenyans).

Lagat chimed in with more praise, describing Opicho as disciplined, hardworking, and dependable. He apparently had a special talent for "building social capital and networks" across the public service, a trait that all his colleagues should apparently "emulate." So, basically, he was good at schmoozing. And on top of his professional achievements, he was also devoted to his family. How utterly… human.

The Inspector General extended condolences to Opicho's wife, Irene, and their children, promising the "Service's solidarity." Meanwhile, Lagat decided a bit of scripture from Psalm 34:18 was in order to comfort the bereaved. Because nothing cures the sting of death quite like a well-worn Bible verse. The police fraternity gathered to honour his life and service, a testament to how well-connected he must have been.
