So, President Ruto's got a new pet project: dropping seeds from the sky to meet this wildly ambitious 15 billion tree target by 2032. Apparently, throwing seeds from a helicopter is the next big thing in forest restoration. It's all about "scaling up," he says, while conveniently forgetting the billions we've already "planted" that probably didn't even sprout. Reminds me of all those grand pronouncements that disappear faster than a promised salary increment. At least the minister for environment and forestry got to pose for a photo op.

They're pulling out all the stops, using satellite imagery and AI - fancy buzzwords to mask a lack of actual, tangible results. Meanwhile, village elders are being roped in to mobilize communities. Because obviously, the people who are already struggling to put food on the table are the ones who need to worry about nurturing trees that might or might not survive. It’s the usual playbook: blame the grassroots when the lofty plans fail. And let's not forget the Sh200 seedlings for cocoa, a supposed "game-changer" that will make farmers rich. Heard that one before, haven't we? It’s like watching another episode of ‘Can’t even lead a cow’: Ruto slams opposition, urges more voters to register in northern Kenya, just with more leaves.

We're told 5.3 million trees were planted on Mazingira Day. Great. And how many of those are still standing? The numbers they throw around are always impressive on paper. They've planted "about two billion trees" since the initiative began. Two billion? Really? And the Interior PS claims we're at 14.7 percent of the goal. Yet, in Homa Bay, they planted nearly 6,000 trees against a target of 10,000. Typical. It’s always a race to meet targets, not to actually achieve anything sustainable. The only thing that seems to be growing consistently is the government's budget for these initiatives.

And then there's the whole cocoa farming push. Apparently, it’ll bring in half a million shillings per acre, compared to maize's paltry Sh35,000. Sounds like a dream, doesn't it? A quick fix to our agricultural woes. But will the farmers actually see that money, or will it just line the pockets of a few well-connected individuals? We'll probably find out when the next "innovative approach" is unveiled. It’s all about diversifying livelihoods, they say, while ignoring the systemic issues that keep most of our farmers in poverty.

Waste management is another area where we're "strengthening efforts." Plastic pollution is a "major challenge," and they're pushing for a "collaborative approach." Translation: more meetings, more committees, and very little actual cleaning up. They're talking about enhanced rainfall and possible heavy downpours in the Lake Victoria Basin, while a few weeks ago we were hearing about The American Export of Institutional Rot. It's the same old story, just with different weather patterns and a fresh coat of paint.

So, while the President flies around in choppers and launches fancy programs, let's remember the reality. Trees need consistent care, not just a one-day aerial bombardment. And farmers need more than just promises of Sh200 seedlings and subsidized crops. Until we see genuine commitment and accountability, this whole 15 billion tree target will likely remain just another pipe dream, a PR stunt for the masses.
