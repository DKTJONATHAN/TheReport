So, the big man, President Ruto, is out here telling us Kenya can't afford to wait for Vision 2030 to fizzle out before we figure out what's next. Apparently, we're already hurtling towards our next five-year development cycle without a clue where we're going. He says it's irresponsible to start a plan whose last two years won't even have a blueprint to hang onto. Frankly, it sounds like he's worried about his own legacy being left in the lurch, not about us actual Kenyans.

He's talking about this new plan needing "legal anchoring" and avoiding the "financing and implementation challenges" that plagued Vision 2030. Of course, they'll throw around words like "public participation" and "multi-stakeholder steering committee," but let's be real. This is the same old script, just with a fresh coat of paint. The opposition's calling it a "2027 campaign tool," and honestly, who are we to argue? When has a politician ever initiated a grand national plan without an eye on the next election, especially when their own administration's plans are about to run out of runway?

The President keeps insisting this is about "the country's constitutional and planning framework," that it's not just his presidential whim. He wants experts to lay out options, but says Kenyans must "ultimately determine the destination." That's rich. We're supposed to trust that this whole exercise, run by civil society, religious groups, and the private sector - all conveniently facilitated by the government - will somehow bypass the usual political shenanigans. It's like asking the fox to guard the henhouse, but with more committees.

He claims that we've done "65 to 80 per cent" of Vision 2030, but "could have done better." Yeah, no kidding. If Vision 2030 was supposed to make us a "newly industrialising, middle-income country providing a high quality of life," I'm still waiting for my share of that high quality life. The talk of mobilising capital through funds and boosting domestic savings sounds good on paper, but we’ve heard it all before. It's a nice story to tell, especially when the country's debt is already threatening to drown us.

Ruto wants us to aspire to be "one of the world's top 10 developing economies on the road towards first-world status." He cites South Korea and Singapore. It's always about comparing ourselves to others, isn't it? Instead of focusing on the tangible, on fixing the leaky infrastructure and ensuring basic services are functional, we're dreaming of being top 10. This feels less like a national vision and more like a presidential vanity project, a way to distinguish himself from his predecessors and lock in his agenda, despite his denials. It makes you wonder if this is just another elaborate game, much like the institutional decay we see elsewhere in global systems.

Ultimately, this is about setting a "destination" for the country, a place that remains relevant whether he's in State House or not. That’s the official line. But in Kenya, plans tend to shift with the political winds. The real question is not what kind of society we *want* to become, but whether we'll ever actually get there amidst the constant political maneuvering and the ever-present desire to leave a mark, even if it's just a smudge.
