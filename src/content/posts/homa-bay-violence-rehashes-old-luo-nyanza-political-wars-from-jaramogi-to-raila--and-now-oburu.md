So, the Linda Mwananchi rallies in Homa Bay have once again descended into a predictable mess of chaos and violence. It’s almost like a rerun, isn't it? This time, it’s all about who gets to sit on the now-empty throne after Raila Odinga’s departure, and naturally, the Luo Nation is tearing itself apart. The same old song and dance, just with different faces, and the same old bitter rivalries that remind us of the dark days after Jaramogi Oginga Odinga’s death. Nothing truly changes, does it?

Now we've got this squabble over ODM's direction for 2027. On one side, you have Governor James Orengo and his crew, like Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and Babu Owino, pushing for ODM to field its own presidential candidate. On the other, it’s Raila’s older brother, Oburu Oginga, who apparently thinks cozying up to President William Ruto is the smart move. It’s a classic case of political opportunism, with each faction trying to carve out their piece of the pie, accusing the other of being manipulated. Honestly, it’s more about personal ambition than community progress.

The immediate trigger is the 2027 election, but the deep-seated animosity goes way back. Remember the Ugunja fundraiser in 1999? Raila versus Orengo, a bitter clash that spilled into outright violence, complete with hired crowds and tear gas. It was a precursor to the kind of political theatre we're seeing today, a desperate scramble for dominance that leaves ordinary people caught in the crossfire. It’s amusing how history keeps repeating itself, especially when you consider how political deals, like those that have been struck in the past, often leave more questions than answers.

This whole drama is just a fresh reminder that the Luo Nation's political landscape has always been a battlefield. The struggle for succession after Jaramogi was fierce, culminating in the Ford Kenya leadership fight where Raila felt cheated. He then went on to form his own party, NDP, and eventually became the undisputed kingpin. Now that he’s gone, the same old hunger for power is resurfacing, this time between Raila’s brother and his former allies. It’s a predictable cycle, fueled by ego and the allure of control.

The Orengo camp has Suba South MP Caroli Omondi, while Oburu is backed by Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga and some Cabinet Secretaries. Meanwhile, figures like Millie Odhiambo and Ruth Odinga are wisely staying on the sidelines, probably watching the inevitable implosion with a mix of amusement and dread. The violence in Homa Bay, which even saw journalists injured and equipment stolen, is just a symptom of this deeper, festering wound. Amnesty International is already questioning if the police did enough. Of course they didn't; why would they interfere with the local theatre of the absurd?

Ultimately, this isn't about the community's welfare. It's about power, influence, and who gets to dictate the political narrative. The cycle of violence, betrayal, and backstabbing is as old as the region's politics itself, and it’s unlikely to end anytime soon. Until the ambition for personal gain is replaced by a genuine commitment to the people, we'll just keep witnessing these same old political wars, rebranded for a new generation.
