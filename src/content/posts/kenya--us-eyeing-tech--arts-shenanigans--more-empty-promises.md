Kenya, US Eyeing Tech, Arts Shenanigans: More Empty Promises?

Another handshake across the ocean, this time peddling "emerging technologies" and "creative economy." Sounds like a recipe for more high-level meetings, fancy speeches, and precisely zero tangible benefits for the mwananchi.

US Under Secretary Sarah B. Rogers, fresh from Silicon Valley's digital dreams, is in Nairobi with her posse to "deepen collaboration." Ambassador Philip Thigo, our own tech envoy, is all smiles, talking about expanding access and connecting researchers. It’s the same old song and dance, just with fancier buzzwords. Remember when they promised us the moon with digital transformation? We’re still waiting for the rocket fuel.

This whole "protecting digital liberties in the AI era" sounds suspiciously like another way for them to get their hands on our data and our innovations. They talk about supporting our creative industries, but what does that really mean? More workshops where everyone sips lukewarm tea and talks about "synergy"? We’ve seen this playbook before, and it usually ends with them taking the best bits and leaving us with the crumbs. It’s like watching some elaborate HOMA BAY SHENANIGANS ECHO JOKER JARAJOLA: LUO POLITICS STILL A CIRCUS, just on a global scale.

Thigo is chirpy about "institutional excellence" and "opportunities for experimentation." Translation: They want to test their latest toys on us and see if our researchers can reverse-engineer them for their own benefit. Meanwhile, our actual problems-like potholes and corruption-remain conveniently ignored in the name of "bilateral partnership."

The visiting delegation, a veritable who's who of U.S. Department of State types, are probably already planning their next round of "strategic dialogues" and "capacity-building initiatives." Let’s not forget the U.S. Embassy folks who are, of course, “guiding” and “collaborating.” They’re the local facilitators of this grand illusion.

So, Kenya and the US are deepening collaboration. Great. Let's just hope this "deepening" doesn't involve us digging ourselves deeper into debt or dependence. We'll believe it when we see actual jobs created and real problems solved, not just more photo opportunities and pronouncements of goodwill.







Kenya and the United States are exploring opportunities to deepen collaboration across emerging technologies, the creative economy and joint research initiatives as the two countries seek to strengthen their bilateral partnership.

Kenya’s Special Envoy on Technology, Ambassador Philip Thigo, said the discussions took place in Nairobi with visiting colleagues from the U.S. Department of State’s Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs team, following engagements in Silicon Valley in July under the Digital NEXT: Protecting Digital Liberties in the AI Era programme. The programme was led by Sarah B. Rogers, U.S. Under Secretary of State for Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs.

Thigo said the discussions built on earlier engagements with American technology companies and institutions and explored ways to expand access to emerging technologies, connect Kenyan researchers and innovators with American institutions, support the country’s growing creative industries and strengthen capacity to develop, adapt and apply technologies to local priorities. “Through the Office of the Special Envoy on Technology, we are advancing these partnerships in support of Kenya's digital transformation, research and innovation priorities, with particular attention to access, institutional excellence and opportunities for experimentation,” Thigo said.

The visiting U.S. delegation included Brian Gibel, Director of the Office of Policy, Planning and Resources for Public Diplomacy; Lilian deValcourt-Ayala, Director of the Office of Public Diplomacy and Congressional and Public Affairs in the Bureau of African Affairs; Yolanda Robinson, Executive Director for Public Diplomacy; and Esther Kim, Special Assistant in the Office of Policy, Planning and Resources for Public Diplomacy.

Thigo also acknowledged the continued support of colleagues at the U.S. Embassy in Nairobi, expressing appreciation for their guidance and collaboration in advancing the engagements and strengthening Kenya-US bilateral relations.
