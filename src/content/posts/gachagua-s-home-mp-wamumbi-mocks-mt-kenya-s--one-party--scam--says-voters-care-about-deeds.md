Gachagua's Home MP Wamumbi Calls Out Mt Kenya's "One Party" Myth, Says Voters Prefer Deeds Over Labels

The man from Mathira, Eric Wamumbi, has decided to burst the "Mt Kenya is one party" bubble, and frankly, who can blame him? He's out here on K24, sounding like he actually pays attention, telling us that come 2027, voters aren't going to be swayed by mere party colours. Apparently, they'll be looking at what these leaders have *actually* done, not just the party scarf they're wearing. About time someone pointed out that this region is a smorgasbord of political outfits, not some monolithic voting bloc.

Wamumbi’s got a point, though he’s probably just doing it to stir the pot. He names names: DCP, Ndindi Nyoro’s People’s Party, Mwangi Kiunjuri’s TSP, and the ever-present Jubilee. It’s a political circus, and he’s pointing out how some folks, like Rigathi Gachagua, are playing all sides. One minute he’s shouting "no zoning," the next he’s hobnobbing with Martha Karua, making it sound like Mt Kenya is a single, unified party. And Karua, bless her heart, is just clapping along. It's a masterclass in political contortionism, really.

Then there's the whole "Jubilee is dead" pronouncement, conveniently made when he’s welcoming some defector. It’s the same old song and dance: praise whoever suits your current agenda, trash whoever doesn't. Wamumbi’s basically saying, "Hold up, if you're out there in Ukambani drumming up support with Kalonzo, don't come back here telling us we're all one happy family in Mt Kenya." It’s a betrayal of the simplest kind of logic, but then again, logic doesn't win elections, does it? It’s a lot like watching the AU make grand pronouncements while the real issues fester; [the AU’s new foreign policy: five suits and a PDF won’t save us](/posts/au-s-new-foreign-policy--five-suits-and-a-pdf-won-t-save-us).

The MP is making it clear that this obsession with a single party is a fantasy. He wants the conversation to shift to what leaders *do*, not what party they belong to. And honestly, he’s not wrong. After years of political games, seeing tangible progress for the common mwananchi should be the benchmark. It’s a refreshing, if slightly naive, take in a landscape dominated by tribal allegiances and party loyalties.

He’s not saying parties are completely irrelevant, mind you. They’ll influence things, sure, but he’s betting on a leader’s track record to be the ultimate decider. It’s a gamble, but given the constant churn and burn of political alliances, it’s probably the most sensible thing anyone’s said on K24 in a while. Let's see if the voters of Mt Kenya actually listen, or if they'll just get swept up in the next predictable wave of party propaganda.







Mathira Member of Parliament Eric Wamumbi has decided to throw a wrench in the works, dismissing the idea that Mt Kenya is the exclusive domain of a single political party. Speaking on K24 TV, he argued that come 2027, voters will be looking at what leaders have *actually* accomplished, not just the colour of their party scarf. He pointed out the absurdity of a multi-party democracy while some are trying to paint the region as a monolithic voting bloc.

Wamumbi highlighted the diverse political landscape in Mt Kenya, naming the Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP), Ndindi Nyoro’s People’s Party, Mwangi Kiunjuri’s The Service Party (TSP), and Jubilee as just a few of the parties with a presence there. He specifically took aim at DCP leader Rigathi Gachagua for his seemingly contradictory stances, one moment rejecting political zoning and the next presenting Mt Kenya as a unified party when with Martha Karua. It’s a classic case of saying one thing and doing another, a performance familiar to anyone who follows Kenyan politics.

The MP also questioned Karua's passive acceptance of Gachagua’s narrative, especially when she herself leads a political party. He pointed out Gachagua's political travels, noting his visits to Ukambani with Kalonzo Musyoka to drum up support for his party, which contrasts sharply with the "one party" message delivered in Mt Kenya. The MP's remarks about Jubilee being "dead" further underscore this perceived inconsistency, showcasing a leader who appears to adapt his message to suit the immediate political winds.

Wamumbi’s stance is that the existence of multiple parties in Mt Kenya shouldn't be seen as a problem but as a reflection of a multi-party democracy. He believes the focus should shift from party labels to the tangible contributions leaders have made to their constituents. This pragmatic approach suggests a hope that voters are finally ready to look beyond party affiliation and hold their representatives accountable for their actions.

When pressed on whether parties would dictate the election outcome, Wamumbi acknowledged they could have some influence but wouldn't be the sole determining factor. He reiterated that, to a significant extent, it’s about what leaders have *done* for the people. This emphasis on individual performance, rather than party consolidation, comes as political actors position themselves for the 2027 General Election, a period often characterized by shifting alliances and strategic maneuvering.
