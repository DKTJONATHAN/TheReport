KOREA'S "SMART CITY" DREAM FOR KENYA: MORE DIGITAL HYPE THAN REALITY











So, South Korea is here again, this time with promises of "smart cities" and "digital infrastructure." John Tanui, the man in charge of ICT and Digital Economy - a department that sounds fancier than it probably is - is all smiles, talking about deepening technical cooperation. A delegation from the University of Seoul and the Korea Development Institute has been poking around Konza Technopolis, South Korea's pet project for our so called "digital future." They're looking at how their development assistance is "supporting" it, as if Konza isn't already a 5,000-acre ghost town being funded by taxpayers.

Apparently, these discussions are about "institutional support" and "strategic execution" through Korea's various "programmes" and "funds." It all sounds very official and very expensive. They're also talking about expanding cooperation into digital media infrastructure and this new "Kenya Advanced Institute of Science and Technology." Because what Kenya really needs is another high-sounding institute that will likely be underfunded and over-bureaucratized, just like everything else. Tanui chimes in with the usual platitudes about "impactful investments, innovation, and sustainable growth." We've heard it all before, haven't we? It’s the same song and dance.

And let's not forget the "multi-million-dollar technology framework" to accelerate Kenya's "Digital Superhighway" agenda. Sounds grand, but remember how many of these grand digital projects have ended up being elaborate ways to funnel money around? It's reminiscent of how even big tech deals, like the $800 Million Handshake with Epic Games, can end up feeling like a sell out for the very people they're supposed to empower. This Korean deal is no different; it’s a shiny promise built on loans - over $100 million in "concessional loans" to be precise - which means we'll be paying it back, with interest, for years to come.

Tanui claims they've "identified immediate areas of action that will realise benefits for Kenyan youth, innovators and technology professionals alike." Right. Because when has foreign aid ever directly benefited the average Kenyan youth struggling to make rent? These initiatives are usually designed to benefit a select few, the connected few, while the rest of us are left to deal with the inflated costs and the empty promises. It’s just another round of jargon and grand pronouncements from officials who are more interested in the photo opportunities and the international travel than in actual, tangible change for the people on the ground. They'll probably use cookies to track if you even bothered to read this far.
