So, November 18, 2026. Another year, another State of the Nation Address from President Ruto. He’s going to stand before Parliament and talk about his administration's "pledges" and "commitments." Like we haven't heard it all before. The public is drowning in taxes, and the public service is a cesspool of corruption, but sure, let's hear about the "milestones." It’s just another pre-election performance, a desperate attempt to convince us that his grand Sh5 trillion plan is actually going somewhere, when all evidence points to the contrary.

Last year, remember? He promised a "first-world economy" in Africa, a "seven-to-10-year transformation blueprint." His magic wand? A Sh5 trillion budget financed by two shiny new vehicles: the National Infrastructure Fund (NIF) and a Sovereign Wealth Fund (SWF). Sounds impressive, doesn't it? Except the funding is still a "pipedream," as even his own Treasury Cabinet Secretary, John Mbadi, admits. The Sh310.6 billion "seed capital" is a joke when you consider the Sh5 trillion target. It's like trying to build a skyscraper with pocket change.

And the "priority areas"? Investment in people, turning us from net importers to exporters, 50 mega dams, 10,000 MW of power, and upgraded transport. Of the dams, only inherited projects are nearing completion. The SGR extension is underway, sure, but the modernization of JKIA and the ports? Still stuck in procurement. The energy target sounds good, but let’s be real, Kenya’s total installed capacity is a fraction of that, and private investors are hardly lining up to fill the gap. It’s all talk, no action, just like the days when Rapcha was calling out Spiro Motors for their shady dealings, a similar dance of promises versus reality.

The truth is, the real challenge, the one Ruto conveniently avoids, is corruption. It’s the gaping hole where all that Sh5 trillion is supposed to go. Add to that the crushing public debt, economic policies that are strangling businesses, and taxes that are driving people out of jobs, and you’ve got a recipe for disaster, not a first-world takeoff. We’re seeing strikes from teachers, lecturers, and healthcare workers. State agencies are struggling to pay salaries on time. This is not the foundation of a developed nation.

The National Infrastructure Fund, meant to be a "game-changer," is now facing uncertainty after a High Court decision nullified the Safaricom PLC stake divestiture, which was supposed to provide a chunk of that precious "seed capital." So much for "shifting Kenya away from debt-driven development." It’s all hanging by appeals and the slim hope of private sector buy-ins. They talk about economic viability, but when political interference is a given and funding is questionable, what are we really investing in?

As the August 2027 elections loom, the opposition will be having a field day. And rightly so. Ruto’s speech is less about genuine progress and more about managing perceptions. He'll quote JFK about difficult challenges, but unlike reaching the moon, his ambitious plans seem to be stuck firmly on the ground, weighed down by a lack of tangible results and the ever-present shadow of corruption. We’ll listen, we’ll nod, and then we’ll go back to paying our inflated bills, waiting for the next empty promise.
