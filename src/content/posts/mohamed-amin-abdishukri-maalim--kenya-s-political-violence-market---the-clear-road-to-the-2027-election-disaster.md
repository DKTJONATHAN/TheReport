Another day, another grim reminder that Kenya is just a few shillings away from chaos. Mohamed Amin Abdishukri Maalim lays it bare for anyone who cares to read past the headlines - we're not just teetering on the edge, we've built an entire industry out of political violence. Remember 2007-08? That was just the warm-up. Now, we've got a surplus of young, jobless men, organized into hundreds of gangs, ready to be deployed. We saw them in June 2025, whips and clubs in hand, riding into Nairobi to break up protests, with police standing by like glorified spectators. This isn't a problem; it's a business model, and the 2027 election is shaping up to be its grand harvest.

The "goon economy" is thriving, a proper gig platform for disruption, intimidation, and injury. A ward rep pays KSh500, an MP KSh1,500, a governor KSh2,000. Land seizures? KSh1,500 to KSh2,000. It’s all paid in untraceable hard cash at petrol stations, managed by recruiters who clean up during election season while the foot soldiers barely make KSh10,000 a month. From boda boda stages and matatu termini to WhatsApp groups, the recruitment is slick, fast, and driven by pure desperation. "Education is the key," one criminology graduate observed, "but honestly speaking we never find the padlock open."

So, where do these "goons" come from? Simple-demography. We churn out 800,000 young Kenyans into the job market annually for a measly 100,000 formal jobs. The demand side? That's our beloved politicians, of course. NCIC found almost 90 percent of criminal gangs are bankrolled by political figures. Interior Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen admitted over 104 gangs are active under political patronage. Yet, when police arrest 300 suspects, not a single politician is among them. As Faith Odhiambo, former Law Society of Kenya president, puts it, it's a "procurement system of violence." We all know the buyers and sellers, but no one ever prosecutes the buyers. Shocking, isn't it?

This isn't new; it's just a more digitized, efficient version of the same old rot. The 1990s had KANU's YK92, with a young William Ruto among its enforcers, and a price list for violence - KSh1,000 for a killing, KSh10,000 for a permanent house torched. We’ve seen this script before, a recurring theme of [male dominance](/posts/kadhi-courts--kenya-s-last-bastion-of-male-dominance--and-guess-who-s-not-invited) in our political systems that simply reinvents itself. The Waki Commission confirmed 1,133 dead and 600,000 displaced in 2007-08, yet ICC cases collapsed, witnesses vanished, and sponsors were merely "proscribed," only to rename and diversify.

The "goonification" of our public life has only hardened. From the Gen Z protests of June 2024, where hired thugs attacked alongside uniformed police, to the Saba Saba protests of July 2025 where 38 died and police reportedly cooperated with machete-wielding gangs. By-elections are now blood sports. Vihiga Senator Godfrey Osotsi beaten in daylight, a civil society meeting stormed by an armed gang *with* uniformed police in June, and masked gunmen stalking campaigns in Ol Kalou. The digital wing mirrors this, with online operators paid handsomely to push narratives and abuse critics on X.

A goon isn't quite a militiaman, yet. He's a casual laborer, loyal only to the day's pay. But the gap between that and a standing organization with a command structure is shrinking with terrifying speed. The cash nexus isn't *inside* the mobilization; it *is* the mobilization. And the convergence with the police? That's the real kicker. We're on a fast lane, and the destination is clear: a more organized, state-sanctioned brand of chaos for 2027. Don't say you weren't warned.
