THE MOUNTAIN'S KINGPIN JOB: KINDIKI GETS A SHINY CROWN, BUT THE REAL WORK IS A MESS











So, Kithure Kindiki gets a shiny new title from some elders in Meru. "Kingpin of Mt Kenya East," they declared. Sounds grand, doesn't it? Like a promotion where you get a fancier desk but twice the paperwork and triple the office politics. This isn't about tradition; it's a political nudge, a demand for Kindiki to steer the whole mountain, especially with 2027 looming like a poorly planned budget. Easy for them to say from their side of the range.

But here’s the catch, the bitter pill in this otherwise sweet-sounding endorsement: Mt Kenya East is one thing, but convincing Mt Kenya West is the real challenge. That's where Rigathi Gachagua, the former DP, has his turf dug in. He’s not just some guy who used to sit in the big office; he’s built his own base, and his Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) is his war chest. Gachagua knows the mountain’s back alleys better than Kindiki knows his own inauguration speech.

The Ol Kalou by-election was a clear slap in the face, a reminder that a pat on the back in Meru doesn't magically win votes in Nyandarua. Kindiki’s talk of peace with Gachagua is a good start, a diplomatic handshake. But will Gachagua take it? He's got his own ambitions, and he's not likely to just roll over. It’s like expecting Eric Omondi to stop making noise for the sake of a county referendum - it just doesn't happen.

And let's not forget the real reason anyone cares about the mountain: economics. Farmers want better prices for their tea and coffee, miraa growers need support, and everyone wants better roads and markets. If Kindiki can't translate his proximity to President Ruto into tangible gains, all these pronouncements and endorsements will just be more hot air, just like that Kenya-France partnership that promised the moon and delivered dust. He’s got the office, but what has the region actually *gained*?

Then there's the whole circus of other influential figures in Mt Kenya East itself - Kawira Mwangaza, Peter Munya, Mithika Linturi. These aren't mere supporters; they have their own followings and agendas. Kindiki can't just expect them to pack up their political bags and disappear because some elders said so. He needs to play the long game, build alliances, and avoid creating more enemies than he can defeat.

Ultimately, this isn't about being a "kingpin." It's about convincing everyone from east to west that he's their guy, not just Ruto's running mate. He has the title, the president's ear, and the elders' blessing. But real leadership is built on more than ceremony. It's about navigating egos, delivering results, and getting a region that’s always spoken in its own tune to finally sing in harmony. That's the real coronation, and it's going to be a messy affair.
