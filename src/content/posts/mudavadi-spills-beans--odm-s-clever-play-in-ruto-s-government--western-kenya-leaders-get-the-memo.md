So, Musalia Mudavadi, our resident political soothsayer, has decided to bless us with his "wisdom" again. Apparently, the ODM chaps are now Ruto's biggest cheerleaders, and anyone who doesn't see it is just a fool who wasn't paying attention back in 2022. He’s out there in Vihiga, telling bishops - yes, bishops, because who else listens to these pronouncements - that Raila's party is playing the long game. They’re not just in government; they’re strategically planting their flags, and some of Ruto’s original loyalists are apparently going to find themselves in the political cold. It’s all part of the "broad-based government" strategy, which seems to translate to "ODM gets what ODM wants."

Mudavadi even threw in the National Assembly Budget and Appropriations Committee chairmanship as proof. Remember how Ndindi Nyoro was there, and suddenly Alego Usonga MP Samuel Atandi pops up, elected unopposed? Mudavadi’s making it sound like a chess move, not just a reshuffling of deck chairs. He’s basically telling everyone, "I told you so," when he predicted Raila would eventually be cozying up with Ruto. Some people thought it was a joke, but now they're left wondering how their own political space is shrinking. It’s a stark reminder that political alliances are less about ideology and more about who gets the better seat at the table.

And for those Western Kenya politicians still sniffing around the opposition, Mudavadi has a blunt message: you’re wasting your time. He’s basically saying the opposition is a dead end, a black hole for political ambition. The "real" game, according to him, is happening right here, within the government, where ODM is clearly calling the shots. He’s trying to herd everyone into staying put, arguing that there’s no viable alternative. It’s all about controlling the narrative and keeping the region’s leaders from rocking the boat, especially when you consider the ongoing competition for influence in Western Kenya.

Mudavadi's message is pretty clear: ODM isn't weak just because they're cooperating with Ruto. They're strategically positioning themselves, and anyone who thinks otherwise is either naive or wilfully ignorant. He's using the example of the Budget committee leadership change to show how influence is won and kept. It’s not about sentiment; it's about cold, hard political calculation. This whole situation reminds me of how politicians can twist narratives, much like the controversies surrounding the [Sh64 billion edible palm oil scandal tax probe stalls - nation.co.ke](/posts/sh64-billion-edible-palm-oil-scandal-tax-probe-stalls---nation-co-ke) where accountability seems to vanish into thin air.

Essentially, Mudavadi is painting a picture where ODM is the indispensable player in Ruto's government, the "biggest defender," no less. He's warning those who supported Ruto from the start that their political fortunes might not be as secure as they thought. The game, as he sees it, is about securing power and influence, and ODM, with its calculated moves, seems to be winning. The takeaway for Western Kenya leaders? Don't look for greener pastures in the opposition; the real action, and the real danger, is right where you are.
