So, the gospel circuit is rolling into Uhuru Park again, promising salvation and a 'free' ticket to heaven, or at least a two-day music marathon. Israel Mbonyi, Mercy Chinwo, and Moses Bliss are headlining Grace Encounter 2026, and everyone’s buzzing about September 26th and 27th. 'Free entry,' they say. But in Nairobi, darling, nothing is ever truly free, especially when apostles and 'ministries' are involved.

Mbonyi, apparently, has a 'strong following' here, which translates to good business. And of course, Pastor Poju Oyemade will be there to sprinkle some wisdom, no doubt. Then you have politicians like Peter Salasya, already declaring his commitment to 'cry when I worship in the spirit.' Spare us the theatrics, baba. We know a photo op when we see one. It’s less about spiritual awakening and more about being seen at a high-profile event.

While thousands will gather at Uhuru Park, singing praises and chasing that elusive 'grace,' one has to wonder about the grace missing from the daily lives of many Kenyans. The very same city that hosts these grand events is also home to mothers in Mathare who get [no rest after birth, just more sufferings](/posts/nairobi-s-mathare-mothers-get-no-rest-after-birth--just-more-sufferings). It’s easy to sing about blessings when you’re not worried about the next meal or a safe place to sleep. A two-day concert might offer a momentary escape, but it won’t fix the systemic issues.

Apostle David Owusu and Grace Arena Ministries are behind this grand spectacle. Expect the usual blend of music, preaching, and an unspoken expectation for 'offerings' or merchandise, regardless of the 'free' admission. It’s a well-oiled machine, this gospel industry, always ready to draw in the faithful with promises of spiritual upliftment. Just remember to keep an eye on your pockets and your critical thinking cap firmly on. Because while they're selling you grace, someone else is counting the returns.
