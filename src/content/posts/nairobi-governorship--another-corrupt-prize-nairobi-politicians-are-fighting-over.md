So, the August elections are "less than a year away," and the usual suspects are already crawling out of the woodwork, all wanting a slice of the Nairobi pie. Johnson Sakaja's got the seat now, but it seems everyone and their cousin wants to replace him. It's always the same old song and dance, isn't it? A bunch of big shots announcing their "bids," as if they're offering us a favour.

And this whole song and dance about "trusted, independent news" is just a fancy way of saying they want to sell your information. "Subscribe for 1 Euro and refuse cookies," they say. Ha! As if refusing cookies is going to stop them from sniffing around your personal data. They'll collect your IP address, your browsing habits - everything - all in the name of "improving your experience" and "content personalization." It's a con, pure and simple.

You can either sell your soul (and your data) for "free access" or pay up for "full access with no advertising cookies." Either way, you're paying. They claim they need your data to fund their "editorial work." Funny, because I recall reading about how [Kenyan Tea Trades Sudan, Iran For China, South Africa: Still Pinned Down](/posts/kenyan-tea-trades-sudan--iran-for-china--south-africa--still-pinned-down), which sounds like a lot of economic maneuvering that probably doesn't trickle down to us anyway.

These politicians, they're all lining up, eyeing that governorship like it's a goldmine. They promise development, progress, all that jazz. But what we usually get is more of the same: inflated tenders, backroom deals, and a city that continues to struggle. It's a "coveted political prize" for them, meaning it's a prize they can steal from us.

Don't be fooled by the shiny promises or the "manage my preferences" links. They want to track you, target you, and ultimately, milk you dry. Whether it's for their campaigns or their partners' advertising schemes, your personal information is just another commodity in this grand Nairobi circus.

So, when you see these prominent figures announcing their bids, remember what it really means. It means more promises that will likely go unfulfilled, more money changing hands under the table, and more of our data being packaged and sold. The only thing that's truly "independent" here is our right to be cynical.
