Mudavadi's 'Love Knows No Borders' A Day After Charlene Ruto, Yunge Wedding, And So Does The Bill

The photos are out, the champagne has likely gone flat, and Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi is waxing poetic about "love knows no borders" after Charlene Ruto and Isaya Yunge finally tied the knot in Tanzania. Months of "introductions to formalise the union" - that's political code for "making sure the right people were paid off and the optics were right" - have culminated in a shindig that probably cost more than some counties' annual budgets. And Yunge, the tech whiz behind an app meant to help students find scholarships, is now part of the family. Let's hope his algorithms can find him a good deal on wedding photos, because this was one expensive affair.

The whole song and dance kicked off with a traditional Kalenjin Koito engagement ceremony back in August 2026 at the Ruto family's Intonna Heritage Farm. Because nothing says "authentic tradition" like a meticulously staged event for the cameras, right? Kilgoris, Narok County - beautiful scenery for what must have been a tense pre-nuptial negotiation disguised as cultural bonding. One can only imagine the discussions about dowry and future political alliances.

Then came the main event in Tanzania, a guest list that reads like a who's who of the Kenyan political elite. Kapseret MP Oscar Sudi, Governors Gladys Wanga and Anne Waiguru, Nairobi Governor Johnson Sakaja, Cabinet Secretaries Aden Duale and John Mbadi, and National Assembly Majority Leader Kimani Ichung'wah were all there. Mama Ida Odinga and her son Raila Odinga Junior too. It wasn't a wedding; it was a strategic summit disguised as a celebration. These are the same people who are supposed to be running the country, dealing with issues far more pressing than who's marrying whom, but here they are, flown out for a VIP bash. It’s almost as if our politicians have more time for lavish ceremonies than for addressing the real problems Kenyans face, problems that often feel as inescapable as the AI Paradox we’ve seen rocking the markets.

And what about Yunge, the supposed tech guru? His SomaApp, launched in 2016, was supposed to connect African students with scholarships. Now he's founder of Goodsam Technologies. One wonders if the "good sam" part refers to him finally getting his hands on some government tenders through marriage. Because in this country, innovation often seems to be less about changing lives and more about navigating the political maze. It’s a far cry from the kind of desperate stunts we see online, like that Nairobi streamer who played dead on a grave for TikTok likes, because why not? At least that’s honest about its pursuit of attention, unlike the elaborate charades we witness in high society.

So, while Mudavadi waxes lyrical about love transcending borders, the rest of us are left contemplating the borders of our wallets. This wedding, with its political heavyweights and the apparent merger of a tech entrepreneur with political royalty, is less a fairy tale and more a calculated maneuver. The focus might be on Charlene and Yunge's "key milestone," but for those of us watching from the ground, it's just another reminder of how the circles of power operate, where personal unions are as much about political currency as they are about affection.

Let's just hope this union brings some tangible benefits to the students Yunge's app was supposed to help, rather than just adding another name to the list of well-connected individuals. Because while love might indeed know no borders, our financial realities certainly do, and they tend to be quite unforgiving.
