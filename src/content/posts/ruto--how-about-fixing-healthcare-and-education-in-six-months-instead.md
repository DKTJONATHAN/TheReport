Ruto, How About Fixing Healthcare And Education In Six Months Instead?











So, President Ruto graced us with another "special national address," this time unveiling "Beyond Vision 2030" - a 30-year master plan. He’s talking about constitutional imperatives, public participation, and copying the Asian Tigers’ playbook on stability for export-oriented growth. Sounds lovely on paper, doesn't it? Almost as lovely as those state lodges he’s so fond of building while basic schools lack toilets. The problem, you see, isn't the ideas themselves. It's the gaping chasm between the polished rhetoric and the grubby reality we've been forced to endure for the past four years. Where's the honesty about what’s gone wrong?

He waxes lyrical about social and economic rights, but conveniently forgets that healthcare and education are in a perpetual nosedive. Universal health insurance? A joke riddled with mismanagement and outright theft. Education? A chaotic mess of funding woes, curriculum confusion, and crumbling infrastructure. While he dreams of global competitiveness, our schools can barely afford blackboards, let alone laboratories. It’s like planning a five-star feast when your kitchen is on fire. This reminds me of the same script we see playing out, a predictable near-miss with disaster time and time again, like [Nairobi’s Annual Near-Miss with Disaster](/posts/same-script--different-date--nairobi-s-annual-near-miss-with-disaster).

And this invitation for "national conversation"? A bit rich, considering how dissenters are treated. Remember last year when the youth protested that punitive tax bill? They were treated like vermin, mowed down in the streets. Abductions and torture of anyone daring to speak out continue. Gatherings to discuss budgets or hear opposition voices are met with violent disruption. The "goon terrorism" he *doesn't* mention is a constant shadow, even drawing international concern, with warnings of upcoming elections potentially plunging us into violence.

So, Mr. President, instead of dreaming 30 years ahead, how about fixing healthcare and education in six months? How about cracking down on the goons and the opulence that screams of wastage? Curbing criminal violence and corruption would be a start, wouldn't it? Because that's what truly discourages investment, not a lack of blueprints. If you can achieve even a fraction of that, you'll create the stability you crave. But honestly, the real problem isn't the hardware; it's the rotten software of governance, ethics, integrity, and honour that's fundamentally broken.
