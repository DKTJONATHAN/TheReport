Another year, another UN General Assembly in New York, and President William Ruto is there, ready to deliver Kenya's "official statement." The theme this time? "Restoring Trust, Managing Transformation: A United Nations that Delivers for All." You have to laugh, really. Because back here, trust feels like a luxury we can barely afford, and transformation often means more of the same, just with a fresh coat of paint. While the big men talk of global priorities like climate finance and AI governance, we're left to wonder if anyone's actually delivering for *us*.

Ruto's trip kicked off with a stop to meet the Kenyan diaspora, unveiling a "Diaspora Impact Platform" and signing declarations of intent with Equity Group. He spoke of building "a stronger bridge" for Kenyans abroad, founded on "trust, transparency and measurable results" to convert assets into "long-term wealth." It's a nice thought, isn't it? Except while grand platforms are launched for those outside our borders, one can't help but remember the grim realities facing others, like the forgotten souls still caught in the human trafficking networks that plague places like [Ruai's Human Cargo: Another Day in the Trafficking Hub of Africa](/posts/ruai-s-human-cargo--another-day-in-the-trafficking-hub-of-africa), far from the glittering promises of New York.

The irony is thick enough to cut with a panga. While Kenya's delegation will be pushing priorities on peace and security, sustainable development, and human rights, the reality on the ground paints a starkly different picture. The same week, a report from Outright International screams that nine out of ten rights groups globally, including many in Kenya, have lost critical funding. This isn't just numbers; it's shuttered shelters, laid-off peer educators, and clinics unable to provide HIV care. These are the "measurable results" of global politics, just not the ones they talk about in those high-level roundtables.

Take the Mamboleo Peer Empowerment Group, for example. They had to let go of 100 dedicated staff - people who were the very backbone of their community. "Not just staff but trusted faces within the LGBTQ community," the report says. So, while President Ruto cautions that "trust and transparency are crucial" for his new diaspora initiatives, the very systems meant to support the most vulnerable amongst us are crumbling due to a complete lack of funding and, dare I say, trust from international partners.

The Assembly's new president, Khalilur Rahman, summed it up perfectly: "Trust cannot simply be requested, it must be earned... through the decisions we take, the commitments we honour and, above all, the results we deliver for the people we serve." Perhaps someone should whisper that into the ears of all delegations, including our own. Because right now, the only results many Kenyans are seeing are the fading hope of those on the margins, while the same old rhetoric echoes in the grand halls of New York.
