Treasury Officials Among Top Suspects in Ksh 1.87B Scandal: A Familiar Story











So, the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC) has dropped another bombshell, and surprise, surprise, the National Treasury is once again at the heart of it. They’re sniffing around for a cool Ksh1.87 billion that’s apparently vanished into thin air, courtesy of corruption and economic crimes. This latest report, covering a mere three months, reveals that the Treasury is where the biggest chunk of the looting - Ksh1.57 billion - is suspected to have happened. Just another Tuesday in Kenya, really.

The star of this particular show is a program called PROFIT, or the Programme for Rural Outreach of Financial Innovations and Technologies. Sounds noble, doesn't it? Except, according to the EACC, some clever devils decided to reanimate its closed bank account with forged Treasury documents and then proceeded to stuff over Ksh1.56 billion into it. Of that, a staggering Ksh784.7 million allegedly went to 23 companies, while another Ksh768.2 million was apparently withdrawn in cold, hard cash. Classic. It’s almost as if some of these grand development projects are just elaborate fronts for personal enrichment. Perhaps it’s not so different from how we’ve seen Western countries and billionaires experimenting on Africans in the past, just on a smaller, more insidious scale right here at home. But it's not just the Treasury hogging the limelight for this alleged grand larceny. The rot seems to have spread its tentacles to the counties. Garissa County is being investigated for a Ksh187.8 million procurement scandal related to a pavilion and a perimeter wall at a primary school. Then there’s Nakuru County, where Ksh95 million was apparently flushed down the drain for a land purchase for a health centre that was neither budgeted for nor properly procured. The land was overpriced and unsuitable, mind you, but Ksh50 million had already been paid. Sounds about right.

And the list goes on. Turkana County has a Ksh12.95 million conflict-of-interest case involving a county employee who conveniently used their own company to snag nine government contracts with dodgy paperwork. Wajir County is grappling with two procurement cases totalling Ksh6.46 million for solar installations and bush clearing, where officials are suspected of secretly owning the winning companies and using forged documents. It’s a well-oiled machine, this corruption business, a testament to our country’s unwavering commitment to creative accounting and exploiting loopholes. The EACC is busy recommending charges, but we all know how this usually plays out. The DPP will likely ponder, files will get sent back, and eventually, a few low-level individuals might take the fall while the big fish swim away, leaving taxpayers to foot the bill. Same old song, different tune.
