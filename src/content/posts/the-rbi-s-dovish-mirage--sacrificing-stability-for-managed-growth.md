This is not just a local concern. In a global economy where central banks are struggling to balance terminal rates with recessionary fears, the RBI’s creative accounting - treating a six percent tolerance limit as a comfort zone - suggests a dangerous precedent. It tells the world that emerging markets are willing to let the inflation tax erode the purchasing power of the poor as long as the headline GDP numbers look presentable to foreign investors. This mirrors the themes explored in "The RBI's Delusional Resilience in a Fragmenting World," where the gap between institutional rhetoric and economic reality continues to widen.

The international impact is clear. By underplaying core inflation and relying on optimistic forecasts for the 2026-27 fiscal year, the RBI risks a sudden, violent correction later. If the rupee begins to slide against a stubborn dollar, the "subdued" core inflation will evaporate, replaced by the harsh reality of imported costs. Market participants are not fooled by the official mandate. They privately recognize the true hierarchy of the RBI’s objectives: sustaining government borrowing and headline growth first, maintaining currency stability second, and treating the inflation target as a distant, ceremonial third.

Ultimately, this policy stance is a gamble on global conditions remaining benign. It ignores the fragmentation of supply chains and the volatility of energy markets. The RBI is not being resilient; it is being stubborn. When inflation peaks later this year, the tolerance limit might prove to be a floor rather than a ceiling, leaving the Indian economy exposed to the very shocks the committee claims to be mitigating. The consensus in the boardroom is rarely a sign of wisdom; more often, it is the sound of a herd heading toward a cliff.
