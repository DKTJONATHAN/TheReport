The whole "Ndindi Nyoro bolts from Ruto's UDA to take over the People's Party of Kenya" saga is just another episode in the never-ending Kenyan soap opera. Mt Kenya, as usual, is the stage for this grand theatre of ambition, promising to disrupt alliances and intensify the quest for who gets to be the region's spokesperson - as if one person can speak for a whole diverse population. Forget strengthening the opposition; this move just throws more spanners in the works, exposing the laughable disarray within the anti-Ruto camp. Should they all huddle under one umbrella like Gachagua wants, or should they continue scattering their efforts across multiple parties, each hoping to be the next big thing? It's a classic case of too many cooks spoiling the broth, with predictable results.

The launch of Nyoro's PPK brought out the usual suspects - Orengo, Mwashako, Omogeni - all flapping around like startled pigeons. Their presence, of course, immediately sparks chatter about whether this new outfit can actually become a "pole of opposition politics." Spare me. It's more likely to be another flash in the pan, designed to grab attention and maybe a few shillings. While established formations are busy trying to consolidate their meagre support, Nyoro’s grand entrance is just adding more noise to the cacophony. The real tremor, however, is in Mt Kenya, where Gachagua's "One Mountain, One Agenda, One Party" mantra is now being challenged. Nyoro's ambition is the equivalent of throwing a grenade into Gachagua's meticulously planned strategy.

And the reaction from Gachagua's camp? Predictably vicious. Calling PPK a "green wheelbarrow" is such a tired insult, especially when it’s a direct jab at Nyoro's past associations. It’s clear they’re terrified of anything that might dilute their claim to absolute control over Mt Kenya. This whole "government project" narrative is just a way to discredit Nyoro and force everyone back into the Gachagua fold. But Nyoro, bless his ambitious heart, claims the wheelbarrow has been abandoned. Too bad the label sticks, especially in this highly polarized political climate. It’s a constant reminder of the mudslinging that passes for political debate in this country.

Political analysts, bless their hearts, always trot out the "political rights" argument. Yes, Nyoro has a right to his ambitions, but let's not pretend this is purely about democratic principles. This is about power, pure and simple. The hostility towards Nyoro highlights the desperate scramble to control the Mt Kenya political space. Nobody has a "paternalistic right" to an entire region, yet that's exactly what Gachagua and his allies seem to believe. The stakes are high because Nyoro isn’t just a disgruntled UDA member; he’s eyeing the presidency, while Gachagua faces his own legal headaches after that messy impeachment. Nyoro could very well siphon off the very voters Gachagua desperately needs.

The claims that Nyoro is still secretly working for Ruto are designed to kill two birds with one stone: undermine Nyoro's credibility with anti-Ruto voters and reinforce the narrative that Mt Kenya needs Gachagua's iron grip. It's the same old trick. This whole saga raises the age-old question in Kenyan politics: can you make it on your own, or do you need the blessings of the established political networks? At 40, Nyoro is a prominent young politician, but whether his departure from UDA is a sign of independence or just a calculated move for future opportunities remains to be seen. His ambition has certainly shaken things up, forcing some of Ruto's allies to act as his unwilling spokesmen, which is, frankly, suspicious.

The drama continues with Nyoro now aligning himself with figures like Orengo and Musyoka, all while pledging to "liberate Kenya" and send Ruto home. It’s all grand pronouncements and promises, but behind the scenes, it’s just the same old scramble for positions and influence. The opposition principals are meeting to establish a secretariat - presumably to coordinate their squabbles. Whether Nyoro ends up being the flagbearer or just another contender, the underlying motive is clear: to displace the current power. It’s a game of musical chairs, and the music isn't about to stop anytime soon.
