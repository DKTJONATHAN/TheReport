So here we are again, eh? The Media Council of Kenya, bless their hearts, trotted out the usual song and dance about threats to journalists, all in the name of some "International Day to End Impunity." As if anyone in power actually cares. They had a "national dialogue," brought in government types, media bigwigs, and human rights folks. The theme? "Enhancing Journalists’ Safety and Protection Mechanisms ahead of the 2027 General Election." Translation: They're getting nervous because the election circus is coming and the truth-tellers are in the way.

The MCK CEO, David Omwoyo, whined about "verbal attacks" and "hostile environments." Oh, boo hoo. He wants leaders held accountable for threatening journalists. That's rich. These are the same leaders who have their cronies out there doing God knows what. And let's not forget the economic side - a joint survey by MCK and the Kenya Editors Guild found a whopping 52% of journalists earn less than Sh50,000 a month. Most don't even have permanent jobs. They're supposed to be the watchdogs, but they're barely making rent. It’s no wonder 83% have depressive symptoms. Frankly, I'm surprised it's not higher. How can a broke, stressed-out reporter be expected to chase down the truth when they're worried about their next meal or their mental breakdown? It’s a recipe for disaster, especially when you consider how leaders like those discussed in [Kindiki's Mt Kenya Unity Plea Rings Hollow, Defends Ruto's Ship Of Unsinkable Promises](/posts/kindiki-s-mt-kenya-unity-plea-rings-hollow--defends-ruto-s-ship-of-unsinkable-promises) are often the source of the pressure.

Then there's the actual physical stuff. Rebecca Mutiso from the MCK let slip they documented 47 cases of threats and attacks between July and September *2026*. Assaults, threats, arrests, intimidation, abduction, harassment, surveillance. The usual suspects? Police and "political goons." Quelle surprise. They're worried about journalists covering political events. You think? With outdated laws like the Official Secrets Act and others still on the books, our so-called leaders are practically handed a playbook for keeping things under wraps. They talk about aligning laws with the Constitution, but we all know how quickly that happens when it inconveniences the powers that be.

UNESCO’s consultant, Lea Simonini, chimed in about global impunity, saying nine out of ten crimes against journalists go unpunished. Nine out of TEN! And now we have AI throwing fake news around like confetti during elections. Deepfakes, manipulated speech - it's a whole new level of mess. And women journalists? They’re getting it from all sides, especially online. It's a tough gig, being the one trying to tell it like it is in this place.

The government's Ministry of Information, Communications and the Digital Economy, represented by Temesi Mukani, gave their usual "commitment to press freedom" speech and urged media players to "take advantage of emerging policy frameworks." Yeah, right. Meanwhile, the Kenya Editors Guild President, Zubeidah Kananu, is begging media owners to just pay their staff on time and provide basic medical and mental health support. It’s not rocket science. Journalists need support to do their job, not be fed platitudes and empty promises.

Ultimately, it boils down to this: They're talking about protecting journalists before the 2027 elections, but the same old problems persist. Outdated laws, economic precarity, and a steady stream of threats and violence. The Office of the DPP did pledge continued action, citing a case where three people got 15 years for assaulting a Nation correspondent. That's one case out of how many? They need more than pledges and the occasional victory lap; they need a complete overhaul of how journalists are treated in this country, before there's nobody left to report the mess.
