MPs Selling Teacher Jobs? Wetang'ula's Claims Are Just Another Tuesday in Nairobi.

So, the esteemed Members of Parliament, those paragons of virtue we send to Nairobi, are apparently running a black market for teaching jobs. Sh400,000 a pop for a chance to serve the nation, according to the esteemed Speaker Wetang'ula. And where do these magical forms come from? State House, of course. Because why wouldn't the "house on the hill" be involved in peddling opportunities to the desperate? This is Kenya, folks. What did you expect, a meritocracy?

This whole charade has the usual suspects - qualified teachers who can't catch a break - understandably fuming. They're the ones who actually did the studying, got the degrees, but can't navigate the murky waters of Kenyan "connections." It's a classic tale of who you know, not what you know, and it's as predictable as the traffic on Mombasa Road. This is precisely the kind of systemic rot that AM Live probably spends its mornings dissecting, trying to shock some sense into us.

It’s a stark reminder that even with all the talk about economic policy from platforms like Business Redefined, or the digital dreams explored in that gig economy podcast, the foundational issues - like basic job opportunities - remain stubbornly corrupt. It’s not just about making money; it’s about people’s livelihoods being treated as commodities. One wonders if this is how we’re "fixing the nation," as Eric Lattif and his crew on that show might be trying to convince us.

Frankly, this is business as usual. The same system that benefits the connected few while the majority scramble for crumbs. It makes you wonder if any of these "bold and groundbreaking" shows or insightful panels on NTV actually achieve anything beyond generating fleeting outrage. Perhaps Gumzo La Sato could dedicate an episode to the "matters of the heart" of those who've paid Sh400,000 and still have empty classrooms waiting.

The sheer audacity is almost impressive. It's a microcosm of how everything, from healthcare in "Your World" to the aspirations of young creatives featured on VIP ACCESS, gets filtered through the lens of profit and political patronage. This isn't just a scandal; it's a deeply ingrained symptom of a society where integrity is a foreign concept, and opportunity is a price tag. It's a messy, unequal Kenya, and these MPs are just playing their part in the grand theatre of corruption, much like how political maneuvering impacts us all, as seen in discussions about [US-Israel-Iran War: How Nairobi's Pockets Will Empty](/posts/us-israel-iran-war--how-nairobi-s-pockets-will-empty).
