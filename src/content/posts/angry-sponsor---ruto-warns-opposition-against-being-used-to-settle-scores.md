'ANGRY SPONSOR': RUTO WARNS OPPOSITION AGAINST BEING USED TO SETTLE SCORES

Ruto claims opposition is a puppet show for 'political sponsors', predicting a landslide victory in 2027 and attacking his predecessor for past failures.

President William Ruto addresses a crowd in Migori.

Politics

Ruto, Opposition, 2027 Election

Rally

Ruto is at it again, pointing fingers and calling for unity while conveniently forgetting his own role in the political circus. He's blaming some shadowy 'political sponsors' for the opposition's actions, a classic deflection tactic. Apparently, his predecessor, Uhuru Kenyatta, is the main culprit, apparently "angry" because Ruto is now doing what Uhuru allegedly failed to do in health, housing, water, and education. It’s a tired script, this blame game, and Kenyans have heard it all before.

The President’s speech reeked of a man trying to shore up his image, touting his administration's "progress" while conveniently sidestepping any real accountability. He’s predicting a "wider margin" win in 2027, which, let's be honest, is just noise. What about the real issues? The ones that leave Kenyans struggling to make ends meet? It’s easy to talk about first-world status when you’re not the one worrying about where the next meal comes from, a situation not entirely dissimilar to the unpaid bills at KNH we've seen piling up. [KNH: Sh867m Unpaid Bills - 18,000 Patients Vanish, What's New?](/posts/knh--sh867m-unpaid-bills---18-000-patients-vanish--what-s-new)

His promises in Migori are a classic example of campaign handouts disguised as development. Sh124 million for a bridge repair, Sh700 million for town upgrades - it all sounds good on paper, but we’ve seen these promises evaporate like mist. And the audacity to ask for assurance that the bridge won’t be destroyed *after* it's repaired, when the MP himself admits to encouraging its destruction? It’s a circular argument that highlights the broken trust between the government and the people.

Ruto’s assertion that his government is the "only formation that has a vision" is laughable. He paints rivals as "personality cults" or "tribal political parties," which conveniently ignores the fact that politics in Kenya often boils down to exactly that. His call to "unite Kenyans and eliminate tribalism" sounds noble, but it rings hollow when his own rhetoric often leans on divisive narratives.

The Migori visit also brought to light the deeply cynical nature of political maneuvering. Junet Mohamed, admitting to encouraging the burning of the bridge, now pledges allegiance to the "broad-based government." This is not about vision or progress; it's about survival and power. Residents are being offered development projects in exchange for political loyalty, a transaction that has always left the common mwananchi on the losing end.

Ultimately, Ruto’s speech is a predictable mix of bravado, blame, and empty promises. He’s selling a vision of a first-world Kenya while the reality on the ground for many is still a struggle for basic necessities. The 2027 election will likely be another spectacle, not a genuine choice for a better future.
