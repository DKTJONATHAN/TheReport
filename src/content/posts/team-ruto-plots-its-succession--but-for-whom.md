So, the President has gathered his "technocrats" and "loyalist bruisers" at State House. Twenty of them, apparently. Enough to manage a "fragile broad-based government" and steer the economy, which, let's be honest, is about as stable as a tuk-tuk on a potholed road. But the real juicy bit? They're all eyeing the next election. Naturally. Why build a nation when you can build your own political future, eh?

These aren't just advisors; they're the engine of a machine, a machine presumably designed to keep Ruto in power, or at least ensure his chosen successors get a sniff. It’s the same old song and dance in this country. Power is a currency, and these folks are just trading their current roles for a shot at the next big stake. Forget national interest; this is about personal ambition, thinly veiled as governance.

It’s fascinating, in a grim sort of way, how much power is being concentrated in this small group. They're the ones making the calls, pulling the strings, all while the rest of us are left to wonder if our money market funds are even safe in Kenya. This isn't about public service; it's a calculated power play, a scramble for position before the next electoral battleground is even properly drawn.

And the irony? They talk about "preparing for elections." As if we haven't been in a perpetual election campaign since the last one ended. This "team" is less about steering the ship and more about ensuring they have a comfortable cabin on the lifeboat when the current voyage inevitably hits stormy seas. We've seen this playbook before; it usually ends with more of the same old problems for the mwananchi.

It makes you wonder about the "detail and the dirt" that outlets like Africa Confidential promise. If this is just the surface, what’s brewing beneath? The talk of "decaying diplomacy and the myth of technocratic independence" is a constant reminder that promises of good governance often evaporate faster than morning mist on the Nairobi express.

Ultimately, this is just another chapter in the Kenyan political saga. A new set of characters, a slightly tweaked script, but the same underlying motives: power, position, and the perpetual pursuit of self-interest. Don't expect any miracles; expect the usual maneuvering, the backroom deals, and the quiet reassurances that all is well, while the important decisions are being made by a select few with their eyes firmly fixed on the next ballot box.
