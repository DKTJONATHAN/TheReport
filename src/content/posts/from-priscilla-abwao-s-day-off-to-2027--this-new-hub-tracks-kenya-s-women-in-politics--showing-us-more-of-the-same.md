From Priscilla Abwao to 2027: This New Hub Tracks Kenya’s Women in Politics, and It’s Already Showing Us the Usual Mess

So, they've launched another online platform, the Gender Resource Centre, supposedly to "bring Kenya's women's rights organisations, women's political data and advocacy tools together." Fancy talk for another place to dump information that probably won't change much. They even found a “mistake” in the date Priscilla Abwao entered the Legislative Council. A whole *day* off. Apparently, this proves the whole thing is trustworthy. Right. Because a single date correction is the pinnacle of data integrity in this country.

The whole idea, they say, came from grassroots women feeling “invisible to donors.” Shocking. Apparently, Nairobi bigwigs get all the cash while county folks doing the real work are left in the dark. This GRC, funded by some international bodies who like to pat themselves on the back, is supposed to fix that. They claim their directory now lists over 1,200 organisations and has been used by over 100,000 people. Numbers. They always love the numbers.

But let's be real. The data already shows that in the 2022 elections, women didn't win a single ward seat in six counties. They only got in through the “gender principle,” which is just a fancy way of saying they're filling quotas. This platform, grckenya.org, apparently has all this “surprising” data, but is anyone *actually* surprised? We've seen this movie before, and it usually ends with the same people in charge. It’s not unlike the situation where rich kids always seem to win, as seen in stories about our sports teams.

They also boast about a memorandum generator and a petition builder. Great. Because filling out paperwork is *exactly* what keeps women from being marginalized. And the "Kenya Women Election Watch" counting down to 2027? A digital ticking clock reminding us how little has truly changed. They’re hoping to get data from the IEBC, which is about as reliable as a weather forecast during El Niño. And the fact that women haven't accounted for more than 49% of registered voters up to 2022? That’s not a trend; that's a consistent reflection of who has historically been excluded.

Ultimately, this GRC is for grassroots groups wanting donors to notice them and for anyone wanting to download forms. It's a data dump, a digital filing cabinet for women's issues. They talk about “shared responsibility” and “collaboration,” but what we’ll likely see is more reports, more meetings, and the same old power structures. Let’s hope at least the journalists find something useful to write about, beyond the usual hand-wringing.
