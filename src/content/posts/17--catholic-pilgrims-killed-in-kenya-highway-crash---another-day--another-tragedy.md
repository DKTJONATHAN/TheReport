So, another 17 souls, most of them on their way to some holy site, have been extinguished on our Kenyan roads. This time it was a truck driver, apparently trying to be a Matatu driver and overtake another truck - a classic Nairobi move, really - that caused this pile-up. The result? A 14-seater van full of what I can only assume were the hopeful and the faithful, now just statistics on a highway to Mombasa. The archbishop is "saddened with lots of grief," the President offered condolences. Standard procedure, isn't it?

The NTSA, bless their bureaucratic hearts, have launched an investigation. They've even admitted the stretch is a "black spot," a place where death seems to have a permanent reservation. You know, the kind of place where recommendations have been made, probably gathering dust on some politician's desk. It's a broken record at this point: poorly maintained vehicles, speed demons, and roads that look like they've been through a war. This isn't news, it's just the predictable outcome of a system that prioritizes making money over saving lives.

And it's not just a one-off. Kenya recorded over 5,000 road deaths last year. That's more than most small wars. This latest incident is just a blip in the grim statistics, a stark reminder of the daily gamble Kenyans take just by stepping out their doors. We see similar patterns in other sectors, like how [Ndindi Nyoro's New Political Vehicle Shakes Up Mt Kenya Opposition Battle - More Drama, Less Progress](/posts/ndindi-nyoro-s-new-political-vehicle-shakes-up-mt-kenya-opposition-battle---more-drama--less-progress), where promises of progress often lead to more of the same old chaos.

The prayers and condolences might offer some solace to the grieving families, but let's be real. Until someone actually takes responsibility and fixes the rot - the corruption that allows dangerous vehicles on the road, the lack of enforcement, the disregard for human life - these "black spots" will continue to be bloodstains on our maps. Another group of hopefuls, another tragic end. Just another day in Kenya.
