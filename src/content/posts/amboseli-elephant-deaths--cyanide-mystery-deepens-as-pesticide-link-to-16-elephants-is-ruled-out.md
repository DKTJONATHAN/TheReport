So, 16 elephants gone. Between June and July 2026, poof. Mostly mothers and calves, because life always finds a way to twist the knife. They say the University of Nairobi found cyanide. Shocking, right? Especially when these animals, some barely able to stand, were supposed to be the pride of Amboseli.

But here’s where it gets interesting, or rather, predictably frustrating. The pesticide board, bless their hearts, went sniffing around tomatoes in the area. Found zilch. Zero cyanide. They even clarified that no such poison is *authorised* for use on our crops. So, if it's not the tomatoes, and it's not authorized pesticides, where the bloody hell did the cyanide come from? This is classic Kenya, a puzzle wrapped in an enigma, served with a side of incompetence.

The Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) is now scrambling, testing water, soil, grass - you name it. They’re even looking at giant star grass, which can apparently produce cyanide. As if nature itself is conspiring against these poor creatures. It's a nice little deflection, isn't it? Blame the grass. Meanwhile, the actual culprits, the ones who *used* the cyanide, are probably sipping chai somewhere, watching the news with a smug grin. It’s as if they’re haunted by ghosts, except this time the reason for the "phantom feelings" is a very real, very deadly poison.

The whole thing is a mess. Preliminary tests say cyanide, but the source? Nowhere to be found. It's like finding a bullet but not the gun. KWS is promising to get to the bottom of it, but let’s be honest, they’ll probably just point fingers at some poor villager who was just trying to protect his cabbage from baboons. We'll get a report, a few arrests that go nowhere, and then everyone moves on. Just another day in this beautiful, chaotic country where the truth is as elusive as a clean government contract.

They're still doing "toxicological, microbiological and related analyses." Meaning, they're throwing money at the problem hoping it fixes itself. This isn't just about elephants anymore; it's about who’s accountable. But in Kenya, accountability is a myth, a rumour, a whisper on the wind. We’ll keep hearing promises, but don't hold your breath for any real answers. The cyanide mystery will likely remain just that - a mystery, conveniently unsolved.
