So, here we are. August 15, 2026, and KNUT’s Secretary-General, Collins Oyuu, is proudly declaring from Bomet County that he's "totally two-term" for President Ruto. No apologies. None needed, apparently, because according to Oyuu, it's just KNUT's "very direct" policy to cozy up to whoever's in power. It’s all about the "government of the day," and if that government wants another term, well, so does KNUT. Makes perfect sense if you squint hard enough and ignore all the usual political theatre.

Oyuu’s justification? The sweet, sweet sound of new employment contracts for teachers. He’s out here bragging about "Serikali gani ishawahi kuajiri walimu on PNP up to 100,000? Mweona wapi? Hakuna." While he’s busy patting himself on the back for this supposed hiring spree, one wonders if he's ever taken a moment to consider the long-term implications, or if it's just about the immediate win. It's a classic case of "what have you done for me lately," a mantra that seems to guide much of our leadership, echoing sentiments we've seen in other countries grappling with their own leadership challenges. Think of it like [THE DEVELOPMENT MIRAGE: NIGERIA AS THE GUINEA PIG FOR A COLLAPSING GLOBAL ORDER](/posts/the-development-mirage--nigeria-as-the-guinea-pig-for-a-collapsing-global-order).

He’s practically throwing down a gauntlet, daring anyone to "come to my office for more" if they dare to criticize his political alignment. The priority, he insists, is "more employment for our teachers." It's a noble goal, no doubt, but one that conveniently overlooks the broader economic landscape and the sustainability of such promises. When the dust settles and the election is long over, will these employment numbers translate into genuine progress, or will they simply be another talking point in the ever-spinning carousel of Kenyan politics?

As the 2027 General Election looms, this endorsement is just another ripple in the intensifying political waters. Leaders and groups are jockeying for positions, making their alliances clear. Oyuu’s bold declaration, delivered with such unwavering conviction, is a testament to the enduring pragmatism, or perhaps cynicism, that defines the political landscape. It’s a reminder that in this game, loyalty is often a currency, and endorsements, however loudly proclaimed, are simply part of the transaction.
