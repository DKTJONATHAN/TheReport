So, the criminals have AI now. Big surprise. While we were busy arguing about "digital transformation" and "visionary leadership," the bad guys were busy downloading the latest toys. GhostGPT? Sounds like something out of a cheap sci-fi flick, but it’s apparently the new best friend for guys who can barely string a coherent sentence together. They’re building fake websites, crafting perfect scam messages, and even tricking bank security with deepfakes, all without needing a single technical certificate. The good old days of spotting a scammer by their shoddy grammar are long gone, thank you very much. It’s all about fake identities now, swiping your photos and voices from social media to bypass any checks.

And Kenya, ever the willing participant in global chaos, is right in the thick of it. This wave of AI-assisted social engineering has already swiped Sh491 million from mobile wallets and bank accounts, messing with over 123,000 SIM cards. We’ve seen deepfakes of politicians peddling crypto scams, and even President Ruto’s website got hacked for some Bitcoin. It’s like a bad joke, except it’s our money. Even our neighbours aren’t safe; Tanzania saw a fabricated disaster warning during floods, and Uganda is using stolen IDs to hijack SIMs and empty accounts before you even notice.

But here’s the punchline, the "how crime makes Kenya 'better'" part that the original headline so cheerfully points out. We’re a society that only truly wakes up when it’s staring down the barrel of disaster. Remember the 1998 embassy bombings? Suddenly, our border security went from ink-stained ledgers to digital scanners overnight. It took that level of horror to drag us into the modern age. Then came the 2007 post-election violence, which, in its own brutal way, pushed M-Pesa from a niche idea to a lifeline, making money transfers by text a matter of survival. The Westgate and Garissa attacks? That’s what finally forced our disjointed security agencies to, you know, actually talk to each other and build that central cyber intelligence unit. It’s always the pain, never the plan, that forces us to "grow up."

Now, we’re facing yet another evolution. Terror groups are rocking Starlink, operating from places cell towers can’t reach, streaming propaganda and coordinating ambushes. Fighters in Sudan are using satellite terminals to bypass dark phone networks. Militaries are scrambling to track satellites, and banks are rushing to deploy AI to catch fraud in real time. It’s a frantic race, driven by the fear of being left behind, or worse, being the next victim. It’s a classic pattern: criminals get smarter, and society, kicking and screaming, is dragged along, forced to upgrade its own defences. It’s not progress; it's a perpetual state of damage control, where each new nightmare is just the painful midwife for the next slightly less outdated system. This constant cycle of crisis driving innovation is why [Agentic AI Will Soon Ruin Kenyan Finance, BCG Gloats](/posts/agentic-ai-will-soon-ruin-kenyan-finance--bcg-gloats).
