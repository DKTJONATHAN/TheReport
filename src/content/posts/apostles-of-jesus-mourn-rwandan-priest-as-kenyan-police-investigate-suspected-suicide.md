POLICE PROBE SUSPECTED SUICIDE AS APOSTLES OF JESUS MOURN RWANDAN PRIEST

So, another one bites the dust. This time it's a priest, Fr. Alex, and the Apostles of Jesus are supposedly in mourning. Kenyan police are poking around, sniffing for clues in what they're calling a "suspected suicide." Convenient, isn't it? Just when things get messy, it's always a matter of the mind, not the hands of man.

Fr. Alex, we're told, was a loyal soldier in the Apostles of Jesus, taking his final vows back in '91 and becoming a priest in '93. He spent his days shaping young priests, trotting off to Rome for fancy spiritual studies at the Angelicum - probably learned all the best ways to keep secrets there. Then, back in Nairobi, he was the Spiritual Director at their Major Seminary, no doubt dispensing wisdom and making sure everyone stayed on the straight and narrow.

His last gig was all about playing historian, digging through the founders’ archives, dusting off old papers, and preserving their sacred rooms. All while, no doubt, the real dirt was being swept under the rug. This whole "Apostles of Jesus" outfit, started by a couple of Comboni missionaries in Uganda way back when, seems to be all about keeping up appearances. They’re asking for prayers for the departed, his community, and his family. Typical.

Meanwhile, ACI Africa, the outfit sending this out, is begging for donations to keep their "truthful" reporting going. They're all about "fairness, integrity, and fidelity to Jesus Christ and his Church." Yeah, right. Sounds like another bunch trying to make a buck off misery, just like some of those characters peddling influence in [Nairobi’s Passport Bazaar: Selling the Soul of the City for Sudan’s Warlords](/posts/nairobi-s-passport-bazaar--selling-the-soul-of-the-city-for-sudan-s-warlords). The circular itself throws in a Bible verse about nothing separating them from Christ’s love. A nice touch to mask whatever really went down.

The Apostles of Jesus Theologicum community in Lang’ata is left to pick up the pieces, while the police do their usual song and dance. Because when a priest ends up dead, and it’s not a dramatic murder, the official story is always "suspected suicide." It’s a neat little bow to tie up loose ends, leaving everyone to ponder the mysteries of the spiritual life, and conveniently avoiding any uncomfortable questions about institutional rot. It’s Nairobi, after all; things rarely add up neatly.
