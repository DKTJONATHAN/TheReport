WHO Says No Ebola Outbreak in Kenya, But Don't Start Celebrating Just Yet

WARNING: The WHO might say there's no Ebola outbreak, but that's based on tests of people who are already sick. The real test is whether it's already spread before anyone noticed.

CAPTION: Another day, another pronouncement from the World Health Organization.







So, the World Health Organization is out here telling us there's no "ongoing" Ebola outbreak in Kenya. Apparently, the few unfortunate souls who showed up sick have been tested, and-surprise, surprise-they came back negative. It’s almost as if they caught it *after* they arrived, or maybe they’re just good at hiding it. Either way, the big bosses in their fancy offices are breathing a sigh of relief.

But let’s be real, a "negative result" from a single imported case doesn't exactly make me want to throw a party. Dr. Martins Chibueze, WHO Kenya’s Head of Health Emergencies, is busy with "contact tracing" and "surveillance." Sounds important, doesn't it? It means they're chasing down anyone who might have breathed on the sick person, hoping they haven't already turned into super-spreaders. It’s the same old song and dance - identify and follow. Critical, yes, but does it actually *prevent* anything before it’s too late? We’ve seen how well that works out sometimes, haven’t we? Remember when Dennis Lusava walked into a police post and ended up dead? [A Nairobi Story](/posts/dennis-lusava-walked-into-a-police-post--he-was-later-found-dead--a-nairobi-story). Vigilance, indeed.

The WHO and the Kenyan government are "working together" to "strengthen disease surveillance." Oh, how comforting. It’s a partnership built on the hope that we can catch a deadly virus before it decides to settle in for a long stay. They’re coordinating, supporting, and managing "suspected cases." The question is, how many "suspected cases" are out there that they *don't* know about yet? And how many are quietly going about their business, blissfully unaware they’re carrying something that could turn this whole country upside down?

Look, I’m not saying the virus *is* here and spreading like wildfire. Yet. But until they can prove with 100% certainty that it’s completely gone and hasn’t left any little surprises behind, I’ll remain cautiously… unimpressed. This constant cycle of imported cases and frantic "prevention" feels less like a solid strategy and more like damage control after the fact. We’ve seen powerful people build dictatorships, and we've seen supposedly hip hangouts fail. [The Butcher of Dodoma: How Samia Suluhu Hassan Built a Dictatorship on Bones](/posts/Samia-suluhu-the-dictator). The big institutions always sound reassuring, but the reality on the ground? That’s a different story entirely. We’ll just have to keep our eyes open and our hand sanitizers ready.
