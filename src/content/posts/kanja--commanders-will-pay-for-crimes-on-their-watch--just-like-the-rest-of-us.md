So, Inspector General Douglas Kanja has finally woken up and smelled the gunpowder. He’s telling his top dogs, the commanders, that they can’t just sit in their air-conditioned offices pretending not to see the chaos on the streets. If crimes are happening on their watch, and they were too busy counting their own money or looking the other way, they’ll be held personally responsible. It’s about time someone pointed out the obvious: if the neighbourhood is burning, the guy in charge of the fire brigade usually gets blamed.

This whole "personal accountability" thing sounds nice, especially with elections looming and the usual suspects gearing up to cause trouble. Kanja wants reports on arrested goons, as if that's going to magically clean up the streets. He’s threatening to boot out commanders who can’t get their act together, which, let's be honest, is most of them. We've seen this song and dance before. It’s usually a lot of noise and then… more of the same. It reminds me of the endless political drama we saw in [Meru County Crisis: Victimhood Politics and Personality Cults That Block Development](/posts/meru-county-crisis-victimhood-politics-kawira-mwangaza), all talk, no action.

The warning also touches on the behaviour of police officers themselves, particularly those guarding VIPs. Apparently, these guys aren't supposed to be running around like they own the place. Commanders need to account for every officer and every piece of equipment, which is a revolutionary concept. And then there's the constant stream of deaths in police cells. Kanja wants the records officer, the sentry, and the duty officer instantly thrown out when someone kicks the bucket. As if that fixes the systemic rot.

The Inspector General is also pushing this new Nairobi Metropolitan Police Unit, which sounds like a fancy way of shuffling deck chairs on the Titanic. Apparently, it'll cover Nairobi and some neighbouring counties, dealing with traffic, public order, and, you guessed it, criminal activities. Let’s see how that pans out. The restructuring is meant to make the police more responsive, but let's be real, we'll believe it when we see it. Until then, commanders better start looking over their shoulders.
