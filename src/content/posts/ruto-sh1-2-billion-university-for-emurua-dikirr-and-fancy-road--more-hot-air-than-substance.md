Ruto Promises Sh1.2 Billion University for Emurua Dikirr, Named After Late MP Johana Ngeno - Another Empty Bag of Tricks?

WARNING: Another round of presidential promises, this time a Sh1.2 billion university for Emurua Dikirr and a supposedly international class B road. Expect more talk than action.

President Ruto has once again descended upon a region with a hefty chequebook, this time for Emurua Dikirr, promising a Sh1.2 billion university and a fancy new road. The residents, apparently, asked for it. Because asking for things from politicians always works out perfectly.

The Head of State declared the funds would magically transform into an institution of higher learning, conveniently named after the late MP Johana Ngeno. One can only wonder if this will be another white elephant project, much like some of the initiatives we've seen touted elsewhere, leaving taxpayers footing the bill for little actual progress. Remember those Turkana ECDE coordinators? Prepare for a similar déjà vu.

But it's not just about bricks and mortar for education. Oh no, there's also a road. The existing low-volume seal road, which likely struggles to handle a bicycle, will now be an "international class B road." Yes, you read that right. Apparently, the government has seen the light and decided to do more than just slap some tarmac on a dusty track. This new artery is meant to connect Emurua Dikirr to the "wider regional road network," a grand vision that will surely begin construction later this year, with an official launch planned for October.

Let's be clear: these announcements are always timed for maximum political mileage. The president himself plans to be there in October to "launch the construction." We've heard this song and dance before. How many "launches" have we witnessed, only to see the projects stall or never materialize? This isn't about genuine development; it's about optics and appeasing the masses with pie-in-the-sky dreams before the next election cycle.







President Ruto has once again decided to grace a region with his presence and a generous dollop of promises, this time for Emurua Dikirr. We're talking a Sh1.2 billion university, slated to be named after the late MP Johana Ngeno, and a brand new, "international class B road." Naturally, this all came about because the residents "asked the government to establish a university in the area." As if our leaders ever listen to the common mwananchi without a hefty incentive.

The Head of State even took the time to critique the existing road, a "low-volume seal road," admitting the government's initial plan was to merely "recarpet." But fear not, for a grander vision has emerged: a full-blown reconstruction. This new artery, running from Salgaa through Chebole, Abosi, and Shartuka, is supposed to forge stronger links to the "wider regional road network." It’s a classic political playbook, dangling fancy infrastructure to distract from the everyday struggles.

According to the man himself, construction is slated to kick off later this year, with a grand unveiling of the *launch* planned for October. Yes, he’ll be there personally to "launch the construction." One can only imagine the fanfare, the photo ops, and the pronouncements of progress, all while the real work, if it ever happens, will be left to toil under the radar. We’ve seen this movie before; it usually ends with a sequel of delayed projects and budget overruns.

This is all about the narrative, isn't it? A university, a shiny new road - these are the soundbites that fill the news cycles and keep the populace hopeful, or at least momentarily distracted. It’s a well-worn strategy, and one that often leaves communities waiting for the tangible benefits long after the presidential motorcade has departed. We can only hope this isn't another case of the government preparing for more empty promises, similar to what we've seen in other regions.
