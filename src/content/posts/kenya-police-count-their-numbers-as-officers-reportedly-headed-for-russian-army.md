So, word on the street - which is usually more reliable than these official pronouncements - is that our esteemed police force is being ordered to do a headcount. Why? Because apparently, some of our finest have been moonlighting, or maybe even full-on signing up, with the Russian army. Because clearly, Kenya isn't offering enough excitement or, more likely, enough pay.

This whole charade is about "personalization" and "advertising effectiveness," they say. What they mean is they're tracking your IP address to shove more ads down your throat while they figure out who's still in uniform and who's already booked a one-way ticket to Moscow. It's all about data, darling, data for them to profit from, not for your benefit. Remember how we talked about debt? [Kenyan Youth Call KSh13 Trillion Debt a 'National Emergency' - Parliament Will Talk, You'll Pay](/posts/kenyan-youth-call-ksh13-trillion-debt-a--national-emergency----parliament-will-talk--you-ll-pay). This is the same song and dance, just with more uniforms this time.

It's not exactly a surprise, is it? When the country's running on fumes and the promises of a new "Singapore" are just more hot air, some people are going to look for greener pastures. And if that means picking up a rifle for a foreign power, well, who are we to judge? They're just looking out for themselves, which is more than can be said for most of the people running this circus.

The "reports" are flying, as they always do when something smells fishy. But until someone's actually held accountable - and we all know how that goes around here - this headcount is just another exercise in futility. A few officials will get their numbers, maybe some paperwork will be shuffled, and then everyone will go back to their usual business of looking the other way.

And let's not forget the provider of all this "valuable" data. They're busy collecting your IP addresses, measuring ad effectiveness, and probably selling your browsing habits to the highest bidder. Personalization? Advertising effectiveness? It's all a smokescreen for surveillance and profit. They're not your friends; they're just another cog in the machine that grinds us all down.

So, while the police count their numbers and the government pretends to be surprised, remember who's really benefiting from all this. It's not the common Kenyan, that's for sure. It's the same old story, just with a different headline. And we, the ever-patient citizens, get to pay for it all, directly or indirectly.
