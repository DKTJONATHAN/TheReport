Ruto Promises Pfizer Price Cuts, But Kenyans Know the Drill

WARNING: Another promise from the corridors of power, aiming to slash medicine prices. Will this actually reach the average Kenyan struggling with chronic illness, or is it just more hot air from a health summit? We've heard this tune before.

The President, Mr. William Ruto himself, claims he's going to have a serious chat with the big boys at Pfizer. Apparently, Kenyans have been whining about the cost of life-saving drugs for things like cancer, and the President, in his infinite wisdom, has decided to listen. He's talking about "economies of scale" and negotiating as a government, a concept that sounds suspiciously like something out of a textbook, not a real-world fix.

He even trotted out the Roche East Africa deal, where Herceptin apparently dropped from a king's ransom to a slightly less astronomical sum. A third off. Wow, thanks. Meanwhile, actual Kenyans are still deciding between paying rent and buying their next dose. And let's not forget the "no expense to the taxpayer" medical equipment deal; sure, if you’re a taxpayer who doesn't exist. It all feels like a bit of a show, doesn't it? Reminds me of when I read about [Mandelson Vetting Fears: Foreign Secretary Concerned Ministers Ignorantly Endorsed Security Risk](/posts/mandelson-vetting-fears--foreign-secretary-concerned-ministers-ignorantly-endorsed-security-risk), the same old song and dance with slightly different players.

Then there's the whole song and dance about promoting local manufacturers, which sounds nice, but let's be real, will it actually make your cancer medication cheaper tomorrow? Or is it just another layer of bureaucracy designed to keep the wheels of government turning, regardless of who actually benefits? And the nurses and clinical officers are still on strike over pay. A "win-win solution" for packaging materials sounds lovely, but I'm more interested in a win-win solution for being able to afford to live.

The President also reminded us that health starts with what we eat and healthy lifestyles. Yes, brilliant. Because a kale smoothie is definitely going to cure advanced cancer or manage severe diabetes for the masses. It's almost as if he's suggesting we should all just *not* get sick, which, you know, is a fantastic plan if you've got the money for organic everything and the time to jog around Karura Forest daily.

So, here we are again. A health summit, promises galore, and the same old problems simmering. We'll see if any of these grand pronouncements actually translate into tangible relief for Kenyans who are just trying to stay alive and healthy without going bankrupt. My money is on "probably not."
