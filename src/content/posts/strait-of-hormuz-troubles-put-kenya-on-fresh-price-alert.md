Strait of Hormuz Troubles - Kenya's Wallet Takes Another Hit

Another week, another crisis brewing in the Middle East, and guess who gets to pay for it? Us. Just when we thought fuel prices were a done deal, the vital Strait of Hormuz is suddenly looking like a ghost town, and you can bet your last shilling that means more expensive everything for Kenyans.

Ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has plummeted to single digits, and the Houthi rebels are apparently getting cozy with the entire Yemeni Red Sea coast. This isn't some distant geopolitical chess game; it's a direct threat to our imports. Kenya relies on the Gulf for most of its refined petroleum and a host of industrial supplies - think aluminium, chemicals, fertiliser, you name it. When these critical shipping routes get choked, the ripple effect hits our already strained household budgets and businesses hard. It’s the same old song and dance, just with a new act of regional instability.

Remember how manufacturers were already crying foul about shipping problems? The Kenya Association of Manufacturers, bless their hearts, reported that nearly 80 percent of them were affected by earlier disruptions, with lead times doubling and container shipping costs skyrocketing. One 40-foot container that used to cost under $2,000 (around Sh258,900) was suddenly demanding up to $10,000 (Sh1.29 million). Now, with further supply chain pressures mounting, expect those "higher costs" to magically appear on your supermarket shelves and at the pump. It’s almost as if global conflicts have a direct line to our pockets, far more than some of the domestic issues we see, like [Uncle Sam's New Favorite Side-Piece: Why Kenya is Selling Our Medical Tea for a VIP Trade Pass](/posts/uncle-sam-s-new-favorite-side-piece--why-kenya-is-selling-our-medical-tea-for-a-vip-trade-pass).

The numbers don't lie, or at least, they confirm what we already feel. Inflation in August hit a 31-month high, with transport costs stubbornly above 15 percent for the fourth month running. Petrol is 15.3 percent pricier year-on-year, and diesel, despite a minor dip, is a whopping 26.8 percent more expensive. This isn't just about the cost of your matatu ride; diesel fuels our farms, our industries, and our public transport. When it gets more expensive, everything else follows, ensuring the cost of living continues its upward march.

And just to add insult to injury, our government's ability to bail us out is looking rather shaky. The Petroleum Development Levy kitty, meant to cushion consumers from fuel price hikes, is reportedly running on fumes. They've already had to dip into diesel funds to subsidise petrol users. With global refined product prices on the rise, as indicated by the Platts benchmark, don't expect any relief from the upcoming monthly price review. Brace yourselves; the fuel shock is coming, and the government's safety net has sprung a leak.
