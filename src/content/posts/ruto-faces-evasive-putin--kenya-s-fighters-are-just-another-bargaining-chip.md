So, our own "Hustler-in-Chief," Ruto, apparently trotted off to Russia to have a chat with Vladimir Putin. The big question on everyone's lips? What about all those Kenyans who've mysteriously ended up fighting on the Ukrainian front lines? You know, the ones Russia is suddenly so keen on recruiting from across the continent, now that they've stopped bothering with fighters from 40 other countries. Fancy that.

The story goes that Musalia Mudavadi's visit to the Russian capital was meant to iron this whole mess out. But surprise, surprise, the two sides are "struggling to agree on the agenda." What agenda? The agenda of how many more Kenyans Putin can sign up for his endless war, while our leaders are busy with their own internal squabbles about illicit alcohol and general election noise. It's all very predictable.

And let's not forget the African Union chairperson is also supposed to be at this shindig. Probably there to nod along while Putin talks about his glorious new recruitment drive. Meanwhile, back home, we're still dealing with the fallout of recruitment networks sending our own people off to die in some foreign conflict. It’s like watching the same old Nollywood drama, but with more actual dead bodies.

Meanwhile, Russia's busy setting up new bases near critical infrastructure because, you know, Al-Shabaab might decide to crash the party. Because what’s a global conflict without a bit of regional instability thrown in for good measure? It’s all interconnected, this mess. Just like how we've seen [South Korea expands its presence in Kenya with new finance and development offices for control](https://www.example.com/posts/south-korea-expands-presence-in-kenya-with-new-finance-and-development-offices-for-control), everyone's got an angle, and usually, it's not ours.

The whole thing reeks of a cynical trade-off. Our president probably hoped to get some assurances, maybe even some compensation for the families of those caught up in this mess. Instead, he's likely just a footnote in Putin's grand plan to fill his ranks with cheap labour from Africa. And as for the "fight against illicit alcohol" emerging as a "prominent issue"? That's just political theatre to distract from the real problems, like our citizens being used as cannon fodder.

Honestly, what did anyone expect? Putin is evasive, our leaders are always looking for their next political win, and the ordinary Kenyan just gets to watch from the sidelines, hoping their own relatives aren't the next to be promised a dream job that ends up in a trench. It's a classic Nairobi story, just on a bigger, more global stage.
