Ruto Promises Turkana Oil Extraction, Transport Before December - Don't Hold Your Breath

This is the usual song and dance. President Ruto is at it again, promising the moon and the stars with this Turkana oil extraction. Just like clockwork, a big announcement before the year ends, with promises of extraction and transport. We've heard this tune before, haven't we? Always before December, always a grand plan, and always, somehow, the reality is a lot less shiny.

The big talk now is about a Lamu refinery and a pipeline from Dangote. Yes, *that* Dangote, the one who knows how to make a buck in this continent. Ruto conveniently mentions that Turkana alone won't supply enough for the refinery, so they'll be "getting crude from everywhere." This sounds suspiciously like lining up more deals where foreign interests get the lion's share, while we get the environmental headaches and maybe, just maybe, a trickle of the profits. It's the same old story, much like how [corporate monopolies always win](/posts/the-semaglutide-shakedown--why-corporate-monopolies-always-win).

Ruto is touting this refinery as a way to produce aviation fuel and other products for us. But let's be realistic. The capacity is massive, 700,000 barrels a day. Do we honestly believe Turkana oil, even if it flows like water tomorrow, could ever come close to filling that? This sounds more like a plan to import crude, process it here, and then sell it back to ourselves and the region, all while a select few get richer.

And the "wider industrial complex" in Lamu? Petrochemicals, chemicals, plastics. This is the classic bait. Promise industrial development, jobs, and progress. But remember, these projects rarely benefit the average Kenyan. They benefit the investors, the well-connected, and those who already have a piece of the pie. We'll be left with the environmental risks and the constant struggle to make ends meet. It’s the same old game of promises and dashed hopes.
