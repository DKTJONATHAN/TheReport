So, Rigathi Gachagua is out there in Kirinyaga, doing his usual song and dance, telling people not to be fooled by "William's party wheelbarrow." Apparently, the DCP is the "home party" for their government. It’s all about looking out for number one, isn't it? He’s claiming Ruto is setting up these new parties in Mt Kenya purely to slice up the vote, a classic divide and conquer tactic. Don't fall for it, he says. As if we're all just sheep waiting to be herded.

He’s even gone and pointed fingers at Deputy President Kithure Kindiki and CS Geoffrey Ruku, saying they are the Ruto administration's pawns in this alleged Mt Kenya vote-splitting operation. And then there's the threat about goons. No hiring of thugs, he warns, or you’re out, no matter how popular you are. It’s a tough stance, but considering the usual chaos that surrounds elections, maybe someone has to try and keep a lid on it. Still, sounds more like a power play than genuine concern.

The former Deputy President didn't hold back on broader accusations either, throwing in a jab about the "government killing our youths." He wants the whole lot rejected. Kirinyaga Woman Rep Jane Njeri Maina chimed in, accusing the government of orchestrating violence to postpone elections, which, let's be honest, sounds like a convenient excuse if things aren't going their way. Senator James Kamau Murango added his two cents, telling the youth not to be used as political footballs. It's all a big messy game, isn't it? This whole political theatre reminds me of the shambolic [Ol Kalou Election Tallying Fiasco: People's IEBC Learns Some Harsh Realities Before 2027 Gamble](/posts/ol-kalou-election-tallying-fiasco--people-s-iebc-learns-some-harsh-realities-before-2027-gamble).

The list of accompanying leaders is just a roll call of the usual suspects: Gitonga Mukunji, John Kaguchia, Geoffrey Wandeto, Onesmus Ngogoyo, Chege Njuguna, and John Methu. Meanwhile, Purity Wangui Ngirici, the former Kirinyaga Woman Rep, was once again snubbed, her supporters apparently making noise about it in Kiang'ombe, Gichugu. Some things never change, do they? It's always about who gets introduced, who gets a shout-out, and who's left in the shadows.

And then there’s the little bit at the end, Citizen Digital wants to hear from you. Send us your stories, videos, and photos. Share your perspective. This discussion is waiting for your voice. Be the first to share your thoughts! It's a nice little call to action, but let's be real, how much of that "perspective" actually makes it through the filters? We’ll see.
