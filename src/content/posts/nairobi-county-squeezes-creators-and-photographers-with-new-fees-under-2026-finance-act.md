So, Nairobi County, in its infinite wisdom, has decided to drain even more money from the pockets of anyone trying to create something interesting. Under the fancy new 2026 Finance Act, they're rolling out a whole menu of charges for filmmakers, content creators, influencers, and all those other digital hustlers. Apparently, making a movie or a viral video is now a luxury that needs to be taxed to the hilt.

Local producers get off with a measly Ksh8,000 per shoot, while our foreign counterparts are asked to cough up Ksh50,000. Religious and private filming? Ksh8,000. Music videos? Ksh10,000. And if you dare to run a studio as a content creator, get ready to shell out Ksh40,000 a year. It’s like they’re actively trying to kill any flicker of creativity in this city. Remember how we talked about the fleeting success of some of our local "heroes"? [Suit Up for the Sellout: The Short Shelf Life of Nairobi Heroes](/posts/suit-up-for-the-sellout--the-short-shelf-life-of-nairobi-heroes), well, these new fees will probably make that shelf life even shorter.

And don't even get me started on the influencers. Holding a monetised event? That’ll be Ksh10,000. Streaming platforms, the ones that are supposed to give us some escape from this madness, are now facing Ksh100,000 annually. Digital content platforms? Ksh80,000. It’s a full-blown shakedown, disguised as revenue generation. They’re not thinking about supporting the industry; they're just thinking about filling their coffers, no matter who gets trampled.

The county claims this is about bringing the "digital entertainment economy within the county's revenue framework." What they really mean is they've spotted a new goldmine and are ready to dig in. The distinction between local and external producers is a slap in the face, and the per-shoot charges will cripple independent filmmakers who are already running on fumes. It’s the same old story: the big players might survive, but the small guys, the ones with fresh ideas, will be pushed out.

This isn't about regulation; it's about extraction. They're asking us to pay more for the privilege of contributing to Nairobi's growing digital landscape. It’s a tough pill to swallow when you're trying to build something from scratch. One wonders if they've even thought about how they'll enforce these charges on online businesses, or if this is just another poorly thought-out attempt to grab cash.

Ultimately, these new fees are likely to make Nairobi a less attractive place for filmmakers and creators. Why invest your time and money here when other places might be more welcoming? The county is placing itself at the centre of a debate it doesn't seem to understand, and the creative industry is left paying the price for their financial gymnastics.
