So, a bunch of smart folks - Odipo-Dev and Amnesty International, bless their naive hearts - have dug up some numbers. Apparently, over 450 protests in the first half of 2026. That's a 29% jump from last year. What's driving this supposed outrage? "Economic grievances," they say. Rising prices, fuel hikes, food, housing, education. You know, the usual suspects that make life a living hell for the average mwananchi while our politicians cruise in their V8s. It’s almost as if the system is rigged against us.

The report calls it a "country in which people are repeatedly taking to the streets because their economic, social and political concerns are not being adequately addressed." No kidding. It’s like a broken record. The report also points out that residents and students are the main faces of this "movement." Students, bless their fiery idealism, are always the first to be out there, probably because they have more energy to run from the teargas than the rest of us. Peaceful marches, they say. Right. And sometimes, when frustration boils over, you get road blockages. Shocking, isn't it? It's almost like people get tired of their voices being ignored.

And the government's response? "Not repression," the report pleads. Ha! As if. We saw what happened in June and July of 2025. The Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) counted 65 deaths. Sixty-five. The Kenya National Commission on Human Rights (KNCHR) chipped in with another 19 and 38 on specific days. Injuries? Hundreds. Then came 2026, and May 18th saw the highest single-day arrest figures yet. The Ministry of Interior and National Administration reported 348 arrests, and the Directorate of Criminal Investigations managed to round up another 710. For complaining about fuel prices. The same old story, just with bigger numbers.

They talk about lawful, necessary, and proportionate use of force. Does teargas and live ammunition sound like any of that? Apparently, lethal force is for imminent threats to life, not for people who are tired of being fleeced. But hey, what do I know? I’m just a cynical Nairobi realist who’s seen this movie play out one too many times. The calls for transparency, accountability, and addressing economic grievances through "transparent and meaningful public participation" are just noise. We've heard it all before.

This whole charade is just a reminder that no matter how much we shout, how many reports are written, or how many people get hurt or arrested, the fundamental issues remain. It’s the same old game. And when it comes to building houses, well, let's just say Ruto's affordable housing pitch sounds more like a fairy tale than a plan. [Don't Make Me Laugh](/posts/ruto-s-affordable-housing-pitch--building-houses-to-save-farms--don-t-make-me-laugh).

The report, compiled from media and YouTube clips, is supposed to capture the "full scale" of protests. But we all know the real numbers are probably much, much higher. The real anger is deeper. And the politicians? They'll just keep doing what they do best: ignoring it all until the next round of protests forces them to make a few hollow promises.
