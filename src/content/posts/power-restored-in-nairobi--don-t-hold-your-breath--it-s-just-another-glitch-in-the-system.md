So, the lights are back on in some parts of Nairobi. Big deal. We're supposed to throw a party for this? It's like celebrating that the government finally remembered to send the garbage collectors for a week. Don't get me wrong, a bit of electricity is nice, but let's not pretend this is some grand technological triumph. It's just a temporary reprieve from the usual chaos.

This whole "power restored" charade is just another reminder of how fragile everything is. We live in a city where a slight hiccup can plunge us into darkness, and then we’re lauded for "resilience" when the power flickers back on. It’s like patting a drowning man on the back for managing to gasp for air. We’re not resilient; we’re just used to the struggle.

And speaking of struggles, this whole situation is reminiscent of how dependent we are on things outside our control. Remember when Kenya's 'Independence' became a subscription service to Ethiopia for electricity? It’s the same old song and dance, just with different players. We're always reliant on someone else to keep the lights on. [Borrowed Light: Kenya’s 'Independence' is Now a Subscription Service to Ethiopia](/posts/borrowed-light--kenya-s--independence--is-now-a-subscription-service-to-ethiopia).

Let's not even get started on what powers this whole charade. The website promises "essential cookies" for site functionality, but then floods you with options to track your every move for "ad measurement" and "remarketing." Facebook Pixel, Google Ads - it’s all there, ready to harvest your data while you're trying to figure out if your fridge will start working again. They want to know if you’re sad about the blackout so they can sell you something to cheer you up.

The truth is, the power coming back on is less a victory and more a brief pause in the ongoing saga of a city that seems to thrive on inconvenience. We'll be grateful for the light, yes, but deep down, we all know it's just a matter of time before the darkness creeps back in. And we'll be here, waiting, just like always.

It's the illusion of choice, really. You can "turn on" categories of cookies, but unless you’re a tech wizard, you’re probably just clicking "accept all" anyway. Just like we accept the power outages. It’s all part of the grand plan to keep us vaguely functional, but never truly comfortable.
