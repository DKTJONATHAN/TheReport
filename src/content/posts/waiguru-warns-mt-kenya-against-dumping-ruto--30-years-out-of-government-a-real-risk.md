So, Anne Waiguru, the Kirinyaga Governor, is out here in Laikipia East, dropping truth bombs like they're going out of fashion. She’s warning the Mt Kenya crowd not to be daft and turn their backs on the administration they helped put in power. According to her, if they start singing the "Ruto one term" tune too loudly, they risk being political orphans for up to thirty years. Thirty years, people. That's longer than some of us have been paying taxes.

She’s so sure Ruto will get a second term, she’s practically betting her governor’s salary on it. Apparently, the opposition is a shambles - no unity, no clear leader, just a lot of noise. While they're busy squabbling, Ruto's apparently busy expanding his fanbase across the country. Waiguru's "calculations" suggest he's got a grip on at least 33 counties. So, for the Mt Kenya folks thinking their eight million votes are some magic wand, think again. The national picture is way bigger than your backyard.

Waiguru acknowledges some might be "discouraged" by the current administration's performance. Who isn't? But she’s urging them not to "throw the baby out with the bathwater." Her logic is, it’s not easy to get a President out, and remember 2017 and 2022? She’s essentially saying, don't shoot yourself in the foot trying to punish Ruto, because you'll be the one hobbling around for decades. Her argument is simple: why be outside looking in for 30 years when you can be inside, playing the game, even if it's not perfect? She's seen it all, serving under Kibaki and Kenyatta, so she knows how this circus works.

And let's not forget the history lesson. Waiguru is reminding everyone about Ruto's unwavering support for Uhuru Kenyatta in 2013 and 2017. There was an understanding, she claims, a gentleman's agreement for Uhuru to serve ten years and then Ruto to have his turn. It’s all about loyalty and remembering who stood by whom. She’s also trying to steer the conversation away from ethnic divisions, reminding everyone that business and marriages cross these imaginary lines. Nobody wants their kids to be afraid to travel and do business because of some political tribal nonsense.

She's even defending Ruto's track record, especially on security in Laikipia, claiming things are calm now thanks to his "interventions." It’s all about performance, she insists. You elect someone to work, and if they're doing a decent job, you should say so. In Kirinyaga, the saying is "work should speak for itself." So, she’s urging the Mt Kenya electorate to stick with Ruto for their own interests and for the future. And get this, she’s signalling her own national ambitions, even mentioning the Deputy President role. The woman’s got ambition, I’ll give her that.

The whole community empowerment forum was a lovefest for Ruto, with Laikipia Woman Rep Jane Kagiri singing praises and Kiambu County Woman Rep Anne Muratha also doing her bit. Kagiri highlighted how cooperation with the national government, like improvements in Kirinyaga's roads and street lighting, benefits everyone. They're all pushing for continued engagement, to finish projects and secure national program benefits. It’s a clear message: align with the current power, or risk being left behind. It's a tough game, this politics thing, and Waiguru is playing it with a straight, cynical face. For those still hoping for a revolution, perhaps they should look at the news about botched attempts to storm police stations - not exactly a sign of organized strength.
