So, another kid bites the dust in Laikipia. This time it’s a Grade 7 pupil, apparently shot by the very people supposed to keep the peace. The official story is a "security operation" to clear livestock from farms. Funny how these operations always seem to end with dead children and injured herders. The residents, mostly Pokot, say they had an agreement with the farm owners - pay for the stalks, graze the land. But apparently, the police, the NPR, and the KPR don't do agreements. They just do bullets.

And the audacity. These so-called law enforcers lining up girls on the ground. What kind of "operation" involves that? Makes you wonder what their real agenda is, doesn't it? It’s not about protecting farms; it’s about asserting dominance and probably profiting from the chaos. This whole charade is as predictable as Marvel milking its franchises dry, promising more of the same cynical exploitation. Now everyone's running to IPOA to "establish circumstances." As if we don't know the circumstances. The circumstances are always the same: corrupt officials, unchecked power, and a deaf ear to the pleas of ordinary Kenyans. They want the Ol Moran police station commander gone, accusing him of issuing "killing orders." Shocking, I know. It’s the same old script, just a different village, a different casualty.

The demand to disarm the reservists is the most sensible thing I’ve heard all day. “Don’t have enough discipline to know when to use it and when not to.” That’s putting it mildly. These are the same people who probably get a thrill from brandishing their guns and intimidating defenseless communities. This isn’t a security operation; it’s a power trip at the expense of innocent lives.

The government will probably issue a statement, promise an investigation, and then nothing will happen. The NPR and KPR will keep their guns, the herder-farmer tensions will fester, and another child will be a statistic in a forgotten village. It’s the Kenyan way, after all.

They’ll probably blame the herders for encroaching on farms, conveniently ignoring the fact that these "encroachments" were part of a long-standing, peaceful arrangement. It’s always easier to demonize the victims than to hold the perpetrators accountable. Just like how governments always launch an "investigation" into things like the Epstein files, and then the wheels of bureaucracy grind slowly, if at all.
