Ndindi Nyoro's Fence-Sitting Gamble: Mt Kenya's Patience Wears Thin as 2027 Looms

Ndindi Nyoro, our esteemed Kiharu MP, has officially entered the "where do I sign?" Olympics. He promised to declare his political allegiance by July 25th. It's now well past that, and the only thing he's declared is his remarkable ability to procrastinate. Apparently, his political compass is as broken as the promises of most politicians. He’s been in politics for 13 years, and now, just when things were supposed to get interesting, he’s decided to take a scenic route through indecision.

The man claims he doesn't want to repeat a past mistake of jumping ship without a plan. Honestly, Nyoro, your plan seems to be to bore everyone into submission. First, he passionately opposed the Finance Bill 2026, then conveniently disappeared when it was time to vote. His excuse? He was out of the country. Right. And I'm the Queen of England. He also had the pleasure of weighing in on the Rigathi Gachagua impeachment, saying he was against it but, you guessed it, didn't vote. It’s a masterclass in strategic ambiguity, or as most of us call it, pure cowardice. He’s “no longer inspired” by the government’s agenda, but can’t bring himself to say where he’s actually headed.

His allies in Murang’a are warning President Ruto about losing the 2027 election unless he ditches Gachagua for Nyoro. Talk about ambition. But Gachagua himself is now warning Nyoro that the Mountain is no place for the undecided. Politics in our region can be a brutal affair, and sitting on the fence is a sure way to get trampled. Political scientists are saying he's caught in a web of clashing interests, probably because politicians only get sober when their pockets are threatened. It’s not hard to see that Nyoro is being humbled by the economic realities of his political maneuvering.

Meanwhile, his opponents are sharpening their knives. Karanja Mburu is making a third attempt to unseat him, and even the ruling UDA party has a candidate, Charles Karondo, eyeing his seat. In Murang’a, Nyoro is the only big-four elected leader who hasn't picked a side. Everyone else, from Governor Kang’ata to Senator Nyutu and several MPs, has either aligned with Gachagua’s DCP or stuck with Ruto. They’re all playing the game, while Nyoro is still trying to figure out how to tie his shoelaces. He’s being reminded that Mt Kenya voters dictate where their leaders are acceptable, and indecision is a risky undertaking.

The Kikuyu Council of Elders chairman, Wachira Kiago, is even throwing in historical examples, reminding Nyoro how Kenyatta, Karua, Kenneth, and Maathai were punished for taking unpopular stands. Nyoro admits the public has a strong perception against the current government, yet he hesitates. It’s a shame, really, because he seems to have the potential for something more, but he’s letting it all go to waste. Perhaps he’s too busy consulting on how to make an “instant sensation” instead of actually doing the work. We’ll see if he remembers to make a decision before the 2027 election, or if he'll be another casualty of our unforgiving political landscape, much like some of the counties that can't afford their contracts these days.
