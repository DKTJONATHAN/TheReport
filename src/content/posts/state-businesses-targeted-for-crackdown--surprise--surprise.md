State Businesses Targeted For Crackdown, Surprise, Surprise

MPs are finally waking up to the fact that auctioneers are using thugs and brute force to grab property, leaving ordinary Kenyans high and dry. It's about time they noticed; this has been going on for ages. Weak enforcement? That's a polite way of saying the system is rigged.

The National Assembly Public Investments Committee, bless their hearts, decided to grill the Auctioneers Licensing Board (ALB) about their pathetic regulation of the sector. Chairman Luanda MP Dick Maungu pointed out the obvious: there are "quack auctioneers" just like there are "quack lawyers." groundbreaking stuff, really. The committee is concerned about unlicensed types bringing in gangs to intimidate people, as if seizing someone's hard-earned property isn't enough of a violation.

ALB Chief Executive Festus Terer claims they're talking to the police to stop auctioneers from using "goons" and instead opt for "lawful security measures." He even met with the Inspector-General. Great. So now instead of random thugs, we'll have official police goons. This is supposed to restore order? It sounds more like a bureaucratic shuffle while people continue to lose their livelihoods. It's reminiscent of how these French millions are quietly reshaping Kenya’s creative elite, where the powerful always seem to benefit from opaque dealings.

Central Imenti MP Moses Kirima chimed in, saying disciplinary action isn't enough. He wants a compensation mechanism for victims. Finally, someone talking sense! But Terer’s response? The Auctioneers Act, Section 24, is too restrictive. So, we're back to the usual excuse: the law is the problem, not the people breaking it. The board is doing "intensified inspections," finding people without offices. Well, I'm sure that’s a huge comfort to anyone who’s lost their home to one of these cowboys.

The whole situation is a mess. These "rogue auctioneers" are essentially licensed thugs, and the regulators seem more interested in paperwork than protecting citizens. Kenyans are exposed to intimidation, abuse, and financial ruin because the system is designed to favour the predators. This isn’t just about weak enforcement; it’s about a fundamental lack of accountability for those who wield the power to seize assets.
