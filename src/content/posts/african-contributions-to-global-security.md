AFRICAN CONTRIBUTIONS TO GLOBAL SECURITY: MORE LIKE SURVIVAL TACTICS

WARNING: This analysis unpacks the often-unacknowledged, yet crucial, role African nations play in maintaining a shaky global security order. While the world congratulates itself, remember these contributions often come at a steep price for the continent.

AFP/MUSTAFA ABDI: African Union peacekeepers from Burundi patrol a Mogadishu street.

CRIME
Africa, Global Security, Conflict

OIL REFINERIES

So, the big boys in Europe and America are in a panic because some folks in the Middle East decided to play games with shipping lanes. Naturally, who gets the tap on the shoulder? Africa. Nigerian and Angolan oil producers are apparently saving the day by upping their exports. And let's not forget that shiny new Dangote refinery in Lagos - suddenly it's a global savior, shipping jet fuel to the very countries that have likely been eyeing Africa's resources for centuries. It's almost as if our struggles are only recognized when they can be exploited to solve *their* problems.

This whole spiel about Africa strengthening the "international security architecture" sounds grand, doesn't it? But look closer. While we're busy cleaning up messes and plugging holes in the global dam, our own problems are conveniently ignored. The Dangote refinery is also pumping out urea, conveniently easing fertilizer prices that got jacked up because of those same Middle Eastern disruptions. It's a good business move, sure, but let's not pretend it's altruism. And the $10 billion emergency fund from AfreximBank? That's just a band-aid for a continent often bleeding from wounds inflicted by the very global system it's now propping up.

TERRORISM, GLOBAL STYLE

Then there’s the whole terrorism song and dance. Africa is apparently a "focal point," which is a polite way of saying we're the battlefield where global terror networks try to set up shop. We're sending troops to Somalia, fighting ISIS in North Africa - all while the West offers "support" with air strikes. It’s a classic move: let them bleed, then swoop in with a few bombs and claim credit for the "victory." And the numbers? Thousands of fatalities annually, but hey, at least we’re keeping those global terrorist hubs from forming *on their doorstep*. It's a tough gig, and guess who's taking the brunt of the casualties? Yep, us.

PEACEKEEPING, BUT AT WHAT COST?

Africa is the undisputed champion of sending troops to these chaotic global peacekeeping missions. Seventy thousand troops annually, over a decade. And you know what that means? We take a "significantly higher share of combat casualties." So while the UN talks about security, it's our young men and women who are actually doing the dirty work in places like Haiti, facing gangs that control 90 percent of a capital city. And after Kenya is done, Chad steps in. It’s a rotating door of African sacrifice, all for a global security that often feels built on our backs.

MARITIME MAYHEM AND MEDIATION MIRACLES

Now, let’s talk about the seas. Africa is conveniently located between major oceans, making us the perfect place for global shipping lanes and chokepoints. When the Red Sea gets dicey, suddenly all that maritime traffic reroutes around the Cape of Good Hope, and guess who’s anchoring those lanes? Africa. We’re fighting piracy, illegal fishing worth billions, and drug trafficking, all because our strategic location makes us a convenient transit point for global problems. The Yaoundé Protocol and Djibouti Code of Conduct are great, but they're essentially us trying to police a neighborhood that the world’s big boys are using as a shortcut.

And when conflicts erupt - like in the DRC or between Ethiopia and Tigray - it’s often African leaders who are left to pick up the pieces and broker peace deals. The AU, SADC, EAC - they’re the ones doing the heavy lifting in diplomacy, while the international community watches. These African-mediated settlements are apparently more durable, which is just a nice way of saying *we* have to live with the consequences and find a way to make it work, long after the headlines fade. This constant patching up of global problems, while our own development often stagnates, reminds me of how Kenya’s own progress feels like a constant subscription service to external forces, as I discussed in a previous piece.

PUBLIC HEALTH AND POST-CONFLICT PULLING STRINGS

Finally, even our public health efforts are framed in a global context. Uganda sending Ebola aid, Africa striving for vaccine production - it’s all for "global public good." And that pledge of over $45 million to the WHO? Wonderful. But let’s not forget that Nigeria rebuilt Liberia’s armed forces after a brutal civil war. That’s not just global security; that's pulling ourselves up by our bootstraps while also helping others. It’s a constant juggling act, and the narrative always seems to center on how Africa is *helping* the world, rarely on how the world’s actions have contributed to the chaos we’re constantly trying to manage.
