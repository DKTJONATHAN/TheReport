Bomet's "Culture" Is Just a Fancy Word for Letting Predators Walk Free

This is Bomet, where justice for defilement cases isn't found in a courtroom, but in a hushed room. Elders, chiefs, or some relative - call them what you will - they just put a price on a child's dignity. A sum of money changes hands, the "case" is closed, and the survivor? Left to drown in trauma, alone. This isn't news; it's a grim, disturbing routine that has these so-called stakeholders wringing their hands, demanding "stern action" while their own practices shield the perpetrators. They cling to cultural norms, calling violations "private family matters" instead of the crimes against humanity they are.

Forty reported cases of sexual and gender-based violence in six months, 26 with murder threats. The numbers from the Judiciary, police, and government paint a picture of institutions that are supposed to protect, but instead, just turn their backs. Defilement, rape, sodomy, domestic violence - some of it fatal - are the daily bread of women and children here. Forty-four percent of women in this region have faced some form of abuse, all thanks to "deep-rooted cultural practices" that treat these acts as anything but serious crimes. Assaults, defilement, even murder - the statistics, even the ones they admit are understated, are staggering. And who are the enablers? Police, chiefs, parents, guardians - all named in this sordid mess, propping up a system that still believes in FGM and keeping women in the kitchen.

Chief Justice Martha Koome apparently finally woke up and issued a "firm warning" about using the Alternative Justice System for criminal matters. "Sexual Gender-Based Violence is a no-go zone," she declared. She pleaded for respect for children and women, urging them not to come to court "as if they are murderers." Honestly, Chief Justice, they're victims of a failed system that *doesn't* protect them. She was clear: SGBV belongs in the criminal justice system. And anyone mediating these "settlements"? They're now offenders, aiding and abetting the perpetrators. It's a bit late for warnings when the damage is already done, isn't it?

The governor, Prof. Hillary Barchok, is "heartbroken" about teenage pregnancies - 4,365 in nine months. He calls for a "multi-agency approach." What he means is more committees and workshops while fathers defile daughters, teachers prey on girls, and security officers look the other way. He even mentions parents killing their own children. Then there are the boda boda riders, luring girls with promises and leaving them with health complications. Alcohol and drugs are blamed, because of course. It’s a convenient scapegoat for systemic rot. And now, it’s gone digital, with cyberbullying, grooming, and sextortion. So, the "violence has shifted" - as if it was ever confined to a neat little box.

Even with a holding ground at the Bomet police station offering "psychosocial, legal and medical support," it’s a drop in the ocean. Domestic violence is still "deeply entrenched," and victims are told to "speak out" to hold families together, inviting more trauma. People with disabilities are prime targets, facing gang rapes and STDs. Elderly perpetrators are a thing. The local community organisation founder, Getrude Chepkemoi, says victims fear reporting because the suspects are "close relatives or guardians." Of course they do. This isn't just about women and girls; boys are being sodomized too. It’s a whole generational rot, and pretending a few arrests will fix it is just naive. It’s another Nairobi Realist nightmare playing out in slow motion.

This is why [Kenya moves to strengthen protection against tech-facilitated sexual violence](/posts/kenya-moves-to-strengthen-protection-against-tech-facilitated-sexual-violence) is a nice thought, but until these settlements stop being a glorified get-out-of-jail-free card for predators, nothing will change. Money for trauma, "justice" behind closed doors - it’s a business as usual for the morally bankrupt.
