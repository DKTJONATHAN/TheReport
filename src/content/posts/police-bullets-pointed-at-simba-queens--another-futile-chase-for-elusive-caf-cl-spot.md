So, here we are again. Kenya Police Bullets, the undisputed queens of the local league - not that it means much outside our borders - are dusting off their boots for another crack at the CAF Women's Champions League qualifiers. This time, it's Tanzania's Simba Queens standing between them and... well, another chance to underwhelm on the continental stage, I suppose. August 24th, Kigali. Mark your calendars for the usual spectacle of dashed hopes.

They say this CECAFA tournament is about picking the best from the region for the big leagues. Big leagues, big money, big dreams. But for Police Bullets, local glory has been their only trophy cabinet filler for years. They've dominated the FKF Women's Premier League, yes, but when it comes to the real competition, the one that actually matters, they’ve choked every single time. Second place last year? So bad they sacked the coach. Classic. Now they've got a new man, David Bujego, who apparently "understands the magnitude." We’ll see if that translates to anything more than just another headline.

The club's Secretary General, Chris Oguso, is spouting all the usual platitudes about pride and money. Money’s a big factor, he says. Well, no kidding. He bragged about flying out 26 players and 16 staff - a small army - despite "financial constraints." So, they're struggling financially, but they can afford to ferry an entire entourage to Tanzania. Makes perfect sense, especially when you consider how these trips usually end. It sounds a lot like that story about Singapore’s currency fortress, all about appearances and propping things up when the real problems are lurking. And then there are the "familiar faces" on the Simba Queens side - Harambee Starlets players. So, basically, they're going to be playing against some of their own countrymen, who are now lining up for the opposition. It's a bit like watching Kenyan politicians on a foreign stage, isn't it? Except here, at least there's a chance of some actual performance. One of them, Elizabeth Wambui, is injured anyway, so that's one less Kenyan to worry about, or perhaps one less to rally behind.

Let's be honest, the real prize isn't even winning the whole thing. Just reaching the quarter-finals gets you some cash. That's the level we're aiming for. Quarter-finals. For a team that’s supposed to be dominant locally, that’s a pretty low bar. This whole "quest" feels less like a genuine pursuit of greatness and more like an annual exercise in managing expectations and justifying the expenses. It’s the same old story, different year. Just another ghost in the machine, really. Like Mejja’s constant output, is it genuine success or just… more of the same? [Mejja's 'Siaka Siaka' Success and Album: Just Another Ghost in the Machine?](/posts/mejja-s--siaka-siaka--success-and-album--just-another-ghost-in-the-machine).

So, the guns are "trained," the quest "gets underway." Let's see how long this metaphorical ammunition lasts. My money's on a swift return to Nairobi, where the FKF Women's Premier League awaits, ready to crown them champions all over again. A hollow victory, but at least it's a victory.
