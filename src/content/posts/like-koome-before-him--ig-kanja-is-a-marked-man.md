IG Kanja Under Siege: Just Like Koome, He's a Marked Man

The Inspector General of Police, Douglas Kanja, is finding himself in the hot seat, much like his predecessor, with mounting public anger over rogue police actions and the rise of violent goons. This disillusionment is casting a long shadow over his tenure, and some are already whispering about his imminent departure.

The pressure cooker is on. From opposition leaders crying "State terror" to civil society activists chucking mock coffins at Jogoo House, demanding his resignation over unresolved killings, abductions, and enforced disappearances, the chorus of discontent is deafening. Even a church lobby group has joined the fray, citing the escalating chaos as elections loom. It seems everyone's got a beef with Kanja, and they're not shy about airing it.

Then there's the former deputy president, Rigathi Gachagua, playing the concerned neighbor and childhood friend, warning Kanja he's being "set up" for ICC charges - the sacrificial lamb for sins of omission and commission. He’s even dragged General Mohamed Ali and the ghost of the 2007/08 post-election chaos into it. It's a messy affair, with accusations flying like cheap chapatis at a roadside stall. Kanja's two years into his four-year term, and it’s already shaping up to be a battle-hardened chief's toughest fight, a far cry from his days dealing with banditry in Lokichogio.

It's not like Kanja is new to this. He's climbed the ranks, even led the GSU through the Dusit D2 siege. But endurance isn't enough when the system itself seems rigged. His predecessor, Japhet Koome, also bowed out after just under two years under similar fire, right after the anti-Finance Bill protests that left a trail of death and dissolved cabinets. And under Kanja, the body count in protests and suspected police disappearances has only climbed. The Independent Medico Legal Unit (IMLU) is documenting deaths in custody at an alarming rate, a "systemic crisis" that demands more than just a directive to interdict officers.

Even the President's office is seeing the cracks, with a report detailing a power struggle between Kanja and the National Police Service Commission that’s messing with recruitment, promotions, and ultimately, national security. Meanwhile, religious leaders are decrying the violence, the divisive speeches, and the very real threat of armed gangs at political rallies. They’ve even accused the government of trying to weaken the church to hide its own excesses. It's a tangled mess, and while Kanja might be a seasoned fighter, survival here depends on forces beyond his control, much like navigating the economic quagmire that seems to be a constant in this country, as we saw with [The Kitengela Bloodbath: New Year, Same Old Rungus](/posts/the-kitengela-bloodbath--new-year--same-old-rungus).
