So, the big news out of the Ministry of Health is that Kenya has chalked up zero new Ebola cases for 17 days. Apparently, the first few contacts are about to be set free from quarantine this weekend, bless their hearts. They're saying all 14 samples tested recently came back negative. Nine confirmed cases, one dead, all tied up in one little bow, no community spread. Sounds almost too good to be true, doesn't it? Nairobi folks like me know better than to get too excited about official pronouncements.

The eight still in the hospital, including four family members and four poor souls who were brave or foolish enough to be healthcare workers, are apparently "stable and progressing well." The Ministry is all optimistic about them finishing their treatment and getting out. And they've traced all 135 contacts, both here and abroad, even notifying the international health authorities. It's a whole big operation, very official.

Epidemiologists are crowing about how the pattern fits the first case, and how no one got infected in quarantine because they've "strengthened infection prevention and control measures." Right. Because when has that ever failed, especially with our systems? Dr. Amoth himself is quoted saying the 21-day monitoring is key, and anyone exposed to a later case is monitored from that point. It's all very precise, very by-the-book. Let's just hope the book isn't a fairy tale.

The first batch of contacts should be released Sunday, October 25th. The ministry is asking everyone to welcome them back warmly, assuring us they can't pass the infection. Bless their helpful hearts. Meanwhile, they're still screening travellers - 709,093 since the whole thing started, with 3,107 screened in the last 24 hours. And 461 samples tested since May 2026.

Despite all this, the government's message is: "Daily life should continue normally." Travel, trade, public gatherings - all unrestricted. Just follow the Ministry's guidance and cooperate with response teams. And if you feel feverish, tired, achy, have a sore throat, are vomiting, having diarrhea, or unexplained bleeding, you know the drill: rush to the nearest health facility. It's the same old song and dance, really. Just remember, when our leaders make grand pronouncements, it's always good to have your guard up. It reminds me a bit of when [PRESIDENTIAL SECURITY OVERHAUL ORDERED BY POLICE IG KANJA AFTER KILIFI BREACH](/posts/presidential-security-overhaul-ordered-by-police-ig-kanja-after-kilifi-breach), promises were made, and we're still waiting for the real changes.

Dr. Amoth is asking for compassion, not stigma. He says the end of this outbreak is "within reach." We'll see. Nairobi has seen a lot of things, and "within reach" often means a lot further away than they'd like us to believe. Just keep your wits about you, and don't be surprised if the story changes tomorrow.
