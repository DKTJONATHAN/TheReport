So, another week, another heap of gazetted notices. President Ruto, bless his heart, is busy doling out jobs like it's candy on election day. Njeru Peter Ndwiga is now the big cheese at the Coffee Board, replacing some other chap. And the Insurance Institute? Got a whole new council. This is the usual song and dance, isn't it? More titles, more meetings, and probably more tea breaks. Meanwhile, our coffee farmers are still struggling to make ends meet, but hey, at least Njeru Peter Ndwiga has a nice three-year gig.

Then we have the judicial side of things. Chief Justice Koome is shuffling people around in the Employment and Labour Relations Rules Committee. Fredrick M. Nyamora is the new Secretary, and Lady Justice Monica Mbaru is taking over as Chairperson. It's all very official, very important sounding. But does it actually make justice faster or fairer for the common Kenyan? I doubt it. This feels more like musical chairs with gavels.

And let's not forget the circus at the National Council for Population and Development. Ali Wario is the new Non-Executive Chairperson, and a gaggle of others have been appointed as members. What exactly they'll do with "population development" in this country of ours, where everyone's just trying to survive, is anyone's guess. Probably more reports that gather dust, and more funds diverted to "consultations." It's like we're trying to manage a population that's already checked out, or perhaps one that's still trying to figure out how to afford basic necessities, a problem that seems to echo the struggles of creative industries trying to secure funding, as seen in the [Kenya Creative Economy Sucks Up Global Cash, Nairobi Investors Eyeing the Spoils](/posts/kenya-creative-economy-sucks-up-global-cash--nairobi-investors-eyeing-the-spoils) story.

The Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission, bless their struggling souls, managed to forward a few files to the DPP. Fifty investigation files, with 43 recommended for prosecution. Four are already in court, and 24 are waiting for advice. This is what passes for progress? While the system churns out these numbers, the real loot is probably long gone, laundered, and spent on more unnecessary appointments. At least they seized some vehicles. That's something, I suppose. The usual suspects of public procurement and misuse of public resources are still the flavour of the month.

The Saccos are also in the spotlight, with inquiries into Kinatwa and Mathira North Farmers' Co-operatives. Abogeta Farmers Co-operative is still being liquidated. It's a mess out there, isn't it? People’s hard-earned savings tied up in failing institutions, while the same old faces keep getting appointed to committees and boards. It’s a constant reminder that for many, life is a constant struggle, and the "disposable bond" of modern relationships might as well apply to their financial security.

Finally, the political parties are having a field day with their names, symbols, and colours. Usawa Kwa Wote wants to move from Muthaiga Suites to Karen. The Democratic Union is changing its symbol from a house to a kite. The Party for Peace and Development is swapping its triangle for a jug. It’s all very superficial, isn't it? While they’re busy rebranding themselves, the real issues - the economy, healthcare, education - are left to rot. Just another day in paradise.
