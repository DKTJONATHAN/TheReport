So, Vocal Africa is making noise about 22 people kicking the bucket inside police cells. Naturally, they're demanding CCTV cameras in every dingy lock-up. Because, you know, the authorities have been *so* transparent about these sudden "suicides" that conveniently happen when no one's looking. It's always the same story: no independent evidence, just the police report saying "self-inflicted." Convenient, isn't it?

Meanwhile, over in Mombasa, the county is pushing ahead with their Treasury Square Garden redevelopment. Because, you know, prettifying the city is *so* much more important than ensuring people don't mysteriously die behind bars. Priorities, I guess. Maybe they'll plant some pretty flowers over the evidence.

This isn't some new phenomenon. We've seen similar patterns before, where official explanations don't quite add up. Remember when Kenya Energy Execs stepped down Amid Fuel Manipulation Probe? That was another case of "oops, something went wrong" that conveniently benefited some people and left others in the dark. This constant dance of cover-ups and hushed investigations is as predictable as the rising sun.

Vocal Africa's threat of legal action is just more hot air, frankly. How many times have these groups demanded action, only for things to go back to business as usual? The system is designed to protect itself, not the vulnerable. While they're busy pointing fingers and filing papers, more lives will be lost, and the only thing that will truly change is the date on the newspaper.
