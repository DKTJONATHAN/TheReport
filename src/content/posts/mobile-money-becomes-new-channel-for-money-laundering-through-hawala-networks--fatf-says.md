Mobile Money: Now The New Playground For Hawala Criminals, FATF Warns

FATF report highlights how underground banking and hawala systems, once informal channels, are now sophisticated tools for professional money launderers, leveraging mobile money and even virtual assets.

The global financial crime watchdog is raising the alarm, with their latest report detailing how these once fringe networks are becoming preferred routes for illicit funds.

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is out with another report, and it’s not pretty. Turns out, those convenient mobile money platforms that saved many a Kenyan from trekking to the bank are now also a darling for the really shady characters. They're not just moving your mum's pension money anymore; they're facilitating some serious criminal enterprises, making it a nightmare for anyone trying to keep track of dirty cash.

This report, "Investigating Professional Money Laundering, Underground Banking and the use of Hawala and Other Similar Service Providers," basically tells us what we already suspected: if there’s a way to make a quick buck off the books, someone will find it. These underground banking and hawala systems, which have been around forever, especially where formal banking is a joke, are now getting a digital facelift. They’re not physically moving cash across borders, oh no. That’s too much effort. Instead, they’re using trade deals, cash pooling, and even fancy digital assets to settle their dirty debts. It’s a whole new level of sophisticated deception, a far cry from the days of just smuggling goods.

What’s really alarming is how these networks are blending with modern tech. Mobile money, the very thing that’s supposed to be a lifeline for many, is now being used as the entry and exit point for these illicit transfers. Think about it: you send money to your cousin in the village via M-Pesa, and meanwhile, some scoundrel is using that same system to launder profits from fraud or, worse. The FATF report even points out how this is happening right here in Africa, with some corridors linked to Somali diaspora remittances being prime examples. It’s like that story about [From Koinange Street to the Kremlin: Selling Kenyan Meat for Russian Rubles](/posts/from-koinange-street-to-the-kremlin--selling-kenyan-meat-for-russian-rubles) - different hustle, same principle of finding a way around the system.

These aren't just petty criminals anymore, either. The report calls out "professional money laundering" (PML), indicating a new breed of criminals who are treating this like a full-time job. They're not just dabbling in drug trafficking; they're cashing in on cybercrime, corruption, tax evasion, and pretty much anything else that generates a hefty illegal income. And now, with mobile wallets and digital messaging apps, coordinating these operations is easier than ever, making it a monumental task for authorities to even detect them, let alone stop them. It’s a constant game of cat and mouse, and frankly, the cats seem to be getting smarter.

FATF: Informal financial networks, including hawala and underground banking, are being exploited for professional money laundering, with mobile money increasingly integrated as a facilitator.
