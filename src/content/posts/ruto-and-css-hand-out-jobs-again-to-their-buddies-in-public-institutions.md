The President and his merry band of Cabinet Secretaries have been busy doling out positions like party favors. Eng. Gilbert Maluki is back at the National Irrigation Authority, because apparently, three years is just not enough time to get anything done. Moses Wekesa Mwambu Mabonga gets the nod at EPRA, and Dr. Thuo Mathenge is out at KALRO. It’s all very predictable, isn't it? Just a revolving door of familiar faces getting paid to sit on boards.

Over at the National Treasury, John Mbadi is reappointing George Murugu Muthui to the Public Procurement Administrative Review Board. Because nothing says good governance like sticking with the same people who decide where all the taxpayer money goes. And Johnson P. Osoi is chairing the Local Authorities Provident Fund, no doubt ensuring your retirement savings are in the safest, most familiar hands. It's almost enough to make you forget about issues like cross-border crime, though I suppose some things never change. [Kanja and Rwanda Police Chief Convene in Nairobi to Discuss Cross-Border Crime, Likely Achieve Nothing](/posts/kanja-and-rwanda-police-chief-convene-in-nairobi-to-discuss-cross-border-crime--likely-achieve-nothing).

The Interior Ministry isn't left out either, with Kipchumba Murkomen appointing Adan Haji Ali to the Public Benefit Organisations Regulatory Authority. And Sophie Chepkorir Sang lands a spot at NACADA. Meanwhile, in the energy sector, CS Opiyo Wandayi is reshuffling EPRA and the Rural Electrification and Renewable Energy Corporation. It's a symphony of reappointments and fresh faces, all designed to keep the wheels of bureaucracy greased with patronage.

Health CS Aden Duale is keeping his team at KEMSA intact, while Education CS Julius Migos Ogamba is busy appointing and reappointing to university councils. The Water Ministry is also getting a haircut, with Njage Makanga taking over at the Central Rift Valley Water Works Development Agency. It’s a whole lot of shuffling, and for what? To ensure the right people are in the right places, of course.

And it doesn't stop there. The Labour Ministry, Trade, Agriculture, Environment, and ICT all have their own lists of appointments and renewals. David Musila at the National Industrial Training Board, Joseph L. Ole Kasae at the Kenya Leather Development Council, Rose Sang at KALRO, Abdikarim Mohamed Ali at the Kenya Forestry Research Institute, and a few more at the Communications Authority of Kenya. It’s a veritable buffet of government positions, and everyone’s getting a plate.

Most of these appointments are for a neat three-year term, just long enough to get comfortable, maybe make a few useful connections, and then move on to the next well-compensated gig. It’s a system that rewards loyalty and proximity, not necessarily competence or the public good. But hey, at least the gazette notices look official.
