Let’s talk about Eric Omondi. You know, the guy who parades himself as Kenya’s funniest. Turns out, behind the flamboyant suits and loud jokes, there’s a whole lot of drama we never saw. He lost his mother, the one who was supposed to fund his studies - talk about a cruel twist of fate. Then, he’s begging for university fees, only to get a pathetic KSh8,000 from his church. Eight people showed up. Eight. That’s Nairobi for you, everyone’s busy with their own drama.

He eventually makes it to Daystar, still chasing that dream, still broke. Then, bingo! He meets Churchill, gets on TV, and suddenly, he's "extremely famous" but also "extremely broke." The classic Kenyan paradox. He’s living the "expensive Luo lifestyle," splashing cash he doesn't really have, buying cars and paying rent in advance. A familiar story of too much too soon.

But then, like a bad dream, it all crumbles. He jumps ship from Churchill Live to KTN, thinking he's made it big. Three episodes later, the show is axed. Jobs dry up, his reputation takes a hit. Some folks probably saw it as arrogance. And then, the ultimate humiliation: auctioneers at his door, taking his stuff. He ends up crashing at a cousin’s place for three months. That's what happens when you bite the hand that feeds you, or at least when you think you're too good for it.

He crawls back to Churchill, a humbled man, before deciding to build his own brand. Now, he’s not just a comedian, he’s an activist, shouting about the cost of living. He even lost his brother, Fred, this year - another painful blow. It's a story that’s all too familiar in this city: rise, fall, and then a loud comeback, often with a soapbox. At least he's finally getting awards, maybe that'll pay for the auctioneers this time. It's a hard knock life, but some people sure know how to milk it for all it's worth. Remember Dennis Oliech’s Kawangware loitering? Same principle, different players, same Nairobi hustle.
