So, the International Commission of Jurists Kenya (ICJ) and a gaggle of other "stakeholders" have finally noticed what any Nairobi matatu tout could tell you for free: political goons are back in business, and they're eyeing 2027 like a fresh bag of cash. They're wagging their fingers, talking about "cycles of election-related violence" and "political economy of violence." It’s a whole lot of fancy words to say the same old thing - the rich and powerful are lining up their street thugs to do their dirty work, just like they did in 2007/08.

Christine Alai, bless her worried heart, wants to move beyond the "young men" and find the financiers and organisers. Brilliant observation, chairperson! It's almost as if these groups don't just materialise out of thin air. She’s seen the patterns, the "warning signs," which apparently look a lot like the prelude to a massacre. But here’s the kicker: Kenyans are "frustrated" because there's "impunity." Shocking, I know. We're supposed to believe that this time, unlike all the other times, the "rotten apples" in the National Police Service will actually be dealt with. They've even got a "special team" at DCI. Let's see how long that lasts before it gets absorbed into the usual Nairobi sludge.

The police spokesperson, Muchiri Nyaga, gives us the standard denial, "We are not part of the goons." Then he immediately qualifies it with, "even in organizations we have challenges, we still have some rotten apples." So, they *are* part of the problem, just not officially. They're mapping "vulnerable areas" and promising "accountability" from commanders. This sounds suspiciously like the same promises we heard after the chaos at Somer Club, where security seemed to just evaporate. [Nairobi Nightlife Security's Bloody Failure](/posts/somer-club-murder--nairobi-nightlife-security-s-bloody-failure), remember that? It’s the same old script, different election.

Meanwhile, researchers are scrambling to collect data because, apparently, nobody bothered to keep track of how many times we’ve almost burned this country down. Rahma Ramadan from the Centre for Human Rights and Policy Studies is busy "collecting data." Fascinating. I suppose we'll get a nicely bound report sometime after 2027, detailing exactly how obvious it all was. Demas Kiprono from ICJ Kenya chimes in about how elections "present a challenge" and how violence is now creeping into churches and hospitals. Really, Demas? You didn't think the same people who profit from chaos would extend their reach?

The civil society groups aren't satisfied, and they want "tangible results," not just statements. They're "opening their arms" to work with the authorities. How touching. It’s like offering a drowning man a glass of water. The problem isn't a lack of willingness to cooperate; it's the deeply ingrained rot of impunity and the political elite's reliance on these criminal networks. They'll keep talking, keep forming committees, and keep issuing warnings, while the same old machinery of violence gets greased for the next round. It's Kenya, after all. What did you expect?
