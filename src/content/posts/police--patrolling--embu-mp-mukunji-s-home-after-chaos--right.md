So, Manyatta MP Gitonga Mukunji pops back into the news, claiming the police are laying siege to his humble abode. Apparently, this is all a big song and dance over some Saturday skirmish at Deputy President Kithure Kindiki's little gathering in Embu. The officers, a whole contingent dressed in uniform and plainclothes, decided 6 AM was the perfect time for a casual visit to his Mutunduri home, allegedly just waiting to nab him. Mukunji, bless his heart, took to Facebook to whine about how this is just a colossal waste of taxpayer money, suggesting a polite summons would have sufficed. Honestly, you'd think with all the *real* problems this country faces, like Nairobi matatus and boda bodas doubling down on pain for commuters, the police would have better things to do than play cat and mouse.

He conveniently claims he was off at a church service in Mwea Ward when they showed up. "Let them come and arrest me," he declared, because nothing screams innocence like a public challenge. Meanwhile, the DP's side is all huffy, with Kindiki condemning the violence that left one dead and cars burning, and tasking his Embu crew to figure out the "exact nature of the loss and damage." This whole drama is just a classic case of political theatre, where everyone points fingers and nobody takes real responsibility.

Eastern Regional Police Commander Stephen Chebet, doing his best impression of a straight face, denies they're even looking for Mukunji. They were just "patrolling," you see, keeping the peace in Mutunduri. Because surrounding an MP's home with heavily armed officers is apparently standard procedure for a neighborhood watch. He did, however, admit they'll "visit him" if investigations point fingers his way. So, "patrolling" today, "visiting" tomorrow. Got it.

The DP, in between lamenting the "vengeful narrative" of his opponents, decided it was the perfect time to give President Ruto a "fair assessment." Apparently, Ruto has exceeded expectations with his "transformative development projects," while his critics are just spewing "useless slogans" and saying "Kasongo must go." It's all a bit rich, isn't it? From burning cars to political grandstanding, Kenya's favourite pastime seems to be a good old-fashioned blame game, with the common citizen left to foot the bill, whether it's through taxes or the escalating political tensions.
