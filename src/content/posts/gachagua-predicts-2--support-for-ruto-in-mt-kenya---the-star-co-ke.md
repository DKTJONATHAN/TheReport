Gachagua Predicts Ruto's 2% Mt Kenya Vote - A Realistic Look at Political Posturing

WARNING: Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua's pronouncements on President Ruto's 2027 prospects in Mt Kenya paint a picture of deep political division and strategic maneuvering, with little regard for the actual electorate.

Kenya's political theatre is a predictable soap opera, and Rigathi Gachagua is just hitting his marks. He's out here, claiming President Ruto will barely scrape a 2% vote share in Mt Kenya come 2027. According to him, the region has "resolved" to vote as a bloc, and the real contest isn't between candidates, but between Ruto and the "Mt Kenya electorate." It's a nice narrative, especially if you're trying to rally your own troops, but let's be real, these numbers are pure projection, designed to make Gachagua look like the kingmaker he clearly thinks he is. The Ol Kalou by-election, he insists, is proof. Proof of what? That local dynamics are complicated, and a single by-election doesn't dictate national destiny.

Gachagua's argument hinges on a united Mt Kenya, a supposedly monolithic voting bloc that will reject anyone "on Ruto's side." He’s throwing around figures like 10% for any leader aligned with the president, and a measly 2% for Ruto himself. This is less analysis and more wishful thinking, a bid to solidify his own relevance by drawing a hard line in the sand. He’s even dismissing the East/West Mt Kenya narrative as a ploy to divide and conquer. It’s the same old song and dance: unity for power, division for control. It’s a strategic play, sure, but don't mistake it for genuine insight into the minds of voters, who often have more pressing concerns than regional solidarity dictated by politicians. We've seen how quickly alliances shift, a bit like the volatile global markets we discussed in [The Hollow Victory of Sub-100 Dollar Oil](/posts/the-hollow-victory-of-sub-100-dollar-oil).

Then there's the predictable elevation of Gachagua himself, with Governor Badilisha conveniently predicting his presidential ambitions. And the cherry on top? Murang'a Governor Irungu Kang'ata joining Gachagua's Democracy for the Citizens Party. It’s all about building a coalition, a movement, a supposed "national footprint." This is standard political maneuvering, trying to create an impression of momentum where there might be just a lot of hot air. They’re positioning for 2027, no doubt, but the electorate isn’t a herd of sheep waiting to be told which pasture to graze in.

Ultimately, Gachagua is playing the game. He's issuing dire predictions and forming pacts, all while positioning himself as the sole protector of Mt Kenya's interests. It’s a cynical display, but then again, what else is new in Kenyan politics? These pronouncements are less about accurate forecasting and more about influencing perception and consolidating power. The real test will be at the ballot box, not in the pronouncements from a politician’s residence.
