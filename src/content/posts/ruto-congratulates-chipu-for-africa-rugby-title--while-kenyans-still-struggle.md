So, President Ruto has decided to pat himself on the back by congratulating the Chipu team for their little rugby excursion to Uganda. Apparently, they bagged some trophy, the Barthés Trophy, for the fourth time. Good for them, I guess. It’s always nice when a group of young fellas can achieve something, especially when it makes the head of state look good. He’s all smiles and praise, talking about "growing strength" and "potential." Bless his heart.

Meanwhile, back in Nairobi, and frankly, everywhere else, people are more concerned with the cost of fuel and whether they can afford a decent meal. You see, while Chipu was busy conquering Africa on the rugby pitch, the rest of us were navigating the daily grind, trying to make ends meet. It’s a bit like celebrating a fancy dinner when the pantry is bare. This government seems to have a knack for celebrating the spectacular while the mundane - the everyday struggles - fester. It reminds me of how [Mbadi’s Statistical Fairytales Cannot Fill Empty Stomachs](/posts/mbadi-s-statistical-fairytales-cannot-fill-empty-stomachs) - all sound and fury, signifying very little for the common mwananchi.

Ruto even promised government backing for their trip to the World Rugby Junior Trophy. Wonderful. So, while the national budget is stretched thinner than a politician’s promise, we’re going to fund a trip for a rugby team. Let’s hope they don’t return with more accolades than they do actual tangible benefits for the country. Because right now, what we need is more than just symbolic victories. We need practical solutions to soaring inflation and a bit of honesty about where the country's resources are actually going.

The Chipu team apparently had minimal preparation, just nine training sessions. Yet, they managed to win. That’s commendable, in a way. It’s the kind of resilience that’s born out of necessity, something most Kenyans understand all too well. But let’s not pretend this is some grand testament to the government's investment in sports. It’s more likely a testament to the sheer grit of these young athletes, pushing through despite the odds. Let's just hope their global trophy hunt doesn't distract too much from the pressing issues that are causing genuine hardship.
