Ruto's Vision 2060: The Constitution Says We Must 'Listen' - We'll See How Long That Lasts.

Another grand plan, another round of consultation theatre. The President is busy telling us how this new Vision 2060 will be different, all thanks to the "new constitution" that *forces* them to listen to us. They held a big shindig at KICC with 5,000 delegates, all to kick off the "national conversation" for a plan that supposedly lasts until 2060. Because, you know, the last one worked so well.

Ruto himself admitted that Vision 2030 was a bit of a flop because "many people do not understand, do not relate to" it. Shocking, right? Apparently, this time it's "by the people, not by government, not by bureaucrats." He even promised "all resources" to make it "people-centred." We’ve heard this song before, usually right before the money disappears into thin air, much like that [Sh1.5bn theft from a Kenya project](/posts/un-agency-finally-audits-sh1-5bn-theft-from-kenya-project---what-a-surprise).

Kisumu Governor Anyang’ Nyong’o, who's heading the drafting committee, chimed in, saying Vision 2030 "served its purpose" but some targets were "difficult to achieve." Translation: it was a nice idea that didn't quite pan out. He’s hoping this Vision 2060 will be a roadmap that sticks, even when the next crop of politicians comes along, promising the moon and delivering dust. And they want an economy that can "absorb global disruptions" - good luck with that in this climate.

Meanwhile, Deputy President Kindiki was busy parading statistics about education and healthcare since 2022. Funding up, teachers hired, classrooms built, and millions more covered by medical insurance. Impressive numbers, if you believe them. He’s warning against "policy disruption" and wants "national interests" to guide everything. Sounds noble, but we’ll see if it translates into anything other than more talk.

So, the plan is to take this Vision 2060 document to all 47 counties, gather "views," and then shove it through Parliament. After presidential assent, it’ll be ready for implementation. We’ve seen this movie before. The "people-centred" rhetoric is usually just a warm-up act for the same old political song and dance. Let’s just hope this time the script is slightly less predictable, though I’m not holding my breath.







President William Ruto is all fired up about Kenya’s new constitution, claiming it’s the magic ingredient for shaping Vision 2060 and that public participation is now mandatory. He was at the KICC on Wednesday, August 12, 2026, for the grand opening of the "national conversation" on this new development plan, Beyond Vision 2030. Over 5,000 delegates from all 47 counties showed up for this kickoff. Ruto pointed out that Vision 2030 was drafted *before* the current constitution, and now, with a document that *demands* consultation, things are bound to be different.

He was quite blunt about why Vision 2030 didn’t quite land with everyone: "The reason why you find in some cases many people do not understand, do not relate to Vision 2030 was because there was no public participation to the extent that we know public participation has provided for in the constitution at the moment." He’s positioning this as a chance to create a plan "by the people, not by government, not by bureaucrats." And of course, the government is ready to throw "all resources" at this "truly people-centred, citizen-driven, future-looking" exercise.

Kisumu Governor Anyang’ Nyong’o, who’s chairing the Vision 2060 drafting committee, admitted that Vision 2030 had "largely served its purpose," though hitting some targets was a struggle. He's hoping this new roadmap will be more robust, guiding development until 2060 and staying consistent across political leadership changes. Plus, he wants an economy that can handle global shocks without derailing everything. Sounds like a tall order for a country still struggling to make ends meet.

Deputy President Kithure Kindiki decided to impress everyone with his report card since 2022, boasting about increased funding for education, from Ksh500 billion to Ksh784 billion, and university funding nearly doubling. He also mentioned recruiting 100,000 teachers and expanding medical insurance coverage significantly. He warned against changing policies too often and stressed that national interests should lead the planning. It's a nice narrative, but we’ll see how many of these grand pronouncements actually translate into tangible improvements on the ground.

This whole KICC event is just the first step. The proposed Vision 2060 will be paraded across all 47 counties to gather more "views" before being sent to Parliament. After the usual approvals and presidential assent, it will then be implemented. Given how previous development plans have fared, especially concerning transparency and resource management, one can't help but wonder if this is just another exercise in political theater. We’ve seen how easily funds can go missing, much like in that [Sh1.5bn theft from a Kenya project](/posts/un-agency-finally-audits-sh1-5bn-theft-from-kenya-project---what-a-surprise), so let's hope the oversight this time is more than just a catchy slogan.
