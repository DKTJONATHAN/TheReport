KICTANet "Trains" 100 Women to Spread Digital Skills-And Expectations

WARNING: Another program promising to bridge the digital divide, this time with 100 women acting as "multipliers." Let's see how many actually multiply anything beyond their own phone bills.

"Empowering women with technology" sounds nice, but is it just another way to say "here's a phone, now figure it out"?

POLITICS
# KICTANet Trains Women, Hopes for Digital Magic Trick Across Kenya

So, KICTANet is doling out digital lessons to 100 women in Nairobi. The big idea is for these women to go back to their villages and teach other women how to, I don't know, tweet? Or perhaps, finally, figure out how to use M-Pesa for more than just sending airtime to their cousin. It's all under the "SheConnects EmpowerHer Digital Accelerator" banner, which sounds like a lot of effort for what will likely be minimal results.

Dr. Grace Githaiga, the CEO, is apparently urging these trainers to be "multipliers of digital knowledge." Right. Because that's how knowledge spreads in Kenya - through the generosity of a handful of trained individuals. Meanwhile, the urban-rural internet access gap remains a gaping hole, with 56 percent of city folk online versus a dismal 25 percent out in the sticks. So, yeah, 100 women are going to fix that. Good luck with that.

The program also wrings its hands about women micro-entrepreneurs who apparently use their smartphones for "personal communication" instead of, you know, running a business. Shocking, I know. The report blames "limited digital skills and confidence." Or maybe, just maybe, they're too busy trying to make ends meet to bother with fancy apps while their businesses teeter on the edge. This whole thing feels a bit like trying to bail out a sinking ship with a teacup, especially when you consider how often these "initiatives" mirror the grand pronouncements we heard about the "Billionaire Boys Club and the Desperate Housewives of Government."

After this grand workshop, each of these 100 women is expected to train 50 others, potentially reaching a whopping 5,000 beneficiaries. And the whole EmpowerHer program aims for a massive 15,000 women across Nairobi, Tana River, and Nyandarua. They even suggested using "Pochi la Biashara" for financial management, a tool that’s as basic as a handshake. It's all supposed to boost entrepreneurship and financial management. But let's be real, how much genuine change can you expect when the foundation is already so shaky? It's a nice story, but don't hold your breath for a digital revolution.
