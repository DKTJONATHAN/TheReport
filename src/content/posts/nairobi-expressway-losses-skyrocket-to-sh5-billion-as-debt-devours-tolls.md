So, the grand Nairobi Expressway, all shimmery and new, is actually a Sh5 billion hole in our pockets. Surprise, surprise. The Chinese, bless their ambitious hearts, thought they'd be rolling in Sh107 billion over 27 years. Now? They're staring at a Sh5 billion loss. Turns out, building a double-decker road that costs more than my ancestral village is probably not a recipe for immediate profit, especially when the debt collectors are circling like vultures over a wilting carcass.

It's funny, isn't it? They brag about Sh9.62 billion in revenue, but then blithely admit Sh14.73 billion in operating costs. That's like me saying I earned Sh10,000 but spent Sh15,000 - then acting shocked when my bank account is empty. And the traffic? Oh yes, vehicle traffic is up. More cars means more money, right? Wrong. The finance costs from the loans used to slap this thing together are apparently insatiable, like a Nairobi landlord demanding rent during a drought.

The Treasury, in its infinite wisdom, is spinning this as a "success" because it eases traffic. Yes, it eases traffic for those who can afford it, while the rest of us are stuck in the same old jams, paying taxes that probably subsidise this whole operation anyway. And let's not forget, this whole charade is supposed to be a Public-Private Partnership, which is just a fancy way of saying we're taking on the risk while they rake in the potential rewards. It’s almost as if we haven't learned anything from past infrastructure fiascos; remember the [fuel shortage](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/79242-fuel-shortage-kenyans-need-to-know-the-truth)? This feels like a similar pattern of grand promises and murky realities.

They even had the audacity to keep the toll charges the same, even when our shilling decided to do a little jig and strengthen against the dollar. Apparently, the law is there to "shield the Chinese investors from losses." So, our money is good for paying them, but not good enough to get a discount when things improve? This is Nairobi, darling. We expect our infrastructure to be expensive and our wallets to be empty.

And it's not just one shiny white elephant. Oh no. We're already building the next one, the Rironi-Nakuru-Mau Summit highway, also a PPP, also with Chinese involvement. Because clearly, the first attempt at this "innovative" funding model was such a roaring success that we need to do it again, and again. They're even bringing in NSSF, which is essentially our pension money, to fund more of these money pits. What could possibly go wrong?

So, while the politicians pat themselves on the back for attracting private investment, the reality is that we're building expensive roads that are costing us a fortune, not just in construction, but in ongoing debt. The promise was to reduce borrowing, but it seems we're just shifting the burden, repackaging it, and calling it progress. Let's just hope this expressway doesn't become a permanent monument to our financial mismanagement.
