So, China says they shipped over Sh1.3 trillion worth of goodies to us in 2025, but our own Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) only managed to log a measly Sh672 billion. That's a cool Sh629 billion that's just… poof! Gone. Vanished into thin air, or more likely, into someone's very deep pockets. They call it a "puzzling mismatch." I call it the usual Nairobi hustle, just on a global scale.

The experts, bless their innocent hearts, are insisting this is "unusual." Unusual? My dear scholars, this is the standard operating procedure. It screams under-declaration, trade invoicing shenanigans, and all the other creative ways some people find to keep the taxman from getting his fair share. This isn't a one-off; it's a persistent pattern. We're talking Sh2.76 trillion over five years that China claims it sent our way, and Kenya's books conveniently forgot about. It makes you wonder if the KRA is looking at the right spreadsheets, or if they're just playing blind man's buff with our national treasure.

And let's not forget the timing. Just when the Kenyan Shilling was doing its usual dramatic nosedive against major currencies in 2023, the gap widened to a staggering Sh733 billion. Now it's back with a vengeance at Sh629 billion. It’s almost like clockwork, isn't it? This isn't just about statistics; it's about billions in potential revenue that could be funding hospitals, roads, or, let's be real, lining more pockets. It feels like the kind of situation that would make even the most hardened economist sigh, perhaps reminding them of the futility of trying to impose order on economic chaos, much like the debates around central bank mandates.

The Treasury is now talking about working with "counterpart agencies" and focusing on "high-risk imports." Oh, you don't say! Perhaps they could start by looking at the Sh629 billion that never made it into their records in the first place. Trade mis-invoicing, they call it. Manipulating prices and quantities to spirit money across borders. For goods like electronics, where the valuation is already a murky affair, it's a goldmine for those who know how to play the game. It’s a clear sign that despite all the talk of progress, the old ways of doing business - the corrupt ones - are still very much alive and kicking.

The KRA, predictably, didn't respond to questions. Because why bother when you can just keep collecting what little trickles in, while the real money does a disappearing act? Customs taxes are a massive revenue stream for them, contributing a hefty 36 percent of all collections in nine months. Yet, they seem to be missing nearly half of what should be coming in from our biggest trading partner. It’s a slap in the face to every honest taxpayer who diligently pays their dues.

So here we are, staring at a Sh629 billion hole in our national accounts, courtesy of our friends in China and our own lax systems. While the Treasury promises future action and international cooperation, the reality on the ground is that for years, billions have been vanishing. It’s a stark reminder that in Nairobi, the only thing more consistent than the traffic jams is the art of making money disappear when no one's looking too closely.
