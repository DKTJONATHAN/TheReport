MOMBASA ASSEMBLY CHAOS: CIVIC RIGHTS DEBATE STALLS AMIDST POLICE MESS

WARNING: This story details a chaotic scene at the Mombasa County Assembly, exposing the usual Kenyan drama over rights, police ineptitude, and media safety.

Kenyan politics. Always a beautiful mess. So, the Mombasa County Assembly decided to throw a spectacular tantrum on International Youth Day. Apparently, some youngsters and civil society types wanted a county youth policy - radical, I know. Instead of a polite handshake and a memo, it turned into a free-for-all. People got roughed up, including a Nation Media photojournalist. Just another Tuesday in this country, I guess.

The "human rights defenders" are crying foul, demanding arrests. Hussein Khalid from Vocal Africa is questioning the police for… wait for it… not stopping the chaos. Shocking. The Mombasa Press Club is also wailing about the assault on the journalist. Meanwhile, the Assembly, in its infinite wisdom, claims the whole thing was a peaceful gathering of masked thugs who blocked access and disrupted "normal House business." Because obviously, asking for a youth policy is a direct attack on parliamentary decorum. It’s like they want to pretend they’re running Buckingham Palace, not a local government office.

The Coast Regional Police Command is now launching a "multi-agency investigation." Because when things go wrong, the best solution is always to form another committee. They're also telling the Mombasa Police Command to get these young people back to the Assembly to present their petition, but only after consulting with the Assembly leadership. Translation: don't bother them too much, just make it look like you tried. This whole "protected area" and "notification requirements" nonsense is just another excuse to keep the public at arm's length. It’s like they’re running a secret society, not a public institution. Remember when media was actually allowed to do its job without getting their equipment smashed? This whole circus reminds me of how, even when there’s real talent, sometimes it’s just another [Apple Music industry plant](https://www.nation.africa/posts/shoday-s-africa-rising-moment--real-talent-or-just-another-apple-music-industry-plant).

And let's not forget the tired old script about young people in politics. Human rights groups are already warning about unemployed youth being "used" to disrupt things, especially with the 2027 elections looming. It’s the same old song and dance: blame the youth, blame the activists, and blame the journalists. The real issue - a youth policy that might actually help the county’s young population - gets buried under a mountain of finger-pointing and procedural debates. This is Kenya, after all. We’re great at talking about problems and even better at avoiding solutions.
