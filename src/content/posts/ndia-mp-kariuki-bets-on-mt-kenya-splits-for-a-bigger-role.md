Ndia MP Kariuki Positions Himself as the 'Third Force' in Mt Kenya - More Like a Blip on the Radar

Another one thinks they can play chess with the country's big boys. Ndia MP George Kariuki, fresh from serving a decade in his constituency - which apparently qualifies him for national greatness - is now opening his home in Kirinyaga for “consultations.” He claims he’s looking to be the new centre of power in Mt Kenya, conveniently forgetting that the real power brokers are too busy lining their pockets to notice his little get-togethers. He’s throwing shade at Rigathi Gachagua and Kithure Kindiki, which is rich coming from a man who’s only just starting to think beyond his county lines.

Kariuki’s big plan involves a three-month shindig at his Gacharu residence, where he’ll supposedly ponder his political future and that of the entire Mt Kenya region. He’s a loyal soldier for President Ruto, of course, so any “declaration” will likely just be another pronouncement of support for the current regime, perhaps with a sniff at a bigger chair. He’s even talking about the deputy presidency - the man’s ambition is clearly exceeding his grasp, much like some might find the Ministry of Health’s approach to border health issues.

The MP’s pronouncements are predictably met with “speculation” that he might be Ruto’s new pointman in Mt Kenya West. This is the usual song and dance: when one faction in Mt Kenya starts to fray, someone else pops up claiming to be the glue. Kariuki’s dismissal of Gachagua and Kindiki as incapable leaders is a classic tactic to elevate himself, but let’s be real, he’s just another politician trying to ride the waves of division. He claims to want a united Mt Kenya, but his actions scream "look at me, I’m the solution!"

He’s against dividing Mt Kenya into East and West blocs, preaching unity like it’s a novel concept. “We want one region, one people and one nation,” he declares, as if the rest of us haven't heard this platitude a thousand times. The truth is, these regional power plays are less about unity and more about who gets the biggest slice of the national pie. Kariuki's "listening to the people" routine is just a preamble to his own self-serving agenda, a performance as predictable as a DCI raid that "rescues" women in Kiserian and Rongai.

Ultimately, Kariuki’s bid is a desperate attempt to carve out relevance in a political landscape already crowded with ego and opportunism. His consultations are likely to be filled with sycophants echoing his pronouncements, reinforcing his self-importance. The real decisions will be made elsewhere, in boardrooms and backrooms, far from the public eye and the staged photo-ops at his home. He might be a "third force" for a fleeting moment, but don’t hold your breath for him to actually change the game.

Kenyan politics, at its core, is a constant cycle of these ambitious figures emerging, making noise, and then fading back into obscurity when the real players decide their moment has passed. Kariuki is just the latest in a long line of politicians hoping to catch a lightning bolt of opportunity. It’s a cynical game, and one he’s clearly trying to learn, but the real masters of this particular art are already miles ahead, probably laughing all the way to the bank.







Ndia MP George Kariuki is playing the same old game, trying to position himself as the next big thing in Mt Kenya politics. He’s opening his doors at his Gacharu home in Kirinyaga for what he calls “consultations,” which is code for trying to sniff out and exploit the obvious cracks between Rigathi Gachagua and Kithure Kindiki. The man’s been an MP for a decade, apparently now believing he's ready to steer the entire region, if not the nation.

Kariuki, a staunch supporter of President Ruto, plans to host delegations for three months before dropping his “declaration” on his political future. He’s already throwing shade at Gachagua and Kindiki, saying neither has what it takes to run Mt Kenya. It’s a classic move: tear down the existing figures to try and build yourself up. He’s even musing about a national role, perhaps even the deputy presidency, a leap of faith that shows his ambition might be outrunning his current standing.

His ambition, he admits, has outgrown his initial thoughts of being Kirinyaga governor. Now he’s aiming higher, fueled by the perceived weaknesses of the current regional leaders. He claims Gachagua and Kindiki can't manage the region's affairs, a convenient narrative for someone trying to insert themselves into the power vacuum. This whole drama of regional leadership is as predictable as the Ministry of Health’s attempts to control diseases at our borders.

Kariuki’s opposition to dividing Mt Kenya into East and West blocs is also just rhetoric. He talks about unity - "one region, one people and one nation" - but his real goal is to be the one at the negotiating table, representing a unified (and thus stronger) Mt Kenya, with himself at the helm. He wants the region to bargain from a position of strength, which is just another way of saying he wants to be the one calling the shots.

He claims he needs to listen to the people before deciding, a standard political manoeuvre. But in Nairobi, we know these "consultations" are often just staged events to generate headlines and convince the powerful that a new contender has emerged. It’s a calculated performance, no different from the DCI’s dramatic "rescues" that often lead to more questions than answers.

Ultimately, Kariuki is just another politician trying to capitalize on instability. His bid for relevance is built on the misfortunes of others, a common theme in Kenyan politics where ambition often trumps principle. The real question is whether anyone outside his immediate circle is actually listening, or if this is just another temporary blip before the next power play unfolds.
