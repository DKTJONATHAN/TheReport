Well, well, well. It appears the esteemed DCI Amin has confirmed what we already suspected in this city: the very people meant to keep us safe are, in fact, the ones pulling the trigger. Dr. Victoria Mutiso’s life snuffed out in Upper Hill, and guess who’s the culprit? A fellow police officer. Amin himself calls it a “betrayal of the uniform.” You don’t say.

And the cherry on top? This trigger-happy officer has been arrested. As if that’s supposed to bring Dr. Mutiso back or make any of us feel any safer. This is just another episode in the endless drama of Nairobi’s finest deciding their badge is a license to kill, not protect. It’s the same old story, just a different victim.

This whole affair is a stark reminder of how fragile things are in this concrete jungle. We’re supposed to trust these guys, but then you hear about things like this, and you start to wonder who’s really looking out for you. It makes you think about all the other times things probably got swept under the rug.

It's a good thing some journalists are still trying to dig into these messy situations, even if the system itself is rotten. Shows like AM Live and podcasts that dive into the darker corners of our society, like maybe even something that unpacks the messiest of issues on Gumzo La Sato, are probably the only real hope we have for some semblance of truth.

This constant cycle of crime, corruption, and then the inevitable “investigation” is exhausting. It’s like watching a broken record play on repeat. We see these shows, we hear these pronouncements, but the reality on the ground remains the same. The "betrayal" isn't just to the uniform; it's a betrayal of every single citizen who pays taxes and expects a modicum of safety.

And let's not forget the bigger picture. When those in power, those meant to uphold justice, become the perpetrators, it breeds an even deeper sense of disillusionment. It makes you wonder if there's any point in trying to be a law-abiding citizen when the very foundation of law and order is crumbling from within. It’s a betrayal of us all, really.
