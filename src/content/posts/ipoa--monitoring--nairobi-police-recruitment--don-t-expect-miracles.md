IPOA "Monitoring" Police Recruitment: Nairobi Knows How This Goes

WARNING: IPOA says it will "monitor" the upcoming police recruitment. Don't hold your breath for miracles. They'll take complaints, they say. From November 27th, 2026, until January 7th, 2027. Toll-free number 1559. Regional offices. All very official. Prepare for the usual song and dance.

This whole exercise, supposedly guarding "public interest in policing," is just a show. They'll send their "monitors," take notes, and then what? A report. A report detailing "observations" and "concerns." As if the system doesn't already have its own internal, well oiled mechanism for "concerns" - the kind that keep the right people in and the rest out. Just like how the [Energy Noose Tightens as Global Governance Collapses](/posts/the-energy-noose-tightens-as-global-governance-collapses), things will move along their predetermined track.

They boast about their core values: independence, accountability, professionalism. Cute. In Nairobi, those words are just decorations on a very old, very familiar building. People will report irregularities, yes. They always do. And IPOA will collect these reports, bottle them up, and present them neatly bound. The real question is, who actually reads these reports after the ink dries? And more importantly, who acts on them?

The recruitment for Constables on December 10th, 2026, and Cadets on January 7th, 2027, will proceed. IPOA urges the public to use their channels. It’s a great idea, in theory. A wonderful, democratic ideal. But when the grease is already in the wheels and the decisions are made long before any applicant steps onto a parade ground, what difference does a hotline make?

They’ll publish a report, they promise, with recommendations. We’ll see. More likely, we’ll see the usual faces in uniform, a few token changes, and then the cycle repeats. This is less about oversight and more about optics. A managed narrative to assure the public that someone, somewhere, is watching. Don't be fooled.

The only thing this "monitoring" guarantees is more paperwork for IPOA and a brief moment of hope for desperate applicants. After that, it's back to business as usual in this city, where reality bites harder than any official statement.
