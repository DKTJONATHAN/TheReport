KEPSA Hunts Four Circular Economy Trainers: More Hot Air for MSMEs?

Looks like KEPSA, those fancy folks who think they know what's best for us, have decided it's time to throw some money at "circular economy trainers." Four of them, mind you, to supposedly help MSMEs, communities, and the informal sector across this "great nation." Funded by the European Union, because why else would anyone bother with these feel-good projects? Apparently, these trainers are meant to preach about plastic, organic, and textile waste. As if we don't already know our bins are overflowing.

The whole setup, dubbed "Tujenge Pamoja," sounds like another one of those projects that generates a lot of paperwork and not much actual change. They want trainers who can deliver "gender-responsive" training using an "approved curriculum." So, not only will you be lectured on recycling, but it will also come with a side of "social inclusion." Because apparently, teaching someone how to sort their garbage is now a gender issue. And don't forget the 80 contact hours over five-hour sessions. That's a lot of sitting around listening to someone tell you what you already know, probably with a PowerPoint presentation that looks suspiciously like it was made in 2010.

The geographical spread is predictable: Nairobi, Kiambu, Nakuru, Machakos, Makueni, Mombasa, Kilifi, and Kwale. Basically, the places where you'll find some of the most active MSMEs and informal sectors, and also where the most waste is generated. They’re looking for degreed individuals with "demonstrated experience." Translation: people who can talk the talk about circular economy and gender. Skills in "participatory, experiential and problem-based learning" are a bonus, which probably means they want someone who can make a boring lecture sound like a team-building exercise. Much like how we've seen leaders promising economic relief while simultaneously racking up debt; it's all about the presentation, isn't it? This reminds me of [Mandago's Sh1.1 Billion Loan Saga: 'Eat Now, Pay Never' For Taxpayers](/posts/mandago-s-sh1-1-billion-loan-saga---eat-now--pay-never--for-taxpayers).

The application deadline is September 22, 2026. Plenty of time for people to polish their CVs with buzzwords about "enterprise development" and "waste-to-value approaches." Experience with "informal-sector actors, waste workers or community enterprises" is a plus. So, they want someone who has actually been in the trenches, but who can also articulate it in a way that satisfies the EU's reporting requirements. And of course, only shortlisted applicants will be contacted. Typical. Don't expect a phone call if you’re just a regular Kenyan trying to make a living; this is for the "qualified" ones.

This whole initiative is a collaboration, with Hivos, USIU-Africa, SOMO, and KCB Foundation all chipping in. More acronyms, more partnerships, more talk. The goal? To boost MSMEs. Let's see how much of this EU funding actually trickles down to the ground and doesn't just get swallowed up in administrative costs and fancy workshops. I’ll be here, watching, with my cynicism fully charged.
