SPEED, SECRECY AND DIVISION: KENYA'S WEEK FROM OYO TO MILIMANI

President Ruto's administration juggles foreign diplomacy and domestic development, but court rulings and a fractured opposition reveal the cracks ahead of 2027.

Belinda Achieng: Nairobi's political theatre is as predictable as a matatu fare hike, just with more suits and less legroom. This past week was a masterclass in Kenyan absurdity. While Ruto was jetting off to Congo to mend fences over some business deal - because what else matters when billions of shillings are at stake - our courts were busy reminding him that even presidents aren't above the law. Meanwhile, the so-called opposition is busy tripping over its own feet, a clearer sign of impending doom than any IEBC audit.

The DRC drama, supposedly about repairing ties, reeked of economic desperation. Nairobi and Kinshasa had a spat because some Congolese bigwigs decided to host a party in Kenya. Now, Ruto’s flying off to kiss and make up, all to protect the billions Kenyan banks have sunk into that chaotic country. It's less about regional peace and more about protecting the bottom line. And after all that, he lands back in Western Kenya to throw farmers a few million bob. It’s a classic move: show the people you care, while the real deals happen behind closed doors, far from public scrutiny.

Then, the Milimani courts stepped in, a rare moment of sanity in this madness. Judge Aburili, bless her, decided that creating government departments out of thin air, without asking anyone or following the rules, was unconstitutional. Six months to fix it? More like six months for the administration to figure out how to bypass the law more subtly. And the cherry on top? An agreement with the Americans for an Ebola facility at Laikipia Air Base, quashed because it was secret. Transparency? Public participation? These are just funny jokes in Kenyan politics, like [Atwoli blaming Uhuru for Raila’s 2022 loss](/posts/atwoli-blames-uhuru-for-raila-s-2022-loss--says-he-never-delivered-ksh-400m-for-agents).

While the government is busy with its speed and secrecy, the opposition is a disaster zone of competing ambitions. Jeremiah Kioni’s exit from Jubilee? Just another nail in the coffin of a party that’s been dead on arrival for ages. Rigathi Gachagua is out in Eastleigh yelling at regional leaders and demanding an IEBC audit - probably because he's already eyeing the next election. And in the US, Senator Sifuna is declaring his presidential bid. It’s a free-for-all, with everyone out for themselves, proving that unity is just a word they throw around when they need votes.

So, what do we have? A president chasing deals and goodwill, a judiciary trying (and often failing) to keep him in check, and an opposition more interested in their own power plays than presenting a united front. With elections still a ways off, it’s clear that the real contest isn't about policies or promises, but about who can best navigate the murky waters of Kenyan politics, where speed, secrecy, and division are the winning strategies.
