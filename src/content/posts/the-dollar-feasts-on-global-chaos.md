In this landscape, the plight of Indian professionals under the threat of H-1B restrictions is a predictable byproduct of American protectionism. People are treated as volatile assets, useful only until the political climate shifts toward isolationism. The US indictment of Adani further complicates the narrative, proving that even the most powerful entities in emerging markets are vulnerable to the extraterritorial reach of Western law the moment they threaten the perceived integrity of global fundraising channels.

China’s strategic bond sale in Saudi Arabia and Huawei’s launch of a proprietary operating system represent a fractured world. These are not just business moves; they are the early tremors of a financial decoupling that promises more instability, not less. Much like the desperate grasp for relevance seen in Marvel's decades long movie planning, these global powers are clinging to aging strategies in a world that is rapidly losing patience with the old guard.

The reality of the current market is one of managed decline and strategic cannibalism. Whether it is Reliance and L&T going on acquisition sprees to mask internal stagnation or the relentless push for momentum stocks, the goal is simply to stay ahead of the inevitable correction. In a world where oil is the blood and the dollar is the heart, the rest of the globe is merely the body waiting for the next tremor.
