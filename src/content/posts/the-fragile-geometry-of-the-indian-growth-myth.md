The looming shadow of Washington presents a far greater threat than any local inflationary pressure. With the threat of universal tariffs and a renewed assault on the H-1B visa program, India's primary export-its human capital and service expertise-is facing a structural ceiling. You cannot engineer a miracle when your largest customer is actively building a fortress. The recent legal entanglements involving the Adani Group further illustrate that the bridge between Indian corporate giants and international financing is built on shaky regulatory ground, casting a pall over the nation's ability to fund its own ambitions.

Beneath the headline figures, the systemic rot of food-price volatility and stagnant real wages continues to hollow out the middle class. This is not resilience; it is a temporary equilibrium sustained by heavy state intervention and the remnants of post-pandemic momentum. As explored in /posts/the-global-grindstone-crushes-emerging-market-illusions, the cycle of hype followed by a brutal encounter with reality remains the standard for the developing world. India is not escaping the grindstone; it is simply the next piece of grit to be processed by a global system that rewards efficiency over hope.
