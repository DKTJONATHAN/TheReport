THE NAIROBI COPS WANT MORE RECRUITS ON DEC 10: ANOTHER JOB FOR THE HOPELESS?
WARNING: Another police recruitment drive. Expect long queues, broken dreams, and the usual Nairobi chaos. Don't fall for the "free" scam.
Nairobi police hopefuls line up for recruitment.

So, the National Police Service (NPS) is looking for more bodies to fill their ranks. December 10, 2026, is the big day. Apparently, they're opening up 423 centres across the country. For those still clinging to the idea of a stable job and a uniform, get your D+ in KCSE ready, along with your birth certificate and a prayer. Age limit is 18 to 28, and you better be able to stand up straight - 5 foot 8 for the men, 5 foot 3 for the women. Don't tell me you haven't heard about this before; it's the same old song and dance.

They’re calling it a "nationwide recruitment exercise," which in Nairobi usually translates to a massive scramble at Kasarani or Kamukunji. Don't forget your valid ID and a certificate of good conduct, assuming you haven't already crossed paths with the DCI in a less than favourable way - remember those [15 suspected gang members arrested along a busy highway](/posts/dci-arrests-15-suspected-gang-members-along-busy-highway)? They're also eyeing those fresh out of the National Youth Service, as long as their discharge certificate says "GOOD". Because, you know, a positive attitude is key.

The NPS is shouting from the rooftops that this whole thing is "free and open to all eligible candidates." Which, of course, is code for "if anyone asks you for money, they're a criminal." They've even provided a hotline for reporting "malpractices," because we all know how well reporting things usually works out. Just picture it: a desperate applicant, handing over their last few shillings to some "recruiter," only to be left with nothing but a worthless piece of paper and a lighter wallet. Classic Nairobi.

And let's not forget the medical checks. Physically and medically fit, they say. Minimum height. No criminal record. And for the ladies, no baby bumps allowed. Because a pregnant officer is apparently a security risk. It's all about appearances, isn't it? They want the shiny, efficient machine, not the messy reality of life.

So, if you're feeling particularly ambitious, or just plain desperate, mark your calendars. Download those forms from their websites or brave a Huduma Centre. Just don't say I didn't warn you. Another batch of hopefuls, lining up to serve a system that probably won't serve them back. At least they'll have a story to tell when they inevitably get disillusioned, or worse.

This recruitment drive, they claim, is about "strengthening the workforce and enhancing security." We’ll see. More likely, it's about filling the gaps with the next wave of people willing to put up with the abuse and the low pay for the sake of a uniform. Good luck to them. They'll need it.
