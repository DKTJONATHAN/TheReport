Ruto's "Clear Plan" for Kenya: More Hot Air Than Hot Progress

Those who believed President Ruto when he promised the moon and stars in 2022 are in for a rude awakening. He’s out there in Nandi Hills, flapping his gums about keeping promises and shaming competitors, as if we haven't heard this song and dance before. You know, the one about the "clear plan" and "transformation"? It sounds lovely on paper, but let's be real, Nairobi doesn’t run on good intentions and fancy manifestos. It runs on cash, and right now, most of us are just trying to keep the lights on.

He's boasting about development, throwing around figures for roads and housing. KSh25 billion for roads, KSh18 billion for housing and markets. Sounds impressive, doesn't it? But how much of that actually trickles down to the mama mboga in Kawangware or the boda boda rider in Kibra? We've seen these grand announcements before, and then we see the same potholes, the same informal settlements. The Koitaleel Samoei University College project is apparently 90% complete - great for Nandi, but what about the rest of us? Are we just supposed to be satisfied with the crumbs?

And then there’s this SHA healthcare registration in Nandi. 630,000 people. Commitment to making healthcare accessible and affordable, he says. We heard similar promises during that [Ruto's Health Town Hall: A System That Works, But For Whom? The Usual Song and Dance](/posts/ruto-s-health-town-hall--a-system-that-works--but-for-whom--the-usual-song-and-dance). The system might *look* like it works on paper, but for the average Kenyan, the reality is often a lot more complicated and expensive. Let's hope this isn't just another case of impressive statistics that don't translate into tangible relief.

The President also had a go at "hate and division," saying Kenya wants progress. True, we do. We want progress that actually improves our lives, not just the lives of those already swimming in privilege. We want development that feels real, not just a string of photo ops in far-flung counties. Judging leaders by their plans and track record is a good idea, but our track record so far in this republic is a mixed bag, and let's not even get started on the [price of clout](/posts/the-price-of-clout--from-tiktok-flexing-to-a-dci-cell) these days.

So, while Ruto might have a "clear plan," the clarity seems to be more for him and his inner circle than for the everyday Kenyan struggling to make ends meet. We've been promised transformation before, and while some things might have moved, the fundamental issues remain. Let's see if this time, the promises actually manifest into something more than just political rhetoric and impressive ground-breaking ceremonies.

Ruto's promises are a clear plan for Kenya, according to him. We’ll see if his competitors are the only ones shamed.
