THE WORLD HAS CHANGED, SO AFRICAN COUNTRIES MUST ACTUALLY THINK ABOUT HOW TO GROW THEIR ECONOMIES FOR ONCE











So, the Strait of Hormuz decides to throw a tantrum and suddenly Kenya, Ethiopia, Uganda - all of us - are feeling the pinch. Fuel prices through the roof, and our economies, which are basically built on importing the Middle East's petrol, are doing a spectacular impression of a dying gazelle. This whole "structural transformation" nonsense the eggheads keep pushing? It's not just derailed, it's looking like a joke. Trying to move people from subsistence farming to actual jobs feels like planning a beach holiday in the Sahara.

Remember how Japan and South Korea supposedly got rich? Manufacturing, exports, cheap labour. We never even got a proper seat at that buffet, and now the whole global system is a mess of geopolitical games, supply chains that snap like dry twigs, and AI ready to automate the last few low-skill jobs. So, while the economists are busy writing papers about how trade is weaponised (shocking, I know), our real problem is that the old playbook is as useful as a chocolate teapot.

The biggest joke is that manufacturing can't even absorb workers like it used to. Automation is the new boss, and it doesn't need millions of untrained hands. By 2030, we'll have half of all new global workers - about 15 million young Kenyans, Tanzanians, Nigerians a year - looking for something to do. Relying on factories alone is like expecting a bicycle to win the Tour de France. And don't even get me started on China still dominating the cheap manufacturing game. It’s like trying to sell sand in the desert; no one’s buying.

So, what’s the plan? First, stop being a bunch of isolated islands trying to negotiate with giants like the US and China. The African Continental Free Trade Area needs to actually *happen*, not just be another shiny promise. Secondly, electricity. This is so basic it’s embarrassing. We’re still choking on diesel fumes because the power grid is a joke. Forget AI or manufacturing if you can’t reliably switch on a light. And finally, let's play to our strengths: natural resources and our own damn markets. Agriculture still matters, even if your shamba is a bad investment sometimes.

The real kicker is that while the world has changed, our goal - better jobs, higher living standards - hasn't. It's just the route that's become a minefield. We need to stop pretending we can go it alone and start acting like a collective, especially when it comes to attracting investment and building infrastructure. If we don't figure this out, we’ll be stuck in the same cycle of dependency, blaming everyone else while our own economies sputter along, just like the [Kenyan 'Top Influencers' Grab Sh296m As Creator Economy Hits Sh1bn - Mostly Hot Air](/posts/kenyan--top-influencers--grab-sh296m-as-creator-economy-hits-sh1bn---mostly-hot-air) did, looking for quick cash while the real problems fester.
