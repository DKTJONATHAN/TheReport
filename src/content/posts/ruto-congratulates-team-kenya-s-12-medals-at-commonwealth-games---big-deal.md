Ruto Cheers Team Kenya After 12 Medals in Glasgow - For Now.

The President is patting himself on the back for Team Kenya's 12 medals at the Commonwealth Games. While the athletes did their thing, don't expect any real change from this performance.

Just another day of cheering for the usual suspects.

Running. Athletics. Weightlifting. They got some bling. Let's see if it translates to anything more than a few more smiling photos for the President's Instagram.







So, President Ruto is all smiles, congratulating "Team Kenya" for their 12-medal haul at the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow. Three golds, four silvers, five bronzes. The usual suspects, mostly. Edmund Serem, Mathew Kipchumba Kipsang, Faith Cherotich in the steeplechase and 5000m. Big ups to them, I guess. It’s nice for them to get a moment in the sun, but let’s be real, this is the same old script. We're good at running, and apparently, we can lift some weights now. Whoop-de-doo.

The Tourism Cabinet Secretary, Rebecca Miano, is already spinning this into a global brand promotion. Apparently, our athletes’ success is a "high-visibility platform" that "significantly elevates Kenya’s brand worldwide." Millions in organic media exposure, she says, reinforcing our identity as a nation of "extraordinary talent and resilience." Right. Because a few medals in Glasgow are going to magically fix the potholes on my way to work or make the cost of unga any cheaper. This is the same song and dance we hear every time our athletes bring home some hardware. It’s a nice little distraction, but it won't fundamentally alter the struggles of everyday Kenyans.

Miano's also pushing the sports tourism angle, with Iten as a premier destination. Great. So, foreign athletes will come train here, pay their fees, and then what? Does that trickle down to the mama mbogas and boda bodas? Or is it just another way to funnel money into the pockets of the already well-connected? We've seen this before. Remember when they said the 2026 World Athletics Championships would be a game-changer? We’re still waiting for that revolution. It feels like more of the same old play, just with a different set of medals.

And let's not forget the underlying message: Kenya is "truly Magical." Magical because we can run fast? Magical because our elite athletes are world-class? It's a convenient narrative that conveniently sidesteps the gritty reality for most people. While Ruto and Miano are busy celebrating the flag flying high, the rest of us are still trying to figure out how to fly high enough to escape the economic pressures. This performance adds to our history, yes, but history doesn't pay the bills. It's a fleeting moment of national pride, and soon enough, we'll be back to the usual grind. Maybe we should occupy Kasarani Stadium again, but this time, for something that actually impacts our lives.
