So, President Ruto wants our soldiers to be more than just glorified security guards. Apparently, the Kenya Defence Forces have all these "capabilities" that can help strengthen the country. We're talking infrastructure, engineering, disaster response, medicine, logistics, maritime activity, and even cybersecurity. Because, clearly, we've run out of Kenyans to do any of that. It's easier to just deploy the army, right? Saves on paying proper wages and dealing with unions.

DP Kindiki chimes in, waxing lyrical about how Kenya can join the "wealthy nations" with "good leadership and policy continuity." Right. Because nothing screams wealth and continuity like expecting the military to pick up the slack in every sector. It’s a wonder they haven’t asked them to start brewing the chai at State House yet. This is the same sort of thinking that gets us into situations where even billionaires can't buy peace, as we saw in Beverly Hills - problems don't just disappear with money or brute force.

The narrative is that this is all about "nation-building." Funny, I thought nation-building involved things like education, healthcare, and, you know, creating jobs for actual civilians. But why bother when you have an army equipped for… well, everything apparently. It’s a slick way to mask a lack of investment in essential civilian services and a worrying trend of militarizing everyday life.

And let's not forget the "synergies" they're talking about. The KDF's involvement in infrastructure is just another way to bypass the usual procurement processes, I suspect. Less oversight, less accountability, more opportunities for… well, you can guess. It’s like the Sisyphean theater of digital merit, where the rules keep changing and the real issues get ignored while everyone plays along.

So, next time you see a soldier on a construction site or patching up a leaky pipe, remember: it's not just them doing their patriotic duty. It's a government finding the cheapest, most readily available workforce to cover its own shortcomings. Building the nation, one conscripted engineer at a time.

This whole "soldiers building the country" thing is just the latest chapter in a long book of Kenyan political theater. From shady dances with juntas to health ministries doing the Mpox dance with truckers, it's all about the optics and the supposed solutions that never quite fix the root problem. But hey, at least the soldiers are busy, right?
