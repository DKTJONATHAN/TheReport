So, the Middle East is having a wobble, and naturally, our pockets are the first to feel the pinch. The latest fuel price hike is here, and shocker, people are threatening to take to the streets. Because, what else are they supposed to do? They've tried complaining, they've tried electing leaders, and now it's back to the old reliable: shouting.

The energy regulator, in a display of stunning transparency (or perhaps just plain honesty), admits they're short on cash to shield us from these soaring costs. So, the 'limited funds' narrative is out. It's not about not having enough money; it's about who gets to keep it. And it's clearly not going to be you, the struggling Kenyan trying to get to work.

Meanwhile, they're busy pushing us to accept all sorts of cookies just to read the news. Apparently, independent journalism isn't free, but neither is your personal data, or so they'd have you believe. You can either swallow the data-grabbing free access or pay up for the privilege of not being tracked like a common criminal. It's the same old song and dance, just with a digital twist.

This whole fuel price situation is just another reminder that the 'soft life' is a myth for most of us. While some might be dreaming of luxury, others are just trying to figure out how to afford a matatu ride. Perhaps some of those "soft life founders" who ended up in the remand home recently could offer some budgeting tips. [Nairobi’s 'Soft Life' Founders Just Got a New Address: The Remand Home](/posts/nairobi-s--soft-life--founders-just-got-a-new-address--the-remand-home).

They say there are no immediate solutions, no quick fixes. It’s always ‘limited funds’ or ‘global factors’. It’s a classic Kenyan script. The only thing certain is that your wallet will continue to get lighter while the promises of unity and change continue to echo. Just don't expect any actual relief. That's for the people who *don't* need to worry about fuel prices.
