EACC Nabs County Official, Tells Two Others to Jog On Back

The Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission, in their infinite wisdom, has finally decided to yank a Turkana County official and a couple of others into their dusty offices. The crime? Allegedly siphoning Ksh92 million from the county coffers. Because, of course, it's Turkana. What else is new under the Kenyan sun?

Seems like a Senior Assistant Community Health Officer, a director from a private company that conveniently did business with the county, and some NGO chap are the unlucky ones this round. They’re being accused of being chums with companies that apparently milked Turkana County dry to the tune of over Ksh92 million over a decade. It’s almost impressive, if it wasn't so depressingly predictable.

The EACC, bless their investigative hearts, found that two companies, linked to county bigwigs, raked in Ksh92.06 million. One of these golden geese apparently squawked Ksh62.05 million, with a Principal Finance Officer and a Principal Accountant playing fairy godmother, processing payments and even having their name on the company’s bank account. Conflict of interest? Nah, just a friendly handshake between public duty and private profit. Meanwhile, another Turkana official played a different game, using his own company to snag Ksh30.01 million in county contracts. It’s a family affair, you see.

The real kicker? The money was then withdrawn in cold, hard cash. Because hiding a few million in fancy offshore accounts is so last decade. This is pure, unadulterated Kenya: loot it, cash it out, and hope the EACC is too busy chasing shadows to notice. They’re charging these folks with conflict of interest, unlawful acquisition of public property, money laundering, and dealing with the proceeds of crime. As if anyone in these circles actually knows what crime is anymore. It’s just business as usual, and frankly, it reminds me of why so many retirees are just scraping by. [THE GOLDEN YEARS ARE RUSTY: WHY KENYA'S RETIREES ARE JUST SURVIVING](/posts/the-golden-years-are-rusty--why-kenya-s-retirees-are-just-surviving)

The DPP has apparently given the green light for eight people, companies, and directors to face the music. Three were nabbed, and five have been told to report themselves. The EACC also vows to chase after the stolen loot and assets. Good luck with that. They’ll probably need it.

Let’s be real, this is just a drop in the ocean. While they’re busy parading a few scapegoats, the real architects of this grand theft are probably already planning their next multi-million shilling venture. It's the same old song and dance in this country, and we’re all just stuck in the cheap seats.
