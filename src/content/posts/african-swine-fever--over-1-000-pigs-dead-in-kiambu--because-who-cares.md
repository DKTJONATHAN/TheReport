So, the Directorate of Veterinary Services, bless their little cotton socks, are all flustered because African Swine Fever has decided to throw a party in Juja, Thika, and Ruiru since mid-May. We're talking 1,470 pigs, give or take, either sick or six feet under, with some farms looking like a scene from a zombie movie, 100 per cent mortality. Apparently, this is the "most significant" outbreak, which, in Kenya, means it's bad enough that they might actually have to pretend to do something.

This whole mess is just another shining example of how our disease surveillance system is about as effective as a condom in a polygamous wedding. The government, predictably, is "stepping up measures" and "warning of heavy losses." You don't say. And it's not just Kiambu; Tharaka-Nithi, Busia, Embu, Kakamega, Siaya, and Migori are also on the casualty list. Meanwhile, back in 2026, data from their fancy systems show almost 90 per cent of pigs keeling over in Tharaka-Nithi. It's a real party, isn't it?

Dr. Allan Azegele, a man whose title is longer than most Kenyan careers, chirps that the virus, Genotype IX, is a real nasty piece of work, killing pigs faster than politicians break promises. But don't worry, he assures us, it won't infect *us*. Pork that's been inspected is "safe." Right. Because our inspection systems are flawless. The annual losses are pegged at a cool USD 7 million, mostly hitting the smallholder farmers. Because who else would be foolish enough to rely on pigs for a living in this country? It's a burden, and we're all just expected to carry it.

The real kicker? There's no vaccine. The virus mutates faster than you can say "corruption." So, what's the solution? They tell farmers to limit visitors, disinfect vehicles, and cook their food waste. Basically, treat your pigsty like Fort Knox. And if you see a sick pig? Report it. As if the nearest vet office is just around the corner, ready to spring into action. It's all about "biosecurity" and "community engagement," buzzwords that sound good on paper but mean squat when the virus is already in the sty.

And get this: apparently, some pigs are carrying the virus without showing a single symptom. Asymptomatic carriers. Just silently spreading the plague. It's like a bad Kenyan telenovela. The government's latest bright idea is an "Animal Identification and Traceability System" called Anitrac. Because if we can just track every single pig, maybe we can stop this madness. It’s a digital system to address the "animal data gap." We have a data gap in pretty much everything, but sure, let's focus on the pigs. It reminds me of how Kenya's AGOA test was reviewed, with a bad governance record - you can’t expect much when the foundations are shaky.

So, here we are. Pigs are dying, farmers are crying, and the government is issuing directives. They're intensifying surveillance in "high-risk areas." Translation: the places where this has already happened and will probably happen again. Farmer education on "biosecurity" and the risks of "healthy carrier pigs." Because who doesn't love a good dose of paranoia with their breakfast? It's a cycle, a grim, predictable cycle, and we’re all just along for the ride.
