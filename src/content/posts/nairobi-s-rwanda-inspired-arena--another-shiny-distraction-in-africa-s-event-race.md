NAIROBI ARENA PLANS: ANOTHER RWANDA COPYCAT IN THE SHAMEFUL RACE FOR EVENTS

Here we go again. Nairobi is getting a shiny new 10,000-seater arena, part of some grand $294 million Railway City project. Apparently, this is our ticket to hosting "major sporting, business and entertainment events." Because clearly, we're drowning in international tournaments and sold-out concerts, aren't we? It's all about chasing the "MICE" (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences and Exhibitions) tourism dollar, a phrase that makes my stomach churn with the smell of corporate greed. They're building a whole MICE Core Centre, complete with a hotel and apartments, because nothing screams authentic Nairobi like a sterile, manufactured entertainment zone.

This whole setup is a blatant copy of Rwanda's playbook with their BK Arena. Zaria Group, some offshore entity, is backing this local subsidiary. They already manage venues in Kigali, so naturally, they think they can just replicate that here. The Kenya Railway Corporation is beaming about the potential "growth in the tourism and MICE economy," which translates to more money for some, while the rest of us get to sit in traffic around the construction site. And after it's built? QA Venue Solutions, another Zaria affiliate, will be running it on renewable 15-year contracts, with the "intention" of managing it indefinitely. Translation: they'll milk it dry.

Meanwhile, the Democratic Republic of Congo is out here building a 20,000-seater monstrosity, aiming to "exceed the scale" of places like Senegal's Dakar Arena. It’s a frantic arms race for African cities to build bigger and better venues, all to attract the same few international events. We’re hoping to benefit, just like Rwanda did with their $104 million BK Arena, which hosted some American singer and a basketball league. We're told this new Nairobi arena will be multi-purpose, accommodating everything from basketball to banquets. Because nothing says "sports" like a corporate gala dinner, right?

The plans detail a five-story behemoth, a concrete jungle with over 11,000 square meters of space. They're throwing in retail outlets, restaurants, food and beverage facilities, and "sports-related public activation zones." Basically, a commercial complex disguised as an arena. There's even talk of a hotel and serviced apartments, a mixed-use destination for the elite, no doubt. All this is happening right next to the CBD, promising to "decongest" the city. We’ve heard that one before. Just remember the last time they promised a grand redevelopment and we ended up with more potholes and the same old stories of government incompetence, like those viral fraud videos we’ve seen circulating.

The Kenya Railway Corporation is salivating at the prospect of "increased government revenue through various tax streams and statutory levies," plus "long-term land-lease revenue and a gross revenue share." It’s all about the money, the money, and more money. The Railway City project, initially supposed to be done by 2027, is an ambitious plan involving kilometers of new track, new station buildings, and repurposing heritage sites. It’s a whole urban overhaul, promising a "green city" with office blocks and malls. Let's see how "green" it is when the dust settles and the real estate sharks start circling.

This whole endeavour reeks of misplaced priorities. While we're busy building fancy arenas to attract international glitterati, basic infrastructure crumbles. We have road accidents claiming lives daily, a stark reminder of the urgent needs that are constantly overlooked. Nairobi's elite are dreaming of international conferences and concerts, while the average Kenyan is just trying to get to work without navigating a city in perpetual construction and chaos. This arena might bring in some revenue for a select few, but for most of us, it's just another grand project that promises much and delivers… well, we'll see.







Nairobi is getting a shiny new 10,000-seater indoor sports and entertainment arena, plastered as the centrepiece of a $294 million Railway City project. The Kenyan government, in its infinite wisdom, believes this is the golden ticket to attracting major sporting, business, and entertainment events, a direct jab at other African cities vying for the same corporate dollars. This "MICE Core Centre," a term that sounds more like a disease than a development, will be surrounded by hotels, apartments, and shops. It’s all a carbon copy of Rwanda’s model, because apparently, innovation is too expensive.

The project is being bankrolled by Guernsey-headquartered Zaria Group, operating through its local outfit, Metroarena Development Company. They're hoping to replicate Rwanda's success with their BK Arena, which conveniently hosts the Basketball Africa League and has seen international artists perform. Kenya Railway Corporation is ecstatic, anticipating "growth in the tourism and MICE economy" and a boost to investment. Management will be handled by QA Venue Solutions, another Zaria affiliate, on renewable 15-year contracts, meaning they'll be here indefinitely, collecting their fees while Nairobi grinds to a halt around them.

This arena development is happening at a time when African cities are in a frantic arms race to build bigger and flashier venues. The Democratic Republic of Congo is constructing a colossal 20,000-seat Kinshasa Arena, aiming to dwarf existing facilities. It’s a desperate scramble to become the continent’s next big event hub, with modern venues increasingly treated as commercial assets. Kenya, of course, wants its slice of the pie, hoping to replicate the success Rwanda has seen. But is this the best use of hundreds of millions of dollars when roads are crumbling and basic services are a constant struggle?

The arena itself is slated to be a five-storey, multi-purpose facility, capable of hosting basketball games alongside conferences and banquets. It boasts a built-up area of nearly 12,000 square meters, with ample space for seating and back-of-house operations. The development includes a public podium with parking, retail outlets, restaurants, and "leisure uses." They're even planning an amphitheatre and public performance spaces. It sounds like a self-contained bubble for the privileged, designed to cater to a very specific demographic, while the rest of Nairobi grapples with its everyday challenges.

The location, south of the CBD and accessible via major arteries, is meant to integrate seamlessly into the government’s broader plan to transform the railway station area into a commercial, residential, and transport hub. Kenya Railway Corporation anticipates significant revenue from taxes, levies, and land leases. This "green city" concept, with office blocks and malls, was initially slated for completion by 2027. We’ve seen grand government plans before, many of which end up as abandoned blueprints or white elephants, just like some of the ambitious projects that have seen little progress, exposing the government's consistent incompetence.

Ultimately, this arena is another shiny distraction, a grand spectacle designed to generate buzz and attract foreign currency, while the fundamental issues plaguing Nairobi remain unaddressed. It's a race to the bottom, or rather, a race to the top, for those who can afford to attend the events. While the elite rub shoulders at international conferences, the reality for most Kenyans is a daily grind, struggling with the very infrastructure that this project purports to improve. Let's hope this arena doesn't just become another symbol of misplaced priorities and empty promises.
