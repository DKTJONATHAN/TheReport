MT KENYA EAST LEADERS CHART OWN PATH AFTER OL KALOU FALLOUT - AND WHY SHOULD WE CARE?

Warning: More political posturing from the mountain, promising unity while clearly carving out their own turf. Expect more rhetoric, less action.

Meru Senator Kathuri Murungi, Chuka/Igambang’ombe MP Patrick Munene, and Meru County Assembly Speaker Ayub Bundi are all suddenly seeing the light after that Ol Kalou by-election. Apparently, their “brothers from the other side of the mountain” - which is code for Kikuyu leaders - have finally shown their true colours. Now, they’re crying foul that their son, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki, is being disrespected, and their solution is to go their separate ways. Because that always works out so well, right?

These leaders are claiming that for years, Mt Kenya East has supported leaders from Mt Kenya West, but the development record speaks for itself. They paint a picture of neglected villages while Nyeri, Kiambu, and Murang’a are supposedly swimming in tarmac roads and electricity. Now, with Kindiki in the DP’s seat, they feel sidelined and are tired of it. It’s a classic case of "we scratch your back, you forget we exist." This whole kerfuffle sounds suspiciously like a prelude to a new political arrangement, maybe one where they can better leverage Kindiki’s position, or perhaps they’re just looking for a scapegoat for their own development shortcomings. It’s a narrative we’ve heard before, especially when the likes of [failed politicians](posts/religion--the-ultimate-get-out-of-jail-free-card-for-failed-politicians) try to reinvent themselves.

The main fear here, according to Bundi, is losing the Deputy President’s position. He rattles off a list of other regions - Luo, Luhya, Coast - all supposedly eyeing the seat. Apparently, if Mt Kenya East loses this one, history will judge them as the “worst community.” Dramatic much? This is classic fear-mongering, designed to unite their base under the banner of protectionism. They’re also urging residents to vote for “integrity and development records,” which is rich coming from politicians who are now openly admitting to tribal political maneuvering.

MP Munene has taken it a step further, declaring himself chairman of a newly formed Mt Kenya East political bloc. Meru, Embu, Tharaka Nithi, Isiolo, and Kirinyaga are all apparently on board. They’re throwing down the gauntlet: let those on the other side keep their mountain, and if they want to support Ruto, fine, but Mt Kenya East will pursue its own direction. It’s a bold move, or perhaps just a desperate attempt to gain leverage in the murky waters of Kenyan politics. We'll see how long this "unity" lasts.

It’s all very convenient, isn't it? The Ol Kalou by-election, which Public Service Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku also cited as evidence for the need for separate paths, has become the latest excuse for a political realignment. Even Deputy President Kindiki himself is reportedly rejecting these division attempts, though the leaders pushing for separation seem undeterred. It’s a predictable cycle of regional politics, where unity is a fleeting concept and self-interest always comes out on top.
