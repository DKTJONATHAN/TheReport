So, the government is finally deciding to *talk* to us, with less than a year to go before the next election. Head of Public Service Felix Koskei has apparently issued a directive telling Cabinet Secretaries, Principal Secretaries, and agency heads to get out there and defend their record. They're supposed to be "taking greater responsibility" for communicating policies, projects, and achievements. Funny how that responsibility suddenly kicks in when the polls are getting close, isn't it?

They're admitting that "poor and sometimes conflicting communication" has led to declining public trust. You don't say! It’s almost like people can see through the smoke and mirrors when their wallets are empty and promises remain just that - promises. Now, suddenly, everyone's supposed to be on the same page, singing the same tune. This feels less like an epiphany about good governance and more like a desperate scramble to salvage a sinking ship.

The big question is whether this is genuinely about improving how the government communicates, or if it's a thinly veiled strategy to shape the political narrative ahead of the 2027 General Election. Given the timing, I'm leaning heavily towards the latter. It's the classic playbook: if you can't convince them with your results, confuse them with your words. Just look at how some CSs managed to play hide-and-seek when billions vanished, as reported previously. This new directive seems designed to ensure they have a script this time around.

They've got various platforms lined up, from AM Live to Business Redefined and even a podcast about the digital creative economy - a whole ecosystem of communication channels. It’s a full-court press to project an image of competence and progress. But while they're busy polishing their message, perhaps they should spare a thought for fundamental issues, like the massive debt Kenya has accrued. We're still asking where all that borrowed money went, and the answers have been as clear as mud.

It’s a tough gig, trying to convince a weary public that things are on the upswing when the cost of living is through the roof and basic services are a lottery. This communication push feels like putting lipstick on a pig - it might look a bit better for a while, but at the end of the day, it’s still the same pig. Let’s see how long this newfound transparency lasts after the election dust settles.

The government's strategy might be to highlight their supposed achievements, but for many of us on the ground, the reality on the streets tells a different story. We'll be watching to see if these pronouncements translate into tangible improvements or if it's just another election year performance.
