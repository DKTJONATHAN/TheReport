So, there we have it. President Ruto, our esteemed leader, is on another charm offensive in the South Rift. Fresh off his own tour, his former running mate, Rigathi Gachagua, has also been busy trying to convince people he’s not completely out of touch. And then there’s Fred Matiang’i, who apparently believes people will fall for his "Jubilee promises" again. Honestly, it’s a circus, and we’re all just paying for the tickets.

The man is busy jetting around the globe, claiming it’s all for our benefit. Apparently, his endless trips have magically increased tourism from 1.4 million to a whopping 2.7 million. I’m sure the struggling mwananchi struggling to afford ugali will feel the direct impact of that. He’s also touting new airports and roads as saviours, which is great, but let’s not forget the elephant in the room - or rather, the Mau Forest, which is still being carved up and exploited. He’s promising an electric fence, which sounds about as effective as a mosquito net against a charging rhino.

And education? Don’t get me started. He’s suddenly talking about hiring 20,000 teachers and building classrooms, after years of making education feel like a luxury only the wealthy can afford. Remember when his administration’s university funding model was a spectacular failure? Now they’re “streamlining” it again. It’s the same old song and dance, just with slightly different lyrics. He’s also promising 10,000 acres of land subdivided for locals - let’s see how many of those title deeds actually end up in the hands of the deserving and not some connected individual.

Meanwhile, his loyal lieutenants are out there, assuring everyone that the South Rift will never back the opposition. Senator Cheruiyot is particularly vocal, calling Gachagua a hallucinating man out of touch with reality. He conveniently forgets that Gachagua was once the DP, but I suppose when you’re in the thick of a political dogfight, convenient amnesia is par for the course. It’s all about who can sling the most mud and promise the most impossible dreams. We’ve seen this movie before, and the ending is usually the same.

The constant back-and-forth between Ruto, Gachagua, and Matiang’i is just a predictable display of political ambition. Gachagua is off to the US to chase diaspora votes, while Matiang’i is busy decrying Ruto's insults - a rather rich criticism coming from someone in his position. They all promise development and unity, but it’s just noise to distract us from the fact that [THE GLOBAL CONSENT MIRAGE: SURVEILLANCE UNDER THE GUISE OF SUSTAINABILITY] is a real concern for many of us trying to navigate this country.

So, as the elections loom, expect more rallies, more promises, and more deflection. They’re all fighting for a piece of the South Rift pie, and we, the residents, are the ones left to digest the leftovers. It's a battleground, alright, but the real fight is for our attention and our votes, with very little substance to show for it.
