SUPREME COURT HANGS UP SH1.5 BILLION RUARAKA LAND PAYOUT - AGAIN. EXPECT MORE GAMES.

Just when you thought the EACC might actually do its job and claw back Sh1.5 billion in what smells like another grand land grab, the Supreme Court has hit the pause button. Don't get your hopes up; this is Nairobi. Everything here is a drama.

So, here's the tea: The Court of Appeal already said it was illegal to pay companies linked to the late Francis Mburu for land where Drive-In Primary and Ruaraka High schools are sitting. They even wanted the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission to go after the cash. And guess who got a letter demanding a refund? Big names, including former Interior CS Fred Matiang'i. Classic. But of course, the companies, Huelands Limited and Afrison Export Import Limited, have now run to the Supreme Court with their tails between their legs, begging for more time.

And shocker, the Supreme Court, in its infinite wisdom, has granted them an interim order. This means any move to recover that Sh1.5 billion is officially on hold. Investigations, arrests, charges - all paused. It's like watching a rerun of a bad movie where the villains always get a reprieve. The companies are yapping about constitutional rights and compulsory acquisition, claiming the courts are misinterpreting things. They argue they never surrendered the land for public use, and that planning approval shouldn't count as a gift. Honestly, the excuses these people come up with are almost as creative as some of the schemes you hear about, like the ones involving [Kanairo’s Flesh Trade: From Ruai Basements to Russian Trenches](/posts/kanairo-s-flesh-trade--from-ruai-basements-to-russian-trenches).

The EACC, bless their hearts, is fighting this, saying the companies are trying to turn simple factual disputes into grand constitutional battles to get the Supreme Court to re-evaluate what the lower courts already decided. The Court of Appeal had looked at the evidence, seen the schools were built there decades ago, and the companies never complained. They concluded the land was voluntarily surrendered. But logic and evidence are apparently no match for legal loopholes and deep pockets.

Now, the Supreme Court has to decide if the Court of Appeal was right. Will they see through this charade, or will another round of legal gymnastics begin, draining more public resources and leaving those schools in limbo? Given how things usually go in this city, don't bet on a quick or clean resolution. This is just another chapter in the never ending story of how Nairobi's land is 'developed'.
