So, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki is out here playing the prodigal son’s father, calling on Peter Munya, Mithika Linturi, and Justin Muturi to “come back home.” Apparently, there’s still a seat at the table for them in President Ruto’s administration, if they’d just swallow their pride and crawl back. It’s the classic Kenyan political drama - a bit of backstabbing, a dash of betrayal, and now, a desperate attempt at a reconciliation tour. Don’t let the warm words fool you; this is all about numbers and optics, pure and simple.

Kindiki’s talk of forgiveness is just as suspect. He’s supposedly letting bygones be bygones for those who dared to criticise him. Forgiven? In politics? That’s like hoping a snake will suddenly develop a fondness for milk. These leaders, particularly those aligned with the former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua, know the game. They’ve seen how quickly allegiances shift and how easily promises evaporate once power is secured.

This whole charade reeks of a government trying to shore up its support base, especially with an eye on the Mt Kenya East region. Kindiki’s words are less about genuine unity and more about consolidating power. It’s a strategic move, no doubt, but the underlying motive is as transparent as a cheap plastic boda boda seat. They need the bodies, and they’re willing to offer a few crumbs from the table to get them.

And let's not forget Justin Muturi. This is the same man who saw another 'Mega Scandal' brewing in Kenya's new infrastructure fund not too long ago. Now he's being invited back to the same system he was so critical of? It’s almost comical, if it weren’t so depressingly predictable. The political landscape in this country is less about ideology and more about survival of the fittest, or in this case, survival of the most opportunistic.

The "benefit of Mt Kenya East" sounds nice, but in reality, it’s a coded message. It means the region’s political clout is up for grabs, and Kindiki is positioning himself as the kingmaker, or at least the chief diplomat, trying to lure back those who might have strayed. It’s a dance as old as time in Kenyan politics, where loyalty is as fleeting as a good WiFi signal in Githurai.

Ultimately, this is just another episode in the never-ending soap opera of Kenyan politics. We'll see if Munya, Linturi, and Muturi take the bait. But for those of us watching from the sidelines, it’s a reminder that in this game, “home” is just another temporary address until the next election cycle or the next power shift.
