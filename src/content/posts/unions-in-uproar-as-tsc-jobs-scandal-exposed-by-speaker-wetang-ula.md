So, the Speaker of the National Assembly, Moses Wetang'ula, finally admits what everyone with half a brain already knew: State House is dishing out teacher employment letters to MPs. And what do these honourable representatives do with them? They hawk them, of course. Forty thousand shillings here, four hundred thousand shillings there, all for a piece of paper that’s supposed to be earned on merit. It's not a job; it's a commodity, traded like stolen goods. This isn't new; it's just more proof that this country runs on who you know, not what you know.

The teachers' unions are, predictably, "outraged." Knut, Kuppet, Kethawa - they all spout the same tired lines about undermining meritocracy and the independence of the TSC. Bless their hearts. They sound like they genuinely believed the TSC was an impartial body, untouched by the grubby hands of politics. When MPs are seen handing out employment letters at rallies and funerals, what did they expect? It's the same old song and dance, just with a different tune of desperation. This whole affair reeks of the same kind of impunity we see elsewhere, like [Kenya's Expanding Economy of Violence: A Goons' Paradise Fueled by Power and Impunity](/posts/kenya-s-expanding-economy-of-violence--a-goons--paradise-fueled-by-power-and-impunity).

The Teachers Service Commission, the so-called independent constitutional body, is now under the microscope, especially with 20,000 new vacancies up for grabs. Of course, they're defending their process, claiming it's fair and competitive. We’ve heard that one before. When the Speaker himself is admitting to escorting MPs to get these letters, their defence sounds as hollow as a politician's promise. It’s a classic case of denial, hoping that if they just keep saying it’s fair, maybe some people will believe it.

This circus isn't just about teachers; it’s about what this country has become. It’s about qualified graduates, who’ve poured years and money into their education, being sidelined because they can't afford to pay off a local MP. It’s about the illusion of meritocracy crumbling before our eyes, replaced by a system that rewards connections and cash. The unions might be shocked, but for the rest of us, it's just another grim confirmation of reality.

Even the Education CS, Julius Ogamba, is talking about addressing the "selling of employment letters" as a "criminal activity." Well, no kidding. The question is, when will anyone actually face consequences? The TSC might transfer some "senior county officials," but the real architects of this mess, the ones dishing out the letters, are likely still enjoying their power. This is just a theatrical show to appease the public before the next wave of job selling begins.

The TSC claims it conducts its recruitment fairly and competitively. Meanwhile, the Speaker admits MPs are given letters to distribute, and these letters are then sold for fortunes. This isn't a contradiction; it's the whole damn point. The system is rigged, and the loudest voices of outrage are from those who benefit least or are just now realizing the depth of the rot.
