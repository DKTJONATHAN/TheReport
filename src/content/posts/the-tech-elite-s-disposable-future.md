The narrative is shifting from "hiring the best" to "automating the rest." While Amazon claims these cuts are to ensure they move fast, the underlying truth is a global restructuring of labor. CEO Andy Jassy is not just trimming fat; he is re-engineering the company to operate without the very people who built it. This is a cynical pivot seen across Meta, Salesforce, and Cisco. The sector is cannibalizing itself to fund the next arms race in artificial intelligence, a technology that exists primarily to ensure even fewer people are needed in the future.

There is a dark irony in the fact that many of these workers were tasked with building the very AI that eventually rendered them obsolete. This is not just an American problem. By shifting operations to lower-cost hubs like India, Amazon is engaging in a classic globalist maneuver: arbitrage. They replace expensive Seattle engineers with a combination of algorithms and cheaper overseas labor, all while maintaining the veneer of being a "startup." It is a race to the bottom where the prize is a slightly better quarterly earnings report and a lot of empty desks in Washington state.

The internal culture has devolved into a digital Panopticon. Current employees report a "rat race" where AI usage is tracked like a heartbeat. When workers are forced to "tokenmaxx" to climb internal leaderboards, you know the soul of the industry has been replaced by a spreadsheet. The prestige of having a tech giant on a resume is evaporating. When 30,000 people with the same "prestigious" background hit the market simultaneously, that prestige becomes a commodity, and commodities are always cheap.

This systemic erosion of job security is the new global baseline. As we have seen in other sectors, such as the predatory fiscal environments discussed in The Fifteen Percent Tax on Global Sanity (/posts/the-fifteen-percent-tax-on-global-sanity), the goal is never human flourishing. It is the extraction of maximum output for minimum input. The "blessings in disguise" cited by some laid-off workers are merely coping mechanisms for a world that has decided they are no longer necessary. The tech dream did not die; it was simply automated out of existence.
