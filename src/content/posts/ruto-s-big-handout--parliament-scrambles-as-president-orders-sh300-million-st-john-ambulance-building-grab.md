So, the President, in his infinite wisdom and seemingly without consulting anyone who actually handles the money, has decided the Parliamentary Service Commission needs to cough up Sh300 million. For what, you ask? To buy a building from St John Ambulance. Apparently, they're getting a shiny new place in Upper Hill, courtesy of the taxpayer, plus another Sh200 million for construction and a cool Sh100 million annually from here on out, not to mention 15 new ambulances. Because, clearly, our national priorities are perfectly aligned.

Naturally, some members of the PSC, the very people tasked with guarding this money, are spitting mad. They're calling it a "roadside directive," which, let's be honest, is a polite way of saying "bullshit." The Public Finance Management Act, that pesky law that's supposed to prevent this kind of thing, is apparently just a suggestion to the President. And the audacity of Wetang'ula, the Speaker, saying he "cannot oppose the president"? Classic. It’s a shame he can't recall how the constitution actually works, unlike how Google might assert its dominance in generative AI with the launch of Gemini 3.1 Pro.

The whole deal stinks of backroom deals and a complete disregard for due process. Who decided St John Ambulance's building was worth Sh300 million? Was there a valuation, or did someone just pluck a number out of the air? These are the questions the PSC members are asking, the same ones that should be public knowledge, not whispered in hushed tones in meetings that are already being ignored. It’s a classic move: bypass the checks and balances, get what you want, and then expect everyone else to fall in line.

This isn't about St John Ambulance's charitable work; it's about how power is being wielded. The PSC is supposed to be independent, a constitutional commission. But when the President snaps his fingers, and the Speaker just shrugs, where does that leave the notion of independence? It means it's all for show. The PFM Act is being kicked to the curb, audit queries are looming, and everyone involved is setting themselves up for a fall.

And let's not forget the timing. This whole charade conveniently "clears up space" around Parliament. Funny how these 'solutions' always seem to benefit those already in power, while the rest of us are left footing the bill. We're supposed to believe this is for a good cause, but when you see the money flowing and the rules bending, it’s hard not to see the same old song and dance.

The constitution is clear: presidential decisions must be in writing. But a verbal order at a parade? That's just a recipe for disaster and a green light for corruption. It’s a stark reminder that in this country, the real power often lies not in the law, but in who you know and how loud you can shout your directives.
