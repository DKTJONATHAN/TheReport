Ruto to Uhuru: Respect Your Office, Let Kenya Move Forward - Or Else

The President has decided it's time to start throwing shade, and guess who's in his crosshairs? Retired President Uhuru Kenyatta. Ruto, sounding like a jilted lover, is whining about how he supposedly supported Uhuru through thick and thin, only to be met with a cold shoulder and outright opposition. Now, he's lecturing Uhuru on respecting his former office, as if the man's suddenly forgotten how to sit still and sip tea. Apparently, helping someone in four elections means you're entitled to unquestioning loyalty forever. What a concept.

Ruto claims he's "fixed the mess" Uhuru left, tossing around numbers about debt repayment, inflation reduction, and a doubling of foreign exchange reserves. He’s also bragging about hiring teachers, building classrooms, and constructing houses and markets. It’s the usual song and dance: blame the predecessor for all woes, then claim miraculous achievements. One wonders if these 300,000 houses are as real as the promises made during previous campaigns. And let's not forget the jab about "Linda Sponsor" - classic Ruto, always trying to paint his opponents as puppet masters.

The President is accusing Uhuru of actively trying to sabotage his administration, even calling the opposition formations "United Nothing" and "Alternative Nothing." This is rich, coming from a man who seems to thrive on political mudslinging. He’s also implying Uhuru is behind efforts to frustrate programs like universal health coverage and affordable housing. It's a classic tactic: if things aren't going perfectly, blame the retired guy pulling the strings, rather than admit your own administration might have some shortcomings. It’s like watching a toddler blame the furniture for their stubbed toe. We've seen this kind of political theater before, particularly when leaders [can't pick a decent hopeful](/posts/another-poll--another-ruto-lead--and-still-one-in-five-voters-can-t-pick-a-decent-hopeful).

Ruto’s plea for Uhuru to "go and relax" and "stop disturbing us" is less a request and more a veiled threat. He’s essentially telling the former president to step aside and let him run the show without any interference. This whole spat is a clear indication that the political landscape is already heating up for the next election cycle, with leaders scrambling to position themselves. Ruto’s emphasis on leaders presenting "clear agendas, practical plans and workable solutions" is also a bit rich, considering his own administration's economic record is still a matter of fierce debate.
