The Strait of Hormuz is a brutal reminder of how fragile the world's supply chains truly are. With traffic slowing from an average of 34 daily crossings to a mere trickle, the global energy market is bracing for a sustained shock. The death of 14 people in Iranian-controlled territory is a tragedy for some, but for the markets, it is merely a data point used to price in a renewed geopolitical risk premium. As tankers turn back and insurers hike their rates, the cost of living for the global populace is once again at the mercy of ballistic missiles and naval skirmishes.

Bitcoin's attempt to hold the 62,000 dollar level is a desperate act of defiance against a macro-economic environment that is turning hostile. For all the talk of Bitcoin being a hedge against instability, it continues to trade like a high-beta tech stock. It is tethered to liquidity, and that liquidity is currently being drained by the prospect of higher-for-longer interest rates. If oil stays near 80 dollars or climbs further, the Federal Reserve will have no choice but to keep the monetary noose tight, regardless of how much the crypto-faithful pray for a pivot.

The irony is thick. A decentralized currency that claims to operate outside the reach of the state is being dictated by the military decisions of Washington and Tehran. The link between crude oil and inflation expectations is a chain that Bitcoin cannot break. As long as energy costs drive the consumer price index, the central banks will remain the ultimate arbiters of value, and speculative assets will remain the first to be sacrificed when the heat rises in the Persian Gulf.

We are entering a phase where the recent ceasefire appears to have been nothing more than a brief intermission in a long-running theater of conflict. Russia's diesel export ban only adds more pressure to an already fractured system. Investors who believe they have found a sanctuary in digital code are realizing that their portfolios are still deeply rooted in the blood and oil of the Middle East. The floor at 60,000 dollars is made of glass, and it will shatter if the tankers stop moving for good.
