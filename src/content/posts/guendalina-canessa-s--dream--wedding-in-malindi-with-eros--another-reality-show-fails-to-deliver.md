So, Guendalina Canessa, the one from Big Brother circa 2007 - yes, that long ago - decided to get married in Malindi. Apparently, she "said yes to her Eros," which sounds like something out of a bad romance novel. She posted all these photos, looking all regal in a mermaid dress with a sparkly top. Eros, her new husband, wore a white linen shirt and trousers. Barefoot, of course. Because nothing screams true love like sand between your toes and a suspiciously clean outfit.

She’s going on about how "Africa is a land that knows how to bless love." Oh, please. Africa is a land that knows how to host wealthy tourists for elaborate weddings. She claims she found the man she met 15 years ago by "chance." Let's be real, after a few decades in the public eye, "chance" usually means a well-placed publicist and a carefully curated narrative. And this talk of soulmates? How many soulmates do these reality TV types go through? It's like collecting rare stamps, but with more drama and fewer valuable assets.

Her parents were there, with her father walking her down the aisle, surrounded by people in "traditional Kenyan dress." I’m sure that was a touching display of cultural appreciation, or maybe just a convenient backdrop for the photos. And her 16-year-old daughter, Chloe, was present too. Because nothing says "new beginnings" like dragging your teenage child into your latest romantic adventure. It’s a bit like watching that Dennis Oliech skit - trading genuine achievement for a brief moment of attention.

The congratulatory messages are rolling in from other former Big Brother contestants, like Margherita Zanatta, Elisabetta Gregoraci, and a few others whose names I’ve already forgotten. It’s a reunion of sorts, a reminder that some careers never quite leave the starting line. They’re all in on the fantasy, cheering each other on as they chase these fleeting moments of perceived bliss. Meanwhile, back in Nairobi, we're still dealing with the same old circus, wondering when appointments will actually mean something beyond a photo op, like this article on the [Kenya Gazette](/posts/kenya-gazette-2nd-april-2026--more-appointments--same-old-circus).

She claims she "never, ever thought I'd remarry." Yet here we are. It's a narrative as old as time, or at least as old as reality television. The desperate search for "the one," the grand gesture, the public pronouncements of eternal love, all set against an exotic backdrop. It's a performance, plain and simple. Just another episode in the never-ending season of Guendalina Canessa. We’ll see how long this "soulmate" lasts before she's back to searching for her next "Eros."

This whole affair is a stark reminder that while some are busy chasing their fairytale weddings, others are stuck navigating the realities of everyday life. The "dream wedding" might be a nice story for social media, but it doesn’t pay the bills or fix the potholes. It's a distraction, a beautiful, expensive distraction, while the real world, with all its less glamorous challenges, continues its relentless march. We'll be here, watching.
