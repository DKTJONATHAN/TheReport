So, the cops in Nairobi have nabbed some fellow, Mohamud Hared, for sniffing around a cargo company in Eastleigh. Apparently, he was there to pick up a package that had, you guessed it, a disassembled drone and all its fancy bits. Sent from China by someone named Abdi Kafi. The usual international drama, just with more plastic and circuits.

Hared, the bloke in question, even paid for the delivery via M-Pesa - our digital darling, facilitating all sorts of shady dealings. Then, to make sure it was all above board, he asked the company boys to crack it open. Surprise, surprise, it wasn't a box of mandazi. It was a full-blown drone, ready to be put back together. The company, bless their cautious hearts, immediately called the police.

Turns out, Hared sang like a canary to the cops. Said the drone was for Balad Hawo, a town that’s practically kissing the Kenyan border with Somalia. And how was it getting there? Through Mandera, of course, to be handed over to some Yussuf Abdi Abdille. Sounds like a well-oiled, albeit dodgy, operation. Now Hared's cooling his heels at Pangani, while the authorities try to figure out if this is just a hobby gone wrong or something more sinister. Given the government's obsession with "threats," especially near the Somali border, you can bet they're leaning towards the latter.

It's business as usual in this city, really. People trying to get their hands on whatever they can, and the authorities doing their best to look busy by catching the small fish. Remember when we talked about how sometimes technology gets tied to weird narratives? Well, this is just another chapter in that ongoing saga, though thankfully, this time it's not tied to right-wing conspiracy theories, just good old-fashioned border smuggling. We'll see if this "potential use" turns out to be anything more than a nervous cop's imagination.
