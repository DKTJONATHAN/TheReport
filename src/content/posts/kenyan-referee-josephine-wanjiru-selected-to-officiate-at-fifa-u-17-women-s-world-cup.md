Kenyan Referee Josephine Wanjiru Gets FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup Gig - Don't Get Your Hopes Up.
Another Kenyan in the international spotlight, but let's keep our feet firmly on the ground.
She'll be refereeing in Morocco, probably trying to make sense of what she sees.
Sports
Football, Africa, Women's Football
Whistle
Another year, another supposed triumph for Kenyan football. This time it's Josephine Wanjiru, who's been chosen by FIFA to blow her whistle at the U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco. Yes, the same Morocco that hosted it before. And yes, some of her CECAFA colleagues, Alice Umutesi and Fides Banjurambona, were also there in 2025. So, déjà vu, anyone?

Meanwhile, our Junior Starlets have already jetted off to Morocco. The FKF President, Hussein Mohammed, hosted them for a "dinner" before their "scheduled departure." Apparently, it was delayed. This is the same Hussein Mohammed who, in his infinite wisdom, told us on Saturday morning, October 3, 2026, that the team was heading for a "two-week high-performance training camp in Spain." Funny, because they've already arrived in Morocco. Seems like someone's got their dates, or their destinations, mixed up. It’s not exactly the kind of precise planning that inspires confidence, especially when you're talking about young players facing the world's best. It reminds me of that other tale of Gen Z officials blending things - music, culture, and politics - perhaps they're blending destinations too. [The Linda Mwananchi Gen Z Official Who Blends Music, Culture and Politics: Just Another Shenanigan?](/posts/the-linda-mwananchi-gen-z-official-who-blends-music--culture-and-politics--just-another-shenanigan)

The girls did manage to qualify, so credit where it's due, I suppose. A 5-1 aggregate win over South Africa. They even beat Mexico 2-1 in their last outing in 2024, after getting hammered by England and North Korea. So, a win. That's something. The squad itself is a mix of schoolgirls, local clubs, and academies. Which is… fine. At least it shows they're casting a wide net, or perhaps just grabbing whoever they can find.

The tournament’s been expanded to 24 teams. Kenya's stuck in Group E with China, the US, and Samoa. They play China first, then the US, and finally Samoa. All in Rabat. Let's just hope Wanjiru can keep up with the pace and doesn't miss any obvious fouls, unlike some officials we've seen. And let's hope the Starlets don't embarrass us too badly. After all, this is Kenya. We're good at talking a big game, less good at following through consistently.
