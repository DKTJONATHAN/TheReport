So, Mombasa United, a team that probably hasn't seen a win since Noah was building his ark, has managed to scrape together their first-ever KPL point. How? By drawing 0-0 with Kenya Police FC. Yes, you read that right. Two teams that are seemingly allergic to scoring goals managed to bore everyone to death in Mombasa. You'd think with all the talk about football development in this country, we'd at least get some goals to show for it. Instead, we get this pedestrian exhibition.

Kenya Police, who are apparently a professional outfit, started with some spark, or so the report claims. Their striker, Yves Koitima, apparently had two chances. Two chances. And he missed both. Classic. Then Mombasa United, bless their cotton socks, decided to show up to the party around the 25-minute mark. They enjoyed "spells of possession," which in Nairobi terms usually means kicking the ball around aimlessly until someone takes it off them. They struggled to test the Police keeper, which, let's be honest, is not exactly a surprise.

The second half wasn't much better. More missed chances, more dallying around the box. Jackson Watee, who sounds like he has a future in government with all that missing, had a golden opportunity at the end. One-on-one with the keeper, and he still couldn't finish. It's like these guys are actively trying to avoid scoring. Maybe they're saving their energy for the inevitable corruption scandals that plague this nation, like the recent arrests in the [Energy Top Brass Arrested: PS Liban, Epra Boss, MD Sang Nabbed in Fuel Scam](/posts/energy-top-brass-arrested--ps-liban--epra-boss--md-sang-nabbed-in-fuel-scam) saga. At least that's got some action.

Both coaches are spouting the usual platitudes. "Work in progress," "goals will come," "we came to win." It's all a load of hot air. The truth is, these teams are struggling. They've played three rounds and are still looking for their first win. This isn't a sign of potential; it's a sign of mediocrity. While they're out there drawing blanks, the rest of the world is moving on. Here in Nairobi, we're busy dealing with actual problems, like the [KeNHA Announces Eight Day Night Closure of Uhuru Highway Section for Maintenance](/posts/kenha-announces-eight-day-night-closure-of-uhuru-highway-section-for-maintenance). At least that closure is concrete. This football? It's just more of the same old story.

So, congratulations to Mombasa United on their historic point. It's a milestone, I suppose. A milestone on the road to nowhere. Meanwhile, Kenya Police FC will go back to the drawing board, probably to draw some more. The KPL continues to deliver thrills… or rather, the distinct lack thereof. If this is what passes for entertainment, then perhaps we should all just focus on watching paint dry. It's probably more exciting.
