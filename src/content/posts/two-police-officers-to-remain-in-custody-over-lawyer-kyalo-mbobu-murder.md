Two Police Officers Still Stuck Over Lawyer Kyalo Mbobu Murder

The wheels of justice, if you can even call them that, keep grinding in Nairobi. Another lawyer, Kyalo Mbobu, is dead, and surprise, surprise, some police officers are now the main suspects. Julius Kipkoech Cheruiyot and Kennedy Kipkemei Borboroi, two names that will probably mean nothing to most of us until they're forgotten again, are accused of offing Mbobu on September 9, 2025, somewhere along Magadi Road. They're not alone, mind you. Rose Mbithe, Chris Mulwa, and Angela Mulwa are also expected to turn up in court. Just another Tuesday in this city.

What’s really got my cynical Kenyan gut churning is the stench of familiarity. Court proceedings are already hinting at a link between Mbobu’s death and that of Dr. Victoria Mutiso. And guess who’s a central figure in *both* cases? Rose Mbithe. This isn't a grand conspiracy; it's just Nairobi's usual messy entanglement of crime and seemingly no consequence for the truly powerful. They’re all conveniently linked, proving once again that some people just have a knack for attracting trouble, or perhaps, for causing it.

The judge, bless her heart, is trying to look impartial, ordering these officers to get a mental assessment at Mbagathi Hospital. Because clearly, shooting a lawyer on the road is just a sign of a bad day. The defense wanted them at Muthangari Police Station, but the judge, in her infinite wisdom, decided Nairobi Remand was the place to be. She spoke of balancing rights and justice, but let’s be honest, this whole affair smells less of justice and more of a drawn-out spectacle. It's like watching a bad telenovela unfold, but with actual dead people.

The judge’s mention of balancing justice and the need for it to be seen to be done brought to mind other instances where justice feels like a distant dream. Remember the Mandago's Sh1.1 Billion Loan Saga? That whole "eat now, pay never" mentality seems to be a recurring theme, doesn't it? Here, we have police officers possibly involved in murder, and the system is busy with mental assessments and pleas. Meanwhile, the families of the deceased are left with nothing but grief and a long, drawn-out legal process that will likely end with a slap on the wrist for some, and a permanent scar for others.

Seven suspects are apparently linked to Dr. Mutiso’s murder, with five of them, including Mbithe, Mulwa, and Angela Mulwa, now also entangled in Mbobu’s case. Mbithe, it seems, is racking up charges faster than a matatu collects passengers on a Friday evening. She’s the common thread, the one who shows up in multiple tragedies. It makes you wonder how many more such connections we’re not being told about, how many more names are quietly added to lists of suspects in the endless cycle of violence that defines our city.

Ultimately, these two officers, along with the others, will eventually take their plea on October 27th. The judge is even allowing their wives to provide toothbrushes and soap. Because that’s what justice looks like in Nairobi: small comforts in a system that often offers little else. The underlying issues, the corruption, the desperation that fuels such violence, those will remain unaddressed, festering beneath the surface, waiting for the next victim.
