So, you've been putting in the hours, pumping out content, hoping to strike gold. You see these influencers making it sound like a walk in the park, telling you to just "keep at it until something happens." Well, turns out, "something" might just be more hours spent staring at a screen with zero shillings in your account. A recent survey by Odipo Dev on Kenya's Creator Economy is basically screaming the obvious: views do not, I repeat, DO NOT always translate to money.

Look at Elvis Mushila and Funny Team. Billions of views on TikTok. BILLIONS. You'd think they’d be rolling in it, right? Apparently not. They're not even in the top 10 earners. Meanwhile, the "creator economy" is supposedly "awash with cash," raking in over a billion shillings last year. Makes you wonder where all this money is actually going, doesn't it? It's not like the platforms themselves are falling over themselves to pay the African creator. As Kevin Okeyo from Mediakits.io points out, they're just a tiny fraction of Facebook's or ByteDance's income. We're on our own, basically.

Teddy James, or TinjeyTJ as he’s known, spills the beans: most of the cash isn't from direct platform payouts. It's the brand deals, the sweet, sweet endorsements. But even then, it’s not as simple as having a large following. You need to offer "value to the client." Translation: you need to be more than just a pretty face or a funny sketch artist. You need to be a business, a well-oiled machine that can actually deliver results for some company trying to flog their wares. It’s like trying to navigate the murky waters of Kenyan politics; you need the right connections and a solid strategy, otherwise you’re just another voice lost in the noise. It’s a jungle out there, and unlike those who might be dealing with bigger issues like [Bribes And Backstabbing: MP Loses Watchdog Post Amidst Allegations](/posts/bribes-and-backstabbing--mp-loses-watchdog-post-amidst-allegations), the creator's struggle is for rent money.

So, what’s the takeaway? If you're dreaming of making a living from your social media antics, get real. You need to be smart, strategic, and probably have a business plan tucked away somewhere. It's not just about racking up likes; it's about convincing brands you're worth their advertising budget. And even then, factors like watch time, where your audience lives, and the general mood of advertisers play a bigger role than you'd think. It's a hustle, and for most, it’s a hustle that barely keeps the lights on.
