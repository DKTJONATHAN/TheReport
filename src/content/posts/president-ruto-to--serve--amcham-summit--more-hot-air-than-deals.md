The American Chamber of Commerce Kenya (AmCham Kenya) is puffing its chest out, announcing that His Excellency Dr. William Ruto, CGH, President of the Republic of Kenya, will be the chief guest at their upcoming Business Summit. Apparently, this is meant to signal Kenya's grand commitment to a "competitive investment climate" and "deepening commercial ties." Let's see if all this "commitment" actually translates into fewer bribes for permits or more actual jobs.

This two-day jamboree at the Windsor Golf Hotel & Country Club, from September 9-10, 2026, will bring together "senior government and business leaders" from the US and East Africa. They'll be dissecting investment opportunities in agriculture, digital economy, energy, infrastructure, manufacturing, health, critical minerals, and the ever-popular "creative economy." Expect a lot of talk about policy dialogues and B2B matchmaking, which often means a lot of schmoozing for those already in the know, while the rest of us are left watching the crumbs fall.

Susan Burns from the U.S. Embassy Kenya is spouting the usual platitudes about a "strong and growing commercial partnership." She’s hoping the summit "strengthens our ties and turns regional opportunity into tangible outcomes." Right. Because we haven't heard that one before. Angela Ng'ang'a, the AmCham Kenya Board President, chirps that Ruto's presence is a "strong signal to investors that Kenya is ready to compete for capital." Let's hope this competition doesn't just mean more land grabs and resource exploitation, a familiar tune in this country, much like the predictable dread found in other media.

The whole event is co-convened by AmCham Kenya and the U.S. Embassy, with the Kenyan government tagging along. They’re bragging about "building on the success of previous editions" that supposedly "facilitated high-level policy engagement, investment announcements, and strategic business partnerships." In reality, most of these summits feel like elaborate theatre, where promises are made and promptly forgotten. The real question is, when will we see the "predictable investment environment" that actually benefits the average Kenyan, and not just the usual beneficiaries? Because right now, the glass ceiling in Nairobi feels more like reinforced concrete.
