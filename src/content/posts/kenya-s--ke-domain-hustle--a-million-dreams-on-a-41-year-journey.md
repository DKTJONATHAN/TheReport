So, KENIC, the self-appointed gatekeepers of our digital land, are dreaming of one million .ke domains. One million! At the current snail's pace, that’s 41 years away. Now they’ve revised it to 30 years. Generous, I guess. They say the market’s got momentum, which is nice for them, but let’s be real, most of us are perfectly happy on WhatsApp, selling our second-hand suits via Instagram or haggling on Facebook Marketplace. Why bother with a whole website when M-Pesa and TikTok do the heavy lifting? It's the same old story: talk of potential, but the actual uptake is slower than traffic on Waiyaki Way during rush hour.

The numbers are… interesting. A 16% jump year-on-year. Sounds good, right? But when you’re starting from a base that’s barely a blip on the global radar, 16% is just a fancy way of saying "still way, way behind." We’re talking about needing seven times the current registrations to hit this magical million. Meanwhile, the .com and .net domains are chugging along. It’s not that our market is declining, it’s just that the mountain to climb is so colossal, even a decent hike looks like a stroll. They’re focusing on businesses, which makes sense, because the bulk of existing registrations are the .co.ke ones. But the idea of seven million Kenyan enterprises all suddenly needing a .ke domain? Please. Most of them are informal hustles, surviving on word-of-mouth and a prayer.

And let’s talk about the "why." Why would Mama Mboga, who’s doing just fine with her regulars, suddenly invest Sh1,000 a year for a .ke domain, plus hosting, plus design, plus maintenance? She can already take M-Pesa, advertise on Facebook, and chat with customers on WhatsApp. Owning a domain sounds like a whole other job. It’s like telling someone who just mastered hawking mandazi that they need to build a rocket ship. KENIC is trying to make it easier with AI website builders and partnerships, which is a start, but does it create demand? Or just make it marginally less painful to set up something most businesses don't feel they need? It’s like offering fancy packaging for a product nobody asked for.

Then there's the diaspora. They're appointing some "Global Ambassador" to entice them. Bless their hearts. While it's a nice thought for those wanting to keep a connection to home, it's hardly going to bridge the gap to a million. They haven't even bothered to say how many registrations this "initiative" is expected to generate. It feels like another box-ticking exercise. The real issue is making a .ke domain genuinely useful, integrated into the everyday workflow of a business, not just another add-on. Think about Safaricom's MSME services - that's where the real action is. KENIC needs to connect their domain dream to that existing digital ecosystem, not expect businesses to build their entire digital lives around a .ke address. It's a nice idea, but in Nairobi, practicality trumps ambition every time. We've seen plenty of talk about digital transformation, remember when [Kenyan celebrities were caught wearing counterfeit designer fashion]? That’s the kind of superficial engagement we often see.

The numbers are a mixed bag, as usual. Growth is happening, faster than some of our global neighbours, but the million mark is still a mirage in the desert. The real test for KENIC’s new strategy isn’t just about hitting a number; it’s about proving that owning a .ke domain is actually beneficial, not just another expense. They need to convince businesses that it’s more than just a fancy digital signpost; it’s a tool that genuinely helps them make money and grow. Until then, we’ll be here, watching the years tick by, with most of our businesses thriving on platforms that don’t require a .ke domain.
