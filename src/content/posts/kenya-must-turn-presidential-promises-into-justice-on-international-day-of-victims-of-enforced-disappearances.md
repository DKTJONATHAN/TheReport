KENYA MUST TURN PRESIDENTIAL PROMISES INTO JUSTICE ON INTERNATIONAL DAY OF VICTIMS OF ENFORCED DISAPPEARANCES

Amnesty International Kenya is shouting into the wind again, begging the government to stop making people disappear. President Ruto says nice words about rights and dignity, but down here in Nairobi, we know those are just more hot air. Another year, another report, another list of missing people.

It's good that Ruto finally acknowledged the "pain left by past violations," especially since his own cronies are probably the ones causing it. But "acknowledgment" doesn't bring back a son, a daughter, a sibling. The Constitution, this sacred document they always wave around, is clear on paper. But in practice, it’s as useful as a condom in a brothel when the powerful decide they don’t want to listen. Article 29 and 49? Please. Those are for the mwananchi, the ones who don't have the money or the connections to make problems disappear.

We're talking about real people, not just numbers. Six enforced disappearances in 2025, and 19 this year already. Add 125 police killings last year and 69 this year. This isn't some abstract human rights debate for Amnesty. This is about families who are still staring at empty chairs, wondering if their loved one is in some dark basement or dumped in a ditch somewhere. The Kenya National Commission on Human Rights presented a reparations framework back in June, but guess what? Still no compensation for the victims of these disappearances. It's the same old story: promises made, processes established, and then… silence. It’s like [Kenyan blood is the new export for Nairobi's greedy elite](/posts/kenyan-blood-is-the-new-export-for-nairobi-s-greedy-elite), but this time, it’s dignity and freedom that are being trafficked.

Ruto’s speech about not losing liberty for expressing an opinion or protesting is just that - a speech. The real test is what happens when someone actually dares to criticize the government. We've seen it since 2024. Security agencies acting like they’re above the law, making people vanish, arresting them arbitrarily, and using excessive force. This isn't protecting citizens; it’s creating a climate of fear. The State’s job is to protect us, not to be the source of our nightmares.

As the 2027 elections crawl closer, they’ll trot out these same empty promises. They talk about human dignity and social justice, but it’s all for show. The Constitution’s promise of a society founded on rights and the rule of law is a joke when the government itself is the one trampling on those rights. No one should disappear for speaking up. No family should be left guessing. And no perpetrator should get away with it. But here in Nairobi, impunity is just another currency.
