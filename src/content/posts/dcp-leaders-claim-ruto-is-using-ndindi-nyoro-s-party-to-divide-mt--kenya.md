RUTO'S DIVIDE AND CONQUER: NDINDI NYORO'S PARTY A TOOL TO SPLIT MT. KENYA, CLAIMS DCP

Rigathi Gachagua, the man who *thinks* he's the king of Mt. Kenya, is out here flapping his gums about how his Democracy for Citizens Party (DCP) is the only one that matters. He's running around Nyeri like a headless chicken, telling people not to listen to anyone else. Apparently, DCP is the only "chama" and he's the only "kiongozi." Let's see how long that little fantasy lasts.

John Methu, the DCP Secretary General, is just as bad, echoing Gachagua's nonsense and even taking a jab at Kiharu MP Ndindi Nyoro's new outfit, the People's Party of Kenya. Methu, in his infinite wisdom, accuses President Ruto of pulling strings to divide the region. He's so dramatic, calling Nyoro's party a "wheelbarrow" and threatening to "deal with it." Honestly, these guys are so predictable; it's like watching a poorly rehearsed play.

Gachagua, meanwhile, can't stop ranting about President Ruto. He's gone from complaining about Ruto trying to split Mt. Kenya to saying the opposition's grip on Ukambani is enough to foil Ruto's second term. He's even accusing Ruto of trying to redraw the "mountain" boundaries. It's all just noise, trying to sound important while grasping at straws. This whole political circus is as inspiring as that article about the [value addition fairy tale](/posts/the-value-addition-fairy-tale--how-to-starve-while-hosting-a-conference).

Even the Wiper folks tagging along are adding to the din. Kathiani MP Robert Mbui is babbling about Wiper "sweeping" while joining Gachagua's "journey." Then there's Mohammed Ali, accusing the government of sending "goons" to disrupt their rallies. If they can't even protect themselves, maybe they should just pack it in. This whole power struggle is exhausting, and frankly, it's just another mirage of progress, much like the promises of [nuclear energy's African 'development'](/posts/icone-2026--nuclear-energy-s-african--development--is-just-another-mirage).

The whole point here is just more political theatre. Gachagua and Methu are desperately trying to cling to relevance, throwing accusations around like confetti. They want you to believe they have a plan, but all you see is bickering and self-preservation.
