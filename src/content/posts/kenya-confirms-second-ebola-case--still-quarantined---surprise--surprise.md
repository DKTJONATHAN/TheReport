So, Kenya confirms its second Ebola case, barely a week after the first one landed. And who is it? A "close relative" of the original patient, conveniently found already tucked away in quarantine. It’s almost like clockwork, isn’t it? This whole "community spread" thing they're so worried about seems to be… well, contained. For now.

The first unfortunate soul, a 40-year-old who decided to grace us with his presence from the Democratic Republic of Congo, kicked the bucket on October 5th. Now, his relative, a 39-year-old, decided to join the party while already under observation. Makes you wonder how many other little surprises are lurking, doesn't it? Especially with this Bundibugyo virus having an incubation period that stretches for three weeks. Plenty of time for things to go unnoticed, especially if you’re not diligently tracking everyone who sneezes.

Dr. Wessim Mankogia from Africa CDC is out here dropping science about incubation periods and the need for 24-hour reporting. Apparently, there could be "other imported cases." You don't say. We've seen this movie before, haven't we? Remember when viral trends swept through Nairobi, only for some to be dismissed as malaria? It’s a classic Kenyan plot twist.

Meanwhile, our own Dr. Francis Nganga assures us that isolation and "supportive care" are the best we can do, since, you know, we don't actually have a cure. So, we’re isolating, caring, and hoping for the best. Just like with those [Viral Nairobi TikTok Doctor's 'Tragic End' - Kenyans Mourn Sudden Death, More Cynicism Expected](/posts/viral-nairobi-tiktok-doctor-s--tragic-end----kenyans-mourn-sudden-death--more-cynicism-expected), we’ll just have to watch and wait to see how this unfolds.

Kenya has apparently seen over 300 cases, with most turning out to be malaria. That’s a relief, I guess. But with Ebola’s long incubation period, that 21-day window is a constant little tickle of unease. And as for the rest of the world? Well, they’re still dealing with it in the DRC, and we're here, gazetting new rules and praying we don't become the next headline for all the wrong reasons.
