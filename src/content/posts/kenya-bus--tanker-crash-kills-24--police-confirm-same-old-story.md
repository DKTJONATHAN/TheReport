Twenty-four lives gone, just like that, on the main highway connecting Nairobi to Mombasa. Police chief Leonard Kimaiyo confirmed the numbers on Tuesday, detailing a midnight horror show in Kibwezi. A passenger bus, likely speeding as they always are, decided to play chicken with an oncoming tanker full of cooking oil. Predictably, it ended with wreckage, fire, and 20 adults and three children dead at the scene, with another adult following suit in hospital.

The official line? The bus driver was "trying to overtake another vehicle." As if that explains anything beyond the usual Kenyan driving madness we witness every single day. The bus was packed with families making their way back from Easter and students heading to school - just another cruel twist of fate for those who trust our roads.

Nineteen more people are injured, and Kimaiyo is already hinting the death toll will climb. Because it always does. This isn't just an accident; it's a symptom. It's the inevitable outcome of reckless driving, lax enforcement, and the general disregard for safety that plagues our transport sector. You'd think after years of these headlines, something would give, but here we are again.

It's the same old dance, isn't it? The public outcry, the brief promises, then back to business as usual until the next tragedy. One might even wonder if these crashes are just an unfortunate side effect of the constant struggle between [transport operators and the government](/posts/transport-operators-vs--govt--who-will-crumble-first--fuel-prices-continue-to-squeeze-kenyans) over regulations and profit margins. Meanwhile, ordinary Kenyans pay the ultimate price.
