Kenya Aviation Workers End Strike That Disrupted Flights, Officials Say - Big Surprise, Right?

Oh, the drama. Kenya's aviation workers, bless their hearts, have decided to grace us with their presence back in the control towers. After shutting down Jomo Kenyatta International and generally making everyone's travel plans a soggy mess, they've called off the strike. Apparently, some "return to work agreement" was signed. Don't get too excited, though; it's just a temporary ceasefire while they "negotiate." We'll see how that goes.

The whole kerfuffle, mostly air traffic controllers having a hissy fit on Sunday and Monday, left East Africa and beyond in a tailspin. Airlines were cancelling flights faster than you can say "lost luggage." Passengers were stuck, staring blankly at airport screens, probably wondering if they'd ever see their destination. It's the usual song and dance, isn't it? Just when you think things are running smoothly, someone decides to throw a spanner in the works.

Transport Minister Davis Chirchir and union boss Moss Ndiema are out there telling us they've got "clear guidelines" to deal with these suspended collective bargaining agreements from way back in 2015. Twenty-fifteen! That’s almost a decade ago. It’s almost as predictable as politicians making grand promises they never intend to keep. Remember when Karua was demanding CS Wandayi's resignation over fuel prices? [Because Kenyans are still broke](/posts/karua-demands-cs-wandayi-s-resignation-over-fuel-scandal---because-kenyans-are-still-broke), and here we are, dealing with airport chaos.

The union, Kenya Aviation Workers' Union (KAWU), had a whole laundry list of grievances: no CBA at the Civil Aviation Authority, delayed salary reviews, union dues drama at the airports authority, and even "victimisation" at Jambojet. So, they decided the best way to get it sorted was to inconvenience thousands of people. Classic. But hey, at least they're "pleading" with their members to "normalise operations." Let's just hope they don't decide to strike again because their favourite tea vendor ran out of mandazi.
