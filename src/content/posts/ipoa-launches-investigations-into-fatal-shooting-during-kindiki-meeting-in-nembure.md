IPOAPO: Kindiki Meeting in Embu Turns Deadly as Investigations Begin

WARNING: Another political gathering, another dead body. This time, two men are gone and cars are toast. The IPOA is *investigating*, which means they're looking into it. Don't hold your breath for justice.

Embu County: Where political rallies come with a side of bullets.

CRIME
KENYA, POLICE BRUTALITY, POLITICS

TRAGEDY

The Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) has, shocker of shockers, decided to launch an investigation into the chaotic scene at Nembure Stadium. Apparently, a political meeting attended by Deputy President Kithure Kindiki on September 13th somehow resulted in two men ending up dead and six vehicles turned into scrap metal. Because that's just how things roll in Kenya, isn't it? Another Sunday, another reminder that "political meeting" is often code for "potential disaster zone."

IPOA Commissioner John Muchiri, bless his optimistic heart, claims they are "closely monitoring" and will "establish what transpired." He even went on to say that anyone found culpable will face "necessary action." This sounds suspiciously like the same script we've seen play out before, much like the tales of extravagant spending that seem to drain our wallets, as seen in [Nairobi Joins AU's Biggest Spenders - Your Wallet Will Feel It](/posts/nairobi-joins-au-s-biggest-spenders---your-wallet-will-feel-it). After IPOA finishes its little review, it'll be handed over to the Director of Public Prosecutions. More bureaucracy, more waiting.

The families, understandably, are devastated. Margaret Muthanje, aunt to the late Moses Munene, wants justice for her "hardworking young man" who apparently had a "promising future." A future that, predictably, was cut short by the usual Kenyan cocktail of political ambition and state-sanctioned chaos. She's calling for "thorough and impartial investigation." Bless her heart again.

Muchiri also made a point about holding police officers accountable for using "excessive force." This is the part where you can almost hear the collective eye-roll from anyone who's lived in this country for more than five minutes. The system is designed to protect itself. Let's see if any of those "necessary actions" actually materialize beyond press releases and photo ops. Until then, expect more of the same.
