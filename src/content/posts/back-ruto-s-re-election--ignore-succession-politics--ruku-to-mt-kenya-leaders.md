BACK RUTO'S RE-ELECTION, IGNORE SUCCESSION POLITICS: RUKU TELL MT KENYA LEADERS (OR MAYBE NOT)

CS Ruku is out here telling Mt Kenya leaders to stop their petty squabbles and back Ruto for 2027. Apparently, succession politics are "divisive" and will ruin the region's "development agenda." Because, you know, a politician has never ever used "development" as a smokescreen for their own ambitions. He’s at a church service, no less, probably hoping some divine intervention will make everyone fall in line.

He’s waxing lyrical about Ruto’s commitment, as if the President isn’t busy trying to secure his own future. Ruku’s warning about "political hostility" weakening Mt Kenya’s influence sounds suspiciously like a threat - if you don’t play ball, you don’t get your share. He's got the nerve to say they "will inform our people on what the government is doing," as if the public is a bunch of toddlers who need spoon-feeding. Honestly, sometimes I wonder if these guys believe their own press releases.

Then he gets to the UDA faithful, telling them not to be "intimidated" by the "political contest." Intimidated? They’re probably too busy counting their chickens before they hatch. Ruku wants them to "engage residents on the government’s development initiatives." Translation: go out there and sell the dream, even if it’s built on quicksand. This whole spiel about unity and development is starting to sound suspiciously like the same old song and dance we heard before the last election. Remember when we heard about Ruto’s grand vision plans? Ruku Urges Mt Kenya Leaders to Back Ruto's Re-election Amidst Succession Squabbles

Cabinet Secretary Geoffrey Ruku has issued a stern warning to Mt Kenya leaders, urging them to put aside their succession politics and rally behind President William Ruto’s re-election bid in 2027. Speaking at a church service in Limuru, Kiambu County, Ruku emphasized that divisive politics could derail the region’s development agenda. He accused "petty leaders full of hatred" of misleading the public and pledged to keep residents informed about the government’s achievements in their area. Ruku also encouraged UDA-allied leaders not to be intimidated by political competition, urging them to focus on promoting the government's development initiatives instead of dwelling on political differences. He lauded Deputy President Kithure Kindiki as a crucial figure in advancing Mt Kenya's interests, citing an "elder's mandate" for Kindiki to foster unity and development.
