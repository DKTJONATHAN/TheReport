MP Looks For Fan Who Photographed Him On Old Keypad Phone - What A Joke

Politicians are desperate for attention, and this MP is no different. He's hunting for a fan who dared to use an ancient keypad phone to snap his picture, proving once again that some people will do anything for a laugh, or worse, a vote.

The whole circus unfolded at a Democratic Change Party (DCP) rally, where MP John Gitonga Mukunji, draped in his party colours, was busy playing to the crowd. Amidst the sea of modern smartphones, one determined supporter pulls out a relic - a JX keypad phone, the kind our grandparents probably threw away years ago. The contrast was so stark, it was practically a political statement in itself.

Instead of ignoring this blast from the past, Gitonga decides to make a public spectacle of it, taking to Facebook to find the owner. Apparently, this is what passes for heartwarming news these days. It’s a testament to the wave of, well, whatever it is, sweeping Central Kenya, championed by the ousted Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua. The rally itself was apparently a big show, full of enthusiastic supporters, eager to be seen with the "right" people.

Meanwhile, in another corner of Nairobi, Lang'ata MP Jalang'o is busy grandstanding about fundraising paybills. He’s criticising Senator Edwin Sifuna’s efforts to raise funds, arguing the government should be footing the bill. This is rich, coming from politicians who are often the reason for such financial woes. While they’re busy congratulating themselves on planned investments, the rest of us are left to wonder if any of this actually benefits anyone beyond their own circles. It’s a familiar dance, this political theatre, reminding one of how easily political discourse can devolve into [‘No amount of insults, noise will chase me from Mt. Kenya’: Ruto tells opposition](/posts/no-amount-of-insults--noise-will-chase-me-from-mt--kenya---ruto-tells-opposition).

The whole keypad phone saga and Jalang'o's pronouncements are just more proof that the political class is out of touch. They’re either chasing after outdated technology for a photo op or lecturing us on financial responsibility while embroiled in their own games. It's a sad reflection of where we are, with our leaders more concerned with optics and petty squabbles than actual governance.







The lengths some politicians will go to for a bit of limelight is truly something else. This Manyatta MP, John Gitonga Mukunji, has gone and made it his mission to track down a supporter who dared to capture his image on a JX-brand keypad phone, a device most of us retired to the dusty drawers of history years ago. The incident occurred at a Democratic Change Party (DCP) rally, where Gitonga, dressed to impress in the party's signature green, was basking in the adoration of the crowd.

As supporters scrambled to record the moment on their fancy smartphones, one determined individual pulled out a relic. The sheer contrast was, apparently, too much for the MP to ignore. Instead of a chuckle or a shrug, Gitonga decided to turn this into a public quest, posting his search on Facebook. It's a move that some Kenyans have somehow found "heartwarming," which tells you more about the state of public sentiment than anything else.

This whole charade is happening amidst a supposed "DCP wave" in Central Kenya, apparently fronted by the recently impeached former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua. The rally, we're told, was packed and buzzing with enthusiasm. It seems that in politics, even a photograph taken with a phone that barely makes calls can become a talking point, a sign of the times, or perhaps, just a sign of desperation for relevance.

And then there’s the other drama unfolding. Lang'ata MP Jalang'o is making waves by criticising Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna's fundraising paybill. Jalang'o, during a meeting after President William Ruto addressed health workers, declared that the government should be funding such initiatives itself. He’s all for planned investments, naturally, while simultaneously picking apart a fellow politician’s attempt to raise funds. It’s a classic political move: seem like you're fighting for the common good while keeping your own hands clean and your own image polished. This entire spectacle makes one wonder if there's any genuine concern for the populace, or if it's all just a meticulously staged performance, much like some might argue about the grand pronouncements concerning [political movements and their leaders](/posts/no-amount-of-insults--noise-will-chase-me-from-mt--kenya---ruto-tells-opposition).

The constant bickering and the search for validation through outdated technology or public criticism only serve to underscore how disconnected these leaders are from the realities faced by ordinary Kenyans. While they’re busy playing games and seeking validation, the real issues remain largely unaddressed, lost in the noise of political theatre.
