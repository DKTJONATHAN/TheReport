So, Aden Duale, the esteemed Health Cabinet Secretary, has decided his true calling isn't public health but rather international voter recruitment. He's openly admitting he'll be "mobilising Somalis from Somalia" to support President Ruto's 2027 re-election bid. Apparently, after 25 years in politics, he's figured out the best way to win an election is to import voters. It’s a bold strategy, Cotton, let's see if it pays off.

Duale’s logic, if you can call it that, hinges on the idea that the Ruto administration has been a golden age for Muslims and minority communities, particularly Somalis. He’s bragging about how much easier it is now to get Kenyan ID cards, passports, and birth certificates. Gone are the days of "lengthy vetting processes" that apparently stretched "from here to Ethiopia." Now, thanks to "God-given" President Ruto, these crucial documents are handed out like free samples, no pesky questions from NIS or DCI. This is the same government that’s been grappling with a fuel crisis that’s stealing Kenya's gains, but hey, at least getting a national ID is a breeze.

He’s essentially saying that anyone who opposes Ruto must be doing so because they don't like the idea of people from Somalia getting Kenyan documents. It’s a classic deflection, isn't it? Instead of addressing any real policy issues, it’s all about who gets what ID and where they're from. Duale even throws in a jab at the ODM, blaming Edwin Sifuna’s appointment as SG for the party's alleged decline. Because clearly, the fortunes of a major political party rest solely on the shoulders of its secretary general, not on any actual political strategy or public sentiment.

The man is clearly laying the groundwork for a political campaign where the borders of Kenya become rather blurry. He’s even roped in Mombasa businessman Abubakar Joho, who reminisces about helping Somali refugees in the 90s. It's a heartwarming tale of community spirit, sure, but framed against Duale's current announcement, it sounds less like humanitarian aid and more like strategic voter acquisition. The current government seems to have a knack for these "bold" moves, much like when Murkomen claimed the country was safe while police denied gang ties.

Governor Abdulswamad Nassir’s call for equal treatment rings hollow in the face of Duale's explicit plans. While Nassir speaks of diversity being a strength, Duale is busy building a voting bloc that crosses international lines. The entire situation just screams of the same old political theatre - promises, accusations, and the ever-present pursuit of power, regardless of the practical implications or the potential for further division. It’s Nairobi realism at its finest: expect the unexpected, especially when it comes to political maneuvering.
