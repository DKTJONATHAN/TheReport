So, Bungoma Governor Kenneth Lusaka is out there in Amung'ura, Teso South, doing the usual rounds, trying to convince people that President Ruto's first term was some kind of golden age. He's rattling off projects like they're already finished and shining, talking about "visible transformation" and a "strong foundation." Honestly, it sounds like the same old script we've heard for decades. Development can reach every part of Kenya, he says. Right. And a unicorn can win the Derby.

He's peddling the affordable housing dream, the dual carriageway to Malaba, the SGR extension. All these fancy projects, designed to transform lives. But for who? For the select few who can actually afford a piece of this pie, while the rest of us are still struggling to put food on the table. It’s easy to champion these grand schemes when you’re not the one staring at an empty fridge, or when your own pockets are already lined. It’s a classic case of "look over there!" while the real issues are ignored, much like how we've seen global powers stumble while ignoring domestic chaos.

The deputy governor and the Teso South MP are chiming in, urging everyone to forget about who gets to be Deputy President in 2027 and just focus on delivering votes for Ruto. Unite the region, they say. But what about uniting the people with tangible results, not just empty promises? They're touting "meaningful change" and "projects on the ground," but for many, these are just dusty construction sites that never seem to get finished. The women's economic empowerment forum, giving capital to groups to move away from illicit brews? That’s a nice touch, but it feels like a band-aid on a gaping wound, a way to placate and distract.

The whole strategy seems to be about linking Ruto's re-election to "visible development" and "continuity." It’s a well-worn path, a political gamble that relies on people having short memories and a low tolerance for anything resembling actual opposition. They’re banking on the idea that people will judge leaders by "tangible results" rather than "political rhetoric." Well, Governor, let's see those tangible results *before* asking for a second term, shall we? Because right now, it feels like we're just being sold more hot air.
