Ruto Vows Action on "Goons" Amidst Growing ICC Pressure Over Kenya's Political Mess

The President is finally noticing the dust kicked up by the political goons, promising to sort them out. All this, of course, comes as the whispers about the International Criminal Court (ICC) grow louder, thanks to a string of violent incidents that are making Kenya look less like a democracy and more like a boxing ring ahead of the 2027 elections. He’s talking about peace and stability, which is nice and all, but let’s be honest, when has a politician’s promise ever stopped the chaos?

His government, naturally, is pushing back against any international meddling. They’re saying Kenya can handle its own dirty laundry, that our police and courts are perfectly capable. Musalia Mudavadi, the man with the fancy title and even fancier pronouncements, is telling everyone to strengthen local institutions instead of dragging Kenya’s name through the mud abroad. He even threw in President Ruto’s past ICC run as proof of his commitment to the rule of law. Sure, facing the music once doesn't mean you won't start the orchestra again.

Meanwhile, the opposition is practically banging on the ICC’s door, listing incidents from Rongai to Homa Bay. Martha Karua is calling it a "call to action" against electoral violence, pointing out that these aren't just random scuffles between rival supporters. They’re asking how these armed mobs can just spring up and attack anyone - politicians, journalists, even police. It’s almost as if someone’s pulling the strings, and not in a good way. This whole song and dance about political violence reminds me of that time we discussed [Can They Actually Order Anyone to Do Anything?](/posts/kenya-parliament--can-they-actually-order-anyone-to-do-anything) in Parliament.

The ICC prosecutor is being flooded with information, with groups like Vocal Africa flagging "political intolerance" and "organised violence" as red flags. They’re listing incidents from Homa Bay where people died and journalists were attacked, all the way to Nairobi and Kisumu. The International Commission of Jurists and even groups tracking conflict are issuing warnings about gang mobilization and how this Homa Bay fiasco could be a taste of what's to come. It’s a real circus, and we’re all just paying for the front-row seats.

Even Rigathi Gachagua, the former DP, who’s been crying foul about the government not acting against these goons, has dismissed Ruto’s promises as mere talk. He’s seen his convoy attacked with axes and stones while police apparently watched. The government insists they've got it covered, but the opposition and civil society groups are convinced this is more than just petty crime. They’re looking for a pattern, a systematic campaign that might actually meet the ICC’s high bar for crimes against humanity.

So, here we are, waiting to see if these incidents will be treated as just another Kenyan political drama or if they'll escalate into something that forces the world’s attention. Ruto says he'll "deal with" the goons, but the real question is, will his actions speak louder than his words, or will this just be another election cycle filled with the same old threats and the same old chaos?
