So, Rigathi Gachagua is now *shocked* that the government he was part of, and now criticizes, is still using the same old tricks? He’s on X, crying about President Ruto and Kipchumba Murkomen breaking their "promise" not to allow goons. Apparently, "no goons anywhere in Kenya" was just more lip service, typical political theatre. He alleges the government is still relying on hired muscle to scare off opponents. Honestly, who’s surprised? It's the same script, different actors, just like watching the same old drama unfold in the tech world, wouldn't you say? [RECEIPTS OR RETRACTIONS? THE MESSY TECH DRAMA IN THE MATLALA CASE](/posts/receipts-or-retractions--the-messy-tech-drama-in-the-matlala-case).

The man’s convoy got mobbed with axes and rocks in Nakuru, and he’s pointing fingers at Susan Kihika and Samuel Arama. He claims the police just stood there and watched. The only thing that saved him and his media pals was "wananchi" stepping in. So, the people who are supposed to be protected by the state are now the ones providing security? That’s rich. It just goes to show that when the government loses its grip, the streets become the arbiters of power.

Gachagua’s argument is that this violence is just a sign of a government desperate for attention, having lost public support. They’re resorting to thuggery because they can’t win hearts and minds anymore. It's the classic tactic of the cornered politician - if you can’t persuade them, intimidate them. He even went on to warn that this "goonism" is bad for Kenya's image, scaring off tourists and investors. As if investors haven't seen this movie before.

He's now saying that tourists, foreigners, and investors "have to accept that goonism is now a way of life." That’s a beautiful way of saying the government has completely surrendered to chaos. And his solution? The 2027 election. He’s banking on Kenyans to fix this mess at the ballot. Meanwhile, his supporters in Njoro, Molo, and Rongai are promising to provide their own protection. So, it's not just about politics anymore; it's about survival of the fittest on Kenyan soil.

This whole spectacle is just another chapter in the never ending Kenyan political soap opera. Heightened tensions, competing claims - it's all just noise. Gachagua’s now the opposition voice, calling out the very system he was a part of. It’s a familiar song and dance, and the tune hasn't changed much over the years. The promises are hollow, the violence is real, and the public is left to fend for themselves.

This is the reality of Kenyan politics: always promising change, always delivering more of the same. The goons might change their outfits, but their job remains the same. And the politicians? They’ll keep making noise, pointing fingers, and hoping the next election will magically fix everything. Don't hold your breath.
