RUTO'S POLICE WARN POLITICIANS: THINK YOU'RE ABOVE THE LAW? THINK AGAIN.

Nairobi's finest, the National Police Service, have issued a stern, albeit predictably toothless, warning to Kenya's political class. After presiding over the passing-out parade of over 6,000 new recruits - future enforcers of whatever the current regime deems lawful - President Ruto's men are finally noticing the usual Kenyan political circus: the thuggery, the intolerance, the organized goons turning rallies into gladiatorial arenas. They're reminding everyone, with the gravitas of a broken record, that public order matters.

Of course, the practicalities of this newfound police zeal are laid bare in the details. Need to hold a rally or a procession? Give them three to fourteen days' notice. Full names, addresses, venue, date, and time. And don't even think about overlapping with another group’s pre-booked slot - you'll be rescheduled, probably to a time when no one actually cares. This is less about public order and more about controlling the narrative, ensuring only government-sanctioned noise pollution is permitted. It's a classic move to stifle dissent before it even gets a chance to breathe, a tactic that echoes past political machinations.

The NPS, in their infinite wisdom and newfound commitment to "competence, professionalism and integrity," vow to deal "firmly" with anyone disrupting lawful gatherings or sponsoring criminal gangs. This includes the hired youth or "goons" that have become as common a feature of Kenyan political rallies as unfulfilled promises. They claim to be apolitical, yet they're the ones who will be deciding whose "lawful gathering" gets priority, and who is disrupting what. One can only imagine the selective enforcement that will follow, as has happened before, turning what should be basic rights into a bureaucratic obstacle course.

It’s a predictable dance. Politicians play their usual games of disruption and intimidation, stretching from open grounds to even places of worship, and the police issue pronouncements about upholding the rule of law. They remind us that "no person, regardless of social status, political affiliation, or public office, is above the law." A noble sentiment, truly, but one that’s rarely applied with equal measure in this country. Let's see how "firmly" they deal with the well-connected sponsors of chaos, and how quickly they’ll shut down legitimate opposition when it inconveniences the powers that be. It’s a broken system, and these pronouncements are just more window dressing.
