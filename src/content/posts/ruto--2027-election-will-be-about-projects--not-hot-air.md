Ruto: 2027 Election Will Be About Projects, Not Hot Air

Don't get your hopes up, Nairobi. President Ruto is telling us that come 2027, Kenyans will somehow magically forget all the noise and instead meticulously examine every pothole filled and every market stall built. He was at some church do in Machakos, telling folks that development records will be the ultimate decider, not the usual tribal rants and insults we've come to expect. Apparently, the days of sweet talk and empty promises are over. Who are we to argue with divine inspiration, right?

He's promising development for *all* regions, not just the ones that reliably send him to power. Rift Valley, Eastern, Central, Nyanza - apparently, they'll all get their fair share. It's a beautiful vision, isn't it? A Kenya where every corner gets a taste of progress. It's almost enough to make you believe it, if you haven't been living under a rock. Remember those girls "rescued" from a Gulf job trap? Sometimes, the promises of rescue and progress sound a lot alike until you dig a little deeper. [Njoro Police 'Rescue' 67 Girls From Gulf Job Trap - Don't Hold Your Breath](/posts/njoro-police--rescue--67-girls-from-gulf-job-trap---don-t-hold-your-breath)

The President also seems to be calling for an end to divisive politics, asking leaders to unite Kenyans instead of pushing them apart. He wants politics that "unites the people of our nation because it is God’s will." Bless his heart. Let's see how long that pious sentiment lasts when the election drums start beating louder. Unity is a lovely concept, but it’s rarely the currency that buys votes in this country.

And then there’s this whole "Beyond Vision 2030" thing. Ruto is roping in the Church to help shape this grand, long-term development agenda. Because, obviously, what we need now is more committees, more visions, and more strategic planning. Meanwhile, he was also out launching a road and inspecting a market. Because, you know, tangible things. It's all very impressive on paper, if you ignore the persistent whispers of how some influencers peddle illusions of wealth. So, there you have it. Ruto wants us to focus on what leaders *do*, not what they *say*. It’s a nice thought, a very clean, optimistic thought. We’ll just have to wait and see if the development record can truly trump the age-old art of political manipulation and ethnic appeals. My money's still on the hot air, but hey, maybe this time will be different. Stranger things have happened, I suppose. Like a Kenyan football team actually winning a European title, which, speaking of dreams diminishing, seems about as likely as a politician keeping all their campaign promises.
