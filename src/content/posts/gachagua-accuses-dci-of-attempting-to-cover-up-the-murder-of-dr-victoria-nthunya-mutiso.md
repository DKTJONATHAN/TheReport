Gachagua Accuses DCI of Covering Up Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso Murder

*Gachagua claims DCI is trying to protect police officers involved in Dr. Mutiso's killing.*

"Another Day, Another Drama: Gachagua Claims DCI Is Covering Up Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso Murder"

This whole thing is just a Nairobi special, isn't it? Former Deputy President Rigathi Gachagua, bless his cynical heart, is out here pointing fingers at the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI), claiming they're trying to sweep the murder of Dr. Victoria Nthunya Mutiso under the rug. He's even threatening to drop names if they don't cough up the details themselves. Frankly, I'm not surprised. This city thrives on whispers and backroom deals, and the DCI playing innocent is just another episode in the never ending drama.

Gachagua, with his usual flair for the theatrical, alleges the DCI is planning to pin the whole thing on some poor boda boda riders while the real culprits, the police officers, waltz away scot free. He claims he's getting intel straight from the horse's mouth - the very officers supposedly involved. He's demanding the DCI name names, specifically an inspector and two constables from Kikuyu Police Station and Karinde Police Post. It’s almost like a poorly written script where everyone knows the ending.

Meanwhile, DCI boss Amin Mohamed has his own version of events. He’s admitting that Kenya Police Service officers were involved, even identifying one Inspector Kenneth Kipkemboi Sang as the "link person" and source of the murder weapon. Apparently, this Sang character is now on the run. Then there's Rose Mbinde Mulwa and her son Christopher Mulwa, conveniently labeled as the masterminds and financiers. It's a convenient narrative, isn't it? Everyone gets a role, except perhaps the truth.

But wait, there's more! DCI Amin also claims they've found the trigger man: PC Elijah Kibelion Kimoi, attached to Karinde Police Post. He’s been arrested and, surprise surprise, forensically placed at the scene. It’s all neatly tied up, a classic case of blame-shifting and narrative control that we’ve seen play out time and again in this city. It makes you wonder if any of these stories ever truly end with justice, or if it's just about managing public perception, a bit like how people trade influence for algorithms in the digital space these days. Gachagua’s outburst, while dramatic, highlights the deep-seated distrust many have in the very institutions meant to protect them. He’s demanding that a so-called "Nairobi Sierra killer squad" be allowed to testify, which sounds more like a bad action movie plot than a real investigation. Ultimately, this is just another sordid tale from Nairobi, where the powerful try to manipulate the truth and the rest of us are left to pick through the debris, wondering who’s really pulling the strings.
