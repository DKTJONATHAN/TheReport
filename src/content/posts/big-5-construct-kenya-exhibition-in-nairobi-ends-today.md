BIG 5 CONSTRUCT KENYA EXHIBITION: NAIROBI'S BUILDING SHOW CONCLUDES, PROMISES MORE OF THE SAME OLD CONCRETE

WARNING: Another year, another construction expo closes its doors in Nairobi. While they talk about Vision 2030 and affordable housing, remember our reality.

The Sarit Expo Centre saw its fair share of suits and shiny brochures this week, all for Big 5 Construct Kenya. Three days of talk about advancing the construction sector, all under the grand banner of Kenya Vision 2030. Dr. Erick Rutto, some chamber of commerce bigwig, kicked it off with Kennedy Onditi and a parade of international trade folks from Italy and Egypt. They all waxed lyrical about attracting investment and strengthening local manufacturing. It's the same song and dance, year after year.

Josine Heijmans from dmg events chimed in, calling Kenya one of East Africa's most active construction markets. She's right, of course. We build a lot. Mostly overpriced boxes that few can afford, but hey, they're projects. With over 150 exhibitors from 25 countries - China, Egypt, Italy, Türkiye, and a new friend, Germany - they showcased everything from sanitaryware to building systems. It's a marketplace for the same old solutions, dressed up as innovation.

This year, they’re pushing this "sustainability" angle with the "Big 5 Impact Trail." They're talking about VOC-free paints and energy-efficient panels. Cute. Meanwhile, back on the streets, our actual buildings are falling apart, and the only "impact" most Kenyans feel is the rising cost of that concrete. It’s a bit like talking about the future of travel while conveniently forgetting about the SGR travel hacks and the audacity of clout that plagues our public transport.

Then came the "Big 5 Talks," a panel of experts dissecting how to build confidence in Kenya’s changing construction landscape. They discussed risk management, regulatory compliance, and contractual obligations. Dr. Cecilia Wandiga even said managing risk is about building trust and accountability. It sounds good on paper, but for most of us, it's just another layer of jargon before the inevitable dispute or shoddy workmanship. Susan Elisha talked about pricing professional expertise fairly, which is hilarious given how many "professionals" cut corners.

The National Construction Authority's acting CEO, Qs. Susan Rutto, stressed the need for quality, safe, and sustainable buildings. Collaboration, investment in skills, and evolving practices were the buzzwords. It's all about strengthening standards. While they gather to exchange expertise and explore emerging solutions, remember that for many, the "construction supply chain" means trying to get a decent house built without being fleeced.
