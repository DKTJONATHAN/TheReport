So, Deputy President Kithure Kindiki is out there, holding court with thirty thousand elders in Embu, trying to convince everyone that his seat is, you know, *occupied*. Apparently, there's a lot of noise from people "eyeing the position," but Kindiki, the brave soldier of the state, assures them he's too busy serving Kenyans - and, by extension, President Ruto and the government’s success - to be bothered. It’s a real nail-biter, this game of political musical chairs.

These elders, a formidable bloc from Meru, Tharaka Nithi, Embu, Kirinyaga, and Isiolo, have decided to throw their weight behind Kindiki, declaring him the political pointman for Mt. Kenya and demanding that anyone wanting to talk to the region must go through him. They've even gone and issued a "historic resolution" demanding respect for the DP, citing his "political seniority" and "maturity." It's all about reciprocity, you see. Decades of supporting leaders from Mt. Kenya West, and now it's time for them to return the favor, "unconditionally." Funny how political solidarity seems to come with a hefty price tag.

Kindiki, himself a Njuri Ncheke elder, is playing the victim card too, lamenting the "insults and verbal abuse" he's receiving, especially from his own region. He even had the audacity to bring up his predecessor, Rigathi Gachagua, implying Gachagua hurled insults at him despite Kindiki showing him "respect" when he was Deputy President. It’s a real pity party, isn't it? While they're busy patting themselves on the back, one wonders if they've forgotten about the bigger issues, like the [Ruto's And Friends Ksh2.1 Trillion Theft: Auditor General Exposes Biggest Government Heist Since Independence](/posts/ruto-and-friends-ksh-2-trillion-theft-from-2022-to-2025) that’s likely still draining the national coffers.

Despite the chest-thumping and the demands for respect, Kindiki did offer a glimmer of hope by agreeing to the elders' call for structured talks with their Mt. Kenya West counterparts. He also promised to push for development projects, including a dual-carriageway and bridge redesign. And, of course, the pièce de résistance: voting for President Ruto next year is "voting for ourselves." It’s always about the votes, isn't it? The elders are backing the DP, the DP is backing the President, and the ordinary Kenyan is left to foot the bill, hoping that somehow, somewhere, all this political maneuvering translates into something tangible beyond well-attended rallies and pronouncements.
