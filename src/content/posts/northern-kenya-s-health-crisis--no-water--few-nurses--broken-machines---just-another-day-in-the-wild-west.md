So, the senators finally bothered to look up north and found what any Nairobi realist already knows: our healthcare system is a joke, especially in Mandera, Wajir, and Marsabit. They're talking about grounded ambulances, dialysis machines that haven't seen a functioning circuit in years, and staff who are either AWOL or moonlighting at their private clinics while the public pays their salaries. Honestly, it's almost impressive how thoroughly they've managed to dismantle basic human services.

And the "facilities"? Leaking roofs, missing light bulbs, exposed wiring - it’s like a horror movie set, but the only thing being scared is your life expectancy. In Wajir, the maternity wards are so dry there’s no running water, which I guess is one way to "control" infections. Marsabit's women's wards apparently don't even have bathrooms inside. Dignity? Hygiene? Those are luxuries for people who don't live in the counties the government "forgot." It reminds me of that old piece about how [Your Payslip is a Crime Scene, and the Government is Holding the Knife](/posts/your-payslip-is-a-crime-scene--and-the-government-is-holding-the-knife), except this is about dying before you even get to the "crime scene."

Then there's the "staffing shortage." It's not just a shortage; it's a full-blown desert. Critical units are running on fumes, and they’ve got fancy equipment gathering dust because there’s no one trained to operate it. And the cherry on top? Untrained personnel, including community health promoters who wouldn't know a paracetamol from a poison, are doling out meds. Patient safety? Quality of care? Those are just abstract concepts for the folks up north, apparently.

The report even talks about medicine being "diverted" to private pharmacies. Shocking, I know. Meanwhile, expired drugs are found lying around like stray dogs, sometimes in the most unhygienic places imaginable. And how do they fund all this chaos? By pouring money into recurrent expenditure - salaries, salaries, and more salaries. Development? Infrastructure? Equipment? That’s for the next budget cycle, which, knowing Kenya, will never come. It’s a miracle anyone survives long enough to be disappointed.

The report makes it clear: ambulances are repurposed as storage units, diagnostic tests are sent miles away because local labs are useless, and the referral systems are so broken, mothers arrive in critical condition because the roads are so bad and the system so incompetent. It’s a masterclass in how *not* to run a country. The senators wagged their fingers, but we all know nothing will change. It never does.
