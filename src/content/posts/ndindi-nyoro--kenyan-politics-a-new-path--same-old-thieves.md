So, Ndindi Nyoro, the man who was practically glued to Ruto's wheelbarrow, has suddenly remembered he has a heart for Kenyans and has decided to "break ranks." Apparently, months of prayer and consultation led him to the dazzling revelation that governing is harder than campaigning. What a shocker! It's almost as if he hadn't noticed the "trial and error" happening right under his nose in government. But hey, at least he's joined the "opposition" now, complete with a fancy new party name and a policy blueprint longer than my last rent increase notice.

He's been busy hobnobbing with pretty much everyone in the opposition camp - Gachagua, Matiang'i, Kalonzo, Karua, Orengo, even Sifuna he calls "brother." It's a real reunion of the disgruntled and the ambitious. One wonders if they were comparing notes on how to best exploit the Kenyan public's trust or just arguing over who gets the choicest cabinet positions post-2027. His claim that this isn't about personal ambition is about as believable as a politician promising to cut taxes.

Nyoro's sudden epiphany about the struggles of ordinary citizens is… convenient. After being a key figure in the "wheelbarrow government," his newfound burden for the "aspirations of Kenyans" feels a bit like a crocodile lamenting the scarcity of gazelles. He’s talking about delivering "after the August 2027 election," as if that’s some guaranteed future. This is the same old song and dance we've heard a million times. Remember when they promised so much and we ended up with things like the Shiny Portals, Same Old Thieves scam?

His grand policy blueprint covers everything from education to energy. It’s a buffet of promises, designed to distract from the fact that the system itself is rigged. It’s easy to talk about fixing debt management when you're not the one holding the purse strings, or when you're hoping to be the one controlling them next. This is just another political realignment, another attempt to capture power by whichever means necessary.

He’s not quite saying he’ll run for president, but the hints are as subtle as a matatu tout's "special offer." The "People's Party of Kenya" is supposed to be about the people, but in Kenyan politics, "the people" usually just means the people who vote for the party leader. It’s a carousel of political opportunists, each claiming to be the savior, while the rest of us are left to deal with the consequences.

It’s a shame, really. You’d think after all this time, some of these politicians would learn that true change isn’t about forming a new party or switching sides. It’s about genuine accountability and a system that actually works for everyone, not just the connected few. But that, my friends, is a story for another day, and frankly, one I doubt we’ll ever see unfold in this country.
