So, the big brains in the security world have finally noticed what we already know: al Shabaab and their mates aren't just blowing things up, they're also brilliant at making money. We’re talking extortion, money laundering, even using your mobile money to fund their nonsense. They’ve got networks stretching from here to Yemen, and they’re getting smarter while our coordination stays stuck in neutral. It’s like trying to stop a flood with a leaky bucket.

These groups have figured out how to blur the lines between terrorism and organized crime. It’s not just about ideology anymore; it's about business. They're taxing everything from sugar to real estate, raking in an estimated $200 million a year. And why not? Our borders are porous, the government is often busy with its own dramas - remember when [99 Congolese 'Protesters' Nabbed in Nairobi During Africa-France Summit - Police Say It Was All for Security](/posts/99-congolese--protesters--nabbed-in-nairobi-during-africa-france-summit---police-say-it-was-all-for-security)? Security theater is alive and well while the real problems fester.

The FGS is making some noise about reforms, freezing accounts and what not. Good for them. But al Shabaab just shrugs it off and finds new ways to get their cash. They're adapting faster than our slow-moving bureaucracy. It's a global game now, with these groups linking up with Houthis, getting training, and moving arms across the sea like it's a local market run. The whole region is becoming a playground for international militants and criminals.

And then there's IS-S, a smaller player but still a headache, even managing to fund other global affiliates. They're squeezing cash out of extortion and illicit goods, and now they’ve wised up to crypto and online gaming. Even when local forces like Puntland manage to land a blow, these groups have a knack for bouncing back, like a bad penny.

The real joke is the talk of regional coordination. It’s like a broken record. Our financial intelligence units are supposed to be the heroes, but the reports they get are often useless - lacking details, coming too late, or just plain missing. And don't even get me started on the private sector's reporting compliance. It's loose, inconsistent, and often just for show. Fear of getting caught themselves means some businesses don't report anything.

Honestly, trying to apply fancy financial regulations designed for stable economies to our chaotic reality is like trying to put a suit on a camel. These digital platforms, from mobile money to crypto, have moved faster than any laws we’ve got. So while they're planning their next big move with our money, we're still trying to figure out how to file the right paperwork. Just another day in paradise.
