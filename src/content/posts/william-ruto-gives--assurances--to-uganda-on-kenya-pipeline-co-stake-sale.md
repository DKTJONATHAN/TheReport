Ruto's "Assurances" to Kampala: Kenya Pipeline's Future Up for Grabs, Just Like Everything Else

A warning to anyone who thought Kenya was done selling off its assets: think again. President William Ruto is busy making promises to Uganda about the Kenya Pipeline Company, while simultaneously planning to offload two-thirds of the state's shares. Looks like "assurances" are just fancy words for "we're getting rid of it, but don't worry, your neighbours will still be able to afford their fuel imports... for now."

The Lebanese contractor Zakhem, embroiled in a messy dispute over the Mombasa Nairobi oil pipeline launched way back in 2018, is apparently desperate for cash. And guess who's looking to negotiate a "reduced settlement"? That's right, our own government. It’s a classic Nairobi dance: problems pile up, everyone denies responsibility, and then a "settlement" magically appears, likely at the taxpayer's expense. This whole saga smells as bad as those substandard fuel imports the police are so sluggishly investigating.

Speaking of investigations, the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission has finally decided to step in, probably after realising the police were more interested in their chai breaks than actual justice. They might have limited powers, but they're not afraid of stepping on the toes of cabinet members who seem to have presidential backing. The president's allies are fuming about the removal of "senior officials" after the fuel scandal, calling it a double standard. Well, surprise, surprise. It seems some people are more expendable than others when the presidential ego needs stroking. It’s one of the government's "most profitable assets," which means, of course, it’s ripe for privatization. Because why should the public benefit from something that actually makes money when it can be handed over to some well-connected consultant to cream off the top?
