WOCCU, WFCU Chip In $2.5 Million For Kenyan SMEs, Banks Still Don't Care

**WARNING:** Another initiative promises to throw money at small businesses. Don't hold your breath waiting for it to solve all your problems.

**CAPTION:** More forms to fill, more promises to break.







So, the World Council of Credit Unions and their pals at Worldwide Foundation of Credit Unions have apparently found $2.5 million for 93 Kenyan small and medium enterprises. Ninety-three. That's barely enough to keep a decent boda-boda fleet running for a month, let alone ignite a business revolution. They're patting themselves on the back for "expanding access to capital" for folks traditional banks "often overlooked or turned away." Shocker. Banks are businesses, they want profit, not charity for your roadside stall.

This whole song and dance is about convincing SACCOs - Unaitas, Boresha, Kenya Police SACCO, the usual suspects - that lending to SMEs isn't a one-way ticket to the poorhouse. Apparently, the issue wasn't just a lack of collateral, but that these SACCOs were too dim to figure out if a business could actually, you know, *pay them back*. So, WOCCU swooped in with "technical assistance" to teach them to look at cash flow. Revolutionary stuff, I tell you. It's like teaching a fish to swim. The result? A whopping less than 1% in non-performing loans. Astonishing. It’s almost like when you give people money to run businesses, they actually *run* them. Who knew?

We've got stories like Gaceri Wallace, who apparently expanded her electrical shop thanks to a loan. Sales went from KES 1.5 million to KES 2.0 million. That's a nice bump, sure, but let's not pretend she's suddenly a multinational. And Geoffrey Kagwanja Karuma, who makes animal feed. His sales are KES 1.6 million. Good for him. It’s a nice trickle-down effect for local farmers, I suppose. Meanwhile, the rest of us are still trying to figure out how to afford a decent meal, much like the issues highlighted in [Benni McCarthy: Harambee Stars Showed Character, Lost Anyway](/posts/benni-mccarthy--harambee-stars-showed-character--lost-anyway) where the outcome was ultimately disappointing despite some effort.

Beatrice Mathu from Unaitas is quoted saying, "The business of the member is more important than the collateral." That’s rich. They probably said that after they finally figured out how to assess risk. And Nephat Murimi is talking about a "dedicated SME strategy." They're going to roll out new appraisal tools. Good for them. Meanwhile, the SACCO sector is sitting on KES 1 trillion. A lot of money, a lot of potential, and a lot of people still struggling to get a piece of it. They'll distribute five training manuals - SME market research, loan appraisal, KYC/AML, emerging risk and ESG. Because, you know, that's what really makes a business boom: paperwork and jargon.
