BOOK REVIEW: FROM MAU MAU TO RUTO-MUST-GO: KENYA'S ENDLESS SCRAPBOOK OF STRUGGLE AGAINST IMPERIALISM

WARNING: Prepare for another dose of Kenyan intellectual gymnastics, dissecting our glorious history of being kicked around by outsiders and our own supposed leaders. Spoiler alert: it's not pretty, and it's definitely not over.

This book, "From MAU MAU to RUTO-MUST-GO," is apparently a deep dive into Kenya's "struggle against imperialism." Willy Mutunga, our former Chief Justice who now teaches at Kabarak Law School - fancy that - reviewed it. He’s waxing lyrical about how brilliant it is, how it gets the "undercurrents" and how resistance is an "on-going, incomplete struggle." Honestly, it’s like reading a novel about a broken record player. We've been here before, and we'll be here again.

Mutunga’s little piece is really a love letter to Kenyan artists, bless their souls, for supposedly "breathing life into the Constitution." Freedom of expression, they call it. While the ruling elite find our constitution "inconvenient and abstract," these artists are out there, using cartoons and theatre to "craft political demands" and "energize non-reformist reforms." You know, doing the actual work while the politicians just posture. The cartoons, they say, expose corruption and "billions of shillings being ‘siphoned and stashed in offshore accounts in the West." Shocking, I know. These artists are apparently brilliant at having multiple interpretations of their work, just in case the powers that be decide to come knocking with their "machinery of violence."

Then there's the theatre crowd, the Social Justice Centres Travelling Theatre (SJCTT), who are apparently inspired by the likes of Brecht and Boal. They use "common language rooted in local knowledge" to "disrupt the established order" and offer a "way forward." Sounds intense. They're carrying on the legacy of the University of Nairobi Travelling Theatre and the Kamirithu outdoor theatre, which were, of course, linked to underground movements. And let's not forget Mau Mau's poems and songs - the "most revolutionary beacon of Kenyan class struggles." It all sounds very noble, doesn't it? Meanwhile, the rest of us are just trying to pay rent and avoid the latest tax hike.

The real kicker is when Mutunga starts talking about President Ruto and the "comprador bourgeoisie." He likens them to Malcolm X's "house niggers," serving the "masters of the plantation" - which, in his view, are the US, Europe, Japan, and even China and Russia. Apparently, our leaders are just puppets, signing off on military bases and sending our police to Haiti against court orders, all while the West lectures us about democracy. It’s all very dramatic, and frankly, it sounds about right. The burning political question, he concludes, is the quest for an "authentic and patriotic alternative political leadership" that's anti-imperialist and loves the working people. Good luck with that. It's like searching for a unicorn in a herd of goats.

The whole thing boils down to this: we’re still fighting the same old battles. The British might be gone, but the imperialists and their local collaborators are still very much in business. The artists are doing their bit, but until we get leaders who genuinely care about the working class and not just lining their pockets, we'll be stuck in this endless cycle. This book, and Mutunga's review, is just another reminder that the struggle is indeed ongoing, and honestly, it's exhausting. It makes you wonder if any of this actually changes anything, or if it’s just more intellectual musings while the actual Chess Pieces and Clout Chasers run the show.
