So, the grand Sh64 billion edible palm oil scandal? It's officially hit the Kenyan version of a brick wall - a wall built by incompetence and painted with corruption. Two years ago, our honourable MPs in the National Assembly’s Finance and National Planning Committee decided to play detective, sniffing out how billions were being siphoned off by simply lying about what kind of palm oil was coming into the country. Now? Crickets. The investigation has gone as silent as a politician’s promise on election eve.

The usual suspects are at play here. Former KRA boss Humphrey Wattanga, the man who was supposed to be watching the till, has been playing hide-and-seek with the committee, showing up only to present documents so poorly prepared they might as well have been scribbled on a napkin. Then there’s the alleged "obstruction" by National Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi - which, in Kenyan terms, probably means he's busy counting his own cut. The committee chairman, Molo MP Kuria Kimani, is running around like a headless chicken, lamenting the Treasury’s inability to “discharge its mandate” while other MPs wail about investigations just "disappearing" because some people can’t be bothered to show up. Honestly, it’s disheartening to watch the same old movie on repeat.

The numbers themselves are staggering. We’re talking Sh16.5 billion lost in 2022, Sh32.54 billion in 2023, and another Sh13.83 billion so far this year. All thanks to a little trick of blending crude and refined palm oil and declaring the whole lot as the cheaper crude. This isn't just about dodging import duties; it’s a calculated scheme to pocket the difference, a difference that should be funding our schools and hospitals, not funding the lavish lifestyles of the elite. It’s like watching the same old story of how [Waiguru Says Ruto Has Best Interests for Mt. Kenya, Deserves Second Term](/posts/waiguru-says-ruto-has-best-interests-for-mt--kenya--deserves-second-term) - there's always a narrative, and rarely is the public's interest at the heart of it.

And it’s not just the KRA playing along. The Kenya Bureau of Standards (KEBS), the Government Chemist, the Agriculture and Food Authority (AFA), the Kenya Ports Authority (KPA), and even private labs were supposed to be questioned. None of them have bothered to show up. Neither have the companies implicated, like Vipingo, Mazeras, ACEE, Mvita Oils, LDC Kenya, LDC PTA Asia, and of course, the National Treasury. The PBO document is blunt: a "large-scale tax evasion scheme" is happening at Mombasa Port. LDC Kenya, in particular, is named for misdeclaring shipments destined for Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, and Rwanda.

The whole thing is a masterclass in how to game the system. Imported refined edible palm oil gets hit with a hefty 35% import duty, plus other levies and VAT. Crude palm oil? A sweet 10% duty. By blending them and calling it all crude, these brilliant minds not only avoid the higher import duty but also get a kickback from exporters in Indonesia and Malaysia who slap on an export tax for crude but exempt refined oil. So, importers save on both ends, thanks to a little help from our "friends" in high places. It’s a beautiful, albeit sickening, dance of corruption.

This committee, as one MP rightly put it, has "lost meaning." They launched a probe, got bogged down by evasions and alleged obstruction, and now the report is nowhere to be seen. Meanwhile, the Sh64 billion is well on its way to being forgotten, just another ghost in Kenya’s long list of unpunished economic crimes. Don't expect any accountability; that's just not how things work here. The money is gone, the corrupt are likely richer, and we, the taxpayers, are left holding the empty bag, as always.
