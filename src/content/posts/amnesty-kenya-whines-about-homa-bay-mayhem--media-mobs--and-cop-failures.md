So, Amnesty International Kenya has dropped another press release, this time about the "reported attacks" in Homa Bay. Apparently, some hired goons went around smashing property, targeting places where "Linda Mwananchi leaders" might be hanging out. You know, the usual political theatre where everyone pretends to be surprised when things get ugly.

And, of course, the police and other "security agencies" are being called out for their "failure to take timely and effective action." This is the part that always gets me. As if anyone truly expected them to swoop in like superheroes. We've seen this movie before, haven't we? It’s the same old song and dance, and frankly, it’s exhausting. This constant cycle of violence and then the obligatory hand-wringing from human rights groups just feels like background noise now. It reminds me of that piece about how "global consent" is often just a mirage, used to justify things while the real problems fester. [The Global Consent Mirage: Surveillance Under the Guise of Sustainability](/posts/the-global-consent-mirage--surveillance-under-the-guise-of-sustainability)

Amnesty is calling for investigations, accountability, and a general "restoration of security." Noble sentiments, I suppose. But let's be real, how much of this ever actually leads to meaningful change? They want the police to act professionally, impartially, and follow human rights standards. That's a nice dream, but in Nairobi, professionalism is often about knowing who to bribe and when. And when it comes to political violence, the lines between private thugs, political actors, and public officials get blurrier than a drunk's vision on a Friday night.

As Kenya inches closer to the 2027 General Elections, these pronouncements are just part of the pre-election noise. Everyone's posturing, and the usual victims - journalists, ordinary citizens, anyone caught in the crossfire - are left to pick up the pieces. They demand that authorities act decisively, uphold rights, and ensure accountability. But for how long will we keep feeding these same pleas into the void, hoping for a different outcome? It's a tough pill to swallow, but sometimes, the system is just the system.
