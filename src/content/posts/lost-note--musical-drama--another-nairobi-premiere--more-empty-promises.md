So, 2026 is the year we’re supposed to get emotionally invested in a Kenyan coming-of-age musical called ‘Lost Note’. Premiering at Prestige Cinema, because where else would such artistic endeavors grace us? Apparently, it’s about a young singer whose father kicks the bucket and leaves her with more existential dread than answers. Just what Nairobi needs, another tale of woe dressed up in song.

Director Hanigun Kwenya claims this film is deeply personal, dredging up his own daddy issues and the regret of not saying goodbye. Good for him. Meanwhile, we’re left with the promise of six original songs, churned out at Paza Studios, and the hope that music somehow magically helps Leila process her daddy’s departure. Because in this city, if you can’t afford therapy, you sing your problems away, right?

The cast list reads like a who's who of actors trying to make a name for themselves, along with a newcomer named Worship Shekinah. Let’s hope their performances are more inspired than the plot description. Produced by Lislo Films and written by Tecla Joan, it’s also getting a boost from Africa No Filter’s Entertainment and Media Hubs Program, which is all about "gender-equitable, faith-focused stories." Sounds like a recipe for something bland and preachy, designed to tick boxes rather than tell a compelling story.

This whole "faith-based projects" angle from Africa No Filter is particularly interesting. It reminds me of how some outfits in this city seem to operate, focusing on the optics rather than the substance. It's like those "Sharp Boys" we keep hearing about, minting millions while the regulations sleep. Perhaps this film will offer a similar, albeit more melodious, form of escapism.

And let's not forget the whole "support for adolescent girls and young women" narrative. While noble in theory, in practice, these initiatives often just churn out more of the same. We’ll see if ‘Lost Note’ manages to break the mold or just adds another predictable chapter to Kenya’s cinematic output. Don't hold your breath.

It’s a grand premiere, a shiny new film, and a lot of talk about emotional journeys and finding oneself. Let's just hope the audience doesn’t feel like they’ve lost something themselves - like their time and money.
