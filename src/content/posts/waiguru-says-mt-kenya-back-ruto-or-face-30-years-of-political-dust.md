So, Governor Waiguru is out here doing her best impression of a doomsday prophet, telling the Mt Kenya folks they’d better stick with Ruto or face 30 years in the political wilderness. Apparently, if they don’t keep President Ruto in power, they might as well pack their bags and forget about getting any development projects. It’s all about the "political influence" and "development opportunities," you see. Because God forbid they miss out on a few more tarmac roads or whatever goodies the government doles out. It’s the same old song and dance, isn't it? Stick with the devil you know, or you'll be begging for scraps for decades.

Waiguru’s calculations, based on her "regular engagements" with other governors, suggest Ruto has a grip on 33 counties. She paints a rosy picture of the opposition as a fragmented mess, incapable of mounting a serious challenge. It’s a classic scare tactic: either you’re with us and get a piece of the pie, or you’re against us and starve. She even throws in the "don't cut your nose to spite your face" line, as if the people haven't already been "punishing themselves" by voting for leaders who might not be delivering. It's a neat way of shifting blame, isn't it?

The governor, who’s had a taste of government under Kibaki and Kenyatta, seems to think her long tenure gives her some special insight. She reminds everyone about Ruto's supposed loyalty to Uhuru, conveniently forgetting the political machinations that led to where we are. Apparently, there was an "understanding" that Uhuru would serve two terms and Ruto would get his turn. Funny how these "understandings" only seem to surface when it’s politically convenient. She’s also pushing the narrative that regional interests are tied to national government participation, which, let's be honest, is a convenient argument for maintaining the status quo.

And then there's the bit about security in Laikipia. Waiguru claims Ruto has brought calm to the region, a stark contrast to our previous reports on the KDF's Laikipia 'Security Efforts' where it was suggested there was "More Talk, Less Action." It seems the truth is a flexible concept depending on who you ask and what they're trying to sell. She tells people to judge leaders by their performance, yet conveniently glosses over any policies that might be hurting the common mwananchi. It's always "governing is difficult," and we should just acknowledge the "achievements."

She even gets personal, hinting at her own ambitions for higher office, perhaps even Deputy President. It's a calculated move, aligning herself with the winning side, or the side she believes is winning. The applause she received suggests her message, however cynical, resonates with some. Meanwhile, other politicians are joining the chorus, campaigning for Ruto and praising community empowerment programs. It's all about the optics, isn't it? Presenting a united front, even if it's built on shaky ground and veiled threats.

Ultimately, Waiguru's plea is for pragmatic self-interest. She's telling the Mt Kenya community to forget their grievances, swallow their pride, and support Ruto because it's the "wiser" choice for their long-term interests. It’s a stark reminder that in Kenyan politics, loyalty is often transactional, and the promise of continued access to power and resources trumps all else. So, the choice is clear, according to Waiguru: stay with Ruto and get a slice of the national cake, or walk away and face decades of political irrelevance. It’s not exactly inspiring, is it?
