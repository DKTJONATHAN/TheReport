Politicians Selling Teacher Jobs? MP Says It's All Above Board, Because Of Course It Is.

This latest drama from Parliament is just more of the same old song and dance. You’ve got the Speaker, Moses Wetang'ula, spouting tales of MPs dishing out TSC employment letters like party favours, even mentioning a parent allegedly fleeced KSh400,000 for a teaching gig. Then, bam! The chair of the Education Committee, Julius Melly, swoops in, calling it all unprocedural and, shocker, saying it "doesn't happen." Apparently, it's all about the clear procedures, the appropriated funds, the TSC adverts, and the county-by-county staffing needs. You know, the textbook stuff.

Melly's got his nose in the air, questioning where Wetang'ula got his juicy gossip. He claims that despite his lofty position overseeing education, no such shady dealings have crossed his desk. It’s almost as if these politicians inhabit different realities, one where jobs are sold to the highest bidder and another where everything is squeaky clean and by the book. This whole circus conveniently distracts from the real issues, much like how some political sides try to distract from their own failings with endless rounds of [El Clasico? More Like El Chaos-ico: Madrid’s Petty War On Barca Is The Messiest Tea In Europe].

The committee, bless their hearts, will now “seek clarification” and "find out the details" from Wetang'ula. This is politician-speak for "let's see if we can bury this under a mountain of bureaucracy and forget about it." Meanwhile, teachers and hopeful jobseekers are left wondering if their futures are being gambled away in backroom deals. The President’s own pledge of 50 teaching positions for a specific denomination earlier this year only adds fuel to the fire, proving that political favouritism isn't exactly a new concept around here.

And let's not forget the KNUT's own grumbles about political meddling in teacher transfers. It’s a clear pattern, but according to Melly, it’s all just a misunderstanding or, worse, fabricated. The TSC's acting CEO has warned politicians to butt out, but you know how that goes. Unless there are "documented complaints and evidence," which, let's be honest, are hard to come by when people are too scared or too desperate to speak up, this whole "sale of TSC jobs" will likely remain another vague allegation tossed around in Parliament.

Ultimately, this is a cynical reminder that while the procedures might look good on paper, the reality on the ground is often far more grubby. For every MP claiming transparency, there’s a constituent being squeezed for cash. The TSC jobs, supposed to be a lifeline, are being treated like commodities by those with the connections. And the promises of a fair system? Well, that's just another story for another day, likely after the next election.
