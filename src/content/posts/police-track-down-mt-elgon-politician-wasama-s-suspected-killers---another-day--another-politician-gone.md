So, Nathan Masai Wasama, a former councillor and aspiring MP, is dead. Gunned down in his own shop. The DCI is patting itself on the back, saying they've nabbed three suspects. Three out of how many? And is this even the end of it, or just the beginning of the usual song and dance? In Nairobi, we've seen this movie before, and it always ends with a lot of noise and very little action. It's a classic Nairobi reality show, a bit like the [Kitengela Crime-Ridden Estate Next to Police Station: A Kenyan Reality Show](/posts/kitengela-crime-ridden-estate-next-to-police-station--a-kenyan-reality-show).

The DCI is busy parading their "haul" - smoke jackets, stun grenades, axes, pangas, knives, arrows. Looks like a whole militia arsenal, doesn't it? They want us to believe this is a breakthrough. But let's be honest, the real question is how many more weapons are still floating around, ready to be used on the next target. This whole saga reeks of political rivalry, especially with the 2027 elections looming. It's always the same story: power, greed, and a trail of dead bodies.

The human rights folks are, of course, making noise. Demanding "conclusive investigations" and a "state protection mechanism." Bless their hearts. They're even offering pro bono lawyers. While it’s good they are trying, it’s like trying to bail out the Indian Ocean with a teacup. The government’s track record on protecting anyone, let alone political aspirants, is as solid as a government promise. They’ll issue statements, form committees, and then… crickets.

This whole business in Mt Elgon has the whiff of the old Sabaot Land Defence Force days. They say Wasama survived an attempt before. Clearly, the message didn't sink in. Or maybe the people who wanted him dead just didn't care. The police boss is talking about illegal firearms. Big surprise. This country is drowning in them, and it seems only the politicians are surprised when they’re used.

The Justice and Peace Centre Kitale is all worked up, calling for swift arrests and justice. They are profiling cases of intimidation. Good. Keep profiling. Keep demanding. But until there's a real shift, a genuine willingness from the top to crack down on this rot, it's just another press release in a sea of despair. We'll see if these three suspects even see the inside of a proper jail, or if they disappear into the system, only to re-emerge with a slap on the wrist.

The DCI says the hunt for more suspects is ongoing. Of course, it is. It always is. They’ll chase leads, interview witnesses, and eventually, the story will fade from the headlines, replaced by the next scandal, the next murder, the next broken promise. And the cycle of violence, fuelled by political ambition and a healthy dose of corruption, will continue. Just another Tuesday in Kenya.
