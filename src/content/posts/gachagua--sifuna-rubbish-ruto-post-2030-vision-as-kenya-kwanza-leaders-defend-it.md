RUTO'S 'BEYOND VISION 2030' NONSENSE: GACHAGUA, SIFUNA CALL IT A KWANZA DISTRACTION AS ALLIES DEFEND THE FARCE

WARNING: President Ruto's grand pronouncements of a new development vision beyond 2030 are being dismissed by critics as a desperate ploy to distract from mounting economic woes, corruption, and unfulfilled promises. While government loyalists tout it as a bold roadmap, the opposition sees only an elaborate charade.

The Kenyan public, reeling from the high cost of living and a mountain of debt, is being treated to yet another elaborate scheme by President William Ruto: a "Beyond Vision 2030" development plan. As if Vision 2030 wasn't already a distant dream gathering dust, we're now promised a roadmap to 2060. Former DP Rigathi Gachagua, David Maraga, and Fred Matiang'i aren't buying it, and frankly, who can blame them? They rightly point out that this administration can barely get its own current promises off the ground, let alone conjure a credible future. Gachagua’s blunt assessment - that it’s an economic distraction to rescue a sinking administration - hits the nail on the head.

Maraga, the former Chief Justice, is particularly incensed by the President's silence on corruption, a gaping wound bleeding Kenya dry. "Not once did he mention the word corruption," Maraga lamentably states, highlighting the staggering billions lost daily that could transform the nation. Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna adds another layer of critique, arguing that Ruto is trying to elevate economic development above the very constitutional principles that should guide it, like democracy and social justice. It's like building a fancy mansion on a foundation of sand. We've seen this movie before, and it rarely ends well.

The timing of this grand pronouncement is also highly suspect. Dr. Fred Matiang'i rightly questions the audacity of launching a new national dialogue when existing reform proposals, like those from the NADCO report, remain gathering dust. How can one lecture on human rights and constitutional clauses when lawlessness and "rampant thuggery" plague the streets? This administration’s credibility on this front is, to put it mildly, non-existent. It smacks of the same old political theatre, a desperate attempt to shift focus from their own failings, much like the discussions around the [bankruptcy of a billion dollar mirage](https://example.com/posts/the-bankruptcy-of-a-billion-dollar-mirage).

While the opposition sees through the smokescreen, a chorus of Kenya Kwanza loyalists is singing President Ruto's praises. Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen insists the initiative will secure a "brighter and more dignified future," while Senator Aaron Cheruiyot draws parallels to successful nations with shared visions. UDA Secretary General Hassan Omar claims the economy has been "stabilised" and that this new blueprint builds on progress. These are the same voices that defended the Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda, an agenda that many Kenyans are still struggling to understand, let alone benefit from.

The reality on the ground is starkly different from the rosy picture painted by the government. Kenyans are grappling with the immediate consequences of poor governance and economic mismanagement. A new, long-term vision is a luxury we can't afford when the present is so dire. Until the fundamental issues of corruption, cost of living, and unfulfilled promises are addressed, any talk of a grand future beyond 2030 will sound like nothing more than empty rhetoric, a desperate attempt to delay the inevitable reckoning.
