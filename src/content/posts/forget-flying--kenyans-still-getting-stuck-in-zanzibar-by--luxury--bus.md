Forget Flying: Kenyans Are Still Getting Stuck in Zanzibar by "Luxury" Bus

This article paints a rosy picture of bus travel to Zanzibar, but let's be real, it's not all reclining seats and free Wi-Fi. While some Kenyans might be swapping planes for buses, the reality for many is likely a long, uncomfortable slog with questionable amenities, and don't even get me started on the "luxury" part. It’s a budget-friendly fantasy that often comes with hidden costs and a whole lot of disappointment.

The idea of a Sh40,000 Zanzibar trip sounds great, doesn't it? Jane Muthoni and her friend thought so, chasing sunsets on a budget. They took the Kidia One bus to Dar, then a ferry. Apparently, these buses have charging ports and entertainment screens - sounds fancy. But let’s not forget the actual journey: long hours on bumpy roads, potential delays, and that "onboard toilet" for "short calls only" that probably smells worse than a Nairobi matatu at rush hour. And that ferry? VIP or not, you’re still stuck on a boat with a bunch of other people trying to escape their own realities.

Nicole Wanjala, bless her heart, also opted for the bus, a 10-day holiday budget of Sh100,000 being the main driver. Flights at Sh70,000 return? Yeah, that’s not happening for most of us. So, it’s the bus, the scenic route, and hopefully, you don’t get stuck in Tanzanian traffic jams caused by accidents - a common occurrence, if you’ve ever been on these routes. And the paperwork! Passport, Yellow Fever, insurance - more hoops to jump through than a Kenyan politician at an election rally.

Then there’s Abdirahman Khalif, the "travel content creator," who thinks a nearly three-day bus journey to Lusaka is a "pleasant experience." Tea, biscuits, and chips served on a bus that smells like a thousand sweaty armpits? Sure, if you consider that luxury. And let's not forget the "business travellers" who form the "backbone" of these routes, likely going to Gikomba to buy clothes or Kirinyaga Road for spare parts. That’s not adventure, that’s just work, folks. It’s the same hustle, just on wheels. It reminds me of the economic mirage sometimes seen in markets, where growth appears strong, but the crude reality stings the senses.

The booking clerk at B. One Coach claims they're "full capacity" most days. Maybe. Or maybe they just pack people in like sardines and call it a premium experience. Air conditioning is standard, not a luxury. Free Wi-Fi? Probably patchy. And that non-alcoholic wine on weekends? A desperate attempt to make a long, arduous journey bearable. This whole "forget flying" narrative is just a clever marketing ploy for companies trying to cash in on people’s limited budgets, disguising a tiring ordeal as an adventure.
