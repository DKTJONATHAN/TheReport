Ruto: Kenya Can't Plan For Tomorrow Using Yesterday's Realities - Unless Yesterday Was A Lie

So, the President wants us to have a "national conversation" about Vision 2060. Apparently, the world has changed - surprise, surprise! Technology, AI, climate change, all these fancy words he’s throwing around. As if we haven't been living through these "changes" on our paychecks and the cost of electricity. Vision 2030 is ending, and naturally, we need a new blueprint. Because the old one, drafted before the 2010 Constitution, is obviously a relic. Funny how they only remember the Constitution when it suits their planning narrative.

The man is talking about a roadmap that "goes beyond individual administrations and political cycles." Noble words. He wants Kenyans to "own" this Vision 2060, implemented by whoever happens to be in State House. Sounds lovely, doesn't it? Like a fairy tale where leaders suddenly forget about elections and their own egos for the sake of "national development." Let's see how many of them can actually separate long-term planning from the desperate scramble for votes, especially with the 2027 elections breathing down our necks.

They're asking us to chip in with ideas on the kind of country we want. Great. Maybe we can start with asking why getting a police clearance certificate in Kenya still feels like a quest for a mythical artifact, even though they claim it can be done within 24 hours. Or perhaps we can discuss the ever-increasing cost of basic necessities. But don't worry, the President has invited the opposition to the party too. Because nothing screams "unity" like sharing a stage to discuss a plan that will likely be reshaped and repackaged by the next set of power brokers.

The plan is supposed to cover economic transformation, industrialization, job creation, technology, infrastructure, energy, climate change, and human capital. All the big hitters. Ruto's already setting his sights high, aiming for a "first-world economy" and using Singapore as his shiny example. Let's just hope he remembers that Singapore didn't get there by endless "conversations" and platitudes. And let’s not forget that Treasury CS John Mbadi wisely pointed out this isn't Ruto's blueprint, it's for "2060." Because, you know, Ruto will only be around until 2032, so it's not like he's personally investing his legacy in this.

So, we're back to the drawing board, or rather, the "national conversation." We need to build on Vision 2030’s "achievements and lessons." Which lessons are those, exactly? The ones that led to us paying exorbitant parking fees at JKIA, perhaps? Or the ones that have us questioning if any of Ruto's past claims about uniting Kenyans hold water? This whole exercise feels like a grand show, a way to appear proactive while the real work, the messy, unglamorous work, is either ignored or handled with the same efficiency we've come to expect.

Ultimately, this is all about setting the stage for the next decade or two. They'll gather "views" from all corners, from citizens to the private sector, across all 47 counties. And then, what? A glossy document will emerge, filled with noble aspirations and impressive statistics. And we, the ordinary Kenyans, will be left to navigate the reality of whatever vision they manage to cobble together, hoping it’s not just another elaborate plan to keep us busy while the status quo remains firmly in place.
