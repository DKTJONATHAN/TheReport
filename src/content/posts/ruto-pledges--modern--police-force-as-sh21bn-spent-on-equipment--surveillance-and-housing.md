Ruto's Sh21 Billion Police "Modernisation": More Shiny Toys, Same Old Song

WARNING: The government is throwing billions at the police, promising a high-tech force while conveniently forgetting where the money usually disappears. Expect more surveillance and less accountability.

PHOTO: President Ruto, looking sharp, inspects a guard of honour at the National Police College, Embakasi ‘B’ Campus, likely wondering if his golf clubs are as well-maintained as his new security equipment.





So, President Ruto has graced us with another grand pronouncement. This time, it’s Sh21 billion - a tidy sum, really - supposedly for "modernising" the National Police Service. We’re talking fancy protective gear, whizz-bang surveillance systems, and even air support. Apparently, this is all to combat "emerging security threats." Because, you know, the old threats like corruption and impunity were just too last century.

They’re also promising specialized training in counter-terrorism, cybercrime, and all sorts of buzzwords that sound impressive but, in reality, often translate to more paperwork and the same old beat-downs. Remember when they were fussing over uniforms again? That was just a taste. This sounds like the main course of shiny new distractions while the real problems fester.

And let's not forget the election is looming. Suddenly, the police need to be "impartial" and secure the "democratic space." It’s almost as if they remember that when it’s politically convenient. He’s warning leaders not to involve the forces in partisan contests, which is rich coming from a government that seems to have perfected the art of weaponizing state institutions. We've seen this playbook before; it's no surprise when you consider what's been happening with [Ruto's Coastal Land Grab Ahead of Election: Because Why Not?](/posts/ruto-s-coastal-land-grab-ahead-of-election--because-why-not?).

On the "welfare" front, they're touting a 36% pay rise for entry-level constables, bringing their monthly earnings to a princely Sh49,000. Plus, they’re building houses - over 28,000 units planned, with a measly 870 already completed. This is after years of promises and probably another Sh55 million "up in smoke" somewhere. And healthcare? Everyone's supposedly under a "medical scheme." We’ll see how long that lasts before the bureaucracy swallows it whole.

The president’s linking professionalism to public confidence, saying trust is key. Funny, because the less I trust the people collecting my taxes and supposedly keeping us safe, the more likely I am to just keep my head down and hope for the best. These billions could probably do a lot more if they were actually used to fix the rot, not just slap a coat of shiny new paint on it.
