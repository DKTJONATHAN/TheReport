RUTO'S RIFT VALLEY DEVELOPMENT CLAIMS A BIG JOKE, SAYS MINISTER

Murkomen Says Ruto Hasn't Neglected Rift Valley, But We All Know That's a Lie.

Nairobi, Kenya - Interior and National Administration Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen is out here defending President William Ruto's so called development record, claiming no part of Kenya, especially his political backyard in the Rift Valley, has been neglected. He insists the Kenya Kwanza government has been fair with development projects. Honestly, it’s the same old song and dance we always hear from these guys. They trot out fancy words like "equity" and "fairness," but when you look closely, it’s mostly just noise.

Murkomen, speaking at some economic empowerment event in Bomet County, said the President is committed to ensuring every corner of Kenya benefits, regardless of who you voted for or where you live. He even quoted the President saying, "as we build every part of Kenya, there must be fairness and equity in development." What a load of rubbish. We've seen this movie before. They promise the moon, then deliver a few dust bunnies and expect us to be grateful. The claims on social media that Ruto has forgotten his roots are just the tip of the iceberg; the reality on the ground is far more grim for most Kenyans.

He went on to boast about infrastructure, specifically mentioning Ksh10 billion worth of road projects in Bomet County alone. Apparently, these are part of a grand plan to connect markets and boost the economy. He even defended the securitisation of the Road Maintenance Levy Fund, saying the opposition is against it because it’s bringing development everywhere. Meanwhile, the cost of basic things keeps climbing, and we’re still dealing with the fallout from global events, like the ongoing struggles of [Kenya fuel retailers running short of supplies due to Middle East war - thecitizen.co.tz](/posts/kenya-fuel-retailers-running-short-of-supplies-due-to-middle-east-war---thecitizen-co-tz).

Murkomen also threw in affordable housing, healthcare, agriculture, sports infrastructure, and economic stabilisation as key achievements. Ksh1.2 billion for housing in Bomet? Sounds like a drop in the ocean. And 28 new stadiums in less than four years? While some people can barely afford a decent meal. The Social Health Authority (SHA) is supposed to be helping with healthcare costs, but ask anyone who’s actually tried to use it, and you’ll get a different story. As for inflation and fertiliser prices dropping, well, a small relief doesn't erase the years of hardship.

The CS wrapped up by urging Kenyans to judge the government by its project delivery, especially with the 2027 elections looming. It’s all about optics, isn't it? They want us to focus on the shiny new stadiums and the kilometers of tarmac while ignoring the crippling debt and the everyday struggles of ordinary citizens. The truth is, development isn't just about building things; it's about making life better for everyone, not just those who are politically connected.
