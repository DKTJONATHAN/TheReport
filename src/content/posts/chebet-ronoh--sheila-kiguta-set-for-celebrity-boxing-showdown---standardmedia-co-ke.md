Chebet Ronoh, Sheila Kiguta Boxing Matchup: Just Another Nairobi Show?











So, the latest buzz in Nairobi is that Chebet Ronoh and Sheila Kiguta are going to punch each other for… entertainment? Kenya's first female celebrity boxing match, they're calling it. Because apparently, we've exhausted all other avenues of public spectacle and now need to see influencers duke it out. It’s all happening at Celebrity Fight Nights 3.0, a series that's apparently "growing." Growing what, exactly? More ego trips for the cameras?

This whole "Celebrity Fight Nights" thing started in November 2025, by HustleSasa and Barak Jacuzzi, who seems to be the ringmaster of this particular circus. They bring "entertainers, media personalities, and content creators" into the ring. The first event saw Barak Jacuzzi beat some content creator called Lumu. The second edition, in June 2026, had Jacuzzi fight Benzema, and Brahim Ouma take on MackLean. Big Yasa beat Zeman. It's a parade of familiar faces, not exactly groundbreaking art.

Now, because a woman podcaster - Mariah - apparently whined about it, they’re finally getting a "women’s celebrity boxing edition." And Barak Jacuzzi himself had apparently "hinted" at it. It's not a bold new step for female empowerment; it's just appeasing the masses and ticking a box. Like the rest of Nairobi's "entertainment," it's all about chasing the clout, a concept I've seen discussed in relation to things like [The Airbnb Chronicles: When ‘Clout’ Becomes the New Colonization](/posts/the-airbnb-chronicles--when--clout--becomes-the-new-colonization).

The organizers are touting this as "historic." Historic for what? For proving that if you have enough followers, you can get paid to pretend to beat someone up? It’s just another symptom of a culture that values noise over substance. We have real issues in this country, like the ongoing health crisis in Northern Kenya where there's [No Water, Few Nurses, Broken Machines - Just Another Day in the Wild West](/posts/northern-kenya-s-health-crisis--no-water--few-nurses--broken-machines---just-another-day-in-the-wild-west), but sure, let's focus on a staged fight between two internet personalities.

This is all happening while other "celebrity boxing" events like Vurugu are also attracting attention. Vurugu II saw some Ugandan internet personality beat a Kenyan singer by technical knockout. So, the trend is clear: if you can get a crowd to watch you pretend to hurt someone, you’re in business. It’s less about sport and more about the spectacle, the drama, and the gossip that fuels these fleeting moments of attention.

So, Chebet Ronoh vs. Sheila Kiguta. Will it be a historic moment? Probably not. Will it generate clicks and conversations for a week? Absolutely. And when the dust settles, these "celebrities" will go back to their phones, and Nairobi will be left with another shallow memory of manufactured drama. Just another Tuesday.
