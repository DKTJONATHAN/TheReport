Ruto's Fancy New Police Uniforms: More Smoke Than Fire, As Usual

The fanfare around President Ruto unveiling new police uniforms next week is just another government show. While they're busy playing dress-up and talking about morale, the real issues plaguing our police force remain untouched. Expect the same old problems, just with a slightly different shade of fabric.

Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja is hyping up the change, saying general duty officers will ditch their navy-blue attire for something "agreed upon" from August 31. Apparently, after a public participation exercise in 2023, they've decided to go back to a previously approved look. This whole song and dance is meant to boost morale, especially for the new recruits graduating from Kiganjo. But let's be real, is a new shirt going to stop officers from being underpaid, overworked, or worse, like those murder suspects who saw through the Mombasa police cells? [Murder Suspects Saw Through Mombasa Police Cells - What Else Is New?](/posts/murder-suspects-saw-through-mombasa-police-cells---what-else-is-new)

The rollout will be gradual, with the graduating class getting the first taste of the fresh threads. This isn't a nationwide overnight transformation, mind you. The Administration Police Service and General Service Unit will be keeping their current getups. So, while some officers are strutting in their new uniforms, others will be stuck with the old. Typical.

The big pronouncements about this being a "force multiplier" and enhancing service delivery sound more like political platitudes than concrete plans. We’ve seen these grand pronouncements before, from football club buyouts smelling of PR stunts [KSI’s Newest Plaything: Why the Dagenham and Redbridge Buyout Smells Like a PR Stunt](/posts/ksi-s-newest-plaything--why-the-dagenham-and-redbridge-buyout-smells-like-a-pr-stunt) to election timelines thrown into chaos [Court Throws Kenya Presidential Election Timeline into Chaos, Experts Warn of Crisis](/posts/court-throws-kenya-presidential-election-timeline-into-chaos--experts-warn-of-crisis). The bottom line is, a uniform change does nothing for the systemic rot.

This whole exercise, stemming from a consultation three years ago, feels like a distraction. While the powers-that-be are busy with fabric choices and unveiling ceremonies, the country is grappling with economic woes and international instability impacting our exports [Middle East Mess Means Kenya Exports Feeling The Pinch, Again](/posts/middle-east-mess-means-kenya-exports-feeling-the-pinch--again). Let’s hope these new uniforms don't cost an arm and a leg, because Kenyans are already feeling the pinch.

The administration is hoping this visual upgrade will translate into better policing. It's a nice thought, but until the fundamental issues of resources, training, and accountability are addressed, these new uniforms are just a superficial fix. Don't expect miracles; expect the same old Nairobi hustle, just perhaps in slightly different colours.
