Kenya Police Beat Posta Rangers, Premier League Rivals Should Probably Just Quit Now

The Police might have won, but don't expect a revolution. Another predictable win in the FKF Premier League, another 2-1 scoreline. Nana Asante and Erick Zakayo did the honours for the law enforcers, putting Posta Rangers to the sword before halftime. Apparently, assisting goals is also part of their job description, with Brian Mzee and Elvis Rupia chipping in. Just another Sunday evening in Nairobi, where the powerful get their way.

Mark Shaban managed a consolation goal for the mailmen, their fourth loss in seven games. Frankly, who’s surprised? This is the same league where defending champions Gor Mahia are apparently second, but somehow behind KCB on head-to-head. It’s all a popularity contest, really. And with Kenya Police now third, one point behind the leaders, you can bet they'll be flexing their muscles.

Anthony Akhulia’s side is on the up, they say. Jumping to third with 12 points. A whole point behind KCB. Riveting stuff. Meanwhile, the rest of us are left wondering if the real "message to rivals" is that the game is rigged from the start. It’s not a sporting competition, it’s a show of force, just like how some politicians seem to operate. We’ve seen it before, with the claims of safety versus the reality of goon activity, and the police denying gang ties. It’s all theatre.

This whole league seems to be a microcosm of Kenya itself. The powerful win, the rest scramble for crumbs. Posta Rangers are the latest to feel the boot, suffering their fourth loss. It’s a brutal cycle, and frankly, a bit boring. Just like the constant talk of fuel prices going up while the government claims we’re beating other countries. It’s all a bit much to stomach, isn't it?

Police Sacco Stadium saw the usual predictable outcome. The law is on the side of the law, apparently. And in this league, that means goals. Whether it’s a genuine display of skill or just another reminder of who’s in charge, it’s hard to say. But one thing’s for sure, the message is loud and clear: some teams just have it easier.
