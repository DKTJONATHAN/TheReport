So, there goes Ruto again, doing what he does best: shouting at ghosts. For two days straight, the man’s been on a crusade against Uhuru Kenyatta, his former boss, the one he supposedly fell out with over the "handshake." Now, with the 2027 elections breathing down our necks, it seems Uhuru’s mere existence is enough to send the current president into a tailspin. Ruto’s busy listing all the things Uhuru allegedly *didn’t* do, while conveniently forgetting that a good chunk of that decade was spent as his deputy. It’s a classic deflection tactic, blaming the retired guy for the current mess.

And who’s stepping in to defend the retired head of state? The usual suspects. Gachagua, Kalonzo, Matiang’i, Natembeya - all telling Ruto to get a grip and focus on his actual opponents. Gachagua, in particular, is having a field day, pointing out that Ruto has achieved nothing in four years and is using Uhuru as a convenient scapegoat. He even threw in a jab about the much-touted Vision 2030, which seems to be perpetually on the horizon but never arriving. Meanwhile, the opposition is busy trying to cobble together a coalition, with Uhuru apparently still pulling strings from the sidelines. It's like a never-ending soap opera, and we're the ones stuck paying for the popcorn.

Ruto's got his stadium-building, sugar-reviving, teacher-hiring list of achievements - all things Uhuru apparently failed to do. He’s even accusing Uhuru of not giving the opposition enough "fuel" money. Honestly, the man’s got more drama than a telenovela. He claims Uhuru is sponsoring the opposition, but then says they're too broke to mount a real campaign. Make up your mind, Mr. President. It’s all a bit rich coming from a man who benefited immensely from the very system he now criticizes. Remember when he and Uhuru were inseparable? Now it’s all sour grapes and desperate attempts to paint a target on a man who’s supposed to be retired.

The whole situation is just a sad replay of Kenyan politics. Uhuru backed Raila in 2022, Ruto won by a whisker, and now Uhuru's trying to rally the troops again for 2027. And guess who’s conveniently part of Ruto’s re-election campaign? Raila’s ODM party, or at least parts of it, with Odinga's brother leading the charge. Meanwhile, the splinter groups, like Sifuna's Linda Mwananchi, are out there saying they want Ruto out, but also admitting to talking to Kenyatta. It's a tangled web of alliances and backstabbing, and the only clear winners are the politicians who get to keep their jobs while the rest of us just get promises. It reminds me of the ongoing saga of Murkomen's promises for Nairobi's police force; it’s always *after* something is established, never *now*.

Kalonzo’s even throwing himself into the fray, telling Uhuru to step aside and let him fight Ruto "man to man." He’s even got a whole analogy about monkeys and slippery trees. Bless his heart. Matiang’i, on the other hand, sees it clearly: Ruto is using Uhuru as a punching bag for his own failures. He’s right, you know. When we, the citizens, dare to ask questions, we get insults from the Head of State. It’s a twisted logic where retired presidents are fair game for presidential rants, but asking for accountability is met with hostility.

And then there's Natembeya, who’s clearly seen this movie before. He points out that even retired presidents like Obama are active in politics. He’s basically telling Ruto, "Chill out, you'll retire too, and you might want a second term someday. Don’t be gagging people now." It’s a fair point. This constant circus, this obsession with past leaders, distracts from the real issues. We’re still waiting for that middle-income status promised by Vision 2030, and frankly, the only thing trickling down is political drama and empty rhetoric.
