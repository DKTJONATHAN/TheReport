HOMA BAY SHENANIGANS ECHO JOKER JARAJOLA: LUO POLITICS STILL A CIRCUS

The goons rampaging in Homa Bay over the weekend, torching cars and trashing a hospital like common thugs, are just the latest act in a long-running, predictable Nairobi theatre of the absurd. It’s the same old song and dance, a bitter echo of the chaos that consumed the Luo Nation after Jaramogi Oginga Odinga shuffled off this mortal coil. Nearly three decades later, they’re still at it, a leadership vacuum and a community’s political direction up for grabs, as if anyone truly has a clue what that direction even is anymore.

At the heart of this latest squabble are the usual suspects, carving out their little fiefdoms from the carcass of ODM. Governor James Orengo, Senator Edwin Sifuna, and MP Babu Owino are duking it out with Raila’s elder brother, Senator Oburu Oginga. Oburu’s lot are busy lining up behind President William Ruto for 2027, while Orengo’s crew insists on fielding their own presidential candidate. It’s a classic case of misplaced ambition and a desperate scramble for relevance, with Ruto happily stirring the pot. This whole charade is a stark reminder that when nothing is sacred, even a church or hospital can become collateral damage in their petty power games.

Remember April 1999? Raila and Orengo, then rivals, had a shouting match in Ugunja that devolved into a full-blown brawl. Raila, riding high with his NDP, was trying to muscle into Orengo's turf. Orengo, with his Ford Kenya crew, including Anyang’ Nyong’o and Joe Donde, wasn’t having it. Raila rolled in with his NDP faithful, chanting slogans, and Orengo responded with his own mob. The result? A riot, teargas, police with dogs, and Orengo hiding in a tree, complaining about hired goons. Sound familiar? Kisumu wasn't much better, with Raila’s meetings repeatedly disrupted by the mayor’s goons. It’s always the same story, just different faces.

The real root of all this is the eternal struggle to be the "big man" in Luo Nyanza. After Jaramogi died in '94, Orengo and Raila went head-to-head for the Ford Kenya vice-chairmanship. Orengo won by a whisker, and Raila, convinced of foul play, threw a tantrum, left the party, formed NDP, and the rest, as they say, is history. He eventually became the undisputed kingpin. Now, with him gone, his brother Oburu is trying to fill those massive shoes, but it’s just a rehash of old rivalries, with Orengo and his allies again on the opposite side. It makes you wonder if any of these leaders ever think beyond their own bank accounts and egos.

The current lineup sees Orengo’s camp boosted by Suba South MP Caroli Omondi, while Oburu’s faction is backed by Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga and Cabinet Secretaries Opiyo Wandayi and John Mbadi. Millie Odhiambo and Ruth Odinga? They’re wisely keeping their heads down, probably realizing the futility of it all. The police spokesperson, Muchiri Nyaga, is talking about an investigation, but let’s be real, they’ll probably just blame the usual scapegoats while the real orchestrators of this violence, whoever they are, will continue to operate with impunity. It’s all about who can bring the biggest, most intimidating crowd, and who has the deepest pockets to pay them.
