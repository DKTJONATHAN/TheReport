So, President Ruto’s been gallivanting in Russia, checking out some 1,140-acre “smart city” outside Moscow. Apparently, it’s got 65,000 housing units, schools, a hospital - the works. A picture-perfect vision of modern urban living, they say. Kenya, we’re told, is “exploring innovative approaches to urban development.” Innovative, eh? Is that what we’re calling looking at someone else’s homework now?

Meanwhile, back in Nairobi, we’re still grappling with the same old problems. Flooded streets, inadequate housing, and infrastructure that’s more of a hazard than a help. But sure, let’s admire a Russian fantasy land. It’s not like we have 65,000 housing units of our own to worry about, or the millions struggling to find decent shelter. This whole ‘smart city’ tour just screams ‘out of touch.’

The powers that be are keen to “examine modern urban planning models.” You know, the kind that might actually inform sustainable and well-planned cities. It’s almost as if they haven’t noticed that the city they’re currently supposed to be running is anything but. This is the same kind of grand vision that birthed Ruto's Sh500 Billion SGR Gamble - Another Train to Nowhere? We’re always looking for the next big, expensive idea from afar, instead of fixing what’s broken right under our noses.

And let's not forget the housing demand and the "challenges of rapid urbanisation." These are the buzzwords they throw around when they want us to believe they’re doing something. But a fancy tour of a foreign city won't magically create affordable homes or fix our crumbling roads. It’s more likely just another photo op, another chance to point at something shiny and pretend it’s a solution to our endemic issues. We need practical, on-the-ground fixes, not a slideshow of Moscow’s aspirations.
