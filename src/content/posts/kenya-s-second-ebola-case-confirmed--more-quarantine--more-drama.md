So, Kenya's got another Bundibugyo Ebola case. A 39-year-old man, apparently a close family member of the first poor soul who shuffled off this mortal coil. He started feeling rough on October 13th, which, surprise surprise, is when they decided he might actually *be* sick. Because, you know, daily monitoring doesn't mean actually *doing* anything until the symptoms show up. Then, *poof*, he's in quarantine. Like magic.

They whisked him off to a treatment center, where he's getting "supportive care." Which probably means IV fluids and a lot of prayers, because let's be real, they're just patching him up while hoping his body does the heavy lifting. The Ministry of Health, in its infinite wisdom, touted "early detection" because he was *already* in quarantine. Groundbreaking. Kenya’s now sitting at two confirmed cases and one death. Feels like a party we didn't RSVP for.

Contact tracing, a thrilling game of "Where's Waldo?" with potentially infected individuals. They've identified 128 potential carriers, and thankfully, most of them are still in Kenya, getting their temperatures checked like it's the hottest day of August. Five brave souls have apparently jetted out of the country, so naturally, they've "notified the relevant authorities." Because that's always effective. The remaining 15 are still playing hide-and-seek. This whole operation smells like a bureaucratic mess, not unlike the perpetual chaos at [Welcome to JKIA: The World’s Most Expensive Parking Lot](/posts/welcome-to-jkia--the-world-s-most-expensive-parking-lot).

The first guy, the OG, flew in from the DRC via Uganda on Jambojet. Landed on October 3rd, then decided to grace us with his presence in a terminal state. Now they're coordinating this whole circus through a "single national command structure," which is just a fancy way of saying someone's in charge of looking busy. No travel restrictions, no public gathering bans. Because why bother when you can just wait for the next shoe to drop?

The ministry's urging everyone to be vigilant, seek medical attention if they have symptoms like fever or unusual tiredness. Because, you know, a little fever can't hurt anyone. They're reminding us it's not airborne, just direct contact. So, don't kiss any sick people, I guess. They're also pleading for privacy for the affected family. Right. As if that ever happens. Just call 719 if you need more reassurances that everything is under control. Or not.
