MORGAN BAHATI BEATS DIAMOND AND ZUCHU TO YOUTUBE TOP SPOT - FOR NOW

Some kid named Morgan Bahati has apparently "scored a major milestone" by topping YouTube's trending chart in Kenya with his song, 'Zoza (Nikifika 18)'. Released a couple of days ago, it's already racked up a cool 1.6 million views. Beats me what people are actually watching, but there you have it. This means he's somehow leapfrogged established acts like Zuchu and Diamond Platnumz, whose new joint 'Mvua Ya Baraka' was apparently also doing its thing.

It's always the same story, isn't it? A new face pops up, gets a bit of hype, and suddenly they're the next big thing. Let's see how long this lasts. This whole thing reminds me of how easily public attention can be swayed, much like [the death of moral posturing in global sport](/posts/the-highest-bidder-buys-the-tee--the-death-of-moral-posturing-in-global-sport). One minute you're on top, the next you're just another forgotten track in the digital noise.

So, Morgan’s song, uploaded through the Bahati Kenya channel, has managed to pull in a massive audience. This is particularly noteworthy when you consider the established artists he's supposedly outshone. Diamond and Zuchu have been around, built up fan bases, and churned out hits. Yet, a debut song from a newcomer has somehow managed to grab the top spot. Don't get me wrong, it's an "achievement" if you believe in these fleeting internet fads.

The attention surrounding this young musician is clearly growing. Whether it translates into a sustainable career or just a flash in the pan remains to be seen. Nairobi's a tough place, and the music industry is no different. Lots of noise, lots of promises, and very little substance for most. We've seen plenty of these "milestones" come and go, just like the promises of development that [Ruto's 'Cartel Crackdown' speech](/posts/ruto-s--cartel-crackdown--speech--nairobi-mcas-get-the-bitter-pill) seems to gloss over for some.

The fact that 'Zoza (Nikifika 18)' managed to climb to the top of the Kenyan YouTube trending chart is a testament to something, I suppose. Maybe it's good marketing, maybe it's just the current algorithm at play, or maybe people are genuinely tired of the same old faces. Whatever the reason, it's happened.

Let's not get too excited. This is YouTube trends we're talking about, not a Nobel Prize. The real test will be whether Morgan Bahati can replicate this success or if he'll be another name that fades into obscurity, much like the lost hopes in [South Sudan's endless cash squeeze](/posts/south-sudan-s-endless-cash-squeeze--elections-and-everything-else-going-down-the-drain). Time will tell, but I wouldn't hold my breath.
