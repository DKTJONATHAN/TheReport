So, Principal Secretary Patrick Mariru, the man in charge of defence, apparently has time on his hands to go poking around construction sites. This time it's the Raila Odinga mausoleum in Bondo, which he’s proudly declared is 50% done. Fifty percent. That’s two years from now, end of September 2026, before this monument to… well, whatever he represents… is finished. Groundbreaking was just last July. They're moving faster than my landlord chasing rent, I’ll give them that.

This whole project is under the Ministry of Defence. Yes, defence. Because clearly, our borders are so secure we can afford to build fancy tombs. The design, apparently, is all symbolic - a pentagonal structure inspired by Raila's "political journey" and something called the "historic Pentagon era." Sounds like a lot of fancy talk to justify spending taxpayer money on a giant grave. They say it’s to preserve his legacy. For future generations. Let's hope those future generations have better things to worry about than where Baba rests.

While Mariru is busy admiring concrete and steel in Bondo, he also apparently squeezed in a visit to Nyamira University College. That one’s only 35% done, launched in October 2025. More classrooms, a library, ablution blocks. All important stuff, I guess, if you’re a student with a future. Then there's the Gusii Stadium, slated to become a 14,000-seater marvel. And Moi Stadium in Kisumu, also getting a facelift. All these projects are apparently part of a "broader programme to modernise sports infrastructure." Right. Because a shiny stadium will put food on the table. It’s like they’re building all these things to distract us from the fact that everything else is falling apart.

Honestly, it’s the same old song and dance. Government officials tour projects, announce completion targets, and pat each other on the back. Meanwhile, the cost of living keeps climbing. We’ve seen this movie before, haven't we? It’s like watching a real-life version of "Roses Are Red, But The Bruises Are Still Purple," just with more concrete and less actual benefit for the common mwananchi. Maybe they should focus on building a functional economy instead of mausoleums and stadiums. But what do I know? I’m just a realist from Nairobi.
