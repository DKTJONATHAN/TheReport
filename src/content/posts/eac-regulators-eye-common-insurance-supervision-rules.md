EAC Insurance Regulators Eye Common Rules - Just Don't Hold Your Breath

WARNING: Another committee meeting, another promise of "harmonisation." Don't be fooled, this is just the same old song and dance with a fresh coat of jargon.

Caption: More meetings, more tea, more promises of a brighter, simpler insurance future.





So, the East African Insurance Supervisors Association (EAISA) has gathered in Nairobi. Six countries, a whole lot of regulators, and a shiny new "harmonised insurance core principles assessment template." Twenty-five principles, they say, to "provide a common basis." Translation: more paperwork, more meetings, more opportunities for officials to look busy. They're talking about "peer learning" and "regulatory convergence." Sounds fancy, doesn't it? It's just a way of saying they'll compare notes and see how far behind everyone else is.

And of course, there's the obligatory mention of "technology-enabled regional insurance supervisory framework" and "stronger emphasis on consumer protection." Consumer protection? Please. These are the same folks who let things slide until the sky is falling. This is all about making life easier for the big insurers who are already hopping across borders like gazelles. Jubilee, Britam, ICEA LION - they're already there. This is for them, not for the mwananchi trying to get a decent policy that actually pays out when their house floods. It’s not unlike the global debt trap, where the little guy always gets caught. [The Global Debt Trap: Wall Street’s AI Hallucination and the Death of Low Rates](/posts/the-global-debt-trap--wall-street-s-ai-hallucination-and-the-death-of-low-rates)

They've even approved guidelines for "complaints management" and "monitoring and evaluation." Like that's going to change anything. The real game is the "regional insurance sandbox framework" and the "Regional Integrated Insurance Supervisory Software (RIISS)." More tech buzzwords to mask the same old inefficiencies. They want to "strengthen information sharing." What they really want is a common platform to track how much money they can extract. And Kenya gets to host the secretariat for five years. Great. More tea and biscuits for the Nairobi elite, while the rest of us hope our premiums don't skyrocket.

So, what’s the real outcome? A lot of talk, a few more committees, and the vague hope that by December 2026, things will magically be "more integrated, coordinated and technology-enabled." Don't get your hopes up. This is East Africa. Progress is measured in decades, not years, and usually involves a healthy dose of bureaucratic inertia. They might eventually get there, but don't expect it to be smooth, easy, or beneficial for anyone but the suits in the corner offices.
