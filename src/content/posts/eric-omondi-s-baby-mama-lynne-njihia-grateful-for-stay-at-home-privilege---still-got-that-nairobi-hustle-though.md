So, Lynne Njihia, Eric Omondi's baby mama, is out here on the gram, waxing lyrical about being a "full-time stay-at-home mum." Apparently, Eric is footing the bill, allowing her to witness all those precious baby milestones. She calls it a "privilege" and is "so grateful." You know, the kind of gratitude that comes with someone else picking up the tab for your domestic bliss.

She’s quick to clarify, though, that this isn't some sort of retirement plan. Oh no, Lynne is still a "paid content creator," still hitting the road, seeing friends, and, naturally, enjoying her "own life." Because nothing screams "full-time mum" like a flexible work schedule that allows for travel and a vibrant social calendar. It’s the Nairobi dream, I guess, where even a break from the grind is meticulously curated and branded.

It’s funny, isn't it? While some of us are juggling rent, matatu fares, and the sheer existential dread of Monday mornings, others are being "enabled" to be "fully present" with their kids, all while maintaining their careers. It’s a stark reminder that while we all might be in the same city, our realities are worlds apart. This whole narrative of "privilege" is something we see often, especially when the topic turns to money and influence, like in the ongoing drama surrounding [BRIBES, BETS, AND THE NEW BOARDROOM BOSS: CAN GEORGE KITHI FIX THIS MESS?](/posts/bribes--bets--and-the-new-boardroom-boss--can-george-kithi-fix-this-mess?).

Now, they're "cordially co-parenting," and Lynne is showering praise on Eric's fathering skills. It’s a neat little package, isn’t it? A testament to how relationships can evolve, and how financial support can smooth over a lot of rough edges. It's a story as old as time in this city - a bit of drama, a bit of cash, and a whole lot of social media updates.
