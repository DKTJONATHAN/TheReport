TWO KENYA POWER COPS CAUGHT RED-HANDED SNIFFING FOR KSH.100K BRIBE IN MOMBASA

Another day, another pair of uniformed vultures circling the gullible. This time it's two Kenya Power police officers in Mombasa, caught with their beaks in the till. Apparently, accusing a man of illegally hooking up his lights is now a Ksh.100,000 business opportunity for these fine gentlemen.

Inspector Benjamin Ochieng and Corporal Said Bakari, according to the so-called Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC), thought they’d found themselves a nice little earner. They threatened to make life difficult for some poor soul in Bombolulu, promising to look the other way for a hefty sum. This is the same old song and dance we hear in this country.

Thankfully, this particular complainant wasn't as dumb as they thought, or perhaps just had enough of the constant extortion. They ran to the EACC, who, for once, actually bothered to do their job and set a trap. The officers, in their infinite wisdom, escorted the victim to a bank to collect their ill-gotten gains. Imagine the nerve.

They snagged them receiving half the loot, Ksh.50,000, right outside a bank. Of course, they were processed and let out on a measly Ksh.30,000 bail. Because, in Kenya, even when caught red-handed, the price of "freedom" is often less than what you'd pay for a decent car part. This whole circus makes you wonder how much of Kenya's domestic debt scandal, [The Elite's Personal ATM](/posts/kenya-s-domestic-debt-scandal--the-elite-s-personal-atm), is fueled by such petty thuggery.

And the EACC says investigations are ongoing. How inspiring. I’ll hold my breath for the outcome, which, knowing our justice system, will likely involve a slap on the wrist and a promise to do better. Meanwhile, that Ksh.50,000 is probably already funding someone’s weekend getaway.

The only surprising thing here is that they were actually caught. Most of these guys operate with impunity, their pockets perpetually lined with the "taxes" they extort from the common mwananchi. But hey, at least this time, the photos are out there. Small victories, I guess.







Two Kenya Power police officers found themselves in the EACC's net in Mombasa, allegedly for shaking down a complainant for a cool Ksh.100,000. Inspector Benjamin Ochieng and Corporal Said Bakari, according to the anti-graft commission, decided that accusing someone of illegally connecting electricity was a golden ticket to easy money. They apparently offered their silence in exchange for the hefty sum.

The report states that the officers were aiming to deter any investigation or legal action against the individual, provided their palms were greased sufficiently. This kind of shakedown is hardly news; it's practically a national pastime. The EACC, for all its bluster, claims it received the report and launched an inquiry.

On Monday, August 24, 2026, the EACC staged an operation. The two officers, playing their part in this sordid drama, allegedly accompanied the complainant to a bank on Moi Avenue to collect the promised cash. It's a sad testament to how brazen these characters have become, conducting their illicit business in broad daylight.

The EACC's own officers then nabbed the suspects outside DTB Bank after they had allegedly received Ksh.50,000 as part of the demanded bribe. They were booked at Central Police Station, processed, and then, in true Kenyan fashion, released on Ksh.30,000 cash bail. This whole charade makes one ponder the real cost of enforcing the law, especially when considering the vast sums lost to issues like [Kenya's Domestic Debt Scandal: The Elite's Personal ATM](/posts/kenya-s-domestic-debt-scandal--the-elite-s-personal-atm).

The commission insists that investigations are ongoing. One can only imagine the level of "investigation" that will actually take place, given the typical outcomes for such cases. In the meantime, these officers are back on the streets, likely plotting their next "opportunity" while the complainant is left to wonder if justice will ever truly prevail.
