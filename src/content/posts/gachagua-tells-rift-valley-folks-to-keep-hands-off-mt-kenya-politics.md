So, Rigathi Gachagua, the man who used to be second-in-command and now fancies himself the kingmaker of Mt Kenya, has decided to wag his finger at the Rift Valley. He’s got this big beef with leaders from that side of the country trooping into Mt Kenya, apparently trying to drum up support for President Ruto. According to him, these visits are a waste of time, as if the people here haven't already made up their minds. As if we’re easily swayed by some roadside rally.

Gachagua’s argument is simple: these Rift Valley chaps should go back to their own turf and figure out how to help Ruto fix the mess Kenyans are in. Instead of "persuading our people," they should be "advising their kinsman." It’s a classic political maneuver, trying to paint himself as the protector of Mt Kenya’s interests, while subtly reminding everyone that Ruto, their "kinsman," has apparently dropped the ball. He's even claiming Ruto betrayed the region, which, let's be honest, is a narrative that’s been brewing for a while. This feels like another chapter in the ongoing saga of selling the pipe for a trillion-shilling pipe dream, where promises are made and then conveniently forgotten.

He’s gone further, declaring that Mt Kenya won’t be giving Ruto another chance in 2027. Bold words, indeed. It paints a picture of a region united and ready to chart its own course, with Gachagua, naturally, at the helm of this supposed political awakening. Whether the ground actually feels this way is another story, but for now, he’s certainly making noise.

And it’s not just about wagging fingers at others; Gachagua is also planning some payback. He’s announced he'll be touring constituencies of MPs who apparently went against his "Democracy for the Citizens Party" during that Ol Kalou mini-poll. It’s a tit-for-tat strategy, designed to hold leaders accountable in their own backyards. The goal? To ensure that anyone who dared to oppose his vision for Mt Kenya faces the music from their own constituents before the 2027 elections. This whole political dance is starting to feel like a broken record, full of predictable moves and grand pronouncements.
