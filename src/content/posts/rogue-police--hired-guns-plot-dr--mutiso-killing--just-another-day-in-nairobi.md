So, Dr. Victoria Mutiso is dead. Shot dead in an Uber, no less. Just another Tuesday in Nairobi, isn't it? The Directorate of Criminal Investigations is now sniffing around, claiming it's a "complex" case with "multiple strands." Translation: they're wading through a swamp of land disputes, simmering family tensions, and whispers of rogue police officers. The usual suspects, really. It’s almost like living in a bad Nollywood movie, if Nollywood had more concrete and less glitter.

The whole mess seems to be centered around a measly 20 acres near the Mlolongo weighbridge. Apparently, Dr. Mutiso had slapped a caveat on it, which is like putting a giant, flashing "DO NOT TOUCH" sign on something everyone wants. Naturally, this upset some people. And when people are upset about land in Kenya, things tend to get ugly. This is the kind of mess that makes you wonder if even billionaires can buy peace, like they say they can in Beverly Hills. [Bullets In Beverly Hills: Even Billionaires Can Not Buy Peace](/posts/bullets-in-beverly-hills--even-billionaires-can-not-buy-peace).

The DCI, in their infinite wisdom and after a "Friday briefing," has identified six prime suspects. Six! And guess who's on the list? Three police officers and four civilians. Amongst the civilians are the ex-wife, son, and daughter of Dr. Mutiso's husband, Prof. David Musyimi Ndetei. Talk about keeping it in the family, or rather, the dysfunctional extended family. The whole operation, allegedly coordinated by a Daniel Mwangi Njoroge and facilitated by Kelvin Ngugi Kinuthia, involved blocking her Uber with a Prado and a motorcycle-riding hitman. Classic Nairobi execution.

And of course, there's a possible link to the September 2025 killing of lawyer Mathews Kyalo Mbobu. Because why have one high-profile murder when you can have two, linked by the same shady characters and a potentially valuable piece of dirt? The bullets, by the way, were fired from a police-issue Mini Jericho pistol. Shocking, I know. Inspector Kenneth Kipkemboi, allegedly the intermediary and gun-supplier, is also in the hot seat. It’s a well-oiled machine of crime, funded by who knows who, carried out by men who probably think they’re in some action flick.

Then there's the whole sordid affair with Rose Mbithe Mulwa, the ex-wife of Prof. Ndetei, and her children. They’re suspected financiers. Apparently, there were threats, attempted shootings, and even a property demolition before this whole mess. It’s a tangled web of alleged land fraud, forgery, and good old-fashioned greed. The DCI is running around, collecting CCTV footage, call data, and forensic evidence, hoping to stitch together this disaster. But let's be real, in this city, some stories never truly end, they just get buried under more crime.
