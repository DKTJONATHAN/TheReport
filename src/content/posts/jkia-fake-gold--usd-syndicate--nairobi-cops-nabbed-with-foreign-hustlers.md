So, the DCI, in their infinite wisdom, has managed to round up 13 individuals, a motley crew of locals and foreigners, right after they’d supposedly graced us with their presence at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport. The alleged crime? Running a sophisticated international operation peddling fake gold and counterfeit US dollars. Honestly, you can't make this stuff up. Three police officers, no less, were part of this grand scheme, using fancy cars and fake government stickers to escort some unsuspecting foreign client to their little hideout along Mombasa Road. It’s almost amusing if it wasn't so depressingly predictable.

According to the boys in blue (or at least, the ones who weren't busy being arrested), this whole operation was a meticulously planned affair. They tracked the suspicious movement from the airport, leading them to a local den where they found 65 fake gold bars and a cool USD 750,000 in fake notes. Oh, and they also found all the ingredients for their charade: plastic seals, strapping rolls, cement bags, and assorted chemicals. It’s like a DIY kit for becoming a criminal mastermind, Nairobi style. The arrested officers are apparently from the Security of Government Buildings unit. How ironic.

The foreign contingent in this particular circus hails from Nigeria, Sudan, Canada, and the Democratic Republic of Congo. A truly international flavor to our city's brand of crime. It’s hardly surprising, though. These gold scams are becoming as common as matatu fares, and they’re always targeting foreigners and anyone foolish enough to believe a deal that sounds too good to be true. It makes you wonder if these syndicates are just exploiting the desperation that seems to permeate every corner of this city. Perhaps this is just another symptom of how [Our National Security Is Just A Side Hustle For Nyayo House Cartels](/posts/our-national-security-is-just-a-side-hustle-for-nyayo-house-cartels).

And as if to add a cherry on top of this sordid affair, the Kenya Revenue Authority also managed to intercept 40 undeclared iPhones at JKIA, fresh off a flight from Dubai. Apparently, these were some fancy iPhone 18 Pro models, worth a cool Ksh8.2 million. So, while the DCI was busy busting the gold and dollar peddlers, the KRA was dealing with the petty shoplifters of the tech world. It’s a whole ecosystem of hustles, from the grand international scams to the slightly less glamorous but equally illegal importations.

This latest drama unfolds just weeks after a city lawyer was nabbed for a Ksh37 million gold fraud. It’s a constant cycle, isn't it? The authorities make a show of arrests, the media reports it, and then the next week, there’s another headline about a similar scam. It’s enough to make you lose faith in everything, from the authenticity of your boda boda driver’s fare to the very concept of justice in this city. Just another day in Nairobi, where the only thing more fake than the gold is often the promise of a better tomorrow.
