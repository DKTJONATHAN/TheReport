Harambee Stars Held At Home By Eritrea - Just As Expected, Really

The only surprise is that it wasn’t a loss. Another day, another pathetic showing from Harambee Stars, proving that even Eritrea, a team that probably trains in borrowed boots, can hold us to a draw. Thousands of fans, bless their naive hearts, showed up expecting a miracle. What they got was a familiar taste of disappointment, served with a side of unpaid allowances. Typical.

Our boys, apparently too busy striking over paltry sums of money to actually train, managed to concede a goal thanks to some amateur defending. Manzur Okwaro, bless his confused soul, lost the ball outside the box - a move we’ve all seen on dusty streets, not at Nyayo Stadium. Ali Suleiman and Nahom Girmai did the rest, firing past a goalkeeper who probably also wondered what he was doing there. The scoreline, 1-0 to Eritrea, remained until the very last minutes, a testament to our ability to snatch defeat from the jaws of mediocrity.

It took a late tap-in from Ryan Ogam to salvage a point, a point that feels more like a consolation prize for participating. This isn't a victory, it's a reminder that our last win against Eritrea was when dinosaurs roamed the earth, or at least in 2003. Meanwhile, Eritrea was here since Tuesday, getting their act together, while our team was apparently practicing their protest songs. It’s no wonder their defence looked like a wall and ours looked like a sieve.

Coach Benni McCarthy, brought in with much fanfare, seems to be performing magic tricks, turning potential into… well, draws against lowly ranked teams. He’s shuffling players, making substitutions - all the usual managerial smoke and mirrors. Yet, the result is the same. It’s enough to make you wonder if the whole system is designed for us to fail, a bit like the promises made in the [The 2026 ICT Budget: Another Digital Mirage for a Hungry Nation](/posts/the-2026-ict-budget--another-digital-mirage-for-a-hungry-nation).

The introduction of subs like Ben Stanley Omondi eventually led to the equalizer, but let’s not get carried away. This isn’t a triumph; it’s damage control. We should be dominating these teams, not scrambling for a draw at home. The sad reality is that this is Harambee Stars: a team perpetually living on the edge of disappointment, fueled by fan hope and manager desperation.
