So, the Kenya Kwanza crew is all in a tizzy about supposed leaks from State House to their former deputy, Rigathi Gachagua. Bungoma Governor Ken Lusaka and Kakamega Deputy Governor Ayub Savula are huffing and puffing about national security threats. Honestly, this is the same old song and dance. You put people in power, they start squabbling over crumbs, and suddenly everyone's a spy. It's pure theatre.

Gachagua, bless his dramatic heart, apparently knew about Ruto's State of the Nation address beforehand. He even predicted it would be all fluff and no substance, just like a politician's promise. And lo and behold, Ruto goes and launches this "Beyond Vision 2030" grand plan. It’s almost like Gachagua has a direct line to the presidential teleprompter, or perhaps, just a good understanding of how these guys operate. The masses are already groaning under taxes, but hey, let's talk about the next 60 years, shall we?

Gachagua, in his usual dramatic fashion, admitted he has "many sources" in government, including "patriotic men and women" who are supposedly worried about the country’s direction. He's practically running a shadow intelligence agency from his Nyeri home, thanking civil servants and police for feeding him scraps of information. It’s a classic power play, trying to stay relevant by being the "man who knows." Reminds me of that [Dragon's Ledger report](https://example.com/posts/the-dragon-s-ledger--beijing-s-paper-growth-masks-a-looming-global-fracture), where everyone's playing a game with hidden motives.

He claims the information comes "right inside State House," painting a picture of a disgruntled workforce and a President too busy to notice. According to Gachagua, people in the Presidential Delivery Service are complaining. Of course they are. Who wouldn't be, when the big boss is supposedly working for himself instead of the country? It’s the same old story, just with a new coat of paint.

The leaders hailing Ruto's address as "good governance" are either incredibly naive or just playing their part in this grand political circus. The truth is, the whole country is probably feeling the pinch, and talk of long-term visions while people can barely afford a loaf of bread is just rubbing salt in the wound.

So, while Kenya Kwanza leaders demand Ruto hunt down these phantom "moles," the real issue is far more mundane: power struggles and the desperate need to be seen as "in the know" in this city where information is currency. It's all just noise to distract from the real problems.
