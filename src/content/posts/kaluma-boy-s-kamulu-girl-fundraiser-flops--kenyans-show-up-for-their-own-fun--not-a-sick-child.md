Honestly, you have to hand it to Kaluma Boy for trying. He mobilised, he begged, he even threw in a cheesy Kenyan flag stunt. All for Risper Wambui, a Kamulu girl supposedly battling stomach cancer. And what was the grand response from the very Kenyans he thought would rally? A pathetic trickle. The photos speak for themselves - rows of empty chairs, a testament to our collective apathy when the cameras aren't permanently rolling. It’s almost funny, if it weren’t so utterly depressing.

Kaluma Boy, bless his naive heart, is still chirping about “God’s plans” and his commitment. He’s promising to keep supporting Risper, even though the very people he rallied behind him - the TikTokers, the content creators, the masses - apparently couldn't be bothered to show up. He spent days pushing this, painting a picture of unity and solidarity. Turns out, that unity only stretches as far as our social media feeds, not to an actual physical location when real effort is required. It's always a circus, isn't it? Just like when [UDA, ODM Zoning Clash: Another Kenyan Political Circus Starts](/posts/uda--odm-zoning-clash--another-kenyan-political-circus-starts), everyone gets excited about the drama, but few want to do the actual work.

He even acknowledged the haters, as if their negativity was proof he was doing something good. Newsflash, Kaluma Boy: some people are unhappy because it’s a scam waiting to happen, and others are just too busy living their own lives to chase after every sob story plastered online. You can’t force people to care when they’d rather watch cat videos or argue about politics. The Kenyan flag thing? Pure theatre. It was supposed to symbolise unity, but it ended up highlighting the stark reality: we’re united in our indifference.

So, while Kaluma Boy is patting himself on the back for his unwavering dedication to Risper, the harsh truth is that the numbers don’t lie. His grand mobilisation effort amounted to a few sympathetic souls and a whole lot of wasted effort. We talk a big game about compassion and pulling together, but when it comes down to it, most of us are just looking for the next viral trend. Risper’s battle against cancer is real, but the support she needed turned out to be as hollow as Kaluma Boy's hopes.
