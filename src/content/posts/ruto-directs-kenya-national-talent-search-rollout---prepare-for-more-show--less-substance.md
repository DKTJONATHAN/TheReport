Ruto Directs Kenya National Talent Search Rollout - Brace Yourselves for More Empty Promises

President Ruto, speaking from a State Lodge in Bungoma, has decreed the establishment of the Kenya National Talent Search. Apparently, we're going to scour the country for talent in music, comedy, acting, poetry, film, dance, and sports. Because clearly, that's what Kenya needs right now, not addressing the sinking economy or the perennial water woes. The State Department for Youth, the Creative Economy Office, and the Sports Fund are apparently supposed to get this rolling with "urgency." Let's see how long that lasts.

The President’s grand vision is to "identify, nurture and support talent wherever it exists." Sounds lovely on paper, doesn't it? It’s all part of this grand scheme to "formalise the creative economy" and present it as a viable career path. Because what’s better than a stable job with benefits? A stab at stardom, of course! He's even proposed 'Kenya Beyond 2030' as the theme. Let’s hope the youth are more imaginative than the government in picturing that future, especially when we're still dealing with the fallout from things like the [Fuel Scandal Sees 3 Top Energy Officials Resign - What Else Is New?]( /posts/fuel-scandal-sees-3-top-energy-officials-resign---what-else-is-new).

Adding to the fanfare, winners of the National Talent Search will apparently be recognized by the President himself at the next Kalasha International Film and TV Awards. And for those performing at the 2026 Kenya Music Festivals State Concert, their institutions will receive a cool Ksh. 5 million. Meanwhile, the rest of us are left wondering if we'll even have enough for bus fare. The Ministry of Education is also tasked with bringing Science and Engineering Fair winners to State House. Because meeting the President is surely more important than, say, ensuring proper infrastructure or access to clean water.

The President also directed that beginning next year, school creative events should carry the 'Kenya Beyond 2030' theme. The idea is to get young people to "imagine and shape Kenya’s future" with their talents. It’s a nice thought, but I’ve seen enough of these "initiatives" to know that what usually ends up happening is a lot of fanfare and very little follow-through. It’s the same old song and dance, just with a new tune.

And let's not forget the promise of meeting the Science and Engineering Fair winners at State House. "I want to meet them, see what they have built and hear their ideas. Above all, I want them to know that their country believes in them and values them." I sincerely hope their country’s belief translates into more than just a photo op. Because right now, it feels like we're living in a [Traveling Circus of Plastic Soccer]( /posts/the-traveling-circus-of-plastic-soccer), full of dazzling displays and very little substance.

This whole talent search sounds like another government attempt to distract from the real issues. While Ruto is busy planning galas and award ceremonies, the rest of us are just trying to survive. It's a classic move, really. Keep the people entertained with shiny new programs while the important stuff gathers dust. Let's just hope this doesn't become another example of the government's "we're all in this together" rhetoric while [Ruto Leads Labour Day Festivities in Vihiga, While City Drowns and Pockets Empty]( /posts/ruto-leads-labour-day-festivities-in-vihiga--while-city-drowns-and-pockets-empty).
