So, these BCG chaps in Nairobi are flapping their gums about how Kenyan banks have a "narrow window" to get their act together against AI-driven financial fraud. Apparently, the same fancy tech that's letting criminals churn out fake identities, cloned voices, and all sorts of digital nonsense at a dizzying pace can also be used by the banks. What a revolutionary idea. It's like telling a drowning man he can use his own hands to swim.

The real kicker is that this is supposed to be urgent because Africa, and Kenya in particular, is going all-in on mobile banking. Which, in plain Swahili, means more open doors for these digital bandits. They’re already seeing more organised attacks, with people losing Ksh108,482 on average. But apparently, that’s just the warm-up act. The real nightmare, according to BCG, are these "agentic systems" that can run scams from start to finish without a single human in sight.

BCG reckons these autonomous scam machines could slash the cost of fraud by 90 percent. Imagine that. Suddenly, individuals or small groups can pull off the kind of heists that used to require entire criminal syndicates. It's like giving every street kid a Kalashnikov. They’re saying this could automate the entire fraud chain in one of Africa’s most "digitally connected" ecosystems. Sounds like a recipe for disaster, not progress. It reminds me of how, even today, [30 Years to the First World? We Can’t Even Cross the Road Without a Bribe](/posts/30-years-to-the-first-world--we-can-t-even-cross-the-road-without-a-bribe), simple things still feel like a monumental struggle.

The report drivels on about banks needing to "act now" to build "adaptive, AI-enabled defences." They suggest things like better threat monitoring, "red-teaming" their own systems, and keeping an eye on what the open-source AI models are up to. Because, you know, those fancy "frontier AI models" with their built-in safeguards won't stay locked down forever. Within six to twelve months, the criminals will have their own versions, probably with fewer ethical constraints.

And get this, agentic AI capabilities have supposedly improved tenfold every year since 2024. If that pace keeps up, we're looking at scams that can run for days or weeks in a year or two. Banks are being urged to build internal AI teams and speed up approvals for new tech. They also need "fire breaks" and "surge playbooks" for when the fraud activity inevitably spikes. It's all about being proactive, apparently. But let's be real, when has "proactive" ever truly stopped the determined scammer?

The bottom line is this: BCG says the transition to agentic scams could be swift, catching many institutions off guard. Banks that delay are essentially handing a free pass to adversaries who are learning faster than they are. So, while BCG offers a slew of recommendations, the cynical Nairobi realist in me sees it as a fancy way of saying: "Get ready for a whole new level of mess."
