He's been handed a staggering 200-year sentence for the murders of 191 people, most of them children, who he convinced to starve themselves to death. It’s the kind of grim, twisted logic that makes you wonder about the sanity of anyone who followed him.

Justice Diana Kavedza certainly didn't mince words, sentencing him to 100 years for starvation murders and another 100 for direct homicides. He could have faced the death penalty, but Kenya, like a lot of places, has gotten comfortable with the idea without actually doing it since 1987. Still, 200 years is a long time to reflect on how you led hundreds of people to their doom in the Shakahola Forest. His so-called church was in operation since 2003, which means he had over two decades to spread his deadly gospel.

Mackenzie, naturally, denies it all, claiming he didn't *force* anyone to starve. Oh, so it was a voluntary starvation party? How reassuring.
