DCI Nabs a Few in Mt Elgon MP Aspirant Murder - Don't Get Your Hopes Up

The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) is patting themselves on the back, announcing the arrest of three suspects in the brazen shooting of Nathan Masai Wasama, a 61-year-old man who dared to dream of being an MP for Mount Elgon. Apparently, this businessman and philanthropist, a devoted Seventh-day Adventist to boot, was gunned down at his home. Three arrests, they say. Good for them. This is what passes for progress in this country, isn't it? A few arrests, a bunch of recovered junk, and the promise of more investigation.

The DCI’s statement paints a picture of a swift operation: four men on a motorcycle, one hops off, walks into the shop, shoots, and they all vanish. Classic. And what did they find during their raid? A "military-style smoke jacket," a stun grenade, axes, pangas, knives, arrows, three motorcycles, phones, and an iPod. It sounds less like a murder scene and more like a pawn shop that had a bad day. Are they trying to impress us with their haul, or is this just the usual assortment of tools for anyone wanting to cause trouble?

This whole affair reeks of the usual political theatre. Wasama had just declared his intention to run for the parliamentary seat in 2027. Suddenly, he’s a target. And let's not forget, this isn't his first dance with death; he survived a previous attempt in 2024. It makes you wonder if he was truly a philanthropist or just a rich man with enemies. We’ve seen it all before - the grand pronouncements of intent followed by a sudden, violent end. It’s a tired script, really.

The DCI is still hunting for the fourth suspect, because of course they are. They’re following leads to figure out the "motive." As if the motive isn't staring them in the face: politics. It’s always politics, or money, or both. This nation thrives on a steady diet of ambition and betrayal, and frankly, watching these dramas unfold is starting to feel like observing [The Mediterranean Theater of Managed Decline](/posts/the-mediterranean-theater-of-managed-decline). You know how it ends, it’s just a matter of who gets the last bloody act.

So, three suspects are in custody. The search for the fourth is ongoing. We’ll get more statements, more "progress reports," and eventually, if we're lucky, a conviction that will be appealed into oblivion. The headlines will shift, the public’s attention will wander, and another aspirant, or perhaps a businessman who got too big for his boots, will end up as another statistic. Just another day in the grand theatre of Kenyan "justice."
