SSCLAfrica Launches in Nairobi: Another Green Scheme to Empty Pockets?

Africa's workforce is being 'equipped' with green skills, so the suits tell us. SSCLAfrica has landed in Nairobi, promising practical know-how for the 'emerging green economy'. More like emerging opportunities to shuffle money around while pretending to care about the planet. It's an e-learning platform, naturally, because who has time for actual classrooms when you can stare at a screen and get a certificate that probably costs more than the actual knowledge gained? They're touting climate action, sustainable finance, ESG - buzzwords to make you feel important while the real issues fester.

The launch at some "Green Works 4 Africa Forum" sounds like another shindig for people who like talking about problems without actually solving them. Apparently, skills development is key to Africa's "response to climate risks." Translation: we need more people to navigate the mess someone else created. Energy, finance, manufacturing, agriculture - it’s all getting a green makeover. This means professionals, whether they want it or not, will have to understand "ESG reporting" and "responsible business practices." Because, of course, corporations only care about doing right when they're forced to or can spin it for some PR points.

They brag about their "African-centred" approach, using local examples. Cute. As if the problems facing a Kenyan renewable energy market are that different from the usual corporate greed and governmental ineptitude elsewhere. They’ve even got a free three-hour course. For free! Must be good, right? Or just a teaser to get you hooked on more paid courses that will likely be less useful than a screen door on a submarine. It’s self-paced, of course. Because flexibility is just another word for "we don't have enough actual instructors."

The International Labour Organization chimes in, predicting millions of jobs. Millions of *additional* jobs, they say. While transforming existing ones. Sounds like a lot of upheaval for a workforce that’s already struggling to keep up. For African economies, where jobs are scarcer than common sense, connecting green investment with local "capabilities" is the real challenge. And let's be honest, the real benefit will likely go to the folks running these platforms and the companies that can afford the training, not the everyday person trying to make ends meet. It’s another layer of complexity added to the already convoluted system, much like the convoluted dealings of Nairobi’s 'elite' that we’ve covered before. SSCLAfrica’s CEO, Dr Enoch Opare Mintah, claims it's about "skilling, upskilling, and reskilling." In other words, making sure everyone is adaptable enough to keep up with the latest corporate jargon and demands. Engineers, accountants, lawyers - they all need "sustainability competencies." Because why have a job that just pays the bills when you can have one that requires constant retraining to satisfy the whims of the market? It’s a way to keep people busy and dependent on these training programs, ensuring a steady flow of cash.

Ultimately, this SSCLAfrica launch is another reminder that the so-called green transition is more about creating new industries and revenue streams than genuinely solving anything. They talk about human capabilities and translating green investment into local skills. But the reality is likely to be more of the same: a lot of talk, a lot of training, and a select few benefiting while the rest of us are left to navigate the fallout. Don’t be surprised if these "green skills" end up being just another way to justify higher costs and more complex regulations, much like the endless foreign trips Ruto seems to enjoy. SSCLAfrica has rolled into Nairobi, trumpeting its arrival as a vital step in equipping Africa's workforce for the "emerging green economy." This e-learning platform claims to offer practical skills for climate action, sustainable finance, and ESG - essentially, the latest corporate buzzwords packaged as career advancement. It’s another layer of complexity added to the already challenging landscape for African professionals, promising a pathway to relevance in a world that seems determined to reinvent itself every few years.

The platform's debut at the Green Works 4 Africa Forum sounds like a typical gathering of well-intentioned individuals discussing problems that are far more entrenched than a few online courses can fix. They're positioning skills development as Africa's answer to climate risks and economic upheaval. This means anyone with a job, or looking for one, will likely need to understand concepts like "ESG reporting" and "resource efficiency," whether they understand the real implications or not. It’s about adaptation, sure, but often it’s just about keeping up with the latest demands of the market.

SSCLAfrica proudly boasts an "African-centred" approach, using local examples to make sustainability more palatable. As if the fundamental issues of economic disparity and resource exploitation are unique to this continent. They're offering free introductory courses, a clever tactic to lure people into a system that will undoubtedly lead to more paid training. The self-paced nature, while convenient, also suggests a lack of personalised guidance, leaving learners to navigate complex topics largely on their own.

The International Labour Organization chimes in with promises of millions of jobs, while conveniently downplaying the inevitable disruption to existing livelihoods. The platform's CEO, Dr Enoch Opare Mintah, talks about "skilling, upskilling, and reskilling" the workforce.
