Citi Says Investors Upbeat About Kenya's Prospects - Don't Be Fooled

This whole "investors are still keen on Kenya" song and dance from Citi? It's the same old tune, just with a few new, shiny chords. Apparently, while the rest of the world is scrambling for safety like rats from a sinking ship, these global players are still sniffing around Nairobi. They're not pulling their money out, not even with the Middle East looking like a tinderbox and our own election circus about to kick off. So, according to Citi's CEO, Martin Mugambi, your Sh129 to the dollar is "stable" and our Eurobonds are actually *improving*. Right.

Never mind that global capital is supposed to be fleeing emerging markets for gold and the almighty dollar. According to Mugambi, Kenya is somehow immune, with "significant portfolio investment" and "FDI flows" pouring in. Unctad claims we've seen a 37.7 percent jump in FDI, landing us at a record $3.2 billion, thanks to multinational companies apparently falling head over heels for our digital infrastructure and AI. It’s a good thing we're East Africa’s "fastest-growing investment destination," especially when the global race for capital is so fierce. Makes you wonder if they’re investing in Kenya or just in our politicians’ promises.

And the ratings agencies! Moody's upgraded us to B3 from Caa1, saying the debt default risk is lower. Fitch gave us a stable outlook at B-. All this while our forex reserves are supposedly at a record $14.17 billion, enough to cover six months of imports. It’s almost enough to make you believe things are actually looking up, if you squint hard enough and ignore the actual realities of everyday life here. It reminds me of how some Kenyan 'prophets' are looking to blockchain for tithing, a sort of faith in the digital, even when the real world feels shaky. [The Crypto Clergy: Why Kenyan 'Prophets' are Adopting Blockchain Tithing](/posts/crypto-clergy-kenyan-prophets-blockchain-tithing)

But here's the kicker, the part they conveniently gloss over: local corporates are a different story. While the foreigners might be feeling flush, our own businesses are treading water. Consumer demand is drier than the Nyika plateau thanks to inflation, and the looming general elections are making everyone hit the pause button. "Sitting on the fence" is the phrase of the day, with cash flows still weaker than a student's budget after rent is due. Non-performing loans are still hovering around 15.3 percent, a stark reminder that not everyone is enjoying this "investor confidence."

So, yes, Citi might be touting a rosy picture for the international crowd. They see big numbers, stable shillings (for now), and a country that's somehow weathering global storms. But down here, on the ground, the reality is a bit more…sticky. Our banks are struggling with their corporate clients, who are too busy worrying about election outcomes and weak sales to make any big moves. Growth in credit to the private sector is picking up, sure, but it's still miles away from what we actually need. It’s a classic case of the big players seeing one thing, while the rest of us are just trying to keep the lights on.
