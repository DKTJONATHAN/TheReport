So, Wiper leader Kalonzo Musyoka has decided to throw a few more names into the 2027 election blender, hoping something tasty comes out. He's paraded Nyali MP Mohammed Ali and former CS Aisha Jumwa as his new Coast "dream team" to go head-to-head with President Ruto's allies. Apparently, they're supposed to "reclaim the Coast for the opposition." What a joke. This is just another desperate attempt to stay relevant in a political landscape that's already moved on.

Kalonzo's grand plan seems to be propping up Jumwa, even proposing her as his deputy at Wiper and for the gubernatorial race. He's crowing about Wiper replacing ODM as the region's dominant force. The man's living in a fantasy land. Remember when he and Raila were the unstoppable duo? That ship sailed, and now he's trying to cling to any lifeboat he can find. He's even boasting that the "former ODM stronghold now belongs to Wiper," as if the voters are just pawns to be moved around.

Meanwhile, Mohammed Ali, comparing himself and Jumwa to "Ronaldo and Messi," is promising an "aggressive mobilisation campaign" to "remove this regime." Right. Ali's also busy throwing mud, accusing Kenya Kwanza of abandoning its allies. It's all a bit rich, coming from a man who’s hopped between parties more times than a tourist on a safari. This is just the usual political theatre, designed to confuse and manipulate the electorate. We've seen it all before.

Danson Mwashako, the Wundanyi MP, claims he’s been pressured and even offered bribes to ditch Wiper and join the government, but he’s standing firm. He says he refused their money and threats, even resigning as Coast Parliamentary Group chair to avoid defecting. This is supposed to be a heroic tale, but let's be real, it’s just another bid for attention. Everyone knows these politicians are just looking for the best deal, and if the price is right, their "principles" can be surprisingly flexible. The behind-the-scenes jockeying for power and money is as predictable as the sunrise.

Jumwa’s advice to voters is a masterclass in double-speak: accept government goodies but vote for the opposition. She’s warning about the government deploying resources but urges people not to be swayed by "goodies." It’s a classic move - tell people to take the bribe but vote with your conscience, as if that ever works. This whole charade is about consolidating power and lining pockets. It’s not about liberation; it’s about who gets to feast at the trough next. The whole UDA done for narrative is just another propaganda piece.

This whole exercise is just the latest installment in the never-ending drama of Kenyan politics. The same faces, the same promises, the same hollow rhetoric. Whether it's Kalonzo, Ruto, or anyone else, they're all playing the same game. The only difference is who's holding the dice at any given moment. Don't get fooled by the grand pronouncements or the celebrity endorsements. It's all about power, and the people at the Coast, like everywhere else, are just collateral damage in their endless pursuit of it. We've seen how the global distraction factory works, and this is just more of the same.
