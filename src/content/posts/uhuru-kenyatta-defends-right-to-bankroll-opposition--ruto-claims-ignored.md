So, Uhuru Kenyatta, the man who handed over the reins, is now telling President Ruto to mind his own business. Apparently, the idea of a former president having opinions and, gasp, engaging in politics is too much for some. Kenyatta was out in Kitale, probably enjoying a breather from the presidential circus, when he decided to swat away Ruto's claims of him bankrolling the opposition. Like it's some grand conspiracy and not just a former leader having a right to speak his mind.

Meanwhile, at the same memorial service for the late Vice President Michael Kijana Wamalwa, the opposition crowd was busy plotting their next move. Unity and a single presidential candidate for 2027 - the usual song and dance. It’s almost quaint, isn't it? All this talk of strategy and unity, while the current administration is busy pointing fingers at the retired guys. One wonders if they ever stop to think about the actual issues affecting Kenyans, or if it’s all just a perpetual game of thrones.

It's a familiar script in this country. Politicians, whether in office or out, seem to have an endless supply of drama. You have shows like AM Live trying to make sense of it all, and platforms like Business Redefined dissecting economic policies that often feel like they're written in a foreign language. Then there are the podcasts, delving into everything from the digital creative economy to the messy realities of marital bliss and struggles on Gumzo La Sato. It's a lot to digest, and frankly, most of it circles back to the same old political theatre.

And speaking of political theatre, we have "Fixing the Nation," a show that, bless its heart, attempts to tackle the challenges facing Kenyan society. Anchored by respected journalists, it sounds noble, but how much fixing can really happen when the players keep changing the rules of the game? It reminds me of President Ruto's fuel 'cushioning' promises, where patience and a measly KSh10 savings are offered as solutions. Feels a bit like rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic, doesn't it?

You've got your "The Politalk" podcast, bravely promising to pull no punches on Kenyan politics. Good for them. They’ll need it. Because while Kenyatta insists on his right to political engagement and Ruto hurls accusations, the rest of us are left trying to navigate the complexities of our own lives. From the "Health Diary" trying to explain diseases to "Twende" showing us beautiful places we probably can't afford to visit, life goes on. And through it all, the political noise continues, a constant hum of accusations and defenses.
