And so it begins. Two cases of Ebola, one poor soul already dead, and the whole country's pretending to be shocked. The Ministry of Health, bless their cotton socks, is telling us not to panic. "No need for panic," they chirp, as if we haven't seen this movie before with COVID. This first case, a 40-year-old traveler from DRC who apparently sailed through Ugandan roads and then, *miraculously*, boarded a flight to Nairobi without anyone noticing he was carrying the Grim Reaper. Now one of his relatives is sick, naturally. They say there's no wider spread, but who are we kidding? We'll see.

The usual suspects are out, wailing about government unpreparedness. Former Chief Justice David Maraga, who’s already eyeing the 2027 presidential seat, is demanding answers about airport screening. Kalonzo Musyoka is pointing out the obvious: doctors at Nairobi Hospital, not our supposed border control, caught the problem. It's a classic Kenyan show, where the real heroes are the ones cleaning up the mess left by those in charge. It makes you wonder if they're learning anything at all. Maybe they should focus on things that actually matter, like political alliances. We've seen how those play out, for instance, when [Kindiki and Waiguru pledge Mt Kenya support for Ruto](/posts/kindiki--waiguru-pledge-mt-kenya-support-for-ruto---the-star-co-ke).

Medical unions are having a field day, calling our health system "not pandemic-ready." Apparently, we had since May to get our act together, with Uganda next door already grappling with cases. But no, someone "dropped the ball." And let's not forget the "lapse of leadership in governance of healthcare." It's a mess of broken pillars, unclear responsibilities between the Ministry of Health and the 47 county governments. Just imagine the chaos when resources are already stretched thin, with rural facilities lacking basic gear and a shortage of health workers, to boot.

And who's going to pay the price? The healthcare workers, of course. Many of them are still exposed, still lacking proper cover, and now facing another deadly disease. Remember how many doctors and nurses we lost during COVID? Some agreements with neighbors are being questioned too. Borders are still largely open, despite the rising death toll in the region. We're expected to trust these agreements, without any real audit. It's all a bit too convenient, isn't it? Just fill out the health declaration form truthfully, they say. As if that's ever been a guarantee.
