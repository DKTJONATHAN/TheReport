So, the President was at it again, this time at the "Agriculture and Food Systems Transformation Summit 2026." Apparently, the plan is to turn Kenya into some sort of agricultural export hub, all while making sure we don't have to import so much food. Lots of talk about expanded irrigation, lower costs, stronger industries, and better returns for farmers. Sounds lovely on paper, doesn't it? The government’s touting increased maize production, up from 34.3 million bags in 2022 to a staggering 71 million in 2025. It’s enough to make you believe the rain always falls and the pests are always absent.

The usual suspects are being blamed, of course. Dependent on rain? "We cannot continue to depend on rain to feed our country," he declared, as if we haven't known this for decades. The solution? Dams, water storage, and irrigation, aiming for 2.5 million acres. Meanwhile, farmers are still wrestling with the cost of animal feed, sugarcane payments, and cotton production. They also raised concerns about livestock losses, a direct consequence of these "unpredictable rainfall" patterns that seem to catch the government by surprise every single time. It’s a broken record, and the needle is stuck.

Then there's the fertiliser subsidy, bringing a 50-kilo bag down from over Sh7,000 to a more palatable Sh2,000. They say it's to improve access and yields. But will it actually trickle down to the smallholder farmer who’s drowning in debt? The Cabinet Secretary, Mutahi Kagwe, echoes the President’s sentiment, saying the transformation agenda must be measured by what farmers experience on the ground. Let's see if that translates to more shillings in their pockets, or just more government reports to gather dust. This whole commercially driven agricultural model, with private sector investment playing a central role, sounds a lot like the same old song with a slightly different tune. It’s almost as predictable as the current political climate, which reminds me of the time [Starmer's Mandelson Mess Continues to Haunt Him: Incompetence Laid Bare](/posts/starmer-s-mandelson-mess-continues-to-haunt-him--incompetence-laid-bare).

The sugar industry, bless its heart, is also getting a makeover. Payments up from Sh4,500 to Sh5,500 per tonne, and national production has supposedly jumped. They're even aiming to be net exporters by 2029. And Mumias Sugar? Getting a facelift, with more farmers and plans to revive its distillery. The Sh1.2 billion set aside for farmer bonuses in November 2026 is a nice touch, assuming the money actually reaches the people it's meant for and doesn't get lost in the bureaucratic maze. The Sh4 billion collected from the sugar levy, intended for cane development and infrastructure, is also a point of contention, with disputes delaying the constitution of the Kenya Sugar Board. It’s always something, isn't it?

Cotton production is also on the President's radar, with the introduction of Bt cotton and its inclusion in the subsidy program. They talk about improving yields and reducing costs, but farmers still need support with pesticides, fertiliser, financing, and mechanisation. And let’s not forget the livestock farmers, still crying about the high cost of animal feed. Removing import duty on yellow maize and concentrates is a step, but the real issue is local production of raw materials like soya and sunflower. The government wants manufacturers to pass on the benefits, but we all know how well that usually works out in this city.

Ultimately, it’s the same cycle. Big promises, grand visions, and a lot of jargon. The President is keen on expanding fish production through aquaculture, as if that will suddenly solve all our protein needs. And the pilot cultivation of Juncao grass across all counties to reduce animal feed costs? Sounds promising, but we'll believe it when we see farmers thriving, not just surviving. Until then, it's just more talk in Nairobi.
