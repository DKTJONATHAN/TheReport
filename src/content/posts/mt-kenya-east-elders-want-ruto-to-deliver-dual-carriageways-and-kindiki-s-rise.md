MT KENYA EAST ELDERS WANT MORE FROM RUTO: DOUBLE CARRIAGEWAYS AND KINDIKI'S CROWN

A gaggle of elders from Mt Kenya East, fresh from a "solemn assembly" that sounds suspiciously like a pre-election endorsement session, have laid out their demands to President William Ruto. It seems their decades of "unwavering support" for the West, including Jomo, Kibaki, Uhuru, and even Rigathi - bless their hearts - haven't bought them enough infrastructure. They want a dual carriageway from Makutano all the way to Maua, a road project that makes Uhuru's Kenol to Marua look like a footpath. Because, you know, potholes are the real enemy of progress, not the gaping holes in common sense.

But it's not just about asphalt and tarmac. These esteemed gentlemen have decided it's time for Kithure Kindiki to ascend. They're pushing for their counterparts in Mt Kenya West to reciprocate the "mutual respect, reciprocity, solidarity and trust" by backing Kindiki for DP and, dare I say it, his future presidential ambitions. Apparently, Kindiki is the "opportunity for the region to consolidate its political influence." Translation: they want their guy in State House, and they’re using the elders’ assembly to grease the wheels. It’s all very charming, this idea of political relationships built on anything other than naked self-interest.

The resolution from the Nembure grounds is quite clear: anyone wanting to talk politics with the Mountain must go through Kindiki. He’s now the designated gatekeeper, the national leader through whom "Mt Kenya East would engage." It’s a bold move, essentially crowning him the regional kingpin, whether he likes it or not. And the structured engagements with their counterparts in the West? That’s just fancy talk for a united front to secure their slice of the national cake, all while reaffirming their "unwavering support" for Ruto's second term. It’s a well-oiled machine, this politicking, reminding me a bit of how bureaucracy can just grind everything down, like in THE CARBON REGISTRY: KANAIRO’S NEWEST BUREAUCRATIC PLAYPEN.

Beyond the political horse-trading, they’ve thrown in a request for the Nithi Bridge. Because, naturally, a dual carriageway and a bridge are the solutions to all the region's woes. They dream of a "united, peaceful and economically empowered Mt Kenya region." Sure, and I dream of a Nairobi where matatus actually adhere to traffic laws. It’s all about maintaining engagement with communities across the country, they say. Which, in Eldin-speak, means keeping their options open while making sure their own nest is well and truly feathered. And let’s not forget, Ruto already congratulated the Chipu rugby team while Kenyans still struggle - the priorities are always so clear.













A delegation of elders from Mt Kenya East, after what sounds like a rather solemn gathering at Nembure grounds in Embu, has decided it's high time President William Ruto delivered on more than just promises. Their primary demand? A swanky dual carriageway stretching from Makutano all the way through Embu, Chuka, Meru, and Maua. Apparently, the infrastructure projects undertaken by previous administrations in Mt Kenya West, like the Kenol to Marua road, have set a benchmark they now expect Ruto to meet, or rather, exceed. It’s a clear message: roads are the currency of political loyalty.

But the elders aren't just angling for better roads; they're also orchestrating a political power play. They've urged their counterparts in Mt Kenya West to finally reciprocate the "decades of political support" by throwing their weight behind Deputy President Kithure Kindiki and endorsing his future presidential ambitions. The Njuri Ncheke Supreme Council and other powerful elder groups have adopted a resolution seeking "greater political unity" across the wider Mt Kenya region, which conveniently positions Kindiki as the undisputed leader for the Mountain. It's a shrewd move, leveraging historical solidarity for future political gain, a tactic that feels as old as time itself.

The elders meticulously detailed their region's history of unwavering support, listing off their backing for Jomo Kenyatta, Mwai Kibaki, Uhuru Kenyatta, and even Rigathi Gachagua. They've now deemed it the "historic moment" for Mt Kenya West to return the favour by backing Kindiki, not just as DP, but as the potential first president from Mt Kenya East. This resolution also establishes Kindiki as the central figure for any political engagement concerning the Mountain, effectively making him the gatekeeper of regional interests. It’s a clear signal that, in this political theatre, Kindiki is being handed the leading role, whether others are ready or not.

Beyond the political maneuvering, the elders also threw in a request for the Nithi Bridge, citing improvements in connectivity, road safety, and economic activity. Their overarching goal, they proclaim, is a "united, peaceful and economically empowered Mt Kenya region" while maintaining engagement with communities nationwide. This is all part of their pledge to back Ruto’s re-election bid in 2027, a classic case of "we support you, but here’s what you owe us." It’s a pragmatic approach, seeking to solidify their region's political standing and economic development, a strategy that often feels like a well-rehearsed play, rather than spontaneous progress.
