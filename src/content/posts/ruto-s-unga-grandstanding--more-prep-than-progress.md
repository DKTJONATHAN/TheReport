So, our man Ruto is off to New York again, strutting his stuff at the 81st UN General Assembly. The pictures show him looking all serious, poring over his speech on his phone, with Hussein Mohamed and the usual entourage hovering like anxious pigeons. Apparently, he's going to deliver Kenya's "National Statement" and present our "position on key global and regional issues." Because that’s what we need right now, more talk, more foreign pronouncements, while the ground here is crumbling.

He was spotted strolling through the concrete jungle of New York, a far cry from the dusty roads many Kenyans navigate daily. One can only imagine the kind of "pressing global challenges" they'll be deliberating on, probably while sipping expensive water and discussing aid packages that rarely trickle down. It’s a performance, a show for the international community, a chance to score some points on the world stage. But what about the actual issues here, the ones that keep us up at night?

We've seen this song and dance before. Leaders jetting off to these grand assemblies, making lofty promises, and then returning to the same old problems. Remember when we thought things might shift after all the political back-and-forth? Turns out, [Kenya in Transition: Economic Hurdles, Political Fractures, and Global Security Shifts] are still very much our reality, regardless of how many podiums our President graces.

And the entourage? All those Kenyan leaders and officials, presumably on the public's dime, accompanying him. While they're busy strategizing on the global stage, one can't help but wonder if any of them are thinking about the everyday struggles of Kenyans. It’s a bit rich, really, seeing them prepare for an address on global issues when the local ones are screaming for attention. This is just another day at the office for the chosen few, a lucrative business trip disguised as national duty.
