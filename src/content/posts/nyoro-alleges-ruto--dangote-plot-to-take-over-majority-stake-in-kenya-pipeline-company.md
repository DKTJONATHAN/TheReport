Nyoro Alleges Ruto-Dangote Gambit for Kenya Pipeline Control











This Ndindi Nyoro fellow, bless his oppositional heart, is crying foul over Aliko Dangote and President William Ruto’s supposed dance with Kenya Pipeline Company (KPC). Apparently, Dangote snagging nearly a quarter of the KPC pie is just the appetizer, the warm-up act for a full-blown takeover. Nyoro’s painting a picture of the President himself pulling strings, moving state money like chess pieces, all to funnel shares into Dangote’s already overflowing basket. It’s the same old song and dance: politicians mixing business with governance like it’s some kind of blended juice.

Nyoro’s got a point, though. When the line between President and businessman blurs, what else do you expect? We've seen this movie before, haven't we? It’s the same playbook that leaves ordinary Kenyans footing the bill. He’s pointing fingers at state entities like NSSF and the Unclaimed Financial Assets Authority, claiming their investments were merely pre-approved steps in this grand scheme. Your pension money, supposedly working for you, is apparently being used to prop up share prices so that “friends” can buy low and sell high. It’s a classic.

The whole charade, according to Nyoro, is orchestrated by the President himself. Directing your hard-earned cash into KPC shares at KSh.9.0, then orchestrating a “stabilisation” that barely nudges the price to KSh.9.02 or KSh.9.36, only for the government to then tell these same entities to "sell, sell, sell." It’s enough to make you want to just stuff your cash under your mattress. You have to wonder if the interests of actual Kenyan workers and pensioners, the ones whose money is being gambled, were even a blip on anyone's radar.

And the endgame? Nyoro claims it's for Dangote to reach at least 51%, maybe even a whopping 75% control. The next move in this alleged chess game? A rights issue, where the government conveniently “lacks funds” to participate, allowing Dangote to swoop in and gobble up the rest. It’s a narrative as old as time in this country: public assets becoming private playgrounds. We are left here, the public, asking basic questions about government involvement in a refinery, and the response is silence, or worse, a presidential decree that we, the owners, don’t get to look. It’s like [Death, Choppers, and the High Cost of Political Saint-making](/posts/death--choppers--and-the-high-cost-of-political-saint-making) all over again, just with more spreadsheets and less drama.

So, here we are. Dangote is now the second-largest shareholder, breathing down the government's neck, which barely holds 35%. Uganda is comfortably third. It’s a cozy arrangement, isn't it? This isn't about nation-building; it’s about consolidating power and wealth. And we, the citizens, are left to watch, to dissect, and to hope that our representatives, the ones we’re told are working for us, aren't just lining their own pockets and those of their buddies.
