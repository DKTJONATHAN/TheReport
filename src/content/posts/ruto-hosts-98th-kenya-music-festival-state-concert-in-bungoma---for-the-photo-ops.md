Ruto's Bungoma 'Concert': A Symphony of Empty Gestures

The President, William Ruto, decided to grace Bungoma with his presence for the 98th Kenya Music Festival State Concert. Apparently, our esteemed leaders - including the First Lady Rachel Ruto and Deputy President Kithure Kindiki - felt the urgent need to witness school kids belt out tunes while they, presumably, planned the nation's next big financial windfall. It's always reassuring to see them take a break from the serious business of governance to appreciate the arts, especially when the rest of us are just trying to keep our heads above water.

One can only imagine the profound impact this little excursion had. The students got to interact with the "big wigs," a real treat, no doubt. Meanwhile, Cabinet Secretaries like Lee Kinyanjui Wandayi and Julius Ogamba were also present, likely to ensure the national anthem was sung with the appropriate level of subservience. The festival itself, a grand platform for musical talent, creativity, and cultural expression, was undoubtedly elevated by the presence of those who often seem to stifle all three with their policies.

And then there's the photo op: students interviewing the President and First Lady. It’s touching, isn't it? A moment of connection, a fleeting glimpse of humanity before they all returned to their important duties. One hopes these young musicians weren't too distracted by the flashbulbs and the sheer weight of political attention to remember their scales. Perhaps they'll compose a song about the rising cost of living, or maybe a ballad on how infrastructure projects always seem to benefit those already in power.

While the shiny photos suggest a successful event, one has to wonder about the real purpose. Is it genuine cultural appreciation, or just another well-photographed spectacle designed to project an image of accessibility? It’s a classic move, especially when the country is grappling with more pressing issues, like the ongoing floods which seem to necessitate more "drip" than "drop" in terms of actual aid. One might even say these grand events are a distraction, a well-orchestrated performance to keep our minds off the less glamorous realities.
