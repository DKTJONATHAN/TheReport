So, we've got these Mwendwa and Shioso characters, big shots from the police and coast guard, telling us terrorists are all over the internet, shouting about their plans, recruiting like it's a Black Friday sale, and hopping on encrypted calls. And get this, we're supposed to believe that even with all this "visibility," most attacks still happen? It’s a classic Nairobi hustle: tons of talk, little action. They analyze old evidence, learn their lessons, and then wait for the next bang. Pathetic.

They're talking about millions of social media posts daily, and our boys can only look at a measly 50,000 a month. Honestly, it’s like trying to drink the ocean with a straw. The internet is a dumpster fire, and they're sending in tweezers. This whole "digital traces" thing sounds good on paper, but in reality, it's just more noise to sift through. Reminds me of when Ruto was asking spy chiefs to sort out AU reforms; felt like they were just spinning wheels instead of actually fixing anything.

Now, some research drone claims that 78% of attacks have "detectable digital precursors" a month or so before they happen. A whole 18 to 42 days! Enough time to do something, they say. But the real question, the one that keeps them up at night in air-conditioned offices, isn't if AI can help. It's how they're going to screw it up this time. Will it be used fairly? Effectively? Or will it just be another expensive toy that doesn't work, leaving us right back where we started?

They want us to believe AI is the magic bullet. "Proactive intelligence driven by artificial intelligence." Sounds fancy, doesn't it? But I've seen these initiatives before. Plenty of hype, lots of money spent, and then… crickets. Remember Nairobi trying to build its digital future? We’ll probably end up paying for it, and for what? More systems that only catch the bad guys after they've already done the damage, or worse, after they've slipped through the cracks because the AI was trained on the wrong data, or someone's cousin got a lucrative contract.

The real problem isn’t the lack of information; it’s the utter incompetence in processing it. They're drowning in data and can't even see the lifeline. AI is just the latest buzzword to distract from the fundamental flaw: a system that’s too slow, too bureaucratic, and too easily fooled. While they’re busy debating how to implement AI "responsibly," terrorists are out there, busy. It's the same old song and dance, just with a more expensive rhythm section.
