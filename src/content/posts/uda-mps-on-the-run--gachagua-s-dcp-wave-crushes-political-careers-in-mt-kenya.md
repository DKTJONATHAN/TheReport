UDA MPs Under Siege: Gachagua Wave Smashes Mt Kenya Political Hopes

These UDA guys in Mt Kenya are in deep trouble. They thought they could just jump ship and everything would be fine, but former DP Gachagua's DCP party is the new king of the mountain, and these defectors are getting a very rude awakening. It's a total mess, and most of them deserve it.

Take Gatundu South MP Gabriel Kagombe, for example. He went from riding Ruto's coattails to trying to hug Gachagua, only to get booed by his own people. Turns out, folks aren't forgetting that he was one of the guys who impeached Gachagua. Now he's begging for forgiveness, calling it a "Damascus moment," but the crowd just screamed "one-term" at him. It’s pathetic, really. They’re realizing that just switching parties doesn't magically make you popular, especially when you've burned bridges like that. This whole mess reminds me a bit of the leadership wrangles we see in places like Nairobi Hospital - people think they can maneuver their way out of trouble, but it usually just creates more.

The Ol Kalou by-election was the nail in the coffin, with the DCP candidate wiping the floor with the UDA guy. Nearly 30,000 votes? That's not a margin, that's a declaration of war. Gachagua’s camp is practically dancing on UDA’s grave in Mt Kenya. It’s a clear sign that Ruto’s popularity has tanked since his fallout with Gachagua. These UDA politicians are finally realizing their ship is sinking, but DCP isn't just letting anyone in; they've got a whole vetting committee to make sure these defectors aren't just opportunistic snakes.

Senator Joe Nyutu, part of this vetting committee, is all about sniffing out UDA "moles" and making sure people believe in the "cause" - as if any of these guys actually believe in anything other than their own survival. He's worried about "Saul masquerading as Paul," which is rich coming from politicians who change their allegiance faster than a taxi driver changes lanes on Uhuru Highway. And to top it off, even pro-government guys like Mwangi Kiunjuri are telling residents to reject these defectors. He's right - if they haven't delivered anything, switching parties won't make them suddenly useful.

Gachagua himself is in a bind. He needs these elected officials to build his party, but taking everyone in could upset his loyal supporters who stuck with him through thick and thin. The Kagombe incident proves it: Gachagua might welcome the defectors, but his people don't necessarily see them as allies. They remember who backed Gachagua's impeachment, and loyalty is now the currency. For those who impeached him, just switching parties isn't enough; they have to explain their betrayal. It's a political circus, and most of these guys are just clowns looking for a new tent.
