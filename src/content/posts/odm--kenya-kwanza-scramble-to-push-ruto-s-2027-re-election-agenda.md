So, the usual suspects are out, running around like headless chickens, trying to drum up support for Ruto's re-election. ODM, bless their hearts, are in Turkana, ostensibly honouring Raila Odinga but really just doing their part in this grand circus. Gladys Wanga, their chair, is making noise about unity and working with Ruto, because, let's be honest, survival is key in this game. "Usawa ni Sawa," she declares, as if that’s some profound revelation. Simba Arati chimes in, trying to assure everyone that ODM isn't going to disappear just because their “Baba” is gone. It’s all about clinging to whatever platform offers them a seat at the table.

Meanwhile, the Kenya Kwanza bunch are busy in Western Kenya, telling anyone who'll listen that the opposition is a joke. Moses Wetangula, the Speaker, is out there warning us about "inexperienced politicians," probably meaning anyone who doesn't have a long history of making questionable deals. And then there's Francis Atwoli, the ever-reliable pundit, mocking the idea of anyone helping Edwin Sifuna. His logic? If they didn't help when Raila was around, why would they help now? It's a valid point, in a world where political loyalty is as stable as a Nairobi matatu on a rainy day.

The drama doesn't stop there. Junet Mohammed is out there batting away claims about ODM pushing a bill to restrict livestreaming of election results, calling it "propaganda" and suggesting Gachagua needs an English tutor. You know, the usual political mudslinging. Raila Junior, meanwhile, is pleading with people to stay in ODM, because apparently, "this party is ours," and he’s not keen on joining a "small party." It's a sad reflection when party affiliation seems to be more about personal gain than any actual ideology.

And let's not forget the Kenya Pipeline Company saga. Farouk Kibet, Ruto's aide, is calling for scrutiny of Ndindi Nyoro's remarks, basically telling him he's just a "small wheelbarrow" who shouldn't lecture them on nation-building. It’s a classic case of infighting within the ruling coalition, each faction trying to outdo the other. Honestly, it’s exhausting to keep up with who's betraying whom and over what. It makes you wonder if Ruto is even aware of all this internal squabbling, or if he’s too busy counting his blessings, or perhaps, the latest deals. It all makes one recall the confusion surrounding whether Ruto Cedes Kenya Pipeline Control to Uganda's Museveni.

Even Aden Duale is in on the act, opening health facilities in Homa Bay while simultaneously taking potshots at the opposition. It’s the standard playbook: pretend to care about development while simultaneously undermining any potential challengers. This whole spectacle of campaigning for re-election is just a thinly veiled attempt to solidify their power and ensure the gravy train keeps running. The ordinary Kenyan, meanwhile, is left to sort through the noise and decide which set of empty promises to believe this time around.
