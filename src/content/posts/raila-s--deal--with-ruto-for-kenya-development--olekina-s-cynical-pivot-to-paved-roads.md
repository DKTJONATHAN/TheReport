RAILA'S 'DEAL' WITH RUTO FOR KENYA DEVELOPMENT? MORE LIKE A SELF-SERVING SHIFT.

Forget about principle, folks. Senator Olekina's sudden love affair with President Ruto is as predictable as a Nairobi matatu fare hike. It seems the sweet smell of development, or at least the promise of it, is enough to make even the staunchest critics sing a different tune.

So, the great Raila Odinga, the man who once stood as a bulwark against Ruto, decided to have a "chat" with Olekina. Apparently, this chat involved gathering a few hundred Maasai to give their blessing for a UDA-ODM "partnership." And what was the prize for Olekina's "agreement"? Tarmacked roads. Yes, because without that handshake, the Maasai would apparently be forever relegated to dust tracks. It’s a classic case of "what’s in it for me," disguised as national progress. This whole charade reminds me of the circus we've seen before, where politicians play games while the real issues get sidelined, [The 10k Circus: Why We’re Still Catching Small Fry While the Sharks Swim Free](/posts/the-10k-circus--why-we-re-still-catching-small-fry-while-the-sharks-swim-free).

Now, Olekina is all praises for Ruto's projects - affordable housing, roads, the works. He's even defending these initiatives, conveniently forgetting his past criticisms. It's a neat little package, isn't it? One minute you're lamenting State House lunches as a waste of public funds - and let's be honest, he's right about that, those [Politicians Play Pilot’s PR Team While the Internet Keeps Receipts](/posts/politicians-play-pilot-s-pr-team-while-the-internet-keeps-receipts) - the next you're basking in the glow of newly paved streets, thanks to the very administration you used to oppose. Talk about a convenient memory lapse.

The ODM and UDA are now reportedly working on a joint manifesto, with Olekina insisting on an "equal partner" status. Let's not kid ourselves. This is about power and position, not about genuine service to the people. They're crafting their narrative, making sure they're seen as players in whatever comes next, even if it means sacrificing their supposed ideals on the altar of political expediency.

The entire affair screams of opportunism. The "development" narrative is just a convenient excuse to jump ship and secure a comfortable spot in the next government. Olekina's conversion is less about a change of heart and more about a calculated move to stay relevant and benefit from the spoils of power. Don't be fooled by the talk of progress; this is just another episode in the never-ending drama of Kenyan politics.







So, Senator Ledama Olekina has decided that President Ruto isn't so bad after all. Apparently, his previous harsh criticisms have "fizzled" because his party, ODM, is now cozying up to the ruling UDA for the 2027 elections. Who would have thought? It's almost as if political principles are as flexible as a cheap Nairobi suit.

Olekina claims this UDA-ODM "partnership," spearheaded by the late Raila Odinga himself, is all about bringing development to Narok County. He dramatically recounts how Raila consulted with him and elders, presenting the opportunity to work with Ruto for the betterment of their people. And what is this grand development? Tarmacked roads. Yes, the very idea that Maasai land would remain undeveloped without this political maneuver is frankly insulting. It's a classic example of promises over substance, much like the empty rhetoric we've seen time and again, where politicians promise the moon while we’re left dealing with [Kenya Forex Buffer: Enough for El Niño and Middle East Shocks? Don't Hold Your Breath.](/posts/kenya-forex-buffer--enough-for-el-ni-o-and-middle-east-shocks--don-t-hold-your-breath).

He now proudly boasts about these "tarmacked roads" as a direct result of cooperating with William Ruto. This is the same Ruto he apparently once opposed with all his might. Now, Olekina is defending the Kenya Kwanza administration's projects - housing, roads, airports - and painting a picture of a transformed country. It’s a convenient narrative shift, one that conveniently forgets his own past pronouncements.

Remember when Olekina was calling out State House lunches as a "waste of public funds"? He even urged the President to ditch the "politics of plates" for digital town halls. That Olekina seems to have vanished, replaced by one who sees tarmac as the ultimate justification for political alliances, no matter the cost to principles.

Now, ODM and UDA are apparently setting up a joint technical team to craft a shared manifesto. Olekina stresses they will enter this arrangement as equals, not subordinates. This isn't about genuine unity; it's about securing a piece of the pie, ensuring their voices are heard in the corridors of power, regardless of the ideological gymnastics involved.

In the end, this is just Kenyan politics at its finest: a cynical dance of alliances driven by self-interest and the promise of tangible benefits, like paved roads, for a select few. Don't expect any real change; just expect the same old players in slightly different suits, conveniently forgetting their past grievances when the political winds shift.
