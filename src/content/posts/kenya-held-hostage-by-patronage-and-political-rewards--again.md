Honestly, looking at someone like Onesmus Kipchumba Murkomen, you just shake your head. 'Feckless' is putting it mildly. He’s bounced from one disaster to another, leaving a trail of excuses and inflammatory nonsense. This whole charade of public office in Kenya is just a rotten joke. What the Constitution calls a "solemn public trust" is now just another item on the auction block, handed out to whoever shouts the loudest about their loyalty, never mind if they know their backside from a hole in the ground. Expertise? That’s for chumps. Competence? That’s a foreign language.

Cabinet appointments are now just glorified thank-you gifts for toeing the party line. Never mind if they can actually do the job; can they mobilize crowds? Can they shake down donors? That's the real skillset. The National Assembly's vetting? A theatre production, a slapstick comedy where they pretend to scrutinize while really just nodding along. And who pays for all this incompetence, this impunity, this slow-motion institutional suicide? We do. The Kenyan taxpayer, coughing up for every administrative blunder and every moment of blatant disregard for the law.

Then you have these senior officials, acting like they own the place. Remember when former Health CS Susan Nakhumicha decided she was head of police too, ordering transfers like she was picking out a new hat? "One government approach," she calls it. It's not a "one government approach"; it's a "my fiefdom, my rules" approach, trampling all over the constitution like it’s a doormat. And Aden Duale, openly defending ethnic interests during security operations? It’s clear these folks can't tell the difference between running a country and running their tribal association. This isn't just about gaffes; it's a systemic disease, a pattern of treating these powerful offices as stages for political peacocking and clan signalling. It reminds me of how some people try to solve complex issues with simplistic, often politically motivated, fixes, like how [Germany Discovers the Thika Tunnel] is apparently a thing.

This rot goes all the way down. Once the top brass is chosen for loyalty, not merit, the Principal Secretaries and State corporation heads just fall in line. Competent civil servants are shoved aside for the well-connected cousins and cronies. They operate as if their political connections are a magical shield against any oversight, the Auditor General be damned. When the leadership is built on who you know, not what you know, public trust evaporates. Service delivery grinds to a halt, bills pile up, and our taxes are paying for loyalty, not productivity. Young professionals see their only path is through connections, not hard work. The Executive stays silent to keep fragile coalitions together, signaling that protecting political alliances is more important than the public interest.

Kenya is teetering on the edge of becoming a classic banana republic, where institutions are just tools for the ruling elite to extract whatever they can. We reward loyalty, not merit, unlike countries that actually understand how to build something lasting. Power might offer a temporary escape from accountability, but authority without legitimacy is a house of cards. And let's not forget, the world is watching. International bodies and tribunals are a stark reminder that legal consequences can outlast political careers. Abusing state machinery and violating rights for political survival is a dangerous game.

It all boils down to what the Good Book says: when the wicked rule, the people mourn. Nations that prioritize personal gain over justice eventually crumble. We should be heeding that warning. A country can't build a sustainable future on handouts and political favors. Until Kenya gets serious about merit, enforces its own integrity rules with real lifestyle audits, builds a proper competency framework, and puts the rule of law back at the heart of appointments, this cycle of patronage will continue to drag us down and mortgage our future.
