POLITICS AND STATE MEDDLING: KENYA'S POLICE FORCE OVERHAUL STALLED BY RHETORIC

They want your data for a cheap read, or your money for the real deal. Typical Nairobi. You can pay a euro and pretend to be enlightened, or sell your soul for free news. Either way, someone's profiting. Just like this whole police reform saga.

The grand pronouncements about overhauling Kenya's police force? It's all just hot air, as usual. David Maraga, the former chief justice and now a politician who knows a thing or two about the system's failings, is calling out President Ruto. Apparently, the President is deliberately dragging his feet on implementing recommendations, all for some political mileage. Because why fix a broken system when you can just talk about it and keep the same old cronies in charge?

It’s the same old story, really. We get excited about reports, task forces, and promises of change. Then, when it’s time for actual action, suddenly there are “political reasons” and “state interference.” It’s like watching a replay of how other important issues get kicked down the road. Remember how FKF Women's Premier League teams were threatening to boycott matches over funding? [Funding issues]( /posts/fkf-women-s-premier-league-teams-threaten-match-boycott-over-unresolved-funding-issues) - same old song, different tune.

This isn't about making the police force better for ordinary Kenyans. It's about power plays and keeping the status quo. The report, chaired by Maraga, probably had some good ideas, but good ideas don't win elections. What wins elections is the illusion of progress, the carefully crafted narrative that things are being done, while the wheels of bureaucracy grind slowly, or not at all.

And let's not forget the "partners" who want your personal data to personalize your experience. They talk about "improving your experience" and "editorial work," but it all boils down to them collecting your browsing habits to sell you more things, or influence your opinions. This obsession with data, with control, is just another layer of the same old game.

So, while you’re deciding whether to pay your euro for a slightly less compromised read or give away your digital life for free, remember that the real issue isn't the cookies. It's the same old politicians, the same old excuses, and the same old stalled progress. Don't expect any real overhaul. Just more talk, more reports, and more waiting.
