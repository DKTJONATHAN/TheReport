RUTO DECLARES UDA-ODM PACT IS HERE TO STAY, BUT KICKBACKS ARE STILL ON THE MENU
WARNING: President Ruto insists his coalition with Raila Odinga's ODM is solid, while his deputy eyes the 2027 ticket and other leaders trade insults. The real issue? Food security and the same old promises.
President William Ruto is busy trying to convince his troops that his new bromance with ODM is all sunshine and rainbows. During a church fundraiser, no less, he declared that his political partnership with Raila Odinga's party is "firm and here to stay." Apparently, the ODM folks are "Kenyans" and the government is "big enough for all of us." This is supposed to soothe the nerves of his traditional allies who are probably eyeing the 2027 elections and wondering where they fit in this grand, inclusive vision. It's all about managing the succession buzz, because what else is new in Kenyan politics?

The scramble for the deputy president's position is already in full swing, with whispers about ODM and Western region leaders vying for the spot. This, of course, comes right after Deputy President Kithure Kindiki declared his own ticket with Ruto for 2027 is "non-negotiable." He's even warning others against using the DP office as a bargaining chip, telling them to focus on Ruto's re-election. It’s a classic game of political chess, where everyone's jockeying for position while pretending to be unified. The vision, it seems, is less about Vision 2060 and more about Vision 2027.

While Ruto avoided direct talk about his running mate, he pivoted to his Vision 2060 development blueprint, calling for "broad-based national dialogue." He claims this will align Kenya's development with the Constitution, updating the old Vision 2030. He's also accusing opponents of promoting tribalism and insults. Meanwhile, the North Rift is facing a devastating drought, which even affected the President's own farm. He's promising early-maturing seeds and inputs to help farmers, and a grand plan of 50 mega dams, 200 medium dams, and 1,000 micro dams. Because clearly, building dams is the only solution to weather patterns, much like how we’re still trying to figure out the oil industry's plastic gamble.

The church service, unsurprisingly, turned into an early campaign rally, with North Rift MPs practically begging for Ruto's re-election. In a moment of levity, Ruto told them to preach the Gospel before his second term. But the real drama came from Interior CS Kipchumba Murkomen, who launched a scathing attack on former DP Rigathi Gachagua, calling him a "liar" and a "political conman." Apparently, national security is now a partisan political football.

The fundraiser itself raked in over Sh50 million for church development, with Ruto personally chipping in Sh25 million. It’s always a good look, isn't it? Using faith events for political rallying and fundraising. The promises of dams and seeds are a familiar tune, designed to placate the masses while the political elite continue their endless pursuit of power and influence. One can only hope that Vision 2060, or whatever vision they cook up next, actually addresses the realities faced by ordinary Kenyans, instead of just being another talking point.

It's a familiar script: alliances shift, promises are made, and the public is expected to believe in grand plans while the leaders engage in their usual power plays. Ruto's declaration of a firm UDA-ODM pact is just the latest act in this ongoing political drama. The question remains: will any of these promises translate into tangible change for the common mwananchi, or is it just more hot air from the podium?
