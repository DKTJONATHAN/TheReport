Natembeya Tells Murkomen: Stop Downplaying Goons Crisis

Governor Natembeya blasts Interior CS Murkomen for denying the existence of goons causing chaos, citing recent violence where police officers were casualties.

With elections looming, the Trans Nzoia Governor is accusing the Interior Cabinet Secretary of grossly underestimating the threat of politically motivated violence.

Natembeya calls out Murkomen for his dismissive attitude towards the "goons crisis," a situation he claims is spiralling out of control and exposing Kenyans to anarchy. The governor pointed to the recent "Linda Mwananchi rally" in Homa Bay where, he alleges, police officers were killed and injured by these thugs. He's not buying Murkomen's line that these are just "a few politicians" misusing youth; Natembeya sees a deliberate downplaying of a serious security lapse.

"We have our officers killed by goons armed with crude weapons, yet those in charge of security are in denial, meaning we are exposed and on our own," Natembeya stated, a sentiment that resonates with anyone who's seen the state of things. He argues that the government, particularly the security chiefs, are clearly incapable of handling the escalating insecurity, leaving citizens vulnerable, especially with next year's polls on the horizon. This isn't just about politics; it's about basic safety and the right to exercise democratic freedoms without fear.

Murkomen, meanwhile, is busy telling Kenyans to avoid certain areas if they want to stay safe, a piece of advice that's as useful as telling a drowning man to avoid water. He insists Kenya is "very safe" and that claims of a goons crisis are simply lies peddled by politicians with no agenda. This is the kind of doublespeak that makes you wonder if they're actually living in the same country. Natembeya, rightly, dismisses this as incompetence and an inability to grasp the gravity of the situation.

The governor is adamant that the country is sliding back into the "dark days" and that the Kenya Kwanza regime needs to stop playing politics with security. He vows that the opposition will not be intimidated and will fight for a just society, even cautioning those "looting the economy" that their sins will catch up with them.
