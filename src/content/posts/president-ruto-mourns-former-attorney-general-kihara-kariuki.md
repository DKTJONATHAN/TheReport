Ruto Mourns Ex-AG Kihara Kariuki - Another Judge Hits the Road

WARNING: Another prominent figure in Kenya's legal circles has shuffled off this mortal coil, prompting the usual pronouncements of grief and praise from the powers that be. Don't expect any actual justice to be served because of this, though.

Rest in Peace, Justice Kihara Kariuki. Your legacy will be remembered, presumably.





So, President Ruto has decided to grace us with his sorrow over the passing of Justice Paul Kihara Kariuki. Apparently, this man was an "accomplished jurist" and a "dedicated public servant" whose work supposedly "shaped Kenya’s legal and judicial institutions." We've heard this song and dance before, haven't we? It’s the same script they pull out every time someone high up in the system decides to take an early retirement to the great beyond.

Ruto went on to wax lyrical about Kariuki's contribution to "strengthening the country’s justice system" and his role in building "institutional capacity." This sounds suspiciously like the same kind of performative governance we've come to expect, where the language of progress is used to paper over the cracks. It’s almost as if they think praising dead judges will magically fix the broken system, a bit like how some people believe Mwalimu Andrew can sort out any crisis, no matter how dire.

They even highlighted his work as the founding director of the Judiciary Training Institute, helping to "advance the professional development of judicial officers." Well, isn't that nice? While they're busy developing themselves and their esteemed colleagues, the rest of us are still dealing with a justice system that often feels more like a scripted soap opera for the benefit of a select few.

The President's "heartfelt condolences" are being conveyed to the family, friends, and colleagues. I'm sure they appreciate the sentiment. But let's be honest, in this country, a death announcement from the Head of State often feels more like a political necessity than genuine grief. It’s another name added to the list of esteemed individuals who have served their time, leaving us to wonder what, if anything, actually changed.
