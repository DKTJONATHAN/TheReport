Just five years ago, the league was not nearly so tongue-tied. In 2021, the WNBA’s official channels were busy canonizing male-born track athletes who had systematically displaced female competitors in Connecticut. They knew exactly what a "woman" was back then: it was anyone who served their immediate PR needs. Now that the logic of that position is being tested by former NBA players threatening to enter the draft, the league has suddenly developed a convenient case of biological amnesia.

On the international stage, this indecision serves as a grim bellwether for the erosion of protected categories. When the premier women's basketball league in the world refuses to define its own borders, it sends a message to every global sports federation that female athletics is a negotiable space. The WNBA is effectively telling young girls across the globe that their hard work, their sweat, and their championship dreams are secondary to the "inclusivity" metrics of a corporate board.

The recent task force meeting, which reportedly obsessed over "hate and vitriol" online rather than the material reality of physical competition, is the hallmark of a failing institution. It is much easier to play the victim of mean tweets than it is to look a female athlete in the eye and explain why she is being forced to lose a roster spot to a male. The league has traded its athletic credibility for a social credit score that won't help them sell a single ticket when the product on the court becomes a farce.

The legal language in their collective bargaining agreement states that only "women" are eligible, yet the league remains terrified to define the word. This silence is a betrayal of the very athletes like Sophie Cunningham who are brave enough to state the obvious. If the WNBA cannot find the courage to protect the category that justifies its existence, then it deserves the irrelevance that is surely coming for it. 

In the cold reality of global competition, biology is not a social construct and physics does not care about your "Woman Crush Wednesday" posts. The WNBA spent years cheering for the displacement of girls in track and field, and now they are shocked that the same chaos has arrived at their doorstep. They are not victims of a debate; they are the architects of their own obsolescence.
