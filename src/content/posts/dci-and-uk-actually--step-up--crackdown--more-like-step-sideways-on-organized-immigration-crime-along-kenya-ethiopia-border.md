So, the DCI and their fancy friends from the UK are apparently "stepping up" their efforts to curb "organised immigration crime" along the Kenya-Ethiopia border. Give me a break. These are the same people who can barely sort out their own messes, let alone tackle a problem as complex and profitable as human trafficking. This is probably just another photo-op, another press release to make it look like someone is doing something while the real players keep lining their pockets.

And speaking of DCI denying things, they've also come out to say they *didn't* intercept any IEBC voter materials at the Uganda border. Of course they didn't. Because why would they? It's not like elections in this country are ever a smooth, corruption-free affair. This denial is as believable as a politician promising lower taxes. They're probably just trying to avoid another scandal, which, knowing this lot, is just around the corner.

What really grinds my gears is how these stories always highlight the "vulnerable people" being exploited. Yes, they are. But who do you think is facilitating this exploitation? It's the same well-connected individuals who grease the wheels of power, who probably have connections to whoever is "cracking down" in the first place. It’s a neat little cycle, isn't it? The powerful exploit, then the powerful pretend to fix it, all while the rest of us are left holding the bag. It reminds me of that nonsense about the National Police Service claiming they weren't recruiting in Western Kenya - always a good laugh to see what they deny.

This whole "strengthening investigations and cooperation" rhetoric is just noise. It's the language of bureaucracy designed to sound important without actually achieving anything tangible. They'll hold a few meetings, sign some more papers, and then we'll be back here in a year, reading about the same old problems with slightly different wording. The real crackdown would involve actual consequences for the kingpins, not just the low-level operatives who are easily replaced. But that, my friends, is a story for another day, and a much longer rant.
