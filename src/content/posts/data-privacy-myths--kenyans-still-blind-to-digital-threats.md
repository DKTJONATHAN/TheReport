So, the smart folks at TECHTRENDSKE.co.ke are shaking their heads, telling us about data privacy myths. Big surprise. As if Kenyans needed a reminder that their personal details are basically being auctioned off to the highest bidder. They say it only matters to big companies? Please. Criminals aren't picky; they'll happily steal your identity for a few bob, using your name, phone number, or those banking details you carelessly splash everywhere. It's not about hiding, it's about control, a concept that seems to be as foreign as a quiet matatu ride in Nairobi.

And don't even get me started on "free" apps. You think you're getting a bargain? Ha! You're the product, plain and simple. Your location, your contacts, your mother's maiden name - all up for grabs if you're too lazy to read the permissions. Companies claim it's to "improve services" or "personalize your experience." What they mean is to track your every move and sell it to whoever pays the most. This isn't some advanced concept; it's basic common sense, something many seem to have misplaced somewhere between scrolling through TikTok and complaining about the cost of data.

Sure, Safaricom is trying to play nice, masking numbers and mumbling about privacy statements. And the government, bless their hearts, has this Data Protection Act 2019. It’s supposed to give you control, let you know what they’re doing with your precious data. But how many of you actually know what that means, let alone bother to use those rights? Most Kenyans are still clicking on shady links and granting every permission an app demands, as if the internet is one big, safe playground. It’s a wonder we haven't seen more chaos, though I suspect the real fallout is just waiting to happen. We’ve seen how things can go sideways, even with presidential summits - [Ruto-Macron Summit: 30 Heads of State Flock to Nairobi, France-Africa Deals Keep Coming](/posts/ruto-macron-summit--30-heads-of-state-flock-to-nairobi--france-africa-deals-keep-coming) shows that even big deals have their complexities.

The real problem, as always, is awareness. Or rather, the distinct lack of it. Organizations hoard data like squirrels hoarding nuts, and individuals willingly hand over the keys to their digital kingdoms. We keep hearing about "strong passwords" and "multi-factor authentication" - advice that falls on deaf ears. It’s a shared responsibility, they say. Right. So, while governments and tech companies are supposedly doing their bit, the rest of us are blissfully ignorant, one dodgy download away from a full-blown identity crisis.

It’s a digital age, alright. An age where your personal information is more valuable than the air you breathe, and most of you are giving it away for free. The myths about data privacy are just that - myths designed to keep you complacent while your data gets exploited. Wake up, Kenya. Your digital life isn't a game, and the stakes are your actual life.

So, if you want more of this unvarnished truth about our digital struggles, head over to TECHTRENDSKE.co.ke. And for those of you who are still brave enough to venture online, maybe try updating your software once in a while. Just a thought.
