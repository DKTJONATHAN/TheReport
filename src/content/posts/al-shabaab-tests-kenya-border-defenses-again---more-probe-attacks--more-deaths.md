So, Al-Shabaab decided to play peek-a-boo with our security forces again. This week, it was Garissa County's turn for "probe attacks" on police and military camps. They throw a few shots, the boys in uniform manage to push them back - a triumphant win, right? Except, just days later, eight people are dead in Mandera County. It's the same old song and dance: test the defenses, find a gap, and then hit where it hurts. Our guys might be good at repelling a surprise late-night raid, but they're not exactly winning the war.

The Kamuthe Quick Response Unit Camp got a rude awakening, but they apparently sent the attackers packing. Good for them. Same story in Hulugho and Gesreb, where the militants fired a few rounds and then disappeared. It’s all about gathering intel, figuring out how slow our response is, and where we’re weakest. They're like kids poking a sleeping lion - eventually, they'll find one that’s not as asleep as you thought. And when that happens, we see what happened in Mandera.

Eight people. Road workers, security reservists, a driver. Just trying to build a road, doing their jobs, and they get wiped out. This isn't some random act of violence; this is a calculated consequence. The probe attacks are just the warm-up act for the main event. It’s a pattern we’ve seen repeat itself, and frankly, it's exhausting. It reminds me of how things used to be before the short-term rentals took over the high-end spots in town; a constant, underlying vulnerability, just in a different context. The government will, of course, deploy more troops and promise swift action. We’ll hear about investigations and reinforcements. But let’s be real: this border is a sieve. The militants can pick their time and place, and our forces are spread thinner than a cheap chapati. The probe attacks are nothing more than a loud, clear message: they know our weaknesses. The Mandera ambush is the brutal punctuation mark at the end of that sentence.

For the communities living along this so-called frontier, it's a nightmare they can't wake up from. They’re caught in a conflict that’s been simmering for over a decade, ever since we decided to play soldier in Somalia. Al-Shabaab retaliates on our soil, and we keep sending more boys to fight them over there. It's a vicious, pointless cycle, and the probe attacks are just a reminder that the war we started abroad is now a permanent fixture on our own doorstep. And our security forces, bless their hearts, are mostly just playing defense.
