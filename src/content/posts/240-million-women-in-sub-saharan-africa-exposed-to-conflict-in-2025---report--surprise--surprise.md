So, the powers that be have released another study, and guess what? Over 40% of women in sub-Saharan Africa were playing hide-and-seek with deadly conflict events last year. That’s compared to a measly 15% elsewhere. Because, you know, we have such a knack for attracting trouble in this region. It’s not like we’re not already dealing with enough drama, right?

And the cherry on top of this delightful misery sundae? GBV survivors are apparently stuck between the actual violence and the phantom of lost justice. Add to that shrinking support systems, and you’ve got a recipe for... well, more of the same. Because why fix something when you can just study it to death and then ignore the findings? It’s a classic Nairobi move, if you ask me.

It’s enough to make you wonder if we’re just a perpetual motion machine of suffering. We’ve seen it before, whether it’s the chaos at rallies where thugs are nabbed but the real paymasters remain elusive, like in Homa Bay, or the endless pipe dreams of economic growth that never quite materialize. It’s a pattern, and it’s not a pretty one.

And let’s not forget the little things, like the "professional" shoot fees in Nairobi that make your wallet cry. It’s all part of the grand tapestry of getting by, isn’t it? This report just adds another thread of bleakness to the whole damn thing.

The report, if you can even call it that without rolling your eyes so hard they get stuck, from The Eastleigh Voice, conveniently dropped in 2026. It’s quaint that they still believe in print, but then again, so do many of the problems they highlight.

So, yeah. 240 million women. Exposed. Conflict. 2025. It’s a headline that should shock, but honestly, it just lands with a dull thud of inevitability. What’s next? A report on how the sun rises in the east? Don’t get me started.
