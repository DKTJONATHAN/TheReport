Gachagua Says Ruto Dividing Mt Kenya Using Kindiki; Region Vows Bloc Vote

The Deputy President, William Gachagua, is out here sounding the alarm, claiming President Ruto is trying to split Mt Kenya down the middle, using none other than Kithure Kindiki as his puppet. Apparently, the plan is to stir up trouble between the eastern and western halves of the mountain, weakening the region's political clout. Typical politics, isn't it? Always someone trying to play divide and conquer.

But hold on, Gachagua isn't just complaining; he's also making promises. He's telling the residents of Manyatta Market that this alleged division won't work. He's predicting, with a straight face I assume, that Mt Kenya will stick together like glue and vote as a united bloc to kick the Kenya Kwanza government out in 2027. We've heard these rallying cries before, haven't we?

He's specifically urging everyone to ignore the supposed attempts to pit Mt Kenya East against Mt Kenya West. The message is clear: political unity, for now. It's a classic move - when you're feeling the heat, you try to rally the troops by painting a picture of external threats. Whether it's to strengthen his own position or genuinely for the region's good, well, that's for the voters to decipher.

Deputy President William Gachagua was at Manyatta Market in Embu County on Saturday, making some rather pointed accusations. He’s claiming that President Ruto is orchestrating a plan to divide the Mt Kenya region, allegedly using Kithure Kindiki as a pawn. The alleged strategy, according to Gachagua, is to create rifts between communities in the eastern and western parts of Mt Kenya, effectively weakening the region's political influence. It's the same old song and dance, isn't it? Always someone trying to stir up trouble for their own gain.

Despite these supposed machinations, Gachagua is confidently predicting that Mt Kenya will stand united. He’s telling everyone who will listen that the region will vote as a bloc in the 2027 elections, with the express purpose of removing the current Kenya Kwanza government. He's painting himself as the guardian of this unity, urging residents to resist any attempts to sow discord.

His message to the residents was clear: reject any narrative that pits Mt Kenya East against Mt Kenya West. He insists on maintaining political solidarity within the region. It's a well worn political tactic, to be sure. When the going gets tough, you try to unite your base against a common enemy, real or imagined.
