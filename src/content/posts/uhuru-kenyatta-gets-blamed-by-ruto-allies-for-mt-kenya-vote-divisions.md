So, William Ruto's loyalists are suddenly pointing fingers at Uhuru Kenyatta, claiming he's busy in the shadows, stirring up trouble and uniting the opposition against their "Kenya Kwanza administration." It's the oldest trick in the book, isn't it? Blame the predecessor when things get bumpy. They're already vowing that Uhuru won't manage to split the Mt Kenya vote, as if a united front is guaranteed.

Meanwhile, out in Western Kenya, the usual circus is unfolding. Divisions are popping up faster than potholes after a downpour, all about who gets to be Ruto's running mate for the next election. Governor Ken Lusaka has already made it clear he's not keen on the idea of ODM providing the deputy, which, let's be honest, is hardly surprising. It’s all about who benefits and who loses, as usual.

It makes you wonder if these leaders are more interested in maintaining their own power bases than actually fixing anything. This whole political drama reminds me of "The Dynasty Death Match: Same Script, New Mourners," where the same old narratives keep playing out with different faces. You'd think they'd learn, but here we are.

And it's not just the presidential ticket. The whole country is grappling with issues, and you have shows like "Fixing the Nation" trying to tackle them. But can they really fix what’s broken when the same political games are being played out daily? It’s a tough question, and one that probably won't get a straightforward answer on AM Live or any other show for that matter.

The constant political maneuvering, the accusations, the squabbling over positions - it's all part of the landscape. While some are busy debating political alliances, others are just trying to make ends meet. The "Thamani" show might be looking at small businesses, and the "Tech over Show" might be discussing innovation, but down on the ground, it's the same old story of who's up and who's down.

One thing's for sure: the political winds in Kenya are always blowing, and they rarely blow in a straight line. Whether Uhuru Kenyatta is truly the puppet master or just a convenient scapegoat, the power plays will continue. And the rest of us? We're just here, watching the show, trying to make sense of it all.
