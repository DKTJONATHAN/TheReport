Ruto's IPOA Chair Pick Gets Nod Despite Doubts

The National Assembly has rubber-stamped President Ruto's choice for the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) chairperson, Dr. Duncan Ojwang Oburu, even though his qualifications are apparently as shaky as a matatu on a potholed road. Parliament, bless their hearts, adopted a report from the Departmental Committee on Administration and Internal Security. So, now we have a new boss for the body meant to keep an eye on our police, replacing Justice Ahmed Issack Hassan who has clearly moved on to greener, possibly more judicial, pastures.

Ojwang, who apparently thinks he's got the courage to "speak truth to power," was nominated by Ruto back in July. During his vetting, he was all promises of protecting IPOA's independence and strengthening its oversight. But here's the kicker: the whispers about whether he actually meets the legal bar for the job are louder than a tout arguing fare. The big question is his status as an advocate. Did he actually finish all the hoops, like that Advocates Training Programme and the Bar exams? Some of us are asking if he's even qualified to practice, and when you have the Law Society of Kenya president and a former Attorney-General raising eyebrows, it's not just a minor hiccup.

Despite the legal wrangling and doubts from seasoned legal minds, the parliamentary committee miraculously found him suitable. I suppose when you're in Parliament, the definition of "qualified" can be as flexible as a politician’s promise. Now Ojwang’s got his eyes on using more tech, like body cameras and CCTV, to stop police brutality. It sounds nice, like something out of a fairy tale, but let's be real, this is Nairobi. We've seen enough scandals and promises evaporate faster than a cloud of teargas to be overly optimistic. This whole situation feels a bit like when [Kenya Moore’s luxury hair spa was giving luxury eviction instead]( /posts/the-crown-is-dusty--kenya-moore-s-luxury-hair-spa-is-giving-luxury-eviction-instead).

The whole approval process, as usual, is a spectacle. While they're busy approving nominees, we're still grappling with the same old issues. IPOA's job is to oversee the police, investigate complaints, and promote accountability. Let's just hope this new chairperson, with his fancy US law degrees and claims of speaking truth, can actually deliver on those lofty ideals, or if it's just another case of the system rewarding those who know how to play the game, regardless of what the rules actually say. The final appointment awaits the President's nod, so buckle up, it's not over yet.
