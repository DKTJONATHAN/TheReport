So, this kid Bryson, seventeen and afflicted with some chronic skin thing, is hawking bananas in Bungoma. Apparently, his face is a bit of a turn-off for the usual clientele, so he’s stuck. Then, Eric Omondi, the comedian who probably needs more real problems to focus on, films the whole pathetic display. Cue the internet outrage and the sudden flood of sympathy.

And just like that, the M-Pesa account of Bryson’s father is overflowing. KSh 500,000 in five hours. Kenyans are suddenly feeling charitable, ready to build houses, set up businesses, and send the boy back to school. It's a beautiful, heartwarming story, isn't it? If you ignore the fact that this is the same predictable pattern we see every other week.

Remember that boda boda rider, Rahim, who got a new motorcycle for helping a woman near Kenyatta University? Another good deed, another instant reward. It’s easy to get swept up in these feel-good moments, to believe that a few viral clips can solve systemic problems. It’s much harder to acknowledge that these are often fleeting distractions, band-aids on gaping wounds.

This whole spectacle reminds me of those [The Clout Graveyard: Why Kenya’s Viral Sensations Are Designed to Fail](/posts/top-10-kenyan-artists-viral-disappeared-clout-culture) stories. We latch onto these individuals, elevate them for a moment, and then… what? Do we check in to see if the houses were actually built, if the businesses are thriving, if Bryson is truly getting the long-term care he needs? Or do we just move on to the next tear-jerker?

The M-Pesa is full, the paybill is open. Kenyans are digging deep, and that's… something. But let’s not pretend this is a sustainable solution. It's emotional impulse buying, fueled by a quick scroll and a sympathetic sigh. The real work, the kind that requires more than a few clicks and a hasty donation, remains largely untouched.

So yes, Bryson will likely get his treatment, his family a new house. And Omondi will bask in the glow of his philanthropic prowess. But the underlying issues that leave kids like Bryson selling bananas in pain, those will still be here tomorrow. Just waiting for the next viral moment to get a temporary, superficial fix.
