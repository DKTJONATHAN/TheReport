KANYARI'S MAN UTD FANS: CHURCH AS THE NEW GOAL POST?

Pastor Kanyari, the man who once sold "miracle water," is now a football fanatic, or so it seems. He’s thrilled that Eric Omondi, Nairobi's resident clown, and a squad of Manchester United supporters, have graced his church. Apparently, this pre-match ritual is meant to secure victory on the pitch. Because, of course, what else would you do before a football game?

The comedian and his "tens of Manchester United fans" were treated to front-row seats, a clear sign that divine intervention is now part of game day strategy. Kanyari, ever the opportunist, couldn't resist a jab at Arsenal fans, suggesting they'd resort to "witchdoctors." This is rich, coming from a man who’s had his own share of… unconventional methods for making a living. It’s almost as if he thinks bringing your KES 25,000 offering to church is somehow morally superior to consulting a traditional healer.

Kanyari proudly announced that Omondi had paid a hefty KES 25,000 offering, specifically requesting prayers for Manchester United's win. He then declared victory, a move that would make any self-respecting bookie sweat. He contrasted this with the supposed desperation of Arsenal fans, who he claims are spending their money on witchdoctors. It's a tired narrative, but hey, it keeps the donations flowing, right?

This whole spectacle reminds me of how desperate people get when their team is involved, whether it's praying for a win or, in some darker corners of Nairobi, looking for answers from those who claim to deal with more… ancient powers. It’s a slippery slope from a football match to the kinds of things we’ve seen exposed, like the child trafficking economy that thrives on desperation. But Kanyari is focused on the immediate win, both on the pitch and in his collection plate.

Ultimately, Kanyari is leveraging a football match to bolster his church's coffers and his own reputation. He’s betting on divine intervention for Manchester United, and financial intervention for himself. It's a win-win for him, regardless of the actual match outcome. As for the fans, well, they’re just caught up in the usual tribalism of football, albeit with a slightly more spiritual twist this time.
