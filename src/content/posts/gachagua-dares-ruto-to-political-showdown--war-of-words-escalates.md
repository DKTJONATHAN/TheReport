So, the political circus is back in town, folks. Rigathi Gachagua, the man who seems to have a permanent frown etched onto his face these days, is now throwing shade at President Ruto, warning he's ready to teach the boss "real politics." As if what we've seen so far hasn't been enough political theatre, Gachagua promises a nationwide showdown for 2027. I guess we can all brace ourselves for more mudslinging and less actual governing.

Meanwhile, Ruto's joyride through the Gusii region has apparently reignited the crude insults and personal jibes that were flying around back in March. It’s like a broken record, but instead of music, it’s just noise and ego. They say these leaders are supposed to be running the country, but it sounds more like they’re auditioning for a reality TV show, and a particularly low-budget one at that.

This whole drama unfolds against a backdrop of Kenya’s media trying to make sense of it all. You’ve got shows like AM Live on NTV dissecting the day's events with panels - probably filled with more of the same predictable opinions. Then there’s Business Redefined, trying to explain economic policy when the very people in charge seem to be playing childish games. It’s a wonder anyone takes any of it seriously.

And let’s not forget the endless content churned out by these media houses. You have podcasts like the one about the digital creative economy, exploring the struggles of artists and entrepreneurs, while the politicians are busy fighting over scraps. Then there’s "Fixing the Nation," which sounds more like wishful thinking than a practical plan. It's anchored by "respected journalists," but what can they really do when the nation seems determined to stay broken?

You’d think with all these issues, like the economic playbook being rewritten by global conflicts, we’d see some serious leadership. But no, instead, we get Gachagua flexing his political muscles and Ruto probably wondering how he’ll spin this one. It's all very familiar, isn't it? Just another Tuesday in Kenyan politics, where the real issues get buried under a mountain of petty squabbles. We’ve seen this song and dance before, and frankly, it’s getting tired.
