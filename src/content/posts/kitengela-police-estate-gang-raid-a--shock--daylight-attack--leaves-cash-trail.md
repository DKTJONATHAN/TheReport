You’d think a place called Kitengela Police Sacco Estate, housing all sorts of retired and serving top cops, would be as secure as a vault. Apparently, not. These jokers, four of them, disguised as churchgoers - because nothing screams piety like planning a robbery - just waltzed in. Bypassed registration, the whole nine yards. And these weren't just any residents; it was the home of former GSU Commandant Ranson Lolmodooni, a man who apparently handed over command in 2026. Talk about targeting the big fish.

Then, in broad daylight, they got into his house, about 700 meters from the gate. Used "specialised cutting tools," which sounds a bit much for a quick smash-and-grab. They apparently made off with cash in "carton boxes." What kind of operation are these people running? Anyway, an "undisclosed amount" of money fell out of the boxes as they fled. Neighbors saw Sh1,000 notes strewn everywhere. They even threw money at onlookers and guards to distract them. Seriously, who does that? It’s like they were trying to make a statement, or maybe just gloat.

The CCTV footage shows one of the "suspects" - an average-looking man without a mask, mind you - laying into a security guard with blows and kicks, even hitting him with a stick. Another one joins in. These are the people supposed to protect the estate? And they somehow overpowered the guard to clear the way for their getaway car. Two other women, presumably part of the gang, were seen driving other cars. They abandoned one in Isinya, 21 kilometers away, with multiple number plates and a pistol that apparently belonged to Lolmodooni. Because why wouldn't a retired GSU Commandant keep his firearm at home for easy access during a robbery?

Lolmodooni himself, when contacted, downplayed the whole thing. Said it was an "attempted robbery" and "no money was stolen." He claims they don't keep money at home. So all those Sh1,000 notes scattered everywhere were just… confetti? Or maybe he's trying to save face. Meanwhile, the security chairman was sending out frantic messages on the estate's WhatsApp group, confirming the "serious security incident." They’ve requested more police patrols. Good luck with that; this is Nairobi, and Kitengela is apparently becoming a hot mess. The article even mentions [how women are being used to lure drivers into deadly robberies](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/highway-crime-queens-how-women-are-being-used-to-lure-drivers-into-deadly-robberies), so this whole churchgoer disguise is just another trick in the book.

The Kajiado County Police Commander, Alex Shikondi, is quoted saying "Investigations are active." Translation: they have no clue who did it and are probably still trying to figure out how the "churchgoers" got past the gate. This comes on the back of other recent murders and attacks in Kitengela. Apparently, the town isn't safe after 11 pm. One resident lost a friend to a blunt object to the head. So while these guys were busy robbing a retired top cop, others were out there butchering people. Just another Sunday in Kenya, I guess.

And the cherry on top? The guards at the main gate were subjected to thorough screening *after* the incident. Brilliant. Foresight is clearly not a strong suit in Kitengela. The whole estate, supposed to be a haven for police bigwigs, was breached by a gang that apparently likes to throw cash around like they're celebrating a win. Makes you wonder what kind of "security" they actually have. It’s almost comical if it wasn't so pathetic.
