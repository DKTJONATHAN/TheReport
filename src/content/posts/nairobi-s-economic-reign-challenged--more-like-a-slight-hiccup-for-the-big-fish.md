Alright, so the papers are buzzing about how Nairobi's supposedly losing its grip. Apparently, devolution and some shiny new roads have these smaller cities thinking they can actually *compete*. It’s all very cute, isn't it? They're talking about Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret - positioning themselves as these "specialized investment destinations." Like Nairobi wasn't already doing that, but on a scale they can only dream of. Twenty-seven point five percent of Kenya's GVA? That’s not a typo. The rest of these counties combined can't even touch that.

They're all chirping about lower land prices and "county-led investment." Let’s be real, it’s usually just a different flavour of the same old song and dance. Sure, the infrastructure might be *slightly* better than that dusty village you’re coming from, but Nairobi still has the banks, the HQs, the actual money. These regional players are trying to carve out "complementary economic identities," which in plain Swahili means they're hoping to catch the scraps Nairobi doesn't want. It’s like watching a bunch of kittens try to chase a lion - admirable effort, but ultimately futile.

Mombasa thinks it's more than just a port? Good for them. Dongo Kundu SEZ, blah blah blah. They’re attracting a few gas terminals and hoping for the best. Meanwhile, Kisumu is all about the "blue economy" and reviving Lake Victoria. I've seen more excitement about a forgotten SIM card than these grand plans for lake transport. And Nakuru, with its geothermal energy and World Rally Championship - is that really the best we can do for economic diversification? It's like they're clinging to whatever makes them slightly less invisible.

Eldoret is apparently expanding beyond agriculture. Wow, groundbreaking. They’ve got an EPZ and some universities. Look, it’s great that these places are getting *some* attention, and maybe for a niche investor, these "hubs" might make sense. But let’s not pretend this is the end of Nairobi. It’s just the usual Kenyan drama: a lot of noise, a bit of movement, and Nairobi will still be where the real deals are made. You can forget about relationships falling apart due to modern engineering if you're too busy chasing these ghost economies. The real story is that Nairobi is still the engine. These counties are just trying to add a few extra wheels, hoping they don’t fall off. The economic analysts are saying investors are looking beyond Nairobi because counties have improved infrastructure. Of course, they have. They have to start somewhere. But when it comes to serious capital, serious opportunities, Nairobi is still the undisputed champion. These regional cities are important, sure, but they’re more like the supporting cast than the headliners.

So, while everyone’s celebrating the "diversified national investment landscape," let's remember who's still carrying the weight. Nairobi. The counties might be getting a bit more attention, and some investors might find them attractive for specific reasons, but the idea that they're about to dethrone Nairobi is a fantasy peddled by wishful thinkers and county PR departments. We’ll see how long these "competitive advantages" last when the real economic storms hit.
