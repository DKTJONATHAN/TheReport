Kirinyaga Burial Food Poisoning: 100 Sick, Still More Hospitalized - What Else Is New?

Another day, another public gathering turns into a disaster thanks to shoddy catering. While our leaders promise grand projects, basic hygiene at funerals seems to be a bridge too far for some.

At least 100 people are now nursing upset stomachs, and 14 are still stuck in ACK Mt. Kenya Hospital, two of them toddlers. All this because some mourners at a burial in Kirinyaga decided to trust "Ndungu's Spices" and a random butchery. One would think a funeral, a solemn occasion, would at least be safe from dodgy food. Apparently not.

The details are as grim as you'd expect. Food left out in the sun for hours, then served. Water from a plant that clearly doesn't know what a clean bottle looks like. It's a recipe for disaster, and surprise, surprise, people got sick. Diarrhea, vomiting, headaches - the usual suspects when your food has gone on a joyride in the sun. Some folks even developed high blood pressure, proving that sometimes, the food poisoning is the least of your worries. This whole mess reminds me of the endless promises we hear, like that nuclear power plant for Siaya, promising progress but often delivering little more than hot air. [Ruto Promises Nuclear Power Plant for Siaya in 2027 - Hope or More Hot Air?](/posts/ruto-promises-nuclear-power-plant-for-siaya-in-2027---hope-or-more-hot-air)

The "investigation" found what any sane person could guess: cooked food sat for half a day, exposed to the elements. And the water? Hygiene practices "below the required standards." Fancy talk for dirty. They issued a "compliance notice," which in Kenyan terms usually means a polite suggestion to maybe, just maybe, try to be cleaner next time. Meanwhile, the food handlers are getting a lecture on washing their hands. Groundbreaking stuff.

And who do we hear from? The local MP, Gachoki Gitari, telling people to be "extra careful." As if they weren't already. He's calling for strict observance of food safety standards. Bless his heart. It's the same old song and dance: a crisis happens, then the officials chime in with generic advice. It's a circus we've seen before, like the endless appointments in the Kenya Gazette. [Kenya Gazette 2nd April 2026: More Appointments, Same Old Circus](/posts/kenya-gazette-2nd-april-2026--more-appointments--same-old-circus)

No deaths reported so far, which is a small mercy, but this whole incident just highlights the casual disregard for basic safety that plagues so many public events. We’ll keep an eye on the lab results and any further “investigations,” but don’t hold your breath for any real change.
