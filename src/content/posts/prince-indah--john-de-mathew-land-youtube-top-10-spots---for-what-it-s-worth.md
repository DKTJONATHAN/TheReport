Prince Indah, John De'Mathew On YouTube Top 10 - As If It Changes Anything

So, the big news out of the digital ether is that Prince Indah and the late John De'Mathew have somehow slithered into the top 10 most-watched Kenyan acts on YouTube. Apparently, all those hours spent glued to screens watching vernacular music are now officially counted. Who would have thought? It’s a massive testament to… well, a massive testament to how bored people are, I suppose.

Of course, Otile Brown is still king of the hill, because of course he is. 545 million views. That’s a lot of time people are spending not actually doing anything productive. Sauti Sol, Willy Paul, some gospel groups I’ve never heard of, and the Bahati circus follow. It’s a predictable parade of the usual suspects, with Prince Indah at number seven and the ghost of John De'Mathew clinging on at number ten. At least De'Mathew’s legacy is still getting clicks, unlike some things.

What does this really prove? That people will watch anything, especially if it’s in a language they understand and doesn’t require too much thinking. Indah’s "modern fusion" is just a fancy way of saying he’s using old beats with new toys, and people are digging it. De'Mathew’s enduring popularity years after his unfortunate demise? Maybe it’s a reminder that good stories, even sad ones, stick. But let’s not pretend this is some grand cultural shift. It’s just another Tuesday on the Kenyan internet, where everyone’s looking for a distraction, much like the endless drama we see on our roads, as evidenced by the [AIC Lomut Secondary School Bus Crash: 'No Fatalities' Just Another Tuesday on Kenyan Roads](/posts/aic-lomut-secondary-school-bus-crash---no-fatalities--just-another-tuesday-on-kenyan-roads).

This whole ranking is just a snapshot of what’s popular online, not a measure of lasting impact. It highlights that regional sounds have digital power, sure, but it also shows how easily we can get lost in the scroll. Everyone’s chasing views, everyone’s trying to stay relevant. It’s a digital hustle, same as the one happening on the streets, just with less sweat and more Wi-Fi. The real story here isn't who's on the list, but how much time we're all collectively wasting.
