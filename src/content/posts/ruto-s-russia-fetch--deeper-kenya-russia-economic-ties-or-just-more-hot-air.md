Ruto's Russian Roulette: Deeper Kenya-Russia Ties or Another Empty Promise?

President Ruto's grand pronouncements about deeper economic ties with Russia, while attending the Africa-Russia Summit in Moscow, sound more like a desperate plea than a strategic handshake. While he speaks of mutual prosperity and Ksh.5.2 trillion for infrastructure, the reality on the ground for most Kenyans is a struggle to make ends meet, and a persistent distrust of grand promises.

The President's enthusiasm for Russia's expertise in energy, mining, and manufacturing aligns suspiciously well with the very sectors where corruption often festers. He's hoping for "mutual benefits," but one has to wonder who exactly benefits. Is it the Kenyan people, or a select few looking to tap into new avenues for… well, let's just say "resource mobilization," much like we've seen with [THE PREDATORY STATE AND THE EXODUS OF CAPITAL](/posts/the-predatory-state-and-the-exodus-of-capital).

Ruto's charm offensive to Russian investors, seeking a partnership where both countries "share prosperity," is a familiar script. We've heard it all before, haven't we? Promises of jobs, development, and a brighter future. Yet, for many, the only thing that seems to grow consistently is the national debt, with little tangible improvement for the average citizen.

The fact that this is happening in Moscow, with Vladimir Putin himself at the helm of the economic forum, adds a layer of… let's call it "intrigue." While Ruto paints a rosy picture of educational and agricultural cooperation, one can't help but notice the geopolitical undertones. Are we trading one set of dependencies for another, hoping for a different outcome this time?

The call for deeper cooperation is noted, especially with Russia's touted strengths. But as the President mobilizes trillions for infrastructure, the real question remains: will this money actually build roads and power plants for the people, or will it disappear into the usual channels, leaving us with more debt and fewer answers? Time, as always in Nairobi, will tell the real story.
