IG Kanja's New Directive: Another Day, Another Promise on Deaths in Police Cells

The Inspector General of Police, Douglas Kanja, has apparently discovered that people are dying in police cells. Shocking, I know. Now he’s issued a directive - interdict the officers in charge. The OB recorder, the cell sentry, the duty officer - all on the chopping block, pending an investigation that, let's be honest, will probably go nowhere fast. Because in Nairobi, promises are cheaper than a chai without sugar.

This is the same old song and dance, just with a new chorus. Every time someone kicks the bucket in police custody, there’s a flurry of pronouncements and promises of accountability. Remember Albert Ojwang? Popular influencer, they said, before he was found dead, strangled and beaten. The OCS and his merry band of duty officers got interdicted. And then? Silence. We’ll see how long this new interdiction lasts before it’s just another dusty file in some bureaucratic abyss.

The IG says deaths in cells are a "serious concern." You think? It's almost as if the police service is a magnet for trouble, attracting incidents that are as predictable as the TikTok-fueled visitor deluge at Hothfield Highland Cows. Of course, they’ll throw around words like "demotion" and "legal proceedings" to make it sound like justice is being served. But we both know how that usually plays out.

Take Gedion Makau, found dead in Kilungu Police Station. Suicide, they claimed. The family says otherwise. IPOA is investigating, which is usually a good sign that things will get complicated and eventually forgotten. It’s a classic Nairobi narrative: a life lost, a story spun, and a system that’s more interested in damage control than actual justice. This whole charade is just a way to placate the public until the next unfortunate soul ends up a headline.

Kanja’s directive is just another attempt to put a band-aid on a gaping wound. It’s a performative gesture designed to show that *something* is being done. But until the system itself is overhauled, until the culture of impunity is truly dismantled, these directives will remain just that - directives. Words on paper, while the reality on the ground continues to claim lives.

This is the reality of our streets, folks. The police are meant to protect us, but sometimes, they’re the ones who end up taking us. And when that happens, the best we can hope for is a temporary interdiction and a whole lot of hot air.
