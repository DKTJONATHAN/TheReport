KRU Dreams of Chipu Stardom for Simbas: Another Cycle of Broken Promises?

A desperate gamble to salvage national pride after another Rugby World Cup qualification flop, the Kenya Rugby Union (KRU) is banking on the young guns. But is this a genuine rebuild or just the usual song and dance?

The Simbas, having choked in Uganda and missed out on Australia 2027, are now supposedly being revitalized by the Chipu squad. These are the same kids who just managed to beat Namibia in some regional tournament. Apparently, this is the grand strategy.

KRU CEO Thomas Odundo, speaking from Kasarani - the same place dreams go to die for Kenyan sports - assures us they will "correct the shortcomings." These "shortcomings" are just code for their perennial inability to get the job done when it matters. Now they'll focus on "developing a competitive side." We've heard this before, haven't we? It’s the same script, just with a new cast of hopefuls.

Vice-Chairman Moses Ndale chirps about a "seamless pathway" between Chipu and the Simbas, even involving the U20 coaches. It’s all about "unified training philosophies." Translation: More meetings, more jargon, and probably more expense accounts. Meanwhile, Haco Industries is throwing Ksh2.5 million at the Simbas. Catherine Obwino from Haco is excited about showering players with products like shower gel and lotion. Because that’s what elite athletes need after a gruelling campaign of failure - nice smelling sweat.

The Simbas captain, Samuel Asati, is all smiles, talking about corporate appreciation and accountability. It’s inspiring to see corporates venturing into sports, he says. Yes, Samuel, they venture in when there’s a brand to be leveraged and taxpayer money to be indirectly influenced, not necessarily out of pure love for the game. It’s a classic case of sports being used to polish corporate image, a bit like how some politicians use sporting events to deflect from bigger issues, or how [Prince Harry's latest feud with the press ends for now - revealing only predictable greed and futility](/posts/prince-harry-s-latest-feud-with-the-press-ends--for-now---revealing-only-predictable-greed-and-futility).

The Chipu boys are praised for their "thrilling" rugby. Let’s hope they don’t get disillusioned too quickly. Kenyan rugby has a bright future, they say. We’ll see. History suggests this bright future is more like a flickering candle in a hurricane, easily snuffed out by administrative incompetence and misplaced priorities.







The Kenya Rugby Union (KRU) has unveiled a grand plan to resurrect the Simbas, their men's 15s rugby team, after the crushing disappointment of failing to qualify for the 2027 Men's Rugby World Cup in Australia. Their new strategy? Blend in the fresh talent from the Kenya Under-20 squad, Chipu, who recently snagged the 2026 Barthés Trophy with a convincing win over Namibia. Because nothing screams "future success" like relying on teenagers after the established men have proven they can't cut it.

Kenya's World Cup dreams were, as usual, shattered at the 2025 Rugby Africa Cup in Uganda. A narrow semi-final loss to Zimbabwe, followed by a pathetic defeat to Algeria, landed the Simbas in fourth place. Zimbabwe, meanwhile, is off to Australia. And now, the KRU is talking about "correcting shortcomings" and developing a "competitive side" for the next qualification cycle. It's a familiar refrain, isn't it? The same old promises, just dressed up in new corporate sponsorship.

KRU Chief Executive Officer Thomas Odundo insists they are ready to fix what went wrong. Vice-Chairman Moses Ndale is gushing about a "seamless pathway" between Chipu and the Simbas, even bringing in the U20 coaches to ensure "unified training philosophies." It's all very organized, very strategic. You'd almost believe it if it wasn't Kenyan rugby we were talking about.

Adding to the fanfare, Haco Industries, through its Amara brand, has inked a Ksh2.5 million partnership. Marketing Director Catherine Obwino is thrilled about supporting the Simbas' training and local travel, even mentioning the need for shower gel and lotion after intense activity. Because nothing says "professional sports development" like talking about toiletries. It’s a nice gesture, but will it magically make the team score tries when it counts?

Simbas captain Samuel Asati seems genuinely grateful, speaking of corporate support inspiring accountability. He even praises Chipu's performance, saying they played "such good rugby" unlike his own U20 days. This, he claims, means Kenyan rugby has a bright future. We’ve heard about bright futures before, often as a consolation prize after another spectacular failure. It makes you wonder if this whole initiative is just a way to keep the players busy and the sponsors happy, rather than a genuine attempt to win.
