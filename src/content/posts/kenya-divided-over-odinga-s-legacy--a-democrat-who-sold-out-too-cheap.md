One year on, and the air in Nairobi is still thick with chatter about Raila Odinga. They say he fought for democracy, for reforms. Sure, he did. He also stood centre stage during that 2007 election mess, the one that sent thousands running for their lives. Then, thanks to Kofi Annan's magic wand, he became Prime Minister. Suddenly, the "struggle" morphed into a power-sharing deal. Pretty convenient, eh?

And let's not forget the "handshakes." First with Kibaki in 2008, then Uhuru Kenyatta in 2018, and now Ruto in 2024. Each time, the same song and dance: reconciliation, national unity. What they really mean is selling out the opposition. Rigathi Gachagua, bless his blunt honesty, called it "nonsense," and he’s not wrong. It’s exactly this kind of compromise that makes you wonder if Kenya's so-called opposition is just another part of the elaborate election show, as I've seen play out before. [Kenya's 'Political Gangs' Are Just Another Expensive Election Show](/posts/kenya-s--political-gangs--are-just-another-expensive-election-show).

You hear young people like Beatrice Chebet talking about his ideals, about peace and rule of law. It’s sweet, really. But for those of us who’ve been around, we remember the decades of detentions, the campaigns, the promises. Odinga was an institution, they say, rocking institutions. More like an institution that knew how to make a deal when the pressure was on. His daughter, Winnie, is even running for senator. Keeping the name alive, I suppose, even if the original fire’s gone out.

The Orange Democratic Movement (ODM), his party, is scrambling. Edwin Sifuna admits they’re losing their "party of the people" tag. Shocker. When your leader is busy shaking hands with everyone in power, your core supporters get left out in the cold. It’s a tricky tightrope, this political game. You want to be seen as a bridge builder, but you end up looking like just another politician looking for a seat at the table.

So, what's his legacy? Did he expand democratic possibilities or just create more opportunities for political maneuvering? Kenya’s still arguing about it, and frankly, it’s exhausting. His influence is everywhere - in the squabbles within his own party, in the calls for accountability that seem to fall on deaf ears. The real test, as always, is whether anything he supposedly stood for can survive without the man himself. My bet? Don't hold your breath.
