Ruto's Big Promises: Another Term, More Debt?

Is President Ruto laying the foundation for a stronger Kenya, or just building more sandcastles before the election tide washes them away? He's promising billions for roads and power, but with a debt pile already threatening to drown us, who's footing the bill?

So, the big man in charge, William Ruto, decided it was time for his final curtain call of this term, a State of the Nation Address, no less. He stood there, all smiles and grand pronouncements, telling us Kenya's economy is "recovering" and that he's got a "clear direction" thanks to "immense opportunity." Right. He's also managed to squirrel away a cool 340 billion shillings ($2.6 billion) for some "National Infrastructure Fund" by selling off bits of what we already owned. Progress, they call it.

Now, he's got his eyes on a whopping 5 trillion shillings ($38 billion) over the next decade for more infrastructure. Think more roads, more railways, airports, and a massive jump in electricity generation. Sounds great, doesn't it? Except remember how those fuel hikes have been squeezing everyone dry? We've seen how these "infrastructure" projects can end up impacting the ordinary Kenyan, like those [Digital Drivers' Pockets Get Squeezed After Fuel Hikes](/posts/digital-drivers--pockets-get-squeezed-after-fuel-hikes). It’s a classic case of the big picture always costing the little guy.

And as if that weren't enough, to soften the blow of all this talk of economic recovery (which many of us aren't feeling), he's proposing to exempt the lowest earners from income tax. Workers earning 32,333 shillings ($250) or less per month will breathe a little easier. It's a nice gesture, I suppose, but it's a drop in the ocean when you consider the mounting debt obligations and the rising cost of basic necessities.

The president wants us to believe Kenya is making a "powerful comeback," with healthcare expanding and our infrastructure growing. He's even promising 20,000 new teachers. All sounds very rosy on paper, especially with the 2027 election looming. But let's be real, the man's economic record, his tax policies, and the ever-present struggle with the cost of living are going to be the real topics of conversation when it’s time to vote.
