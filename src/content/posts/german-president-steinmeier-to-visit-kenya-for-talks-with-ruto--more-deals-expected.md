GERMAN PRESIDENT STEINMEIER COMES TO KENYA FOR TALKS WITH RUTO, PROMISES MORE OF THE SAME

Here comes another one. The German President, Frank-Walter Steinmeier, is flapping his wings over to Nairobi next week. Three days of "strengthening bilateral relations" and "expanding economic cooperation." Sounds like the usual song and dance. He’ll be chatting with President Ruto, looking at some tech and health projects, and probably shaking hands with more people than he can remember. Just another photo op for the books, I suppose.

Steinmeier’s grand tour will take him from State House, where he'll be fêted with talks and a luncheon, to Tatu City. There he’ll marvel at a mobile healthcare initiative, whatever that is, and some call centres. Apparently, Germany thinks our digital services are something to write home about. He’ll also grace an art centre for kids. Lovely. Then it’s off to Naivasha, to look at Crescent Island and "engage" with civil society. Because nothing says progress like foreign dignitaries pretending to care about our problems.

This visit is all about "exploring areas of mutual interest," which translates to more deals, more investment, and more promises about skills development and migration. Germany needs workers, we have people looking for work. It’s a match made in… well, not exactly heaven, but somewhere between the ministries and the boardrooms. They've even got a migration and mobility partnership from last year. Let's hope it’s more than just more paperwork and empty rhetoric, unlike some of the promises we’ve heard before.

Don't forget the business delegation tagging along. Over 120 German companies already call Kenya home, making us a "key economic partner." That means more opportunities for them, and maybe a few crumbs for us. They’ve got cooperation frameworks for climate change, energy, agriculture, you name it. It's all part of Steinmeier's swan song African tour, his last hurrah before his successor is picked back home. So, expect a lot of smiles, a lot of handshakes, and then everyone goes back to their own business. We’ve seen this movie before.

This whole song and dance reminds me of how things never really change, even when the players do. It’s like watching the police fumble the bag while someone important walks free; the system just keeps on going.
