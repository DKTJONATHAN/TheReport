So, President Ruto, the man who was once Raila Odinga's fiercest rival, is now out here telling us about their "last moments." Apparently, it was Oburu Oginga who broke the news, calling the President at 6:30 AM to say his brother, Raila, was "gone." Funny how these things happen, isn't it? One minute you're discussing the country's future, the next… poof. Just like that, the rival is a memory, and the "youth leader" is left to collect the pieces. It's all very dramatic, but then again, what else do you expect from these politicians?

Ruto goes on to wax poetic about their decades-long "journey" - from Kanu to Ford, then forging ODM together, and finally parting ways again. He even calls Raila a "great team leader," a sentiment that conveniently surfaces now that Raila is six feet under. It’s easy to praise a fallen comrade, isn't it? Especially when their absence opens up more room at the top. The 2022 election, a mere blip of 233,000 votes separating them, now seems like ancient history. And look at them now, after the "political turbulence" of the 2024 protests, conveniently brought together in a "broad-based government." The timing, as always, is impeccable.

The President mentions these "unusually close" weeks before Raila's death: phone calls, dinners, meetings. Raila, apparently, "came forward and said we must stabilise the country." And Ruto, in turn, believes Raila "stood on the right side of history." It’s all very noble, isn’t it? Especially when you remember that just a short while ago, they were at each other's throats. It’s a classic Kenyan political script: embrace when it suits, discard when it doesn’t. This whole charade reminds me of our leaders' consistent inability to learn from history, especially when it comes to reforming our security sector; it's always [another round of hot air and empty promises](/posts/kenya-s-security-sector-reform-push--another-round-of-hot-air-and-empty-promises).

Then there's the whole medical saga. Raila wasn't feeling well, wanted to see Ruto. Visited him at State House, looking "visibly unwell." Then off to Malindi for a warmer climate, continued phone calls. Ruto even went to Raila's home for dinner, where Raila expressed his desire for a second medical opinion. Ruto, ever the supportive "friend," encouraged him. And finally, India. Initial hopeful calls about finding the "right treatment," followed by news of discharge and a plan to "rest." All leading up to that fateful 6:30 AM phone call. It’s a story that tugs at the heartstrings, or at least, that’s what they want you to believe.

The core of it all, the "hardest part" for Ruto, was the "suddenness." Talking about the future one moment, and then… silence. It's a narrative designed to elicit sympathy, to paint a picture of genuine connection. But let's be real, in this country, "friendship" often has an expiration date, usually tied to political expediency. When the dust settles, and the eulogies fade, we'll be left with the same old political games, the same old scramble for power, and the same old fuel prices hitting [record highs](/posts/diesel-hits-record-sh242-per-litre--kenya-s-fuel-crisis-deepens). This whole "last moments" story is just another chapter in the grand theatre of Kenyan politics.
