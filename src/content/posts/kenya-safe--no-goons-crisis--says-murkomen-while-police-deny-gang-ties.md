So, according to our esteemed Interior Cabinet Secretary, Kipchumba Murkomen, Kenya is as safe as a bank vault guarded by a sleepy chihuahua. He’s out in Eldoret, bless his heart, telling us that anyone claiming we’re drowning in a crisis of goons and criminal gangs is a flat out liar. Apparently, these "incidents" are just the ramblings of politicians who can't campaign without hiring a hooligan posse to do their dirty work. It’s a "bankruptcy of ideas," he claims, and they "sanya" goons to make it look like a national headache. Right.

Murkomen, the man who apparently sees no evil, hears no evil, and definitely feels no insecurity, assures us there's no national crisis. Kenya is safe. Just ignore the whispers of thugs disrupting rallies, intimidating opponents, and generally making life a misery for anyone not wearing a party scarf. It's all just political theatre, orchestrated by desperate politicians. It makes you wonder if they’re living in the same Nairobi we are, or perhaps they’ve perfected the art of [The Global Hall of Mirrors: Why AI is a Death Sentence for Truth](/posts/the-global-hall-of-mirrors--why-ai-is-a-death-sentence-for-truth).

Then we have Deputy Inspector General Eliud Lagat, chiming in with his own brand of denial. He's adamant that no police officers are colluding with these criminal elements. They're just there to "reinforce the laws," he says, with a straight face. He insists the police are not partisan and have the capacity to deal with this "uncalled-for insecurity." He’s even admitted they’ve had "challenges" with goons and gangs, but fear not, strategies are in place. Strategies. Meanwhile, our streets tell a different story.

Lagat also throws in a plea for everyone to chip in - political class, religious class, students, youth. It's not just a police issue, apparently. Everyone needs to work with the police to stop these gangs. It’s a nice sentiment, but it rings a bit hollow when the very people elected to maintain order are busy blaming each other and downplaying the reality on the ground.

Adding a touch of realism, or perhaps just more cynicism, is National Assembly Committee Chairman Gabriel Tongoyo. He’s the one who's actually pointing fingers where they deserve to be pointed: directly at the politicians. He claims these gangs are "almost entirely, if not all, being sponsored by politicians, the man in tie and big offices." Now, *that* sounds like a Nairobi realist talking. The guys in suits, pulling the strings, making sure their hired muscle keeps the opposition in check.

So, according to the powers that be, Kenya is perfectly safe, and any talk of a crisis is just disgruntled politicians fanning the flames. The police are on it, even if they admit to having "challenges." And somewhere in the background, the "men in ties" are likely chuckling, knowing their sponsored goons are doing exactly what they're paid to do, while the public is told to just relax. It's a beautiful day in Kenya, isn't it?
