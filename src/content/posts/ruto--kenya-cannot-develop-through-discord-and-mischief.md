Ruto: Kenya Development Not Through Discord, But Empty Housing Promises

WARNING: President Ruto claims Kenya’s transformation hinges on development projects, not political squabbles. Meanwhile, his administration touts affordable housing and road initiatives, but the reality on the ground remains a work in progress, as usual.

The President is at it again, folks. This time, he's flown down to Taveta to preach about how Kenya will be transformed not by the usual noise and political back-and-forth, but by, you guessed it, development projects. Because obviously, we haven't heard that one before. He was quoted saying, "Mimi niliwaambia kwamba katika mpango ya kubadilisha Kenya, haiwezi badilishwa kwa fitina ama porojo ama maneno tupu ya siasa, ila inabadilishwa kwa miradi mpya na maendeleo." Translation: "I told you that in the plan to change Kenya, it cannot be changed by intrigue or empty political talk, but it is changed by new projects and development." Groundbreaking stuff.

Now, to prove his point, he’s trotted out the usual suspects: affordable housing and roads. In Taita Taveta, we're apparently getting 12 billion shillings worth of affordable housing. That breaks down to 1,674 units across Voi, Taveta, and Wundanyi, costing a cool Ksh4.95 billion. Voi's project is 60% done, Taveta is a measly 14% complete, and Wundanyi's is just awarded. Then there's Mwatate, promising 580 units and a hospital for about Ksh1.7 billion. All this, of course, is part of his "Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda." Sounds familiar, doesn't it? It’s the same old song and dance, much like the predictable tune of international relations we see with countries like China, where [Kenya, China Still Singing the Same Old Tune Beyond Trade: Music as Another Tool for Relations](/posts/kenya--china-still-singing-the-same-old-tune-beyond-trade--music-as-another-tool-for-relations).

And let's not forget the roads. Ruto’s administration is also throwing Ksh2 billion at 200 kilometers of new roads in Machakos County. Seven new projects, eight stalled ones to be revived. He even launched a nearly 20-kilometer road called Makaveti-Kyangala-Liyuni. Apparently, this will connect sections of the county and link existing routes. Because that's what roads do. They connect things. Who knew? The government is also moving to revive those eight stalled projects. Because nothing says "transformation" like picking up projects that have been gathering dust.

The whole spiel is meant to convince us that tangible projects are the real deal, not the political drama. But let's be real, these announcements are often just that - announcements. We've seen this cycle before: big promises, flashy launches, and then… well, we wait. We wait to see if these units get built, if the roads are actually usable, and if the lives of ordinary Kenyans are genuinely improved. Or if it's just another way to get headlines and keep the political machinery humming. It’s the same old Kenyan story.

Hopefully, this time, the development agenda isn't just empty rhetoric dressed up in concrete and tarmac. But if you’re looking for genuine change, don’t hold your breath. Keep your eyes on the ground, not just the podium. Because the transformation of Kenya, if it ever happens, will be a long, hard slog, not a quick fix delivered from a church pulpit.
