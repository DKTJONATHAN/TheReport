The NSE is Selling Its Westlands Headquarters: Another Nairobi Property Play, Or Just Plain Dumb?

They're offloading the building. Big deal. Just another Nairobi cash grab dressed up as progress.

Another prime piece of Nairobi real estate up for grabs, probably so some fat cats can pocket the cash.







So, the Nairobi Securities Exchange is finally getting rid of its fancy Westlands digs. Controlled access, fibre optic cables, a generator that probably only works when it feels like it - sounds like every other office building in this city, doesn't it? They're calling it "reshaping operations" for the digital age. More like realizing they can make a quick buck off prime real estate while pretending it's some grand strategic move.

Forget the bustling trading floors of yesteryear. Now it's all clicks and algorithms. The NSE's got its electronic platform, and everyone's doing their shady deals online. So why hold onto a massive building? It’s the same old song: money. This move, they say, is about "unlocking value from non-core assets." Translation: they've got a golden goose of a property in a city where land is king, and they're about to cash in. Just like how politicians seem to have selective memory when it comes to where taxpayer money goes, *cough* Washington's Choirboy: Ruto's Selective Memory in the Gulf Inferno *cough*.

The funny thing is, property prices in Nairobi are like a stubborn cockroach - they just keep on surviving, even when office occupancy is doing the tango. So, while they're busy patting themselves on the back for this "monetisation" and "financial flexibility," let's be real. The proceeds from this sale will probably disappear into some obscure "capital management initiative" or, more likely, end up lining someone's already well-padded pockets.

They haven't even bothered to tell us where the money's going. Typical. Just another big announcement with no real substance, just like a lot of the pronouncements we hear in this city. They're selling a building that probably cost a fortune to build and maintain, all while claiming it's about embracing the future. The future, in Nairobi, usually just means a new way to make money off the same old things.
