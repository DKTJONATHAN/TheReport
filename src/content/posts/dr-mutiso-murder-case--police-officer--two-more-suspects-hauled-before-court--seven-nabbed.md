So, Dr Victoria Mutiso, a renowned psychiatrist and mental health expert, gets shot dead on July 29, 2026. Gun-wielding gang, Nairobi, the usual script. Now, barely a month later, we've got Officer Elijah Kimoi hauled before the Milimani court, along with Daniel Mwangi and Kevin Ngugi Kinuthia. Seven arrests so far. Makes you wonder if they're trying to find the killers or just fill the cells.

And here's the kicker: the firearm used in the "heinous murder" was issued to a police officer. A police officer, mind you, who's conveniently "at large." DCI and some "multi-agency team" are apparently hunting him. Because, you know, our own police force is always the best bet to catch a rogue cop. It’s a tangled web, like trying to find a needle in a haystack, but the needle is also a gun.

We've got the deceased's ex-co-wife, Rose Mbithe Mulwa, her two kids, Chris and Angella, and a Mr Nelson Masiga already cooling their heels. Now add Kimoi, Mwangi, and Kinuthia to the mix. The investigators want them detained for 30 days. Thirty days. Plenty of time for them to remember anything they might have conveniently forgotten, or to concoct a story that sounds vaguely plausible. It’s all part of the grand theatre, isn't it?

This whole affair reeks of the usual Nairobi drama, a tragic echo of the financial shenanigans we've seen before. Remember how some crypto investor played the victim amidst all that? This feels similar, a desperate scramble for justice, or at least the appearance of it. It makes you wonder if anyone truly gets justice in this city, or if we're all just pawns in a much larger, messier game.

And that firearm? Issued to a police officer who's gone missing. It's almost poetic. The very people meant to protect us are sometimes the ones you need protection from. This isn't about finding the truth anymore; it's about managing the narrative, about making it look like the system is working, even when it's clearly sputtering like a worn-out matatu on the hilly roads.

Let's be real. We'll probably never know the full story. There will be charges, maybe a conviction, maybe not. But the cycle continues. Another life lost, another group of people paraded before the cameras, and the rest of us left to shake our heads and mutter about the state of affairs. The only thing certain is that our wallets will feel it, one way or another, just like with those new tax bills.
