Ruto Signs Law Unlocking Sh71.09bn Extra Funding for Counties - Prepare for More Empty Promises

So, the President has signed some law. Sh71.09 billion extra funding for county governments. Sounds good on paper, right? Except, let's be real. This is mostly Sh56.38 billion coming from loans and grants from "development partners." Translation: more debt for us to eventually pay back. The rest is a trickle from the national government's share of revenue, a pinch of mineral royalties, and pocket change from court fines. Don't get your hopes up for actual development.

They're talking about quarterly reporting and strengthening accountability. That's the usual song and dance. You know how it goes. Money gets allocated, reports get filed, and then... well, have you seen the state of some of our county headquarters? Or the roads leading to them? It’s almost as if these "strengthened oversight" mechanisms are just fancy ways to make it look like they’re doing something while the actual money disappears into thin air. This whole charade reminds me of how counties are still chasing Sh113 billion in unpaid revenue; transparency is often just a suggestion.

This Sh71.09 billion is supposed to fund things like county headquarters, health promoters, and affordable housing. Noble causes, I'm sure. But remember the Sh27.5 million disability funds query that blocked Nakuru County's budget? That's the level of scrutiny these billions will likely face. Expect more finger-pointing and bureaucratic delays. The same old story, just with a bigger cheque.

The law demands the Cabinet Secretary for the National Treasury to publish quarterly reports. And county treasuries need to record these transfers. This is all about "improving transparency." We've heard that one before. It's meant to make sure the money goes where it's supposed to. But let's not forget the Public Finance Management Act - non-compliance is an offense. Yet, somehow, the "offenders" always seem to find a way to walk away with their pockets full.

So, while they're busy signing laws and making grand pronouncements about unlocking funds, remember where most of this money is actually coming from - loans and grants. And remember what happens with most government funds. We’ll see a lot of new reports, a lot of talk about "accountability," and then, if we’re lucky, a few projects that actually benefit someone other than the contractors and their well-connected friends.
