Ruto's Latest Appointments: More Hats for the Usual Suspects

Same old song and dance from State House, eh? President Ruto's busy doling out jobs like cheap sweets, and who pops up next? Lydia Chelagat, apparently, to be the next Data Commissioner. After a "recruitment exercise" that sounds about as transparent as a politician's expense report, she's been whisked through the PSC pipeline. Let's just hope she's got a thicker skin than the rest of us, because managing all that personal data in this country is going to be a real fiesta of complaints.

And it's not just the data game. Oh no, the government spokesperson, bless his heart, has confirmed a whole raft of new CEOs for state corporations. Waweru Karanja, Peninah Wangare Wahome, John Washington Agutu, and Dr. Janerose Omondi are now holding the reins. It's always the same story: competitive processes, recommendations, and then suddenly, *poof*, familiar faces or their chosen few are in charge. Don't expect any revolutions here, folks. This is just musical chairs with bigger salaries and less accountability.

At least Agutu at the National Housing Corporation has some "experience" in estates, apparently. Twenty-four years of it, managing, developing, financing, valuing. Sounds like he knows how to build a good portfolio, which is probably more than you can say for most of the housing projects that actually get finished around here. Let's just hope his expertise translates into actual houses and not more empty promises gathering dust. It all feels a bit like watching [Kenya Police, Tusker FC Scramble for Caf Confederation Cup Spot: Another Flop Gets a Lifeline](/posts/kenya-police--tusker-fc-scramble-for-caf-confederation-cup-spot--another-flop-gets-a-lifeline), another desperate attempt to look like something is happening.

As for Dr. Omondi at KNEST, she's moved up from the Privatization Commission. More experience in structuring deals, they say. Which, in Kenyan terms, usually means navigating the maze of approvals and exemptions that line some pockets. It’s a well-trodden path, this stepping stone from one lucrative government gig to another, all under the guise of public service.

Immaculate Kassait, the outgoing Data Commissioner, did "oversee the establishment and development" of her office. Which, let's be honest, is government-speak for "she was there when it started." Now, Chelagat has the exciting task of actually making it work, investigating violations, and supervising how everyone handles our sensitive information. A six-year, non-renewable term. Let's see how much of that can be achieved before the next political cycle rearranges the deck chairs.

So, here we are. More appointments, more titles, more people in positions of power. The President is assenting to bills, the PSC is recommending, and the spokespersons are announcing. Meanwhile, the rest of us are left to wonder what, if anything, will actually change. Don't hold your breath for a data revolution or a housing boom. It’s just another day at the office for those who know how to play the game.
