So, the bigwigs from Mount Kenya are out in full force, singing the government's praises and waving around shiny new projects like they’re the second coming. Kimani Ichung’wah, the National Assembly Majority Leader, is apparently on a mission to convince everyone that this administration is laser-focused on improving our lives. He’s trotting out affordable housing and modern markets as proof, as if these are magic wands that solve all our problems. Billions of shillings, he says. We'll see if those billions actually trickle down to the *common mwananchi*, or if they just disappear into some politician's offshore account.

And of course, in true Kenyan fashion, the election year posturing has already begun, even though 2027 is still a distant mirage for most of us just trying to get by. Ichung’wah is busy lecturing the opposition about "premature campaigns" and "inflammatory remarks." It’s rich, coming from a government that’s already knee-deep in politicking while people are struggling to afford basic necessities. He wants everyone to "allow the Government to work." Work on what, exactly? More promises? More development projects that are conveniently unveiled right before an election? It’s the same old song and dance.

Murang’a Woman Rep, Betty Maina, is also chiming in, echoing the calls for unity and peace. She’s urging the government to use this "remaining period" to deliver on promises in healthcare, education, and infrastructure. Bless her heart. It’s a nice sentiment, but let's be real, the healthcare system is a shambles, education is a constant struggle for many, and our roads? Passable is a generous word. She tells us our vote can change things, and we should elect "development-oriented leaders." If only it were that simple. It's like telling someone to fix their leaky roof with a vote.

Meanwhile, Mathira MP Eric Wamumbi is lecturing on "mature politics" and cautioning against personal attacks. He’s telling politicians to "keep off people’s marriages" and avoid victimizing others. This is coming from the same political arena where reputations are shredded daily. It’s a bit like a fox lecturing other foxes about not eating chickens. He also wants us to support the government's development programs, claiming they will "benefit communities." We've heard that one before. It sounds suspiciously like the kind of efficiency we discuss when talking about [Data Infrastructure Efficiency Becomes Critical Pillar for Global Enterprise Scalability](/posts/data-infrastructure-efficiency-becomes-critical-pillar-for-global-enterprise-scalability), but for human lives.

These leaders are clearly trying to build a narrative of progress and stability, a narrative that conveniently overlooks the daily grind for most Kenyans. They talk about development while the cost of living skyrockets. They preach unity while political divisions are as wide as the Great Rift Valley. It’s all about securing their positions for the next election, painting a rosy picture that doesn't match the reality outside their air-conditioned offices. They want us to believe in their pipelines and their billions, but I'm more interested in what’s actually in our pockets and on our plates.
