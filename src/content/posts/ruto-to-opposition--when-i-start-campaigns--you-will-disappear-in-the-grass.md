Ruto to Opposition: When I Start Campaigns, You Will Disappear in the Grass

President Ruto dismisses opposition as clueless and warns of an impending campaign onslaught.

President William Ruto, while launching a road project in Narok, declared his re-election campaign has yet to commence, issuing a stark warning to his political rivals that they will "disappear in the grass" once it begins. His tone was predictably combative, painting the opposition as a group devoid of any meaningful agenda, plan, or vision, merely resorting to insults and tribal rhetoric. He mockingly pointed out that their greatest achievement so far has been giving him the nickname "Kasongo," a testament to their lack of substantive policies.

Ruto insisted his current countrywide tours are solely for development, not politicking, but cautioned that his opponents would falter when he officially launches his campaign. He derided their calls to reverse his administration's policies, such as the shift from NHIF to SHA, questioning their lack of viable alternatives. This blatant dismissal of the opposition's concerns mirrors similar political theater seen across the globe, where the focus on infrastructure often serves as a convenient distraction from deeper systemic issues, much like the petrodollar schemes I've seen discussed [Petrodollar Golf: The Slow Death of a Sovereign Vanity Project](/posts/petrodollar-golf--the-slow-death-of-a-sovereign-vanity-project).

The President challenged voters to prioritize development records over ethnic politics and hatred, asserting his administration's commitment to equitable distribution of resources across Kenya, as mandated by the constitution. He highlighted ongoing infrastructure projects in Narok, including the 59-kilometer Enengetia-Olposimoru road and the impending completion of Narok Airport, as evidence of his government's tangible achievements.

Ruto further boasted that the current unity between UDA and ODM would guarantee him an unassailable victory in 2027, particularly in Narok, where he anticipates a unified vote. His visit to Narok, amidst rising political temperatures, was clearly aimed at seizing the initiative and portraying his opponents as relics of outdated political games.

Whether this warning of rivals disappearing "in the grass" will prove prophetic remains to be seen, but for now, it's the same old song and dance, just with a fresh coat of tarmac.
