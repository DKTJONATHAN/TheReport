DP KINDIKI WARNS MT KENYA RESIDENTS: SO MUCH FOR UNITY, EH?

The Deputy President, Kithure Kindiki, recently graced the people of Nakuru with his presence, dispensing wisdom on unity and development. Apparently, the government is busy building "one nation" where everyone is accommodated, regardless of tribe, religion, or age. Because, you know, Kenya has always been a picture of perfect harmony.

Kindiki, while inspecting shiny new markets and housing projects in Bahati - because what screams equality like affordable housing costing billions - waxed lyrical about Nakuru being a "cosmopolitan town." He pointed out the presence of Kikuyu, Kalenjin, Kisii, and Luhya communities as proof of this grand fusion. It's almost as if people just live where they can find jobs and a place to sleep, rather than for some grand ethnic melting pot experiment.

The DP also harped on about President Ruto's commitment to taking development "to every part of the country," with the slogan 'Usawa ni Sawa' - equality is fair. He even suggested two terms for the President because, naturally, equality is best served by sticking around. It makes you wonder if the "equality" they're pushing is just a fancy way of saying "more development for some, while others are still stuck dealing with the audio hustle."

He rattled off figures: Ksh. 25 billion for markets, Ksh. 46 billion for houses and hostels, and Ksh. 38 billion for roads. Over 870 kilometers of road projects, and 25,000 homes getting electricity. All this, he stressed, is to ensure no discrimination between rich and poor, ethnic groups, religions, or genders. It’s a nice sentiment, I suppose, but let's be real, the promise of development often sounds better on paper than when it actually trickles down to the ground.

Of course, he wasn't alone. Bahati MP Irene Njoki, Nakuru Town East MP David Gikaria, and a host of other leaders were there to lend their support to this grand vision. Because nothing says genuine unity like a photo op with the dignitaries while promising the moon and stars. It’s almost as inspiring as that story about the Ghanaian woman trying to traffic a child to Kenya - another day, another hustle for some.







The Deputy President, Kithure Kindiki, recently descended upon Nakuru, armed with pronouncements of unity and a hefty dose of government projects. He declared that the administration is diligently working to bring all Kenyans together, moving beyond petty tribal, regional, or religious divides. The message? A united country focused on national development, where everyone, regardless of their background, feels accommodated.

Kindiki painted Nakuru as the shining example of this supposed national cohesion, a "cosmopolitan town" where various ethnic communities like the Kikuyu, Kalenjin, Kisii, and Luhya coexist. He was there to inspect ongoing government projects, including the Bahati Affordable Housing Project, which has seen significant investment. It's funny how the promise of equality and fairness seems to surface most loudly when billions of shillings are being spent on infrastructure and housing.

The DP stressed President Ruto's commitment to equitable development across the nation, encapsulated by the slogan 'Usawa ni Sawa'. He even went as far as advocating for two terms for the President, linking his tenure to the very idea of equality. It’s a narrative that conveniently glosses over the daily struggles many face, the kind of struggles that make the "audio hustle" seem like a more tangible pursuit than lofty political promises.

Nakuru County, according to Kindiki, has been a major beneficiary of the government's development agenda, with billions allocated to modern markets, affordable housing, and road construction. He cited extensive road projects and the last-mile electricity connection program as evidence of this nationwide push for progress. The numbers are impressive, no doubt, but the question remains whether this development truly addresses the deep-seated inequalities that persist.

Kindiki reiterated that the government is building an "equitable Kenya," free from discrimination based on wealth, ethnicity, religion, or gender. He emphasized that all Kenyans are "one community and one nation." While such rhetoric aims to foster a sense of belonging, it often rings hollow when the reality on the ground involves persistent challenges and a cynical view of political promises.

Joining the DP were local leaders, including Bahati MP Irene Njoki and Nakuru Town East MP David Gikaria, who are undoubtedly on board with this vision of a united and developing Kenya. Their presence underscored the government's commitment to engaging with local representatives as they push forward with their agenda.
