So, they've built Raila Odinga a mausoleum. And surprise, surprise, it's pentagonal. Apparently, this isn't just some architect getting creative with concrete; it's all meant to be some grand gesture reflecting his "raised fist" and "unity." Samson Malaki from the KDF is out here explaining how the five fingers of that fist inspired the five sides. Because, you know, nothing screams unity like a clenched fist, right? It's a real stretch, even for Nairobi.

Then there's the whole daddy-son architectural harmony thing. Jaramogi's tomb is hexagonal, Raila's is pentagonal. They say it's all about continuity. Continuity? More like trying to one-up each other even in death, I reckon. This whole narrative about geometry and symbolism feels like a convenient way to add some gravitas to what's essentially just a burial site. We've seen this kind of political theatre before, especially when it comes to elections. Remember the "Pentagon" in 2007? Five prominent figures jostling for the ODM ticket. History repeats itself, they say, sometimes with more elaborate tombs.

The explanation about the raised fist not being a symbol of defiance but of unity is rich. Raila's political career has been a masterclass in drama, and this mausoleum, with its supposed symbolic meaning, is just the latest act. It's a convenient narrative, linking the physical structure to his public persona. But let's be honest, in this city, we've seen enough clout chasing come with a bill, and this feels like another one. [Clout Chasing Comes with a Bill: Cera Imani and the ODPC Reality Check](/posts/clout-chasing-comes-with-a-bill--cera-imani-and-the-odpc-reality-check).

Ultimately, whether it's a pentagon or a dodecahedron, it's still a tomb. The KDF officers can spin their tales of unity and continuity, but for many of us, it's just another layer of political symbolism that might not resonate with the everyday struggles of Kenyans. The real unity, and the real strength, would be in addressing the issues that keep people up at night, not in the shape of a final resting place.
