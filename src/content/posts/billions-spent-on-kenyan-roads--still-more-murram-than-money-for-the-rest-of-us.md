So, the government has apparently been throwing Sh1.9 trillion at roads over 15 years. Sh126 billion annually, on average. That's a lot of tarmac, a lot of concrete, and a lot of handshakes, I'm sure. They tell us it's the "single largest development spending in any sector." Of course, it is. When has anything actually trickled down to the likes of us from these grand projects? Mohamed Mahad in Turbi might be getting his water and snacks a bit easier, and the tourists might be gawking at Olo Lokwe Mountain, but let's not pretend this is some miraculous transformation. It's just more money moving around, and we all know who ends up holding the bag.

They brag about cutting travel time from three days to 12 hours between Moyale and Nairobi. Brilliant. Now you can get stuck in traffic on the Nairobi Expressway or a new bypass even faster. And the Thika Superhighway? Opened up residential and commercial areas, they say. Sure, for the real estate sharks and the developers who got fat contracts. For the rest of us, it just means more congestion and higher rents closer to the city. It’s all about who’s getting the tenders and who’s getting the land deals, not about whether *you* can get to work on time without selling a kidney. It reminds me of the political theatre we see daily, like [Hobbes, Ruto, Gachagua, and the Cheap Thrills of Political Theatre](/posts/hobbes--ruto--gachagua--and-the-cheap-thrills-of-political-theatre).

And let's not forget the numbers. They say road transport contributes 16% of the economy. Sh11.8 trillion out of Sh74 trillion in five years. Fantastic. That means more matatus and bodabodas crammed with more people, more trucks spewing fumes, and more chances of a nasty accident on those "improved" roads. Economists chirp about "cutting travel times and lowering the cost of production." I hear that a lot. What I don't hear is how that translates to a cheaper loaf of bread or more consistent power. It’s always about "economic growth" that seems to bypass the vast majority.

The truth is, 90% of Kenya's road network is still murram and earth. So while they're busy building these fancy highways for the tourists and the big cargo trucks, the villages are still mud pits. They've spent Sh1.9 trillion, but have they really opened up the *remotest* parts, or just the parts that offer the best kickbacks? These projects are good for the people who get the contracts, the people who own the land along the new routes, and maybe, just maybe, a few lucky vendors like Agnes Musee selling fruits by the roadside. For the rest of us, it's just another grand government spending spree with a flashy report to show for it.

They’re building roads from Isiolo to Garissa, to Wajir, to Mandera. Sounds impressive. But is it to genuinely connect communities, or to facilitate some new resource extraction that will benefit a select few? The exports to Ethiopia have doubled, they say. Great for the traders, but what about the everyday Kenyan who still struggles to get basic services? It’s all about opening up corridors for commerce, which usually means opening them up for exploitation. We’re supposed to be impressed by the sheer amount of money spent, as if that automatically equates to progress for everyone.

At the end of the day, it's the same old story. Billions are spent, promises are made, and the common mwananchi is left wondering where all that money actually went. They build a road, and suddenly land prices skyrocket, pushing out the very people who might have lived there for generations. It’s a predictable pattern. They pat themselves on the back for infrastructure development, while the real issues-healthcare, education, affordable living-continue to be neglected. But hey, at least the tourists have a smoother ride to the Olo Lokwe Mountain, right?
