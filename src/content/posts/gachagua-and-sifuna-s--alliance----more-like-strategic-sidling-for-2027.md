So, on Mashujaa Day, of all days, when we're supposed to be celebrating actual heroes, Rigathi Gachagua decides to have a cozy chat with Nairobi Senator Edwin Sifuna and his "Linda Mwananchi Movement" folks. Up in Nyeri, no less. Because when you're strategizing for the next election, what better place than the heart of the country, far away from the prying eyes and grumbling stomachs of the masses?

This whole "alliance" narrative they're trying to peddle? It's just another episode in the never-ending Kenyan drama of political theatre. They’re not rewriting any playbooks; they’re just shuffling the same deck of cards, hoping you won’t notice the worn edges. It’s all about optics and whispering sweet nothings about unity while the real game of thrones continues behind closed doors.

You see, this is how it works. While you’re wrestling with rising prices and questioning whether your hard-earned cash is worth more when you accept cookies or subscribe for a pittance, these guys are busy calculating their next move. It’s a constant hustle for power, and partnerships are as fleeting as a good Internet connection in Rongai.

It makes you wonder if any of this actually matters to anyone beyond the political elite. Remember that whole brouhaha about Western Mineral Sovereignty? [Western Mineral Sovereignty is a Multi-Billion Dollar Delusion](/posts/western-mineral-sovereignty-is-a-multi-billion-dollar-delusion) - it’s a similar story. Big words, grand pronouncements, and ultimately, very little change for the everyday Kenyan. This Gachagua-Sifuna powwow is just the latest act.

They'll talk about vision, about the future, about serving the people. But let’s be honest, it’s all about securing their positions. The "playbook" they're rewriting is probably just a revised version of "How to Stay in Power Using the Same Old Tricks." Don't get your hopes up for a revolution; just brace yourself for more political maneuvering.

The sad reality is that for most of us, this news is just noise. We're too busy trying to survive the day to dissect the nuances of Gachagua's latest handshake or Sifuna's latest pronouncements. It’s a world away from the daily grind, a world where "cookies" are a bargaining chip and "subscriptions" are for exclusive access to the same old political circus.
