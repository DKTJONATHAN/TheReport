Ruto's Ebola "Response": Another Day, Another Drama

The government's been flapping its wings, ordering an "enhanced response" after Kenya coughed up its first imported Ebola case, and naturally, the poor soul kicked the bucket right here in Nairobi. State House, in its infinite wisdom, tells us President Ruto has summoned the National Security Council. Because, you know, when a dead body shows up with a deadly virus, the first thing you do is call a meeting. Apparently, they'll "assess the situation" and "recommend measures." Groundbreaking stuff, really. Meanwhile, the Health CS, Aden Duale, confirmed the grim news. Just another Tuesday in the land of a thousand promises and one very real death.

Now they're in a frenzy, scrambling to do all the things they should have been doing anyway. "Contact tracing is ongoing," they chirp, like it's some kind of miracle cure. Eight family members, shoved into quarantine, being "monitored daily." Twenty-one healthcare workers, also under the microscope. And let's not forget the poor souls who shared a flight with the deceased - 27 of them, tracked down and presumably given a stern talking-to. It’s all very dramatic, very official, and I bet the paperwork is piling up faster than the excuses.

They buried the body safely, of course. Because that's the easy part, isn't it? The real challenge is figuring out how to manage this mess when it inevitably spreads further, despite their claims of "preparedness." They've screened over 650,000 travellers and tested a few hundred samples. Out of all that, just one positive - the dead guy. No other cases detected. Yet. They've trained nearly 5,000 healthcare workers, blessed them with knowledge. And they've got beds scattered across a few hospitals, ready for action. But honestly, how many of those beds will actually be clean and well-staffed when the real chaos hits? It’s a gamble, like everything else in this country.

The government's urging everyone to "remain calm and vigilant," to wash their hands and avoid anyone looking a bit peaky. You know, the usual song and dance. Fever, headache, vomiting - the whole grim parade. If you've been near someone who might have it, they say, "seek guidance and care." As if the public health system is exactly a well-oiled machine ready to spring into action. Remember how the tourism sector, which relies so heavily on people feeling safe, has been modernised but is still squeezed dry? ([Kenya Travel Agents: Modernised But Squeezed Dry](/posts/kenya-travel-agents--modernised-but-squeezed-dry)). This Ebola scare isn't exactly helping to fill those beds, is it? This whole charade feels like they’re just playing catch up, hoping for the best while preparing for the worst with all the confidence of a wet paper bag.
