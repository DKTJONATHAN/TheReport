TIME FOR WOMEN "BADDIES" TO BE ON THE BALLOT, OR WHAT?

This "women-focused" voter registration campaign? Another Ktalk, darling. They announce it, make a fuss, and then what? Same old story. Women, especially young ones, are supposed to get excited about "Baddies na Ballots". Sounds like a bad Nollywood movie plot. The idea is to break barriers for women and youth in politics. Cute. Let's see how many actual barriers actually crumble and how many just get a fresh coat of paint.

Historically, women have had to fight tooth and nail for the right to vote, often sparked by being excluded from other movements. Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott got their marching orders at an anti-slavery conference. Kate Sheppard rounded up 32,000 women in New Zealand because of booze, can you imagine? Russian women protesting for bread and peace, Nigerians fighting colonial taxes. And here in Kenya, women finally got to vote in 1963, all part of the grand hustle for freedom. Yet, despite all this "mass mobilisation", guess who wasn't in Kenya's first Parliament? Surprise, surprise, not a single woman.

And it's still the same song, different verse. This whole "two-thirds gender principle" shoved into the 2010 Constitution? A nice idea on paper, ignored in practice. Sixteen years later, and Kenya's progress is slower than a herd of turtles. We're at 75th place globally on the gender gap. While women might be rocking it in economic empowerment, forget education and health. They're barely 50% of voters, but look at the numbers in leadership: 14.9% Governors, 23% National Assembly, 31.3% Senate, 28% Cabinet. Only the Judiciary is almost there. It's like they're told to participate, but never to actually lead.

We've had court cases, landmark rulings, even deadlines set for governments to comply with the gender rule. But you know what the real problem is? It's not the laws, it's the raw, grubby political power. The current administration made promises in their June 2022 Women's Charter - cabinet parity, two-thirds rule, economic empowerment. Four years later, we're still waiting. And now they're talking about confronting femicide, pedicide, and online abuse. About time. To get more women in politics, they need to tackle funding issues, the harassment, the digital bullying. Women shouldn't just be there to clap; they should be on the damn podiums. Let's call out these male speaker-only "mallies" for what they are: exclusive clubs. This reminds me of how voter registration targets are often a sham anyway, just like we saw in the `/posts/2027-vote-battle-zones--iebc-voter-enrolment-targets-a-sham` article.

This "Baddies na Ballots" thing needs to go beyond just getting names on the voter roll. They need to look at the *quality* of the women candidates. It's a disgrace that only a couple of our women MPs are even noticed for their contributions. The voters, male and female, need to ditch the dead weight and choose leaders with actual substance, not just pretty faces. Candidates, male or female, who haven't got a clue about empowering women or moving this nation forward should be sent packing.

So, while they launch their campaign at the Uwazi Festival, let's hope it's more than just hot air. We want millions more "baddie" women not just in the queues, but actually making decisions. It's not about honouring Muthoni Likimani, though she's a legend. It's about ensuring that the sacrifices of generations of women finally translate into real power, not just more empty promises.
