So, the Kenya Kwanza brigade, led by Speaker Wetangula and the ever-present Farouk Kibet, decided to grace Likuyani with their presence. Apparently, they're on a mission to convince the good people of Western Kenya to sign up for President Ruto's "tutam" (two-term) brigade. Because, you know, unity, development, and inclusive leadership are best served with a side of re-election pitches.

Kibet, bless his heart, is urging leaders to ditch divisive politics for unity. Meanwhile, they're all here telling Western Kenya residents to back Ruto for a second term. It’s the usual song and dance. Wetangula is warning against political intolerance, saying Kenya is bigger than individual interests. Yet, here they are, pushing a specific political interest. We’ve heard this before, haven't we? It’s like that [Paper Peace Discount](/posts/the-paper-peace-discount--why-sub-100-dollar-oil-is-a-deadly-mirage) - sounds good on paper, but the reality is a lot less rosy.

The Western Kenya leaders are now apparently ready to take over the "national leadership mantle" after Ruto. Malava MP David Ndakwa is practically holding out his hand for the baton. Navakholo MP Emmanuel Wangwe chimes in, wanting to "enjoy the benefits of government" and thanking Ruto for development. It’s always about the benefits, isn't it? And the "development" often seems to be a convenient talking point for political mileage.

Butere lawmaker Tindi Mwale adds his two cents, saying Kenyans want development-oriented leaders, not divisive ones. Pot, meet kettle. They're all citing "various development initiatives" to rally support. It’s the same playbook: promise the moon, point to a few paved roads, and hope everyone forgets the tough economic realities. Remember when [Ruto's Akorino funding pledge](/posts/ruto-s-akorino-funding-pledge-under-scrutiny-as-gachagua-claims-sh100mn-cash-deal) was making headlines? That's the kind of "development" talk we often get.

Ultimately, this is just another political gathering, a show of force disguised as a call for unity and progress. They preach peace while subtly maneuvering for power, and they talk development while the [Kenya's Jobs Crisis](/posts/kenya-s-jobs-crisis--long-term-economic-stability-remains-a-mirage) continues to loom large for many. Let’s see how much of this "unity" actually translates into tangible change for the average Kenyan.

And Citizen Digital wants my perspective? Fine. My perspective is that until these leaders start delivering on the basics - jobs, affordable living, and actual, tangible progress beyond election cycles - their calls for unity and development will remain just that: empty words echoing in a rally ground. Now, if you'll excuse me, I need to go find some real development, like a matatu that actually arrives on time.
