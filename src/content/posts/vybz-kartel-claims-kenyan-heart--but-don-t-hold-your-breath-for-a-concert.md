So, Vybz Kartel, the dancehall chap from Jamaica, has apparently decided he's one of us now. Stoked by his 90.8 million YouTube views in Kenya - more than his home turf, mind you - he's declared himself an "honorary Kenyan." The internet, naturally, is going wild, tossing him names like Mwangi and Onyango. It’s almost as if a few million YouTube views are enough to grant you citizenship.

Meanwhile, as Kenyans celebrate their newest, most distant ‘compatriot,’ they’re also busy asking why he’s never graced a Kenyan stage. You'd think with all this love, he'd at least consider a gig here. But then again, that would involve actual effort, wouldn't it? Better to bask in the digital adoration from afar while performing in some muddy field in the UK. This whole "honorary Kenyan" thing feels a lot like those empty promises politicians make before elections.

And in other news that’s supposed to be heartwarming, some Boni guy from Nairobi tore down his mum's old house and built her a new one overlooking Lake Victoria. Lovely, I guess. It’s a nice story about family and all that jazz. It also conveniently distracts from the fact that many Kenyans are still struggling to afford basic housing, let alone a lakeside mansion. It’s good for the mum, though. She can watch the boats sail in from Uganda. Thrilling.

Then there's the tale of siblings who pooled cash for a Ksh 9.5 million mansion for their mum in Kiambu. Apparently, they lost her home years ago and this is their grand gesture. Look, it's great they did it, but let's not pretend this is a common occurrence. Most families are just trying to keep the lights on. It’s nice to see some people can afford to throw millions at sentimentality. You know, some of us are still trying to get our own houses built without needing a miracle or a windfall, unlike these lucky souls who can just spend big. It makes you wonder about the economic gains President Ruto is always talking about; they certainly aren't trickling down to everyone.

On top of all this, Kartel also dropped a bombshell at Reggae Land - he and his fiancée are expecting. So, while Kenyans are busy giving him fake names, he's busy expanding his empire. It's all very convenient for him, isn't it? He gets to claim Kenyan love without the burden of actually performing here. It’s just another reminder that in this world, it’s often the loud declarations and the social media buzz that matter, not the actual substance.

At least the entertainment editor at TUKO.co.ke, DanVictor Munene, is busy with his work. He's probably still recovering from covering those Gen-Z protests. Racheal Nyaguthie, the Head of Human Interest, is also on the job, no doubt wading through a sea of similarly heartwarming, yet ultimately forgettable, stories. Good for them. Someone's got to report on all this manufactured joy.
