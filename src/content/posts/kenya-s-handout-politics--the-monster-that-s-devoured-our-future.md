Raila Odinga, bless his departed soul, was one of the few who saw this coming. He refused to be the man everyone ran to with their extended palms, asking "Whose cow are you that you demand to be untied?" He called it gonya, and he rightly denounced the sham of paying crowds to cheer or heckle. But his honesty only earned him a reputation for stinginess in a country that's increasingly addicted to quick cash. Now, what was once a bribe for attendance has become an expectation, a price tag on every single interaction, pushing us towards a political marketplace where ideas are worthless.

The situation has gone from bad to worse, folks. Politicians are no longer just paying for applause; they're facing demands just to be heard, to leave a venue, or even to let their vehicles pass. Dr. Oburu Oginga, who should know better at his age, got robbed of Sh200,000 right out of his bag at a fundraiser, while his bodyguards tried to save another Sh3 million. Then there was the chaos in Nyando where one person got killed, a bodyguard beaten, and a helicopter pilot threatened. It’s a circus, and these politicians, who helped create this mess by treating politics like a business, are now crying foul.

Even established figures like Fred Matiang'i are complaining about being hounded in supermarkets, confronted with demands for "one term" or "the sixth." It's a culture of blackmail, pure and simple. But here’s the kicker: some leaders, like Lang'ata MP Phelix Jalang'o, openly admit you have to be "eaten" - meaning, give money. Homa Bay Governor Gladys Wanga agrees, saying if you're stingy, nobody will listen to you. This is the paradox that’s choking our politics: leaders complaining about cash demands while simultaneously believing refusing to give money carries a political death sentence. It's a vicious cycle, and frankly, the way things are going, we might as well start charging people to vote. The [fuel scandal](posts/fuel-scandal--company-reveals-how-it-lost-ksh3-2b-after-govt-cancellation-order) showed us how billions can vanish; now it's personal pockets being emptied for a fleeting moment of political relevance.

The upcoming 2027 elections are shaping up to be a disaster. We're not talking about electoral bribery here, but the sheer impossibility of managing political gatherings when people are fighting over Sh200 after a day of dancing, or when residents get mattresses and gas cylinders without even showing up. These aren't rallies anymore; they're welfare distribution points, where political participation is a survival tactic for the unemployed and the cost of living is just too damn high. Unless politicians stop treating cash as the only currency for attendance, applause, and loyalty, we’re heading for a democracy where only the wealthy can truly "participate." And that, my friends, is a future none of us should want.
