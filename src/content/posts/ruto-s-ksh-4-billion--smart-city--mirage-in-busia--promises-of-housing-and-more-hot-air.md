So, President Ruto’s been busy playing elder in Eldoret, getting himself a new name - "Ojokotau," which they say means "someone with a big heart." Right after this spiritual moment, he’s back to the usual campaign trail talk, this time in Malaba, where he’s promising a Ksh.4 billion "smart city." Apparently, this place will have 2,000 affordable housing units and look less like a "shopping centre" and more like, well, a city. Sounds familiar, doesn't it? It’s the same old song and dance about jobs, business opportunities, and luring investors. One wonders if this is just another chapter in Kenya's Digital Hub Dreams, or if there’s actual substance beneath the spin. [Kenya's Digital Hub Dreams: Another Summit, More Hot Air](/posts/kenya-s-digital-hub-dreams--another-summit--more-hot-air)

The President also mentioned that Busia County is getting a cool Ksh.18 billion for housing, markets, and hostels. He proudly states that the Affordable Housing Programme has created 1.1 million jobs. For those paying the 1.5 per cent housing levy, he assures them they haven't lost out because they've also enjoyed a 10 per cent salary increase. It's a neat little package, isn't it? A bit of give and take, or so they say. And let's not forget the Social Health Authority registration drive - 449,000 people in Busia are now on board. Progress, perhaps, or just another tick on a government checklist.

Then comes the predictable political rhetoric about tribal politics being a thing of the past. Ruto claims Kenyans have moved beyond that, embracing issue-based politics since 2022, and that the "Broad-Based Government" has eliminated discrimination. He's confident that in next year's election, leaders will be judged on their development track record, not empty promises or ethnic loyalties. He's even taken a jab at some politicians he calls "puppets" funded by an unnamed "master" who, apparently, couldn't do the job Ruto is now doing. This is getting richer than a State House banquet.

He’s urging Kenyans to reject his "puppets," who he claims have no vision beyond fulfilling their sponsor's wishes. Apparently, these critics just keep shouting "Ruto must go" without offering any real solutions. The upcoming election, he insists, will be decisive, ending the "stale politics of division" and ushering in unity and transformation. It's all very inspiring, if you don't look too closely at the fine print, or the track record of political promises in this country.

The Iteso leaders, predictably, are singing his praises, pledging their support and unity. They've chosen "partnership" and "participation in building Kenya." They’re looking forward to road projects and are united in supporting his re-election. It's a classic show of solidarity, often seen when development promises are on the table. Whether this unity translates into tangible, lasting progress for the community or just another political photo-op remains to be seen.

Ultimately, while the promise of a "smart city" and improved living conditions is appealing, it’s hard not to be a little cynical. We’ve heard grand pronouncements before, seen ambitious projects announced with fanfare, only to fade into the background. The installation as an elder, the new name, the promises of development - it all paints a picture. But in Nairobi, we’ve learned to watch what happens after the cameras leave, not just listen to the speeches.
