Matiang'i Cries Foul Over New Police Uniform Tender - Another Day, Another Shakedown?





So, Fred Matiang'i, the guy who used to run the show at Interior, is now out here squawking about the new police uniforms. Apparently, a company magically popped into existence in 2024 and by 2026, it’s already snagging a massive government contract to kit out the entire police force. Matiang'i, with his usual dramatic flair, is calling it "wizi na uporaji wa mali ya serikali" - theft and plunder of government property. You don't say! It’s almost as if these deals are never straightforward.

He's even gone on record saying that if he ever gets the top job, he'd never do business with the government. "Mi ni rais naenda kushona uniform ya polisi," he scoffed. Well, that’s rich, coming from someone who’s been in the thick of it. It’s a familiar song and dance, isn't it? Every politician cries foul when they're not the ones in charge of the cookie jar. Meanwhile, President Ruto is out there unveiling the fancy new sky-blue shirts and dark-blue trousers, pretending it's all above board.

The National Police Service, naturally, is denying any shenanigans, claiming this "Nalitex Limited" thing is just noise and that "Bedi Investments Limited" actually got the nod after a rigorous process involving local mills. They're touting the "Buy Kenya, Build Kenya" policy, as if that’s some magical shield against graft. It’s convenient how these denials always surface just when the heat is on. It reminds me of the endless debates around fuel prices, where CS Opiyo Wandayi is always left answering the tough questions.

Matiang’i, however, is throwing shade by comparing it to his own tenure. He claims they used the National Youth Service and local workshops for uniforms back in 2018, saving a cool Ksh700 million. He’s also bringing up the price of boots and bulletproof vests, highlighting the massive markups on imported goods versus locally sourced items. His point? You can't be both the referee and the player. But in Kenya, that's pretty much the whole game, isn't it?

Even former Chief Justice David Maraga is chiming in with concerns about the implementation of these uniform reforms. The government insists it’s a gradual rollout, starting with new recruits. But let’s be real, these big contracts always have more layers than a well-made chapati, and usually, someone’s pockets are getting lined somewhere along the way, much like the whispers surrounding those critical minerals deals. It’s the Kenyan way, I suppose.
