So, seven souls, including five Americans, decided Samburu was the place to be on a Wednesday morning. Turns out, a Eurocopter EC130 B4 had other plans, deciding to introduce itself to the ground near Lolokwe Mountain. The U.S. Embassy, ever so concerned, is "in touch" and offering "consular assistance." Translation: they'll send some paperwork and offer condolences to the families who likely paid a fortune for this "experience."

Among the dearly departed are folks like Ecuador's national intelligence chief and his wife, and some Telemundo bigwig. Apparently, even the movers and shakers aren't immune to a faulty rotor or a pilot who took a wrong turn. It's a real shame, isn't it? All that power, all that money, and all it gets you is a one-way ticket to the afterlife, courtesy of a metal bird with a bad day.

The Kenya Civil Aviation Authority is, of course, launching an investigation. Because that's what they do. They'll tell us the cause was "under investigation" and "not yet determined." Meanwhile, the Kenya Red Cross is slogging through difficult terrain and an active fire to, you know, recover what's left. It's a real-life drama, minus the happy ending. It almost makes you wonder if these luxury tour operators, like &Beyond, are just shuffling the wealthy towards their pre-ordained doom, much like the [billion-shilling zombies]( /posts/billion-shilling-zombies--why-the-og-scammers-put-your-favorite-influencers-to-shame) who prey on the gullible.

The helicopter company, Lady Lori, is also mumbling about investigations. Because, frankly, what else can they say? "Oops, our bad, hope you didn't have too many plans"? The U.S. Embassy even managed to correct its count of dead Americans, moving from four to five. Details, details. What's a dead American between friends, right?

This whole affair just solidifies what we already know: life is cheap, especially when you're flying in a luxury chopper over Kenya. The views might be breathtaking, but so is the fall. And while officials offer platitudes, the families are left with a gaping hole and a hefty bill for a funeral, assuming they can even identify the remains.

So, another tragic incident, another round of official statements, and another reminder that sometimes, the best way to experience Kenya is from the ground, where the only thing crashing is your bank account from the cost of a local beer. At least that's a predictable kind of disaster.
