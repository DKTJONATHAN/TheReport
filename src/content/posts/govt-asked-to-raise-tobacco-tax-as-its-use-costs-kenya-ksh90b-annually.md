GOVERNMENT GAZING AT KSH90 BILLION TOBACCO HOLE WHILE YOUTH GET HOOKED

Here we go again. Another year, another report screaming about how much tobacco is bleeding Kenya dry - Ksh75 billion to Ksh90 billion annually. Public health gurus, bless their earnest hearts, are flapping their arms about how e-cigarettes and nicotine pouches are making it worse, especially for the young ones. As if we didn't see this coming from a mile away.

It’s the same old song and dance. Stakeholders are trotting out data, wagging fingers at Parliament, and begging the government to get serious. Rachel Kitonyo from the Tobacco Control and Development Institute is practically weeping over that Ksh75-90 billion figure, asking what wonders it could do elsewhere. Meanwhile, the average Kenyan smoker is apparently blowing Ksh3,600 a month on cigarettes, adding to the misery of medical bills and lost productivity. It’s a self-inflicted wound, this one.

The real kicker? Kids. Ten to seventeen year olds, apparently 622,000 of them have dabbled, and 244,000 are currently puffing. And don't even get me started on universities, where it’s a free-for-all of tobacco, alcohol, pouches, and vapes. We're just handing them addiction on a silver platter, while our legislators fumble with bills that are probably already obsolete by the time they’re printed. Young Kenyans' dreams of traditional jobs are dead, and now they’re just trying to survive the next craving. [Young Kenyans' Dream of Traditional Jobs is Dead: A Nairobi Realist's Take](/posts/young-kenyans--dream-of-traditional-jobs-is-dead--a-nairobi-realist-s-take)

Ken Michuki, from some institute or other, is talking about strengthening protections with this Tobacco Control Amendment Bill 2024. Good luck with that. The system is designed to be slow and inefficient. They’ll debate it to death, water it down, and by the time it’s law, the next generation of addictive products will be on the shelves. It's all very noble, I’m sure, but when has a bill actually stopped the flow of money or the spread of bad habits in this country?

So, the experts want stronger taxes, more awareness, and targeted interventions. That’s the standard playbook. But let's be real, when have these ‘expert’ recommendations ever led to a drastic reduction in the cost of a problem like this? It’s a cycle of hand-wringing and token gestures. We’ll be having this same conversation in ten years, with even higher numbers and even more exotic ways to poison ourselves.

The government is asked to raise tobacco tax because the current habit costs Kenya Ksh90B annually.
