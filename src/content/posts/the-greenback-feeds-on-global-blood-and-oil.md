Japan's situation is particularly pathetic. For months, Tokyo has engaged in a performance of "verbal intervention," a polite term for shouting at a tidal wave. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama can reiterate readiness all she wants, but the market knows a toothless tiger when it sees one. The yen is languishing at 40-year lows because the Bank of Japan is too terrified to move, and the U.S. Treasury's "advice" for them to hike rates is little more than a colonial mandate to protect American interests.

Speculators are not villains in this story; they are simply the only ones being honest about the yen's trajectory. As strategist Thierry Wizman noted, the yen is a low-yielding currency facing a terms-of-trade shock. It is the perfect victim. While the U.S. remains insulated from the energy shocks that are currently crippling Europe and Asia, the rest of the world is forced to import inflation along with their oil. This is the reality of a dollar-denominated world: when the U.S. starts a fire, it sells the world the extinguishers at a premium.

Even as nations like Kenya attempt to clean up their acts - as seen in the push to exit the financial crime grey list (/posts/kenya-intensifies-fight-against-financial-crime-in-push-to-exit-grey-list---the-star-co-ke) - they are still swimming against a current controlled by the Federal Reserve. The "cool" inflation data from June was a mirage, quickly evaporated by the heat of escalating conflict in the Middle East. The Fed's patience is wearing thin, and the "hawkish dissents" mentioned by J.P. Morgan economists suggest that the interest rate pain is far from over.

Europe is trying to maintain a facade of calm, with Philip Lane calling the inflation shock "medium-sized." This is the kind of bureaucratic delusion that precedes a collapse. You cannot call an energy-driven inflationary spiral "medium-sized" when your populace is choosing between heating and eating. The reality is that the global economy is a rigged game where the house - the U.S. dollar - always wins, and the Japanese yen is just the latest player to be escorted out the back door after losing its shirt.
