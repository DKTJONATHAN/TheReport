South Africa Court Halts Ramaphosa Impeachment Over Stolen Farm Cash

The wheels of justice grind slowly, and sometimes they stop altogether for the politically connected. South Africa's President Cyril Ramaphosa gets a temporary reprieve from impeachment proceedings over a scandal involving a hefty sum of cash, conveniently stolen from his farm. Apparently, keeping Ksh74.8 million under a sofa isn't enough to send a president packing.

The Western Cape High Court has hit the pause button on the impeachment, but don't get too excited. This doesn't mean the committee can't prep for the main event. Lawmakers were already sniffing around after a parliamentary report suggested Ramaphosa had a case to answer. Questions linger about where all that foreign currency came from, if it was declared, and why the police weren't immediately alerted to the missing millions. It's almost as if some people think the rules don't apply to them.

Ramaphosa, bless his heart, is challenging the report and wants this whole impeachment mess sorted *after* his court review. His office chirped about respecting judicial independence, which is rich given the circumstances. This "Phala Phala scandal" has been hanging over him like a bad Nairobi rain cloud since 2018. The whole affair is a classic case of money hidden in plain sight, raising eyebrows about legality and transparency.

This whole circus has been going on for ages. An independent panel saw enough red flags to warrant this inquiry, and even the Constitutional Court, the highest in the land, said impeachment proceedings could move forward. Yet, here we are, with a court order putting the brakes on. One wonders if this is just another way to delay the inevitable, or perhaps a carefully orchestrated maneuver. It makes you think about how systems designed to hold power accountable can sometimes become just another tool for the powerful. You'd think by now, after all the talk about corruption and accountability, we'd see some real action, not just more legal wrangling. It’s enough to make you want to bury your head in the sand, or maybe just focus on something more straightforward, like the [carbon tax]( /posts/the-carbon-tax-guillotine-and-the-end-of-cheap-bulk) making everything more expensive.
