The move toward a 24-team playoff by 2027 is the final nail in the coffin of regional relevance. This handshake agreement with ESPN mimics the same short-sighted greed that led to the NASCAR implosion. By expanding the field, the suits are effectively telling the global market that the regular season is a meaningless preamble. It is a desperate attempt to manufacture content for a television network that treats human athletes as mere pixels in a programming block. 

Even the individual accolades, like the Heisman race led by Jeremiah Smith, feel like market-driven projections rather than athletic assessments. The focus on specific schools like an-Ohio-State or the constant presence of Fox in Bloomington highlights a top-heavy system that is cannibalizing itself. This provincial obsession with a few brands makes the product less appealing to an international audience that values meritocracy over marketing.

As stadiums potentially empty and the bowl system disappears, we are witnessing the NASCAR-ification of a once-unique cultural export. The decline in viewership from 19.4 million to a mere 2.45 million in stock car racing should be a warning, but the greedy a-holes running the show are too blinded by immediate broadcast rights fees to see the precipice. We peer into the abyss, and the abyss is a 24-team playoff sponsored by a debt-saturated media empire.

Check the broader context of this systemic failure in our previous analysis: /posts/the-debt-saturated-mirage-of-digital-wealth

The world is watching a once-great spectacle turn into a series of gaffes and derps orchestrated by suits who do not care if the stadium is empty or if the game clock actually hit zero. When officiating becomes fishy and phantom penalties decide games like Miami-FSU, the international community sees not a sport, but a failing state-sponsored theater. This is the reality of the global realist: nothing is sacred, and everything is for sale until the buyers finally stop watching.
