So, the Kenyan central bank, in its infinite wisdom, has decided to keep its finger firmly on the "maintain status quo" button. The benchmark lending rate remains untouched, because why rock the boat when you can just let it bob aimlessly? They claim their policy is "appropriate," which is a fancy way of saying they've got no clue what else to do. And of course, they'll be "monitoring" global oil prices. As if that's news. We all know the middle east conflict is already making life a hell of a lot more expensive, but hey, at least the central bank is *monitoring* it.

The "growth forecasts" are also unchanged. Which, let's be honest, is probably the most accurate part of the whole announcement. Growth in this country often feels like a mythical creature - people talk about it, but nobody's actually seen it in action lately. It’s about as likely as the Iran war not forcing Kenya's economy to shake, which, as we've seen, is a joke.

They're babbling about "second-round effects on inflation." Translation: the cost of your basic *mandazi* and *chai* is still going to climb, but don't expect them to do much about it beyond issuing stern warnings. It's the same old song and dance, with the same predictable outcome for the average Kenyan just trying to survive.

This whole announcement is just another piece of theatre for the "business leaders, investors and decision-makers" who probably already have their escape routes planned. For the rest of us, it's just more waiting, more watching prices go up, and more hoping that one day, things might actually get better. But judging by this central bank's approach, hope is a commodity we can't afford to invest in.
