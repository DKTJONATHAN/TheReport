RUTO'S GRAND 'FUTURE' PLAN IS ANOTHER SHAMEY DELUSION FOR KENYANS









So, William Ruto wants to talk about "Kenya's Future Beyond Vision 2030." How quaint. While millions of us are still trying to figure out where tomorrow's ugali will come from, the President is busy sketching out grand plans for 2060. It’s the same old song and dance, isn’t it? Promise the moon while the earth crumbles beneath our feet. Mutula Kilonzo Junior is right - who needs a faraway dream when the present is a nightmare?

The timing is particularly laughable. Vision 2030 is barely a twinkle in its own eye, with no one having the guts to admit how much of a colossal flop it’s been. Yet, here we are, already dreaming up Vision 2060. It's like trying to build a skyscraper on quicksand. As Tetu MP Geoffrey Wandeto so aptly put it, corruption is the elephant in the room, ready to devour any blueprint, no matter how shiny. We haven't even done a proper autopsy on Vision 2030, but the vultures are already circling for the next carcass.

Then there's the constitutional side show. Lawyer Harrison Kinyanjui is sniffing around, suggesting this whole 2060 thing might be tied to some African Union agenda, or worse, a sneaky way to rewrite the constitution. Stephen Mutoro from Cofek is rightly warning against this. Trying to entrench some "mandatory vision" into our constitution is pure delusion. Our founding document, with its checks and balances, isn't some flimsy piece of paper to be scribbled on by politicians chasing the next big idea.

The opposition, bless their hearts, are asking for the bare minimum: an honest report card on Vision 2030. Ndegwa Njiru is calling out the unfulfilled promises, the stuff that should be basic rights, not distant aspirations. But the Kenya Kwanza crew are having none of it, claiming they're thinking about future generations, not next year's politics. Oh, please. It’s always about the next election, the next opportunity to distract us with pipe dreams while they pilfer the present.

Meanwhile, the Democracy for Citizens Party, led by Rigathi Gachagua, is calling out Ruto for breaking his "covenant." They're talking about the cost of living, taxes, jobs, and debt - the things that actually matter to us on the ground. They're questioning the government's claims of a booming economy when universities are begging for funds and healthcare is a mess. They’re asking for evidence, for measurable improvements in the lives of ordinary Kenyans. But the government-allied MPs are too busy deflecting, arguing about trivialities instead of facing the harsh reality. It’s all smoke and mirrors, just like those "corporate parasites demanding a war-chest for the coming collapse."

Ultimately, it boils down to this: Kenyans want money in their pockets, not fancy speeches about decades to come. They want to feel the economy in their homes, shops, and farms. Until they see tangible change, until the promises are delivered and the corruption is tackled head-on, any talk of Vision 2060 or beyond is just noise. It’s another grand deception, another way to keep us looking up at the stars while our pockets are being picked clean.
