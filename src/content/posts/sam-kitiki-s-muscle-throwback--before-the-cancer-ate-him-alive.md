So, Sam Kitiki, the guy who used to trim the hair of Nairobi's "celebrities," has decided to remind everyone what he looked like before his stomach decided to stage a rebellion. He’s posted this old video of himself flexing in the gym. Big muscles, tattoos, the whole "I care about my body" routine. It’s the kind of clip you’d see and probably forget about five seconds later. But now, knowing he's battling stage-three gastrointestinal stromal tumour - which sounds like a fancy way of saying aggressive stomach cancer - it’s supposed to hit harder.

Apparently, this rare and aggressive cancer has whittled him down to a shocking 29 kilograms. Twenty-nine! That's less than some of my aunties weigh after a heavy meal. He’s lost all the muscle he so proudly displayed, and now he’s out here pleading for cash. It’s a classic Nairobi story, isn't it? You’re on top, everyone wants a piece of you, and then life throws you a curveball, and suddenly you’re the charity case.

He’s trying to find some normalcy by going back to his barbershop when he can. Good for him, I guess. Anything to avoid dwelling on the fact that your body is literally eating itself. But let’s be real, the real normalcy he craves is a functioning bank account, not just the illusion of it. He’s asking for financial and emotional support, and of course, the usual suspects - the celebrities he used to service - are chiming in. It’s a pity party, but with a hefty price tag for medical bills.

This isn't the first time we’ve seen the rich and famous suddenly needing the public's help when illness strikes. It’s a cycle as predictable as a Bahati flaunting his wife’s spending habits. The kicker here is that his health, his looks, and his ability to earn a living are all gone. He’s left with nothing but memories of his former glory and a desperate need for treatment he can’t afford. It's a harsh reminder that even with all the fame and flashy haircuts, when the body breaks down, we’re all just flesh and bone, and often, just broke.
