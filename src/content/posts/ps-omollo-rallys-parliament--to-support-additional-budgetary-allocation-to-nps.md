PS Omollo Rallies Parliament for More NPS Cash - Because Who Needs Teachers When You Have More Cops?

Nairobi's cynical realism is on full display as Interior Principal Secretary Raymond Omollo trips over himself to sing President Ruto's praises in Nyanza. Apparently, opening a police station is now the pinnacle of "equitable development," and we're supposed to believe it's not about politics. Oh, and MPs should just hand over more money for the police. Because clearly, the biggest problem facing Kenya is not enough uniforms and sirens, rather than, say, the thousands of young people wondering if they'll ever get a decent job or education.

The PS, bless his cotton socks, wants us to forget about "political concessions" and just focus on the "development record." He’s listing roads, teachers, and healthcare as if these are brand new concepts he just invented. Roads like the Chiga-Rabuor and Mamboleo-Chemelil-Miwani? They’ve been promised since before I was collecting bus fare, and now they’re the shining examples of Ruto’s benevolence. It’s a classic move: distract them with a few potholes filled and a shiny new police station while the real issues fester.

And then there's the whole police recruitment spiel. Over 10,000 new officers in a year, and he wants *more*. Apparently, two fresh faces at Manyatta 'B' Police Station are going to magically solve Kisumu East's "security challenges." It sounds like a scene straight out of a bad movie where the solution to every problem is more heavily armed people, ignoring the root causes. This reminds me of the endless chase for digital solutions, like [AI Tools Now Capable of Unmasking Anonymous Accounts, Exposing Digital Cowards](/posts/ai-tools-now-capable-of-unmasking-anonymous-accounts--exposing-digital-cowards) - a lot of noise, but does it actually change anything on the ground for the average mwananchi?

The highlight, though, has to be the Sh3,000 monthly stipend for village elders. A grand total of three thousand bob to appreciate their "peacebuilding" and "dispute resolution." For real? Meanwhile, MPs are debating budget allocations for more police. It's a circus, I tell you. They throw a few coins at the elders to keep them quiet while pouring millions into a system that seems more focused on control than actual solutions. It’s a classic example of putting a plaster on a gaping wound.

Omollo’s plea to MPs for more police funding, alongside the announcement of village elders getting a pittance, is peak Kenyan politics. It's all about appearances and placating different groups with minor gestures, all while the bigger picture is ignored. He warns against violence and property destruction as political temperatures rise, which is rich coming from a government that seems to be fanning the flames with its own political maneuvering. They preach peace while quietly setting up the next round of resource grabs.

So, the message is clear: support Ruto’s "development," don't ask for political favors, and be grateful for the crumbs. The roads are getting built (slowly), the police numbers are swelling (at what cost?), and the village elders get enough for a few extra packets of ugali. Just don't expect any real change for the masses. It's all about the optics, baby.
