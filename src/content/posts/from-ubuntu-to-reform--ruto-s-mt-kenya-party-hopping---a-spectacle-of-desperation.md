So, the UDA is becoming as popular in Mt Kenya as a tax hike at a funeral. President Ruto’s allies, bless their opportunistic hearts, are now busy grabbing the reins of any alternative party they can find. It’s a grand strategy, really: pretend to be a fresh face while secretly shoring up Ruto’s chances for another term. They think we’re fools, easily swayed by new party names when the same old faces are doing the grabbing.

Remember how they swept the region in 2022? All those seats, all those promises. Now, the cost of living is so high, people are choosing cars over bars - a clear sign we're broke and scared, not happy. The government’s promises are gathering dust, and the whispers about Rigathi Gachagua’s impeachment haven’t exactly made anyone feel more secure. The grip is loosening, and they know it.

This whole charade of forming new parties smells of the same old political theatre we’ve seen before. It’s all about optics, not substance. They’re hoping a new coat of paint will hide the rot underneath. But the voters aren’t buying it. They’ve heard the speeches, they’ve felt the pinch, and they’re not easily impressed by a fresh party logo when the fundamental problems remain unsolved.

This isn't about Ubuntu or reform; it's about survival. It’s about politicians clinging to power by any means necessary, even if it means shedding one political skin for another. The cynicism is palpable, and frankly, it’s exhausting. We’re left watching the same old players in slightly different costumes, hoping we won’t notice the strings being pulled from behind the scenes. It’s a desperate bid to stay relevant, and it’s as transparent as a cheap plastic bag in a Nairobi downpour.
