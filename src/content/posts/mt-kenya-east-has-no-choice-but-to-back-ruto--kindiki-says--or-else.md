Mt Kenya East Has No Choice But To Back Ruto, Kindiki Says (Or Else)
The Deputy President is laying down the law for Mt Kenya East, telling them that supporting William Ruto's re-election in 2027 isn't a suggestion, it's a mandate. Apparently, God has remembered them and they've been blessed. Right.

Deputy President Kithure Kindiki, speaking at some elders' assembly in Embu - because that's where the real political decisions are made these days - declared that backing President William Ruto for a second term is "not an option" but "a must." He’s basically telling the region they have a duty to vote for Ruto, framing it as voting for their own interests. Convenient, isn't it? After all, who remembers being left behind and struggling more than the people who are now being told they *must* support the man who allegedly "remembered" them?

Kindiki’s whole spiel about unity between Mt Kenya East and West is starting to sound like a broken record. He’s spent months urging leaders to keep the region together, dismissing any talk of political blocs. Yet here he is, practically drawing a line in the sand, saying supporting Ruto is a "must." It's a classic political move: tell everyone they're one big happy family, then demand unwavering loyalty to a particular person. One can only wonder if this "unity" extends to sharing the spoils, or if it's just a one way street, much like how some parts of Nairobi are always left to [wade through sewage while others stay dry](/posts/nairobi-s-annual--swamp-edition---while-you-wade-through-sewage--your-faves-are-staying-dry-in-kilimani).

The Deputy President argues that voting for Ruto is voting for themselves. This sounds suspiciously like the same old song and dance we hear before every election. The "benefits" he claims Mt Kenya East has received are conveniently vague, and the "struggle" they were in before Ruto "remembered" them sounds like a convenient narrative to justify this "must" support. It’s a well-worn playbook, and one can only hope the residents of Mt Kenya East are seeing through the thinly veiled threats and the promise of divine intervention.

His remarks, of course, place him smack dab in the middle of the 2027 election contest. While he’s busy preaching unity and demanding loyalty, the reality is that political power shifts, and the "blessings" he mentions can dry up quicker than a Nairobi riverbed in August. The idea that a whole region has "no choice" but to back one individual is as insulting as it is predictable.

This whole "you must support Ruto because God has blessed you" line is just another flavor of the same old political stew. It's the same politicians, different year, same promises, and the same underlying pressure. Let's see if this "must" holds up when the real political maneuvering begins, or if it's just another empty promise in the grand theatre of Kenyan politics.
