Ruto's Foreign Traders Crackdown: Kenya Stirs Xenophobia Fears, Strains Burundi Ties

Summary: Kenya is kicking out foreign traders without permits. This is causing panic and making neighbours unhappy.

Caption: Another day, another policy to show who's boss.





So, President Ruto decides it's time to clean house, or at least that's what they're calling this whole "foreign traders out" drama. Ninety days, they've been given, to get their papers sorted or pack their bags and disappear. Like that's going to solve all of Kenya's problems. It's a classic move: blame the foreigner for everything that's wrong, while the real issues just keep festering. This isn't about making Kenya great again; it's about looking tough for the cameras.

This whole charade is already making the migrant communities here sweat. And naturally, it's got our neighbours, particularly Burundi, looking sideways at us. You think they appreciate being told their people are the problem? This kind of heavy-handedness rarely ends well. It just creates more tension, more suspicion. We've seen this kind of thing before, usually around election times or when the economy is doing a nosedive. It's a distraction, pure and simple.

And let's not forget the "support our editorial work" song and dance. You want independent news? Fine. But first, you have to surrender your privacy. They'll track your every click, your every thought, all in the name of "personalization" and "audience measurement." It's a Faustian bargain, really. Pay a euro and let them harvest your data, or get a watered-down experience. Just like everything else in this country, if you want the real deal, you have to give up something.

It’s not like this is the first time the government has shown its true colours. Remember that time the Presidential security was supposedly overhauled after some security lapse in Kilifi? [PRESIDENTIAL SECURITY OVERHAUL ORDERED BY POLICE IG KANJA AFTER KILIFI BREACH](/posts/presidential-security-overhaul-ordered-by-police-ig-kanja-after-kilifi-breach). They make a big show of doing something, but does it actually change anything for the average Kenyan? Doubtful. The powerful seem to get away with whatever they want, while the rest of us are left navigating the minefield of new rules and crackdowns.

This whole crackdown feels like a poorly thought out plan, just like many others that come out of these offices. They announce it with a bang, create a lot of noise, and then what? A few people get deported, a few businesses fold, and the whispers of xenophobia grow louder. It’s the Kenyan way: make a problem, then make a bigger problem trying to fix it, and then blame someone else. Just another Tuesday in Nairobi.
