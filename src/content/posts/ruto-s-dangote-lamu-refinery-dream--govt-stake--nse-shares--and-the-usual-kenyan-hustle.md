So, President Ruto is on X, bless his digital heart, telling us the Kenyan government will have a slice of Dangote's Lamu oil refinery. And not just any slice, mind you. The ordinary Kenyan, the one struggling to afford cooking oil, will apparently be able to buy shares on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Sounds like a generous gesture, doesn't it? Almost makes you forget about the price of maize flour.

This whole "people's shares" narrative is as old as time in this country. It's the same tune they play every time a big project comes around, promising prosperity for all while the usual players circle the wagons. We’ve heard it before, and if history is anything to go by, we’ll be hearing it again. It’s a classic case of telling people they can own a piece of the sky, when in reality, they’ll be lucky to get a cloud.

The Reuters report mentions this is all about reaching Africa's business leaders, investors, and decision-makers. Of course it is. Because that’s who actually benefits from these grand schemes, not the mwananchi queuing for fuel. It’s a story as old as time, much like [the great divorce between global unity and marketing scams](/posts/the-great-divorce--why-global-unity-is-a-marketing-scam), where grand pronouncements often hide a much less altruistic reality.

What’s truly telling is the timing. Just before an election, perhaps? Or is it a strategic move to distract from… well, everything else? We’ll see if this refinery actually materializes beyond the X posts and press releases. Because right now, it feels more like a well-placed announcement designed to create a buzz, rather than a concrete plan for tangible benefit. Let's just hope it doesn't end up being another white elephant.

And when it comes to "business stories shaping Africa," usually it's the same old song and dance. Promises, promises, and then a whole lot of waiting. Meanwhile, the cost of living continues its relentless march upwards, making those NSE shares seem as valuable as a politician's handshake. It’s a tough continent, and realism is often the only currency that truly holds its value.
